CHAPTER III  

第三章  

That night Mrs. Leigh stopped at home—that only night for many months. Even Tom, the scholar, looked up from his books in amazement; but then he remembered that Will had not been well, and that his mother's attention having been called to the circumstance, it was only natural she should stay to watch him. And no watching could be more tender, or more complete. Her loving eyes seemed never averted from his face—his grave, sad, careworn face. When Tom went to bed the mother left her seat, and going up to Will, where he sat looking at the fire, but not seeing it, she kissed his forehead, and said—"Will! lad, I've been to see Susan Palmer!"
那天晚上利太太没有出去——好几个月来的唯一一个晚上。甚至连汤姆这个学者，都诧异地从看着的书本中抬起头来；但是接着他想到威尔最近身体一直不太好，母亲的注意力已经被吸引到这件事情上了，因此她留下来照看他再自然不过了。没有比这更温柔、更无微不至的照看了。她慈爱的目光似乎从未从他的脸庞上移开过——他那凝重、伤心、憔悴的脸庞。当汤姆睡觉以后，这位母亲离开座位，走到威尔跟前，他坐在那里眼睛盯着炉火，眼神却不知道在何处，她亲了下他的前额，说道：“威尔！我的孩子，我去看过苏珊·帕尔默了！”
She felt the start under her hand which was placed on his shoulder, but he was silent for a minute or two. Then he said,—
她放在他肩膀的手感觉到了他身体的震颤，但是他沉默了有一两分钟。然后他说道：
"What took you there, mother?"
“你去那儿干什么，妈妈？”
"Why, my lad, it was likely I should wish to see one you cared for; I did not put myself forward. I put on my Sunday clothes, and tried to behave as yo'd ha' liked me. At least, I remember trying at first; but after, I forgot all."
“唉，我的孩子，我想去看看你喜欢的人是很正常的，我并不是要多管闲事。我穿上了我最好的衣服，尽量按照你希望的方式来表现自己。至少，我记得一开始是努力了，但是后来，我就全忘记了。”
She rather wished that he would question her as to what made her forget all. But he only said—
她有点儿期望他会问她，是什么让她全忘了。但他只是这样说：
"How was she looking, mother?"
“妈妈，她看起来怎么样？”
"Well, thou seest I never set eyes on her before; but she's a good, gentle-looking creature; and I love her dearly, as I've reason to."
“哦，你知道我以前从未见过她；但是她很好，看起来很温和；我很喜欢她，我有理由喜欢她。”
Will looked up with momentary surprise, for his mother was too shy to be usually taken with strangers. But, after all, it was naturally in this case, for who could look at Susan without loving her? So still he did not ask any questions, and his poor mother had to take courage, and try again to introduce the subject near to her heart. But how?
有那么一瞬间，威尔抬起头吃惊地望着她，因为他母亲一向很害羞，不会那么快喜欢上陌生人的。但这次的情况也算自然了，毕竟，有谁能见了苏珊不喜欢呢？因此，他仍然没有提出任何问题，他那可怜的母亲不得不鼓起勇气，再一次努力引导话题向她的内心靠拢。但是怎么做呢？
"Will!" said she (jerking it out in sudden despair of her own powers to lead to what she wanted to say), "I telled her all."
“威尔！”她说道（对自己引导话题能力的突然绝望让她脱口而出），“我把一切都告诉她了。”
"Mother! you've ruined me," said he, standing up, and standing opposite to her with a stern white look of affright on his face.
“妈妈！你毁了我。”他说道，站起身来，站在她对面，苍白严肃的脸上写满了恐惧的神色。
"No! my own dear lad; dunnot look so scared; I have not ruined you!" she exclaimed, placing her two hands on his shoulders, and looking fondly into his face. "She's not one to harden her heart against a mother's sorrow. My own lad, she's too good for that. She's not one to judge and scorn the sinner. She's too deep read in her New Testament for that. Take courage, Will; and thou mayst, for I watched her well, though it is not for one woman to let out another's secret. Sit thee down, lad, for thou look'st very white."
“不！我亲爱的孩子；别看起来那么害怕；我没有毁掉你！”她大声叫嚷着，双手放在他的肩膀上，温柔地盯着他的面庞，“她不会对一个母亲的悲伤硬起心肠来的。我的孩子，她太善良了。她也不会随便评判和蔑视犯错的罪人。她深谙新约中的教义。拿出你的勇气，威尔，你可以的，我留意观察过她了，尽管我不该泄露一个女人的秘密。你坐下，孩子，你的脸色看起来很苍白。”
He sat down. His mother drew a stool towards him, and sat at his feet.
他坐下了。母亲拉过来一条凳子，坐在他的脚边。
"Did you tell her about Lizzie, then?" asked he, hoarse and low.
“那么，你告诉她莉齐的事了吗？”他问道，声音嘶哑低沉。
"I did; I telled her all! and she fell a-crying over my deep sorrow, and the poor wench's sin. And then a light comed into her face, trembling and quivering with some new glad thought; and what dost thou think it was, Will, lad? Nay, I'll not misdoubt but that thy heart will give thanks as mine did, afore God and His angels, for her great goodness. That little Nanny is not her niece, she's our Lizzie's own child, my little grandchild." She could no longer restrain her tears; and they fell hot and fast, but still she looked into his face.
“我告诉她了，我全部都告诉她了！她为我那深深的悲伤，和我那可怜孩子的罪孽而流泪。接下来，她的脸上又有了光彩，因为一些令人欣慰的想法而发抖、震颤；威尔，孩子，你知道那是什么想法吗？不，我丝毫不怀疑你的内心将会和我一样，在上帝以及他的天使面前，感激她那伟大的美德。那个小南妮并不是她的外甥女，而是我们莉齐的孩子，我的小外孙女。”她再也无法抑制，热泪潸然而下，但是她仍然盯着他的面孔。
"Did she know it was Lizzie's child? I do not comprehend," said he, flushing red.
“她知道那是莉齐的孩子吗？我想不通。”他说道，脸色涨红。
"She knows now: she did not at first, but took the little helpless creature in, out of her own pitiful, loving heart, guessing only that it was the child of shame; and she's worked for it, and kept it, and tended it ever sin' it were a mere baby, and loves it fondly. Will! won't you love it?" asked she, beseechingly.
“她现在知道了：一开始她并不知情，但是出于慈悲和同情，她把这个无助的孩子带了回去，猜测她肯定是一个私生女；从她还只是个婴儿开始，她就为她而工作，抚养她，照顾她，而且非常喜爱她。威尔！你会爱她吗？”她乞求地问道。
He was silent for an instant; then he said, "Mother, I'll try. Give me time, for all these things startle me. To think of Susan having to do with such a child!"
他沉默了有一瞬间，然后说道：“妈妈，我会努力的。给我时间，这一切让我感到震惊。想想苏珊和这样一个孩子的关系！”
"Ay, Will! and to think, as may be, yet of Susan having to do with the child's mother! For she is tender and pitiful, and speaks hopefully of my lost one, and will try and find her for me, when she comes, as she does sometimes, to thrust money under the door, for her baby. Think of that, Will. Here's Susan, good and pure as the angels in heaven, yet, like them, full of hope and mercy, and one who, like them, will rejoice over her as repents. Will, my lad, I'm not afeard of you now; and I must speak, and you must listen. I am your mother, and I dare to command you, because I know I am in the right, and that God is on my side. If He should lead the poor wandering lassie to Susan's door, and she comes back, crying and sorryful, led by that good angel to us once more, thou shalt never say a casting-up word to her about her sin, but be tender and helpful towards one 'who was lost and is found;' so may God's blessing rest on thee, and so mayst thou lead Susan home as thy wife."
“可以，威尔！你也可以想想苏珊和这个孩子的母亲的关系！她是那么温柔和善良，当提到我那丢失的孩子，她也是满怀希望，并且会尽量在她过来时帮我找到她，因为她有时会过来在门下塞些钱给孩子。想想这些，威尔。这就是苏珊，善良，纯洁，有如天使下凡，和他们一样，充满了希望和仁慈，和他们一样，会为你妹妹的忏悔感到由衷的高兴。威尔，我的孩子，我现在不害怕你了；我必须得说，你也必须得听。我是你的母亲，我敢这样命令你，因为我知道我是正确的，上帝也站在我这边。如果上帝指引那可怜的流浪女来到苏珊的门前，由那个好心的天使再把她领回到我们身边，你不许对她的过错说一句攻击性的语言，要对这‘失而复得’的人温柔一点儿，尽量帮助她；这样上帝才会保佑你，你才可以把苏珊娶回来当妻子。”
She stood no longer as the meek, imploring, gentle mother, but firm and dignified, as if the interpreter of God's will. Her manner was so unusual and solemn, that it overcame all Will's pride and stubbornness. He rose softly while she was speaking, and bent his head, as if in reverence at her words, and the solemn injunction which they conveyed. When she had spoken, he said, in so subdued a voice that she was almost surprised at the sound, "Mother, I will."
她不再是那个懦弱、哀求、温顺的母亲，而是变得坚定、端庄，仿佛是上帝旨意的阐释者。她的举止是那么反常和庄严，压过了威尔所有的傲慢和固执。他在她说话的时候轻轻地站了起来，低下头，好像在向她所说的话以及它们所传达的庄严嘱托致敬。在她说完后，他说道：“妈妈，我会的。”他的声音是那么顺从，让她几乎感到有点儿震惊。
"I may be dead and gone; but, all the same, thou wilt take home the wandering sinner, and heal up her sorrows, and lead her to her Father's house. My lad! I can speak no more; I'm turned very faint."
“即使我离开了人世，你依然要把那个迷途的罪人带回家，抚慰她内心的创伤，引导她回到她父亲的家中。我的孩子！我不能再说了，我现在很虚弱。”
He placed her in a chair; he ran for water. She opened her eyes, and smiled.
他扶她坐在椅子上，跑去给她倒水。她睁开双眼，面带微笑。
"God bless you, Will. Oh! I am so happy. It seems as if she were found; my heart is so filled with gladness."
“上帝保佑你，威尔。哦！我真高兴。仿佛她已经被找回来了，我的心中充满了喜悦。”
That night Mr. Palmer stayed out late and long. Susan was afraid that he was at his old haunts and habits—getting tipsy at some public-house; and this thought oppressed her, even though she had so much to make her happy in the consciousness that Will loved her. She sat up long, and then she went to bed, leaving all arranged as well as she could for her father's return. She looked at the little rosy, sleeping girl who was her bed-fellow, with redoubled tenderness, and with many a prayerful thought. The little arms entwined her neck as she lay down, for Nanny was a light sleeper, and was conscious that she, who was loved with all the power of that sweet, childish heart, was near her, and by her, although she was too sleepy to utter any of her half-formed words.
那天晚上帕尔默先生在外面呆到很晚。苏珊担心他去了以前经常出入的地方——在某个酒馆里酩酊大醉；这种想法折磨着她，尽管知道威尔爱着她让她不知道有多高兴。她熬了很久才去睡觉，尽可能把一切安排妥当，等着父亲回来。她望着脸色红润，正在酣睡的小女孩，她的床伴，不禁又多了一份温柔和许多虔诚的思潮。当苏珊躺下时，那双小手臂就缠绕着她的脖颈，南妮的睡觉很轻，因此当苏珊（她用甜美、幼小的心灵中所有力量去爱着的苏珊）靠近她，在她身边躺下时，她是有感觉的，即便已经困得无法发出任何牙牙学语的声音。
And, by-and-by, she heard her father come home, stumbling uncertain, trying first the windows, and next the door fastenings, with many a loud incoherent murmur. The little innocent twined around her seemed all the sweeter and more lovely, when she thought sadly of her erring father. And presently he called aloud for a light. She had left matches and all arranged as usual on the dresser; but, fearful of some accident from fire, in his unusually intoxicated state, she now got up softly, and putting on a cloak, went down to his assistance.
不久，苏珊听到了父亲回来的声音，跌跌撞撞，乱摸一通，先是窗户，接着是门闩，断断续续不停地大声咕哝着什么。她伤心地想着她那犯错的父亲，觉得缠绕在她脖颈上的这个小天真越发温柔，越发可爱。不久他又嚷嚷着要灯火。像往常一样，她把火柴摆好放在餐具柜上了，可是害怕他这样酩酊大醉的样子会闹出什么火灾，她很快轻轻地起了床，披上一件斗篷，下楼来帮忙。
Alas! the little arms that were unclosed from her soft neck belonged to a light, easily awakened sleeper. Nanny missed her darling Susy; and terrified at being left alone, in the vast mysterious darkness, which had no bounds and seemed infinite, she slipped out of bed, and tottered, in her little nightgown, towards the door. There was a light below, and there was Susy and safety! So she went onwards two steps towards the steep, abrupt stairs; and then, dazzled by sleepiness, she stood, she wavered, she fell! Down on her head on the stone floor she fell! Susan flew to her, and spoke all soft, entreating, loving words; but her white lids covered up the blue violets of eyes, and there was no murmur came out of the pale lips. The warm tears that rained down did not awaken her; she lay stiff, and weary with her short life, on Susan's knee. Susan went sick with terror. She carried her upstairs, and laid her tenderly in bed; she dressed herself most hastily, with her trembling fingers. Her father was asleep on the settle downstairs; and useless, and worse than useless, if awake. But Susan flew out of the door, and down the quiet resounding street, towards the nearest doctor's house. Quickly she went, but as quickly a shadow followed, as if impelled by some sudden terror. Susan rang wildly at the night-bell—the shadow crouched near. The doctor looked out from an upstairs window.
唉！从她那柔软的脖颈上解下来的那双小手臂的主人是一个睡觉很轻，很容易就惊醒的人。南妮想念着她亲爱的苏西，一个人被扔在这广袤诡秘、无边无际的黑暗中，她吓坏了，溜下床，穿着小睡袍，脚步蹒跚地向门口走去。楼下有一抹灯光，苏西就在楼下，那里很安全！于是她向前朝着陡峭突兀的楼梯走了两步，她睡眼惺忪地停了下来，摇摇晃晃，摔了下去！她头朝下摔在了石头铺的地面上！苏珊飞奔向她，说出了所有温柔、恳求、关爱的话语；但是苍白的眼睑遮住了那双紫罗兰色的双眼，再也没有呢喃声从那苍白的嘴唇下发出来。如雨水般洒落的温热泪水也唤不醒她，仿佛厌倦了她那短暂的一生，她僵直地躺在苏珊的膝盖上。苏珊因为恐惧而变得心慌意乱。她把她抱上楼去，轻轻地把她放在床上，颤抖着双手匆忙地穿上衣服。她的父亲在楼下的长椅上睡着了，一点儿用都没有，如果他醒着，会更加有害无益。苏珊飞奔出门，沿着死寂的街道，朝着就近的大夫家跑去。她跑得很快，但是后面的一个身影跟得也很快，仿佛被什么突袭的恐惧驱赶着一般。苏珊疯狂地按着夜间门铃——后面的身影尾随而至。医生从楼上的窗户往外看。
"A little child has fallen downstairs, at No.9 Crown Street, and is very ill—dying, I'm afraid. Please, for God's sake, sir, come directly. No.9 Crown Street."
“一个小孩从楼上摔下来了，在克罗街9号，她病得很厉害——恐怕快不行了。求求你，看在上帝的份上，先生，马上来吧。克罗街9号。”
"I'll be there directly," said he, and shut the window.
“我马上就过去。”他说道，关上了窗户。
"For that God you have just spoken about—for His sake—tell me, are you Susan Palmer? Is it my child that lies a-dying?" said the shadow, springing forwards, and clutching poor Susan's arm.
“像你刚才说的那样——看在上帝的份上——告诉我，你是苏珊·帕尔默吗？”是我的孩子快要不行了吗？”后面的身影说道，冲上前来，紧紧抓住了可怜的苏珊的手臂。
"It is a little child of two years old. I do not know whose it is; I love it as my own. Come with me, whoever you are; come with me."
“是个两岁的孩子。我不知道是谁的孩子，我爱她就像我自己的孩子。跟我一起来，不管你是谁，跟我一起来吧。”
The two sped along the silent streets—as silent as the night were they. They entered the house; Susan snatched up the light, and carried it upstairs. The other followed.
两人疾步行走在寂静的街道上——和这夜晚一样寂静。她们进了房子，苏珊把灯点亮，向楼上走去。另一个人紧跟着。
She stood with wild, glaring eyes by the bedside, never looking at Susan, but hungrily gazing at the little, white, still child. She stooped down, and put her hand tight on her own heart, as if to still its beating, and bent her ear to the pale lips. Whatever the result was, she did not speak; but threw off the bed-clothes wherewith Susan had tenderly covered up the little creature, and felt its left side.
她站在床边，双眼露出疯狂、炽热的目光，她根本没有看苏珊，而是贪婪地凝视着那个瘦小、苍白、安静的孩子。她俯下身，一只手紧紧地捂住自己的胸口，似乎要终止心脏的跳动，然后把耳朵俯在那苍白的嘴唇上聆听。无论结果怎样，她一句话都没有说；她扯掉了苏珊轻轻盖在小家伙身上的被褥，感受她的心跳。
Then she threw up her arms, with a cry of wild despair.
然后她张开双臂，发出疯狂绝望的哭声。
"She is dead! she is dead!"
“她死了！她死了！”
She looked so fierce, so mad, so haggard, that, for an instant, Susan was terrified; the next, the holy God had put courage into her heart, and her pure arms were round that guilty, wretched creature, and her tears were falling fast and warm upon her breast. But she was thrown off with violence.
她看上去是那么凶猛，那么疯狂，那么憔悴，以至于有那么一瞬间，苏珊被吓到了；但很快，神圣的上帝又让她鼓起了勇气，她用双臂紧紧抱着那个内疚、可怜的人，热泪滚滚而下，温暖着她的胸膛。但是她被用力甩开了。
"You killed her—you slighted her—you let her fall down those stairs! you killed her!"
“是你杀了她——你没看好她——你让她从楼梯上摔了下来！是你杀了她！”
Susan cleared off the thick mist before her, and, gazing at the mother with her clear, sweet angel eyes, said, mournfully—"I would have laid down my own life for her."
苏珊擦去了眼前厚厚的一层水雾，用她那清澈、温柔如天使般的双眼望着这个母亲，伤心地说道：“我宁愿舍弃自己的生命来换回她的。”
"Oh, the murder is on my soul!" exclaimed the wild, bereaved mother, with the fierce impetuosity of one who has none to love her, and to be beloved, regard to whom might teach self-restraint.
“哦，我的心也死了！”这个疯狂的失去孩子的母亲嚷道，失去了爱她和她爱的人，她的情绪极度暴躁，几乎无法控制。
"Hush!" said Susan, her finger on her lips. "Here is the doctor. God may suffer her to live."
“嘘！”苏珊轻声说道，手指放在嘴唇上。“医生来了。愿上帝让她活下去。”
The poor mother turned sharp round. The doctor mounted the stair. Ah! that mother was right; the little child was really dead and gone.
可怜的母亲突然转过头来。医生走上楼来。唉！孩子的母亲是对的，小孩已经不在人世了。
And when he confirmed her judgment, the mother fell down in a fit. Susan, with her deep grief, had to forget herself, and forget her darling (her charge for years), and question the doctor what she must do with the poor wretch, who lay on the floor in such extreme of misery.
得知她的判断被确认属实，这位母亲昏倒在了地上。苏珊，带着一颗沉痛的心，不得不忘掉自己，忘掉她的宝贝（她照顾了多年的宝贝），她向医生询问需要为这个躺在地上，陷入极度悲伤的可怜人做点儿什么。
"She is the mother!" said she.
“她是孩子的母亲！”她说道。
"Why did she not take better care of her child?" asked he, almost angrily.
“为什么她没有好好照顾自己的孩子？”他近乎生气地问道。
But Susan only said, "The little child slept with me; and it was I that left her."
可是苏珊仅仅回答说：“孩子是和我睡在一起的，是我离开了她。”
"I will go back and make up a composing draught; and while I am away you must get her to bed."
“我会回来给她配一些药水，我不在时你一定要把她弄到床上去。”
Susan took out some of her own clothes, and softly undressed the stiff, powerless form. There was no other bed in the house but the one in which her father slept. So she tenderly lifted the body of her darling; and was going to take it downstairs, but the mother opened her eyes, and seeing what she was about, she said—"I am not worthy to touch her, I am so wicked. I have spoken to you as I never should have spoken; but I think you are very good. May I have my own child to lie in my arms for a little while?"
苏珊找出几件她自己的衣服，轻轻地把衣服从那僵硬无力的身体上换掉。除了楼下她父亲睡觉的地方以外，房子里再没有其他的床了。因此她轻柔地抱起了她的宝贝，准备把她带到楼下去，但是孩子的母亲这时睁开了双眼，看到这些，她说道：“我不配碰她，我太可恶了。我永远都不该像刚才那样对你说话，但是我想你是一个非常好的人。我可以把我的孩子抱在怀里一会儿吗？”
Her voice was so strange a contrast to what it had been before she had gone into the fit, that Susan hardly recognised it: it was now so unspeakably soft, so irresistibly pleading; the features too had lost their fierce expression, and were almost as placid as death. Susan could not speak, but she carried the little child, and laid it in its mother's arms; then, as she looked at them, something overpowered her, and she knelt down, crying aloud—"Oh, my God, my God, have mercy on her, and forgive and comfort her."
她的声音和昏倒之前有着天壤之别，苏珊几乎没有听出来：此刻是说不出的温柔，如此令人难以抗拒的恳求；她的面容也没有了之前的狂暴表情，现在几乎是平静得如死人一般。苏珊说不出话来，但她还是把孩子抱过来，放到她母亲的怀里；然后，当她望着她们时，她再也控制不住，跪了下来，大声哭起来：“哦，上帝，我的上帝，宽恕她吧，请原谅她，给她安慰。”
But the mother kept smiling, and stroking the little face, murmuring soft, tender words, as if it were alive. She was going mad, Susan thought; but she prayed on, and on, and ever still she prayed with streaming eyes.
但是孩子的母亲却一直在微笑，抚摸着孩子的小脸，低声发出轻柔、温和的话语，好像孩子还活着一样。她快要疯了，苏珊心想，她不停地在祈祷，眼中的泪水也一直没有干。
The doctor came with the draught. The mother took it, with docile unconsciousness of its nature as medicine. The doctor sat by her; and soon she fell asleep. Then he rose softly, and beckoning Susan to the door, he spoke to her there.
医生带着药水回来了。孩子的母亲服了药，温顺得连药的名字都不去问。医生坐在她的身旁，很快她就睡着了。然后他轻轻地站起来，示意苏珊到门口来，在那里和她说话。
"You must take the corpse out of her arms. She will not awake. That draught will make her sleep for many hours. I will call before noon again. It is now daylight. Good-by."
“你必须把尸体从她怀里拿开。她不会醒的。那药水会让她睡很多个小时。中午前我会再来看看。现在天亮了。再见吧。”
Susan shut him out; and then, gently extricating the dead child from its mother's arms, she could not resist making her own quiet moan over her darling. She tried to learn off its little placid face, dumb and pale before her.
苏珊把他送了出去，然后，轻轻地把死去的孩子从母亲怀里解脱出来，她再也控制不住自己，开始为她的小心肝无声地呜咽。她试图记住那张安详的小脸，在她面前是那么沉默、苍白。
Not all the scalding tears of care Shall wash away that vision fair; Not all the thousand thoughts that rise, Not all the sights that dim her eyes, Shall e'er usurp the place Of that little angel-face.
所有关心的热泪，也不能冲走眼前这番景象；所有产生的万千思绪，所有模糊她双眼的景象，也不能侵占那天使般的面孔，曾经呆过的地方。
And then she remembered what remained to be done. She saw that all was right in the house; her father was still dead asleep on the settle, in spite of all the noise of the night. She went out through the quiet streets, deserted still, although it was broad daylight, and to where the Leighs lived. Mrs. Leigh, who kept her country hours, was opening her window-shutters. Susan took her by the arm, and, without speaking, went into the house-place. There she knelt down before the astonished Mrs. Leigh, and cried as she had never done before; but the miserable night had overpowered her, and she who had gone through so much calmly, now that the pressure seemed removed could not find the power to speak.
然后她想起了接下来要做的事情。她看到房子里的一切都还算正常，尽管昨夜有那么多的噪音，她的父亲却仍然在长椅上酣睡，虽然天已经大亮了，但街道上仍然空荡荡的，她出门穿过安静的街道，向着利家住的地方走去。利太太一直遵守着乡下的作息时间，已经在打开窗户摇杆了。苏珊抓住她的手臂，一句话也没说，进到房子里面。她跪在惊愕的利太太面前，前所未有地失声痛哭；这痛苦的一夜已经击溃了她，之前她一直表现得都很沉着，但现在压力似乎被抛除了，她却找不到说话的力量。
"My poor dear! What has made thy heart so sore as to come and cry a-this-ons? Speak and tell me. Nay, cry on, poor wench, if thou canst not speak yet. It will ease the heart, and then thou canst tell me."
“我可怜的宝贝！是什么让你如此伤心哭成这样？告诉我。不，如果你现在说不出来，继续哭吧，可怜的姑娘。这样会让你感觉好受点儿，然后你可以告诉我怎么回事。”
"Nanny is dead!" said Susan. "I left her to go to father, and she fell downstairs, and never breathed again. Oh, that's my sorrow! But I've more to tell. Her mother is come—is in our house! Come and see if it's your Lizzie."
“南妮死了！”苏珊说道，“我撇下她去找父亲，她从楼梯上摔了下来，就再也没有呼吸了。哦，这是我的悲伤！但是我还有更多的话要说。她母亲来了——在我家里！去看看是不是你的莉齐。”
Mrs. Leigh could not speak, but, trembling, put on her things and went with Susan in dizzy haste back to Crown Street.
利太太说不出话来，只是颤抖地穿好衣服，跟着苏珊，晕乎乎慌里慌张地赶回克罗街。
