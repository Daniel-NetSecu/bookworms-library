CHAPTER XIV  

第十四章  

If I was punctual in quitting Mdlle. Reuter's domicile, I was at least equally punctual in arriving there. I came the next day at five minutes before two, and on reaching the schoolroom door, before I opened it I heard a rapid, gabbling sound, which warned me that the priére du midi was not yet concluded. I waited the termination thereof; it would have been impious to intrude my heretical presence during its progress. How the repeater of the prayer did cackle and splutter! I never before or since heard language enounced with such steam-engine haste. 'Notre Père qui êtes au ciel' went off like a shot; then followed an address to Marie 'vièrge céleste, reine des anges, maison d'or, tour d'ivoire;' and then an invocation to the saint of the day; and then down they all sat, and the solemn rite was over and I entered, flinging the door wide and striding in fast, as it was my wont to do now, for I had found that in entering with aplomb, and mounting the estrade with emphasis, consisted the grand secret of ensuring immediate silence. The folding-doors between the two classes, opened for the prayer, were instantly closed. A maétresse, work-box in hand, took her seat at her appropriate desk. The pupils sat still with their pens and books before them, my three beauties in the van, now well humbled by a demeanour of consistent coolness, sat erect with their hands folded quietly on their knees. They had given up giggling and whispering to each other, and no longer ventured to utter pert speeches in my presence. They now only talked to me occasionally with their eyes, by means of which organs they could still, however, say very audacious and coquettish things. Had affection, goodness, modesty, real talent ever employed those bright orbs as interpreters, I do not think I could have refrained from giving a kind and encouraging, perhaps an ardent, reply now and then; but as it was, I found pleasure in answering the glance of vanity with the gaze of stoicism. Youthful, fair, brilliant as were many of my pupils, I can truly say that in me they never saw any other bearing than such as an austere though just guardian might have observed towards them. If any doubt the accuracy of this assertion, as inferring more conscientious self-denial or Scipiolike self-control than they feel disposed to give me credit for, let them take into consideration the following circumstances, which, while detracting from my merit, justify my veracity.
如果说我离开勒泰小姐的学校准时，我到达那里至少也同样准时。第二天下午，差五分钟两点时，我来到教室门口，开门之前一阵急促而含混的声音提醒了我，“祈祷午餐”还没有结束。因此，我在门口等它结束；祈祷进行中时，我一个异教徒的出现是对上帝的亵渎。这些人是如何叽叽呱呱、喋喋不休地重复着祷告文啊！我以前没听到过，之后也没再听过人这般像蒸汽机一样急促地说话声了。“上天的圣父”像子弹一样飞快，接下来是对玛丽说的话，“天上的圣母，天使的母后，黄金之殿，象牙之塔”； 再接着是向当代的圣人祈祷，然后她们都坐下了，这个庄严的仪式结束了。我进来的时候，把门开得很大，阔步匆匆走进教室，这样走进来已是我的习惯，因为我发现了一个奥秘：镇静沉着地走进教室，迈着有力的步伐踏上讲台可以确保教室即刻安静下来。两个教室之间为祷告而打开的折叠门也即刻关上了。一个女教师，手拿针线盒，坐到了自己的位子上。学生们安静地坐着，钢笔和书本摆在面前；三个美女起到了带头作用，由于我一贯对她们态度冷漠，此刻她们正挺直了腰板，谦逊地坐在位子上，双手合十放在膝盖上。她们放弃了咯咯的笑，停止了互相窃窃私语，也不敢在我面前讲那些粗鲁的话。她们只是偶尔用眼睛和我交流。然而即便用眼睛这个器官，她们也能传达很大胆、很轻佻的讯息。要是她们明亮的双眼传达的是温情、善良、谦逊和才干，我想我不至于抑制自己，会时不时投以和蔼、鼓励，或者热情的目光作为回应；然而现状不是这样，我发现用坚韧自制的注视回应她们虚荣的眼光十分有趣。虽然我很多学生都朝气蓬勃、漂亮动人、光芒四射，但我可以很确定地说，她们在我身上从未看到过除严峻以外的其他姿态，尽管这种严峻的态度可能只会在监护人身上发现。如果有人怀疑我的言论的准确性，不愿意相信我，推断我这种行为只不过是更加自觉地克己行为或是西皮奥式的自控行为，我会让他们考虑以下情形，这些情况不但不会贬低我的优点，还会证明我说的是实话。
Know, O incredulous reader! that a master stands in a somewhat different relation towards a pretty, lightheaded, probably ignorant girl to that occupied by a partner at a ball or a gallant on the promenade. A professor does not meet his pupil to see her dressed in satin and muslin, with hair perfumed and curled, neck scarcely shaded by aerial lace, round white arms circled with bracelets, feet dressed for the gliding dance. It is not his business to whirl her through the waltz, to feed her with compliments, to heighten her beauty by the flush of gratified vanity. Neither does he encounter her on the smooth-rolled, tree-shaded boulevard, in the green and sunny park, whither she repairs clad in her becoming walking-dress, her scarf thrown with grace over her shoulders, her little bonnet scarcely screening her curls, the red rose under its brim adding a new tint to the softer rose on her cheek, her face and eyes, too, illumined with smiles, perhaps as transient as the sunshine of the gala-day, but also quite as brilliant. It is not his office to walk by her side, to listen to her lively chat, to carry her parasol, scarcely larger than a broad green leaf, to lead in a ribbon her Blenheim spaniel or Italian greyhound. No. He finds her in the schoolroom, plainly dressed, with books before her. Owing to her education or her nature, books are to her a nuisance, and she opens them with aversion; yet her teacher must instil into her mind the contents of these books. That mind resists the admission of grave information; it recoils, it grows restive. Sullen tempers are shown, disfiguring frowns spoil the symmetry of the face, sometimes coarse gestures banish grace from the deportment, while muttered expressions, redolent of native and ineradicable vulgarity, desecrate the sweetness of the voice. Where the temperament is serene though the intellect be sluggish, an unconquerable dullness opposes every effort to instruct. Where there is cunning but not energy, dissimulation, falsehood, a thousand schemes and tricks are put in play to evade the necessity of application. In short, to the tutor, female youth, female charms, are like tapestry hangings of which the wrong side is continually turned towards him; and even when he sees the smooth, neat external surface, he so well knows what knots, long stitches, and jagged ends are behind that he has scarce a temptation to admire too fondly the seemly forms and bright colours exposed to general view.
噢，疑心的读者们！你知道，一个老师和一个漂亮单纯，很可能也无知的女学生间的关系有些不一样，这种关系不同于在舞会上抱着女伴跳舞，也不同于散步时对女士献殷勤。老师遇见学生，不是为看她身穿锦缎轻纱，不是为看她撒了香水的、卷卷的头发，不是为看她在梦幻般的蕾丝颈带下近乎显露无遗的脖子，也不是为看她雪白圆润、戴着手镯的胳膊，更不是为看她为翩翩起舞而精心装扮的双脚。教师不会带她伴着华尔兹舞曲旋转，对她大肆恭维，夸大她的美以满足她泛滥的虚荣心。教师也不会和学生在光滑平坦的林阴大道上，或是在绿意盎然、阳光灿烂的公园里私会，无论到哪里她都要穿上好看的外出服，将围巾优雅地搭在肩上，还要戴上几乎遮不住发卷的小帽，帽沿下插一朵红玫瑰，给她原本柔和粉嫩的脸颊增添了新的色彩；她的脸庞、眼睛也在微笑中显得神采焕发；或许这神采像节日里的阳光一样稍纵即逝，却依然灿烂夺目。教师更没有职责和学生并肩散步，听她活泼地讲话，为她撑一把不比一片绿色阔叶大多少的阳伞，用缎带牵一只布伦海姆狗或是意大利猎犬。不。他只在教室里看到她，穿着朴素，面前摆着一些书。由于个人修养或天性，她很讨厌书本，常带着厌恶之情翻开书；然而她的老师必须向她灌输这些书本的内容。她的头脑排斥那些严肃刻板的知识；它退缩，它开始难以驾驭。她的坏脾气显露出来，皱起的眉头毁坏了脸部的匀称美，偶尔摆出的粗鲁姿势让她的举止不再优雅；而她嘟哝的抱怨话语，让人联想起根深蒂固的本地粗话，亵渎了她甜美的嗓音。当她的脾气变得温和，但理解力还是迟钝时，她那无法战胜的麻木感会让她反对任何教导。只要有诡计——而不是活力——时，掩饰、谎言等无数的伎俩都会上演，以逃避用功读书。总之，对于教师来说，女学生的青春活力和女学生的魅力就像挂毯，反面总对着教师，即便看到那平滑、齐整的外表，也很难被诱使着由衷地赞赏大众都能看见的美丽图案和鲜艳色彩，因为他对挂毯背面有多么难看的疙瘩，多么长的针脚，多么参差不齐的线头，再清楚不过了。
Our likings are regulated by our circumstances. The artist prefers a hilly country because it is picturesque; the engineer a flat one because it is convenient; the man of pleasure likes what he calls 'a fine woman'—she suits him; the fashionable young gentleman admires the fashionable young lady—she is of his kind; the toil-worn, fagged, probably irritable tutor, blind almost to beauty, insensible to airs and graces, glories chiefly in certain mental qualities: application, love of knowledge, natural capacity, docility, truthfulness, gratefulness are the charms that attract his notice and win his regard. These he seeks, but seldom meets; these if by chance he finds, he would fain retain for ever; and when separation deprives him of them he feels as if some ruthless hand had snatched from him his only ewe-Iamb. Such being the case, and the case it is, my readers will agree with me that there was nothing either very meritorious or very marvellous in the integrity and moderation of my conduct at Mdlle. Reuter' s pensionnat de demoiselles.
我们的喜好会随着所处环境而改变。画家偏爱多山的村庄，因为那里风景如画；工程师偏爱平坦的村庄，因为那里施工便利；花花公子偏爱他所谓的“美丽女人”，因为她适合他；年轻时髦的绅士追求同样年轻时髦的淑女，因为他们门当户对；辛苦工作而筋疲力尽，很可能脾气也暴躁的教师，对美几乎视而不见，对装腔作势和风度翩翩也无动于衷。而某些良好的心理品质：专心致志、热爱知识、聪明、温柔、真诚、懂得感恩，大多会吸引他的注意力，赢得他的尊重。他在寻找这些品质，却很难找到；假如偶然发现了，他也会乐于将其永远记在心中，当分别将其剥夺时，他会感觉像是有一只残忍的手从他那里抢走了他唯一的羔羊。情况确实如此，既然如此，那么我说我在勒泰小姐的寄宿女校的言行正直而恰当，既没什么值得称道也没什么让人惊讶的，读者们也会赞同我的。
My first business this afternoon consisted in reading the list of places for the month, determined by the relative correctness of the compositions given the preceding day. The list was headed, as usual, by the name of Sylvie—that plain, quiet little girl I have described before as being at once the best and ugliest pupil in the establishment; the second place had fallen to the lot of a certain Léonie Ledru, a diminutive, sharp-featured, and parchment-skinned creature of quick wits, frail conscience, and indurated feelings—a lawyer-like thing, of whom I used to say that, had she been a boy, she would have made a model of an unprincipled, clever attorney. Then came Eulalie, the proud beauty, the Juno of the school, whom six long years of drilling in the simple grammar of the English language had compelled, despite the stiff phlegm of her intellect, to acquire a mechanical acquaintance with most of its rules. No smile, no trace of pleasure or satisfaction appeared in Sylvie's nun-like and passive face as she heard her name read first. I always felt saddened by the sight of that poor girl's absolute quiescence on all occasions, and it was my custom to look at her, to address her, as seldom as possible. Her extreme docility, her assiduous perseverance, would have recommended her warmly to my good opinion; her modesty, her intelligence, would have induced me to feel most kindly, most affectionately towards her, notwithstanding the almost ghastly plainness of her features, the disproportion of her form, the corpse-like lack of animation in her countenance, had I not been aware that every friendly word, every kindly action, would be reported by her to her confessor, and by him misinterpreted and poisoned. Once I laid my hand on her head in token of approbation. I thought Sylvie was going to smile—her dim eye almost kindled; but presently she shrank from me. I was a man and a heretic; she, poor child! a destined nun and devoted Catholic. Thus a fourfold wall of separation divided her mind from mine. A pert smirk and a hard glance of triumph was Léonie's method of testifying her gratification. Eulalie looked sullen and envious. She had hoped to be first. Hortense and Caroline exchanged a reckless grimace on hearing their names read out somewhere near the bottom of the list; the brand of mental inferiority was considered by them as no disgrace, their hopes for the future being based solely on their personal attractions.
我今天下午的第一项任务就是宣读这个月学生的名次，他们的名次由前一天收上来的作文的正确度决定；名单之首照例是西尔维——一个平凡、安静的小女孩。我曾这样描述过她，她是这学校里最棒，也是最丑的学生了；得第二的是一个叫莱奥妮·勒德吕的姑娘，她身材矮小，五官清楚，肤色如羊皮纸一般，她机智灵敏，没什么道德观念，心肠硬，是个律师般的人物，我曾经说过她要是个男生，定会成为一个典型的没有原则而又聪明伶俐的律师。接着是厄拉利，那位骄傲的美女，学校的朱诺女神。尽管智力迟钝，但苦练简单英语语法六年，迫使她机械地熟悉了大多数语法规则。当听到第一个读到的名字是自己时，西尔维修女般冷漠的脸上没有微笑，没有一丝愉悦或满足的痕迹。看到这位可怜的姑娘在任何场合都是如此沉静，我总觉得悲哀。我习惯尽量不看她，不跟她讲话。假若我没有发现她会将我每句友好的话语，每个亲切的举动报告给听她忏悔的神父，神父还误解、诬蔑我的言行，她的极度温驯、勤奋和坚持会大大博得我的好感，她的谦逊和智慧也会让我更温和、亲切地对待她，尽管她相貌近乎丑陋，身材不匀称，面容也如死尸般缺乏生气。一次，为了表示赞扬，我把手放在了她头上。我原以为西尔维会微笑——她黯淡无神的眼睛几乎变得闪闪发亮；然而不一会儿，她就避开了我。我是个男人，还是个异教徒；她呢，可怜的孩子！她注定是个修女，是个虔诚的天主教徒。因此，一堵四重的墙把我们两人的心灵就此分开。粗鲁的假笑，自鸣得意而刻薄的一瞥是莱奥妮显示满足感的方式。厄拉利有些嫉妒，闷闷不乐。她本期望自己能拿第一。听到自己的名字几乎排在了名单的最后，奥尔唐斯和卡罗琳向彼此扮出一个满不在乎的鬼脸。她们并不把智力低人一等认为是丢脸的事，她们对未来的憧憬只建立在自己迷人的外貌上。
This affair arranged, the regular lesson followed. During a brief interval, employed by the pupils in ruling their books my eye, ranging carelessly over the benches, observed, for the first time, that the farthest seat in the farthest row—a seat usually vacant—was again filled by the new scholar, the Mdlle. Henri so ostentatiously recommended to me by the directness. Today I had on my spectacles; her appearance, therefore, was clear to me at the first glance; I had not to puzzle over it. She looked young, yet had I been required to name her exact age I should have been somewhat nonplussed. The slightness of her figure might have suited seventeen; a certain anxious and preoccupied expression of face seemed the indication of riper years. She was dressed, like all the rest, in a dark stuff gown and a white collar. Her features were dissimilar to any there—not so rounded, more defined, yet scarcely regular. The shape of her head too was different—the superior part more developed, the base considerably less. I felt assured, at first sight, that she was not a Belgian. Her complexion, her countenance, her lineaments, her figure were all distinct from theirs, and evidently the type of another race—of a race less gifted with fullness of flesh and plenitude of blood, less jocund, material, unthinking. When I first cast my eyes on her, she sat looking fixedly down, her chin resting on her hand, and she did not change her attitude till I commenced the lesson. None of the Belgian girls would have retained one position, and that a reflective one, for the same length of time. Yet, having intimated that her appearance was peculiar, as being unlike that of her Flemish companions, I have little more to say respecting it. I can pronounce no encomiums on her beauty, for she was not beautiful, nor offer condolence on her plainness, for neither was she plain. A careworn character of forehead and a corresponding moulding of the mouth struck me with a sentiment resembling surprise, but these traits would probably have passed unnoticed by any less crotchety observer.
这件事情处理完，接下来正式上课。利用学生们整理书的短暂间隙，我的眼睛漫不经心地搜寻一排排的座椅，第一次观察到，最后一排最远的那个位置——平常总空着的座位——又被我的新学生坐了。她也就是女校长铺陈了很多，极力推荐给我的亨利小姐。今天我戴了眼镜，因而一眼就看清了她的外貌；我不必再苦苦想她到底长什么样了。她看上去挺年轻，然而要我给出她的确切年龄，我会觉得有些为难。从她纤细的身形推断，她可能就十七岁；然而她忧虑、心事重重的表情像在表明她已达到了更成熟的年龄。她的穿着和其他人并无二致，一件黑色长外衣配白色的领子。她的相貌和其他人都不同——脸没有那么圆，五官比较分明，但说不上匀称。她的头型也不同于其他人——上部长得更大些，底部相当小。第一眼看到她时，我就确定她不是比利时人。她的肤色、相貌、轮廓和身形都和其他人都不一样，很显然她是另一个种族的人——一个身体没那么丰满，脸色没那么红润的种族，一个相比之下少了些幸福感、少了些物质欲望并多了些思考的种族。最初我把目光投向她时，她手掌托着下巴，一动不动地向下看；直到我开始讲课，她都没有变换姿势。没有一个比利时姑娘可以这么长时间保持同一种姿势，况且是思考的姿势。然而，我已经透露过，她的相貌有些奇特，不同于她的佛兰芒同伴们，那么关于相貌我没有其他要说的了。我不能称赞她的美貌，因为她并不漂亮。我也不能对她的平平相貌表示怜悯，因为这相貌还过得去。她的额头显得忧心忡忡，嘴型也是如此，这触动了我的心，心里有种像是惊异的感觉；然而这些特质，对于一个没那么敏感的人很可能不会注意到。
Now, reader, though I have spent more than a page in describing Mdlle. Henri, I know well enough that I have left on your mind's eye no distinct picture of her; I have not painted her complexion, nor her eyes, nor her hair, nor even drawn the outline of her shape. You cannot tell whether her nose was aquiline or retroussé, whether her chin was long or short, her face square or oval; nor could I the first day, and it is not my intention to communicate to you at once a knowledge I myself gained by little and little.
读者们，关于亨利小姐我已描述了一页多。但我深知在你们的脑海中她并没有留下的清晰图像；我没有描述她的肤色、眼睛，或者头发，就连她的外形轮廓也没加以叙述。你无从知道她的鼻子是鹰钩鼻还是朝天鼻，她的下巴是长还是短，她的脸形是方还是椭圆；第一天看她，我也无法了解这些，况且我也无意把自己一点一点了解到的信息一次全传达给你们。
I gave a short exercise which they all wrote down. I saw the new pupil was puzzled at first with the novelty of the form and language. Once or twice she looked at me with a sort of painful solicitude, as not comprehending at all what I meant. Then she was not ready when the others were; she could not write her phrases so fast as they did. I would not help her; I went on relentless. She looked at me; her eye said most plainly, 'I cannot follow you.' I disregarded the appeal, and, carelessly leaning back in my chair, glancing from time to time with a nonchalant air out of the window, I dictated a little faster. On looking towards her again, I perceived her face clouded with embarrassment, but she was still writing on most diligently. I paused a few seconds. She employed the interval in hurriedly reperusing what she had written, and shame and discomfiture were apparent in her countenance; she evidently found she had made great nonsense of it. In ten minutes more the dictation was complete, and, having allowed a brief space in which to correct it, I took their books. It was with a reluctant hand that Mdlle. Henri gave up hers; but, having once yielded it to my possession, she composed her anxious face, as if, for the present, she had resolved to dismiss regret, and had made up her mind to be thought unprecedentedly stupid.
我让她们做了个简短的练习，都要写下来。起初我看到这位新生面露困惑，因为这种形式和语言十分新奇。有那么一两回，她显得很伤脑筋，很焦虑地抬头看我，像是她根本无法理解我的意思。她没有像其他人一样准备就绪。她也无法写得像她们一样迅速。我不想帮她，便没有间断地继续读了下去。她看着我，眼神很显然传递着“我无法跟上你”的讯息。我无视她的恳求，漠然地仰靠在椅子上，并且时不时漫不经心地向窗外看去，读得更快了一些。再次看她时，我觉察到她的脸上满是窘迫之情，然而她仍然孜孜不倦地努力写着。我停了一小会儿。她赶忙利用这会儿时间重新审查自己写出来的东西，脸上明显露出羞愧和挫败的表情；显然，她觉得自己做得非常糟糕。又过了十分钟，听写结束，我给她们留出一点时间修改之后，便把她练习本都收了上来。亨利小姐很不情愿地把本子交了出来。然而一把本子交给我，她那张忧虑的脸就平静了下来，好像现在她已经决定消除心中的懊悔，也打定主意就让别人认为她绝顶愚蠢吧。
Glancing over her exercise, I found that several lines had been omitted, but what was written contained very few faults. I instantly inscribed 'Bon' at the bottom of the page, and returned it to her. She smiled, at first incredulously, then as if reassured, but did not lift her eyes. She could look at me, it seemed, when perplexed and bewildered, but not when gratified. I thought that scarcely fair.
我浏览了一下她的作业，发现有几行落下没写，但是写出来的很少出错。我马上在页面下方写了个“好”字，把练习本归还给她。她微笑，刚开始是有些不相信，接着好像是放心了，但没有抬起眼来。似乎她只在感到茫然不知所措时才看我，感到满足时却不会。我认为这不怎么公平。
