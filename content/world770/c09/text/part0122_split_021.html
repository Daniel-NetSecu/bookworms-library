Chapter 22  THE PROCESSION  

第二十二章  游行队伍  

Before Hester Prynne could call together her thoughts and consider what was practicable to be done in this new and startling aspect of affairs, the sound of military music was heard approaching along a contiguous street. It denoted the advance of the procession of magistrates and citizens, on its way towards the meeting-house; where, in compliance with a custom thus early established, and ever since observed, the Reverend Mr. Dimmesdale was to deliver an Election Sermon.
赫斯特·普林还没来得及集中思绪，想些切实的措施来应对这新出现的惊人局面，就听到临街传来一阵军乐声，越来越近。这表明官民的游行队伍正朝着议事厅行进。按照早已确立并一直遵循的惯例，丁梅斯代尔牧师先生将在那里进行庆祝选举的布道。
Soon the head of the procession showed itself, with a slow and stately march, turning a corner and making its way across the market-place. First came the music. It comprised a variety of instruments, perhaps imperfectly adapted to one another and played with no great skill; but yet attaining the great object for which the harmony of drum and clarion addresses itself to the multitude — that of imparting a higher and more heroic air to the scene of life that passes before the eye. Little Pearl at first clapped her hands, but then lost, for an instant, the restless agitation that had kept her in a continual effervescence throughout the morning; she gazed silently and seemed to be borne upward, like a floating sea-bird, on the long heaves and swells of sound. But she was brought back to her former mood by the shimmer of the sunshine on the weapons and bright armour of the military company, which followed after the music, and formed the honorary escort of the procession. This body of soldiery — which still sustains a corporate existence nd marches down from past ages with an ancient and honourable fame — was composed of no mercenary materials. Its ranks were filled with gentlemen, who felt the stirrings of martial impulse and sought to establish a kind of College of Arms, where as in an association of Knights Templars, they might learn the science, and, so far as peaceful exercise would teach them, the practices of war. The high estimation then placed upon the military character might be seen in the lofty port of each individual member of the company. Some of them, indeed, by their services in the Low Countries and on other fields of warfare, had fairly won their title to assume the name and pomp of soldiership. The entire array, moreover, clad in burnished steel, and with plumage nodding over their bright morions, had a brilliancy of effect which no modern display can aspire to equal.
很快，游行队伍的排头出现在视野里，缓慢而庄严地前进着，转过街角，朝市场走来。走在最前面的是乐队。音乐由各式各样的乐器演奏。也许彼此的配合不是很协调，演奏技巧也不高明，但军鼓与铜号的合奏却取得了极好的效果，为眼前的这一生活场景增添了更崇高、更宏伟的气息。小珠儿起先还拍着手，可后来，有一瞬间，使她一早上都处于亢奋状态的情绪突然消失了。她静静地注视着，似乎是被托了起来，像一只海鸟盘旋在汹涌澎湃的波涛声中。乐队之后是充当队列光荣前卫的军人们。他们的武器和甲胄在阳光下闪闪发光，让珠儿又回到了原先的情绪中。这士兵方阵里没有一个雇佣兵，依然保持着一个整体，带着古老而光荣的声名从往昔齐步走来。队列里有不少绅士，他们感到了尚武的冲动，希望建立一种军事学院。在那里，他们能像在圣堂骑士团那样，学习科学，并在和平时期的演习中学会作战。队伍里的每个人都趾高气扬，可见当时军人是备受尊敬的。其中的一些人确实因为在低地国家服役，以及在其他战场上作战，而赢得了军人的头衔和荣耀。而且，整个队列被锃亮的铠甲武装着，上面插着羽毛，上下晃动，营造了一种辉煌的气概，是如今的阅兵所不能与之媲美的。
And yet the men of civil eminence, who came immediately behind the military escort, were better worth a thoughtful observer's eye. Even in outward demeanour, they showed a stamp of majesty that made the warrior's haughty stride look vulgar, if not absurd. It was an age when what we call talent had far less consideration than now, but the massive materials which produce stability and dignity of character a great deal more. The people possessed, by hereditary right, the quality of reverence; which, in their descendants, if it survive at all, exists in smaller proportion, and with a vastly diminished force, in the selection and estimate of public men. The change may be for good or ill, and is partly, perhaps, for both. In that old day, the English settler on these rude shores, having left king, nobles, and all degrees of awful rank behind, while still the faculty and necessity of reverence were strong in him, bestowed it on the white hair and venerable brow of age; on long-tried integrity; on solid wisdom and sad-coloured experience; on endowments of that grave and weighty order which gives the idea of permanence, and comes under the general definition of respectability. These primitive statesmen, therefore — Bradstreet, Endicott, Dudley, Bellingham, and their compeers — who were elevated to power by the early choice of the people, seem to have been not often brilliant, but distinguished by a ponderous sobriety rather than activity of intellect. They had fortitude and self-reliance, and, in time of difficulty or peril, stood up for the welfare of the state like a line of cliffs against a tempestuous tide. The traits of character here indicated were well represented in the square cast of countenance and large physical development of the new colonial magistrates. So far as a demeanour of natural authority was concerned, the mother country need not have been ashamed to see these foremost men of an actual democracy adopted into the House of Peers, or made the Privy Council of the sovereign.
紧随着卫队的文职官员，有头脑的旁观者认为那些人更值得关注。即便是从外在举止看，他们也颇具庄严之气。相比之下，那些士兵傲慢的步伐即使不是荒谬，也颇显庸俗了些。在那个年代，天才远没像今天这样受到重视，而那些能营造稳定、促成人格尊严的多种因素，却大受青睐。人们拥有世袭而来的尊贵；这在他们的后继者身上，即使侥幸存在，比例也要小得多，而且由于官员的选拔和评估，他们的势力也要大大减少。这变化也许是好事，也许是坏事，也许好坏兼有。昔日，到这荒蛮海滨定居的英国移民，他们虽然抛弃了王公贵族和种种令人生畏的头衔，内心却存留着强烈的尊贵本色和需求，他们将其加诸于苍苍的白发和年迈的额头。加诸于久经考验的正直，加诸于坚实的智慧和富于阴郁色彩的经历，加诸于严肃沉重、给人以永恒之感、以得体为名义的秩序。因此早年这些由人民推举而当政的政治家——布拉德斯特里特、恩迪科特、达德利、贝林厄姆以及他们的同辈——这些人往往并没有多么英明，他们受人尊敬似乎并不是因为他们有什么睿智之举，而是因为他们的沉稳和节制。他们坚定而自信，就像直面怒涛的悬崖之边，能在危难之时为了国家利益挺身而出。这里提及的品格特征，充分体现在这些新殖民地长官的四方脸和大块头上。就这些天生当权者的举止而言，即使英格兰看到民主的先驱们进入贵族院，或是委以枢密院顾问之要职，它也不用感到羞耻。
Next in order to the magistrates came the young and eminently distinguished divine, from whose lips the religious discourse of the anniversary was expected. His was the profession, at that era, in which intellectual ability displayed itself far more than in political life; for — leaving a higher motive out of the question — it offered inducements powerful enough, in the almost worshipping respect of the community, to win the most aspiring ambition into its service. Even political power — as in the case of Increase Mather — was within the grasp of a successful priest.
官员后面是那位声名显赫的年轻牧师，人们期待着从他的嘴里听到本次庆祝日的宗教演说。在那个年代，牧师一职所显露的智能，远多于政治生活中所能显露的。因为撇开更高的动机不谈，在引起居民近乎崇拜的尊敬这点上，它就足以有力地激励并赢得最有抱负之人投身其间。甚至政治权力也会落入成功的牧师手中，英克里斯•马瑟就是一例。
It was the observation of those who beheld him now, that never, since Mr. Dimmesdale first set his foot on the New England shore, had he exhibited such energy as was seen in the gait and air with which he kept his pace in the procession. There was no feebleness of step, as at other times; his frame was not bent; nor did his hand rest ominously upon his heart. Yet, if the clergyman were rightly viewed, his strength seemed not of the body. It might be spiritual, and imparted to him by angelic ministrations. It might be the exhilaration of that potent cordial which is distilled only in the furnace glow of earnest and long-continued thought. Or, perchance, his sensitive temperament was invigorated by the loud and piercing music that swelled heavenward, and uplifted him on its ascending wave. Nevertheless, so abstracted was his look, it might be questioned whether Mr. Dimmesdale even heard the music. There was his body, moving onward, and with an unaccustomed force. But where was his mind? Far and deep in its own region, busying itself, with preternatural activity, to marshal a procession of stately thoughts that were soon to issue thence; and so he saw nothing, heard nothing, knew nothing, of what was around him; but the spiritual element took up the feeble frame, and carried it along, unconscious of the burden, and converting it to spirit like himself. Men of uncommon intellect, who have grown morbid, possess this occasional power of mighty effort, into which they throw the life of many days, and then are lifeless for as many more.
这时，人们注意到，他步态矫健、精神抖擞地走在队伍之中。丁梅斯代尔先生自踏上新英格兰的土地以来，从未像今天这样精力充沛。他的步伐没有以往那么虚弱，不再弓着背了，也不再病态地把手捂在心口了。不过，要是细细观察牧师，就能发现，他的精力似乎不是源自身体。那精力也许源自精神，由天使通过宗教仪式赋予他。那是潜在热情的兴奋表现——这热情只有在长期不断的诚挚思想的熔炉中，才能被蒸馏出来。或者，也许是他敏感的气质被升腾着的响亮刺耳的音乐所鼓舞，把他托起在音乐之浪中。不过，他的表情是如此迷离，人们甚至会怀疑，丁梅斯代尔先生是否听到了这乐声。他的身体在一种异常的力量推动下，向前行进。但他的心灵在哪里呢？他远远地深藏在自己的领域里，忙于超自然的活动，以清理不久将说出的一系列庄重的思想。因此，他什么都看不到，听不到，对周身的事情一无所知。那精神支撑起他虚弱的身体，带他前行，不但对重荷无所察觉，还把它转换成像自身一样的精神。那些病态却具非凡智慧的人，通过巨大的努力获得这偶然的能力，把多天的生命凝聚于一时，而在接下来的许多日子里却是了无生气。
Hester Prynne, gazing steadfastly at the clergyman, felt a dreary influence come over her, but wherefore or whence she knew not; unless that he seemed so remote from her own sphere, and utterly beyond her reach. One glance of recognition, she had imagined, must needs pass between them. She thought of the dim forest, with its little dell of solitude, and love, and anguish, and the mossy tree-trunk, where, sitting hand in hand, they had mingled their sad and passionate talk with the melancholy murmur of the brook. How deeply had they known each other then! And was this the man? She hardly knew him now! He, moving proudly past, enveloped, as it were, in the rich music, with the procession of majestic and venerable fathers; he, so unattainable in his worldly position, and still more so in that far vista of his unsympathising thoughts, through which she now beheld him! Her spirit sank with the idea that all must have been a delusion, and that, vividly as she had dreamed it, there could be no real bond betwixt the clergyman and herself. And thus much of woman was there in Hester, that she could scarcely forgive him — least of all now, when the heavy footstep of their approaching Fate might be heard, nearer, nearer, nearer! — for being able so completely to withdraw himself from their mutual world; while she groped darkly, and stretched forth her cold hands, and found him not.
赫斯特•普林紧紧地注视着牧师，感到一股阴沉的势力袭遍全身，却不知它为何而来，从何而来。她只觉得他离自己的天地好远，全然不可触及。她曾想象过，他俩之间一定会交换一次彼此心照的眼色。她想起了那昏暗的树林，那寂静的山谷，还有那爱情，和那极度的痛苦。在长满青苔的树干上，他们携手而坐，把二人忧愁而热切的交谈融于小溪忧郁的低语之中。当时，他们是多么息息相通啊！眼前的这人是他吗？她几乎不认识他了！他在低沉的乐声中，随着威严可敬的神父们高傲地走了过去。他在尘世的地位如此高不可攀，而她现在察觉到，他那深不可测的思想更是可望而不可即！她想到，虽然自己梦得如此真切，但一切都是幻觉，她和牧师之间没有任何实际的联系，精神便消沉了下去。作为女人，赫斯特简直难以原谅他——特别是此刻，他俩的命运迈着沉重的脚步，声音越来越近！而他竟可以从两人的共同世界里全然而退。她在黑暗中摸索，伸开冰冷的双臂，却遍寻不到他。
Pearl either saw and responded to her mother's feelings, or herself felt the remoteness and intangibility that had fallen around the minister. While the procession passed, the child was uneasy, fluttering up and down like a bird on the point of taking flight. When the whole had gone by, she looked up into Hester's face.
珠儿也许是察觉了母亲的感受，也许是感应到了，要不就是她自己也觉得牧师已笼罩在遥不可及之中。当游行队伍经过时，珠儿像只跃跃欲飞的鸟儿，不安地上下扑腾。当整个队伍经过后，她抬头望着赫斯特的脸。
"Mother, " said she, "was that the same minister that kissed me by the brook?"
“妈妈，”她说，“那个就是在溪边亲了我的牧师吗？”
"Hold thy peace, dear little Pearl!" whispered her mother. "We must not always talk in the market-place of what happens to us in the forest. "
“别作声，亲爱的小珠儿！”母亲低语道。“我们可不能老在市场上讲我们在树林里遇到的事情。”
"I could not be sure that it was he; so strange he looked, " continued the child. "Else I would have run to him, and bid him kiss me now, before all the people; even as he did yonder among the dark old trees. What would the minister have said, mother? Would he have clapped his hand over his heart, and scowled on me, and bid me begone?"
“我弄不准那是不是他，他看上去好奇怪。”孩子接着说。“不然我就跑过去，让他当着大家的面亲我了——就像他在那黑漆漆的老树林里做的一样。牧师会说些什么呢，妈妈？他会把手捂在心口，对我瞪眼睛，让我走开吗？”
"What should he say, Pearl?" answered Hester, "save that it was no time to kiss, and that kisses are not to be given in the market-place? Well for thee, foolish child, that thou didst not speak to him!"
“他能说些什么呢，珠儿？”赫斯特问道，“除了说，这不是亲你的时候，在市场上是不能亲你的？总算还好，傻孩子，你没有和他讲话！”
Another shade of the same sentiment in reference to Mr. Dimmesdale was expressed by a person whose eccentricities — or insanity, as we should term it — led her to do what few of the townspeople would have ventured on; to begin a conversation with the wearer of the scarlet letter, in public. It was Mistress Hibbins, who, arrayed in great magnificence, with a triple ruff, a broidered stomacher, a gown of rich velvet, and a gold-headed cane, had come forth to see the procession. As this ancient lady had the renown (which subsequently cost her no less a price than her life) of being a principal actor in all the works of necromancy that were continually going forward, the crowd gave way before her, and seemed to fear the touch of her garment, as if it carried the plague among its gorgeous folds. Seen in conjunction with Hester Prynne — kindly as so many now felt towards the latter — the dread inspired by Mistress Hibbins was doubled, and caused a general movement from that part of the market-place in which the two women stood.
对于丁梅斯代尔牧师，还有一个人也表达了相同的感受，她竟然荒唐到——或者应该说是疯狂到——做了件很少镇里人敢做的事情：她在大庭广众之下和那戴着红字的人讲起话来。那人是西宾斯太太。她套着三层皱领，罩着绣花胸衣，穿着华丽的绒袍，握着根金头手杖，打扮得一派富贵，出来观看游行。这老妇人为人所知，是因为她在那些持续流行的巫术中担任主角，最终起码让她付出了生命的代价。人群为她让出一条路，似乎唯恐触到她的衣袍，仿佛那华丽的衣褶里携带着瘟疫。看到西宾斯太太和赫斯特在一起——尽管人们现在对后者怀有好感，但人们对西宾斯太太的惧怕还是倍增了——大家纷纷挪离两人站的地方。
"Now, what mortal imagination could conceive it!" whispered the old lady confidentially to Hester. "Yonder divine man! That saint on earth, as the people uphold him to be, and as — I must needs say — he really looks! Who, now, that saw him pass in the procession, would think how little while it is since he went forth out of his study — chewing a Hebrew text of Scripture in his mouth, I warrant — to take an airing in the forest! Aha! We know what that means, Hester Prynne! But, truly, forsooth, I find it hard to believe him the same man. Many a church-member saw I, walking behind the music, that has danced in the same measure with me, when Somebody was fiddler, and, it might be, an Indian powwow or a Lapland wizard changing hands with us! That is but a trifle, when a woman knows the world. But this minister! Couldst thou surely tell, Hester, whether he was the same man that encountered thee on the forest-path?"
“哎，凡夫俗子能想出些什么呢！”老太婆悄悄地对赫斯特说。“瞧那神圣的人！人们觉得他是人间圣者，我得说，他看上去还真像那么回事！现在看着他行进在队伍里的人，谁能想到在不久前，他还从书房里出来，去树林里逍遥呢！——我担保，当时他嘴里还念着希伯来文的《圣经》呢。啊哈！我们清楚那是什么意思，赫斯特·普林！不过，说实话，我还是难以相信他就是那同一个人。我看见这么多教民跟在乐队后面游行——他们都曾随着我踏着同样的舞步，那时还有人拉着小提琴，也许还有一个印第安人祭司或拉普兰法师同我们牵着手呢！当女人看透了这世界，这不过是小事一桩。但这个人是牧师啊！赫斯特，你能肯定地说，他就是和你相遇在林间小道上的那个人吗？”
"Madam, I know not of what you speak, " answered Hester Prynne, feeling Mistress Hibbins to be of infirm mind; yet strangely startled and awe-stricken by the confidence with which she affirmed a personal connection between so many persons (herself among them) and the Evil One. "It is not for me to talk lightly of a learned and pious minister of the Word, like the Reverend Mr. Dimmesdale!"
“夫人，我不明白你在说什么，”赫斯特·普林回答道。她觉得西宾斯太太是有点老糊涂了。不过，老太婆如此自信地肯定，这么多人（包括她自己在内）和那邪恶的家伙有私人联系。对此，她异常吃惊，并且吓得要命。“我可没有资格随便谈论像丁梅斯代尔牧师先生那样的牧师，他学识渊博又虔信《圣经》!"
"Fie, woman, fie!" cried the old lady, shaking her finger at Hester. "Dost thou think I have been to the forest so many times, and have yet no skill to judge who else has been there? Yea; though no leaf of the wild garlands, which they wore while they danced, be left in their hair! I know thee, Hester; for I behold the token. We may all see it in the sunshine; and it glows like a red flame in the dark. Thou wearest it openly; so there need be no question about that. But this minister! Let me tell thee, in thine ear! When the Black Man sees one of his own servants, signed and sealed, so shy of owning to the bond as is the Reverend Mr. Dimmesdale, he hath a way of ordering matters so that the mark shall be disclosed in open daylight to the eyes of all the world! What is it that the minister seeks to hide, with his hand always over his heart? Ha, Hester Prynne!"
“呸，女人，呸！”老太婆冲赫斯特摇着一个指头叫道。“你以为我去树林那么多次，还没有本事判断还有谁去过那儿？虽然他们跳舞时戴的花环没有在头发上留下叶子，但我当然知道！我知道你，赫斯特，因为我看到了那个标记。我们都能在阳光下看到它，而在黑夜里，它就如红色之焰般闪光。你公开地戴着它，因此绝不会弄错的。可是这位牧师！让我在你耳朵边讲给你听吧！当黑男人看到自己的一个仆人，叹着气，盖着章，像丁梅斯代尔牧师先生那样羞于承认有这么个协议时，他自有一套办法让标记暴露在光天化日之下、大庭广众之前！牧师用手捂着心口，到底要隐藏什么呢？哈，赫斯特•普林！”
"What is it, good Mistress Hibbins?" eagerly asked little Pearl. "Hast thou seen it?"
“是什么呢，好心的西宾斯太太？”小珠儿急切地问道。“你见过吗？”
"No matter, darling!" responded Mistress Hibbins, making Pearl a profound reverence. "Thou thyself wilt see it, one time or another. They say, child, thou art of the lineage of the Prince of the Air! Wilt thou ride with me, some fine night, to see thy father? Then thou shalt know wherefore the minister keeps his hand over his heart!"
“别管它，可爱的小东西！”西宾斯太太对珠儿毕恭毕敬地说。“总有一天你会看到的。孩子，他们说你是‘空中王子’的后代呢！你要不要在某个晚上，和我一起腾云驾雾，去见见你的爸爸呀？到时你就知道牧师为什么用手捂着心口了！”
Laughing so shrilly that all the market-place could hear her, the weird old gentlewoman took her departure.
这古怪的老太太尖声大笑着走开了，弄得整个市场都能听到。
By this time the preliminary prayer had been offered in the meeting-house, and the accents of the Reverend Mr. Dimmesdale were heard commencing his discourse. An irresistible feeling kept Hester near the spot. As the sacred edifice was too much thronged to admit another auditor, she took up her position close beside the scaffold of the pillory. It was in sufficient proximity to bring the whole sermon to her ears, in the shape of an indistinct, but varied, murmur and flow of the minister's very peculiar voice.
此时，议事厅里已经做完了场前祈祷，人们可以听到丁梅斯代尔牧师先生开始布道了。一种不可抗拒的心情促使赫斯特走近。这神圣的大楼被挤得密不透风，于是她在靠近刑台的地方占了个位置。这地方足可以听到全部的讲道。牧师的声音虽并不响亮，却富有特色、抑扬顿挫，如流水的低吟。
This vocal organ was in itself a rich endowment; insomuch that a listener, comprehending nothing of the language in which the preacher spoke, might still have been swayed to and fro by the mere tone and cadence. Like all other music, it breathed passion and pathos, and emotions high or tender, in a tongue native to the human heart, wherever educated. Muffled as the sound was by its passage through the church walls, Hester Prynne listened with such intentness, and sympathised so intimately, that the sermon had throughout a meaning for her, entirely apart from its indistinguishable words. These, perhaps, if more distinctly heard, might have been only a grosser medium, and have clogged the spiritual sense. Now she caught the low undertone, as of the wind sinking down to repose itself; then ascended with it, as it rose through progressive gradations of sweetness and power, until its volume seemed to envelop her with an atmosphere of awe and solemn grandeur. And yet, majestic as the voice sometimes became, there was forever in it an essential character of plaintiveness. A loud or low expression of anguish — the whisper, or the shriek, as it might be conceived, of suffering humanity, that touched a sensibility in every bosom! At times this deep strain of pathos was all that could be heard, and scarcely heard, sighing amid a desolate silence. But even when the minister's voice grew high and commanding — when it gushed irrepressibly upward — when it assumed its utmost breadth and power, so overfilling the church as to burst its way through the solid walls and diffuse itself in the open air — still, if the auditor listened intently, and for the purpose, he could detect the same cry of pain. What was it? The complaint of a human heart, sorrow-laden, perchance guilty, telling its secret, whether of guilt or sorrow, to the great heart of mankind; beseeching its sympathy or forgiveness — at every moment — in each accent — and never in vain! It was this profound and continual undertone that gave the clergyman his most appropriate power.
人能发声，这本身就是种珍贵的天赋，听众即便对布道者所说一概不知，却依旧能仅因语气和抑扬顿挫的音调而心驰神往。和其他音乐一样，它传达着激情和悲怆，传达着高昂抑或柔和的情绪。不管你受教于何方，都会感到那是人类的心灵母语。虽然声音因穿过教堂的墙壁而显得低沉，但赫斯特·普林听得那么聚精会神，和布道产生了如此贴近的共鸣。在难辨的字面意义之外，这讲道对她有另一番完整的意义。如果这些话语能被听得更清楚些，也许就只成了一种粗俗的工具，反倒阻碍了它精神的含义。如今她听着低低的声音，仿佛是减弱的风势要歇口气。之后，随着那甜美而充满力量的声音逐渐上扬，她的精神也为之振奋起来，直到那声音最后用敬畏、庄严的气氛将她包裹起来。然而，不论这声音变得多么威严，其间永远透着哀伤的本色。这些听上去或高或低的、表达极度痛苦和苦难人生的低语或叫喊，触动了每个人的心扉！有时，这低沉而悲怆的旋律成为你听到的所有声音，有时——这种情况很少——你也能隐约地听到寂静中的哀叹。但即便牧师的声音变得高亢而威严，当那声音不可抑制地直冲云霄，当那声音最为宽厚有力，充斥着教堂，几乎要破壁而出，融于露天之中时，旁听者要是洗耳恭听，还是能分辨出同样的痛苦呼号。那是什么呢？是一颗满负忧伤，也许也充满着罪恶感的心灵在哀怨，向人类伟大的胸怀诉说心中的秘密。不论是愧疚或是悲伤，每时每刻，它的每个声音都在请求同情或宽恕，从来都不会徒劳！这深邃持续的语调给了牧师最恰到好处的力量。
During all this time, Hester stood, statue-like, at the foot of the scaffold. If the minister's voice had not kept her there, there would nevertheless have been an inevitable magnetism in that spot, whence she dated the first hour of her life of ignominy. There was a sense within her — to ill-defined to be made a thought, but weighing heavily on her mind — that her whole orb of life, both before and after, was connected with this spot, as with the one point that gave it unity.
这期间，赫斯特像一尊雕像般，一直站在刑台脚下。如果不是牧师的声音把她吸引在那里的话，那地方必然有一种磁力——她在那里经受了耻辱人生的第一个小时。她有种感觉——这感觉因其隐约而不能被称为思想，却重重地压在她脑海里——她之前之后的整个人生轨迹都与这地方联系在一起了，似乎是这一点才把她的生活连为一体。
Little Pearl, meanwhile, had quitted her mother's side, and was playing at her own will about the market-place. She made the sombre crowd cheerful by her erratic and glistening ray; even as a bird of bright plumage illuminates a whole tree of dusty foliage by darting to and fro, half seen and half concealed amid the twilight of the clustering leaves. She had an undulating, but, oftentimes, a sharp and irregular movement. It indicated the restless vivacity of her spirit, which today was doubly indefatigable in its tiptoe dance, because it was played upon and vibrated with her mother's disquietude. Whenever Pearl saw anything to excite her ever-active and wandering curiosity, she flew thitherward, and, as we might say, seized upon that man or thing as her own property, so far as she desired it; but without yielding the minutest degree of control over her motions in requital. The Puritans looked on, and, if they smiled, were none the less inclined to pronounce the child a demon offspring, from the indescribable charm of beauty and eccentricity that shone through her little figure, and sparkled with its activity. She ran and looked the wild Indian in the face; and he grew conscious of a nature wilder than his own.
与此同时，小珠儿已经离开了她母亲身边，顾自在市场上到处玩开了。她如同反复无常闪动的光线，令严肃的人群变得活跃起来。她甚至像一只羽毛光鲜夺目的小鸟，飞来飞去，在叶簇幽暗的光影里若隐若现，照亮了整棵被灰尘覆盖的树。她行踪飘忽，时常会作出突然而意外的动作。这表明了她那活泼好动的精神劲儿。今天，由于受她母亲不安之情的拨弄和挑动，那足尖就舞跳得愈发不知疲倦了。珠儿的好奇心一直很活跃，无论何时，只要见到任何能激起她好奇心的事物，可以说只要她愿意，她就会飞似地过去抓住那个人或东西，当作自己的财产，绝不稍稍控制下自己的行动来作为回报。清教徒们看着珠儿。他们即便露出微笑，也没少把这孩子看作魔鬼的后代。因为她那从小小的身躯发散着难以名状的魅力，美丽而古怪，随着她的动作而闪烁。她跑过去，盯着那粗野的印第安人的脸；那人便意识到竟有比自己更加天性狂野的人。
Thence, with native audacity, but still with a reserve as characteristic, she flew into the midst of a group of mariners, the swarthy-cheeked wild men of the ocean, as the Indians were of the land; and they gazed wonderingly and admiringly at Pearl, as if a flake on the sea-foam had taken the shape of a little maid, and were gifted with a soul of the sea-fire that flashes beneath the prow in the night-time.
后来，由于天生放肆、却依旧有些拘谨的性格，她又飞进一群水手中间。那群面颊黝黑的海上野蛮人，就好似陆地上的印第安人。他们惊羡地瞅着珠儿，好像她是海的泡沫化身成的少女，被赋予海火的灵魂，在夜晚的船首下闪烁。
One of these seafaring men — the shipmaster, indeed, who had spoken to Hester Prynne — was so smitten with Pearl's aspect, that he attempted to lay hands upon her, with purpose to snatch a kiss. Finding it as impossible to touch her as to catch a humming-bird in the air, he took from his hat the gold chain that was twisted about it, and threw it to the child. Pearl immediately twined it around her neck and waist, with such happy skill that, once seen there, it became a part of her, and it was difficult to imagine her without it.
其中的一个水手——就是那个同赫斯特•普林谈过话的船长——被珠儿的模样深深吸引，便试图把手放在她头上，打算亲亲她。他发现，要碰到她简直就像抓一只在空中飞鸣而过的小鸟一样，根本不可能。于是他就把围在帽子上的金链拿下来，扔给了孩子。珠儿立马巧妙地把金链围在了脖子上和腰间。一眼看去，那金链就像是她的一部分，让人难以想象她没有金链的样子。
"Thy mother is yonder woman with the scarlet letter, " said the seaman. "Wilt thou carry her a message from me?"
“你妈妈就是那边戴着红字的女人吗？”船长问。“你替我捎个口信好吗？”
"If the message pleases me, I will, " answered Pearl.
“要是那口信讨我喜欢，我就捎。”珠儿回答。
"Then tell her, " rejoined he, "that I spake again with the black-a-visaged hump-shouldered old doctor, and he engages to bring his friend, the gentleman she wots of, aboard with him. So let thy mother take no thought, save for herself and thee. Wilt thou tell her this, thou witch-baby?"
“那就告诉她，”他接着说，“我又和那黑脸驼背的老医生讲过了，他保证带着他的朋友上船——就是你妈妈提到过的那位。所以你妈妈除了照顾好你和她自己，就不用操心别的了。你能告诉她吗，你这小妖精？”
"Mistress Hibbins says my father is the Prince of the Air!" cried Pearl, with a naughty smile. "If thou callest me that ill name, I shall tell him of thee, and he will chase thy ship with a tempest!"
“西宾斯太太说我爸爸是空中王子！”珠儿一边调皮地笑着，一边叫道。“你要是再叫得那么难听，我就告诉他，他会用暴风雨追你的船！”
Pursuing a zigzag course across the market-place, the child returned to her mother and communicated what the mariner had said. Hester's strong, calm, steadfastly enduring spirit almost sank, at last, on beholding this dark and grim countenance of an inevitable doom, which — at the moment when a passage seemed to open for the minister and herself out of their labyrinth of misery — showed itself, with an unrelenting smile, right in the midst of their path.
这孩子沿着弯弯曲曲的路线穿过市场，回到母亲身边，转告了船长的话。看到这不可避免的命运其阴森面目后，赫斯特那坚强、镇定、坚韧不拔的精神，终于崩溃了。就在一条通路似乎为她和牧师而敞开，要领他们走出悲惨的迷宫时，这带着毫不留情的冷笑的面孔，恰恰挡在他们的路中间。
With her mind harassed by the terrible perplexity in which the shipmaster's intelligence involved her, she was also subjected to another trial. There were many people present from the country round about who had often heard of the scarlet letter, and to whom it had been made terrific by a hundred false or exaggerated rumours, but who had never beheld it with their own bodily eyes. These, after exhausting other modes of amusement, now thronged about Hester Prynne with rude and boorish intrusiveness. Unscrupulous as it was, however, it could not bring them nearer than a circuit of several yards. At that distance they accordingly stood, fixed there by the centrifugal force of the repugnance which the mystic symbol inspired. The whole gang of sailors, likewise, observing the press of spectators, and learning the purport of the scarlet letter, came and thrust their sunburnt and desperado-looking faces into the ring. Even the Indians were affected by a sort of cold shadow of the white man's curiosity, and, gliding through the crowd, fastened their snake-like black eyes on Hester's bosom, conceiving, perhaps, that the wearer of this brilliantly embroidered badge must needs be a personage of high dignity among her people. Lastly, the inhabitants of the town (their own interest in this worn-out subject languidly reviving itself, by sympathy with what they saw others feel) lounged idly to the same quarter, and tormented Hester Prynne, perhaps more than all the rest, with their cool, well-acquainted gaze at her familiar shame. Hester saw and recognised the self-same faces of that group of matrons, who had awaited her forthcoming from the prison-door, seven years ago; all save one, the youngest and only compassionate among them, whose burial-robe she had since made. At the final hour, when she was so soon to fling aside the burning letter, it had strangely become the centre of more remark and excitement, and was thus made to sear her breast more painfully than at any time since the first day she put it on.
船长捎来的这条消息，带给了她可怕的困惑，扰得她心神不宁。可是，她还要面对另一个考验。市场里很多从乡下来的人经常听说红字的事，却从来没有亲眼目睹过。对他们来说，数以百计的误传和夸张的谣言，使红字变得十分可怕。这些人看腻了其他的娱乐，此刻便粗鲁无礼地挤在赫斯特·普林身边。他们肆无忌惮地挤过来，却还是停在六七米远的圈子外。他们就这么远地站着，被那神秘之符所激起的反感离心力钉在那里。那帮水手也注意到了围观的人，弄明白了那红字的涵义，也凑过去，把被太阳晒得黝黑的、亡命徒般的面孔伸进圈子里。连印第安人也受到了白人好奇心的无声影响，穿过人群，眯起蛇一般的黑眼睛，盯着赫斯特的胸前。他们或许以为，那戴着耀眼夺目的丝绣徽记的人，准是这群人中德高望重之辈。最后，镇上的居民（看到别人的反应，他们也无精打采地恢复了对这个陈旧话题的兴趣）懒散地挪到这一角落，用他们冰冷惯见的目光注视着赫斯特•普林熟悉的耻辱标记，这或许比别人对她的折磨更甚。赫斯特看到并认出了跟七年前相同的那几张脸，她们是等她走出监狱大门的那群女看守。只是少了一人，就是她们中最年轻，且是唯一有同情心的人；赫斯特后来给她做了葬服。在她即将甩掉这灼人红字的最后时刻，它居然莫名其妙地成为了更能引起谈资和兴奋的中心。自戴上红字以来，她还从未如此痛苦地感到，它在她的胸膛上灼烧。
While Hester stood in that magic circle of ignominy where the cunning cruelty of her sentence seemed to have fixed her forever, the admirable preacher was looking down from the sacred pulpit upon an audience whose very inmost spirits had yielded to his control. The sainted minister in the church! The woman of the scarlet letter in the market-place! What imagination would have been irreverent enough to surmise that the same scorching stigma was on them both!
赫斯特站在那耻辱的魔力圈里，那狡诈而残忍的判决似乎将她永远钉在那里。与此同时，可敬的布道者在神圣的祭坛上俯视着他的听众，他们最内在的精神臣服于他的掌握。这教堂里神圣的牧师啊！这市场里戴着红字的女人啊！怎样不敬的想象才能猜到，他们有着同一个灼热的污名啊！
