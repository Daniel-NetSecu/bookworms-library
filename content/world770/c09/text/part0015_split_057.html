MARCH.  

三月  

THE EVENING SCHOOLS.

夜校

Thursday, 2d.

星期四，2日

Last night my father took me to see the evening schools in our Baretti schoolhouse, which were all lighted up already, and where the workingmen were already beginning to enter. On our arrival we found the head-master and the other masters in a great rage, because a little while before the glass in one window had been broken by a stone. The beadle had darted forth and seized a boy by the hair, who was passing; but thereupon, Stardi, who lives in the house opposite, had presented himself, and said:— 
昨天晚上，父亲带我去看了在我们巴莱缇校舍里的夜校，那里已经点亮了灯火，工人们也开始进校了。我们到了之后，发现校长和其他老师都在大发脾气，因为就在不久之前，一块窗玻璃被石头打破了。仪仗官冲上前去，抓住一个路过的男孩的头发，但是住在对面房子里的斯带地立刻跑过来，说：
"This is not the right one; I saw it with my own eyes; it was Franti who threw it; and he said to me, 'Woe to you if you tell of me!' but I am not afraid." 
“不是这个人，我亲眼看到了，是弗兰蒂扔的，他还跟我说：‘你要是告发我，就要你好看！’但是我不怕。”
Then the head-master declared that Franti should be expelled for good. In the meantime I was watching the workingmen enter by twos and threes; and more than two hundred had already entered. I have never seen anything so fine as the evening school. There were boys of twelve and upwards; bearded men who were on their way from their work, carrying their books and copy-books; there were carpenters, engineers with black faces, masons with hands white with plaster, bakers' boys with their hair full of flour; and there was perceptible the odor of varnish, hides, fish, oil,—odors of all the various trades. There also entered a squad of artillery workmen, dressed like soldiers and headed by a corporal. They all filed briskly to their benches, removed the board underneath, on which we put our feet, and immediately bent their heads over their work.
然后校长表明弗兰蒂应该被永远开除。与此同时，我正在看着工人们三三两两地进教室，并且已经来了两百多人了。我从没看到过像夜校这么有趣的事。其中有十二岁和十二岁以上的孩子；有留着胡须的下了班的大人，手里拿着书和字帖；也有木匠、脸黑黑的技工、手被灰泥染白的石匠、头发上沾满面粉的面包店男孩。在那里还能闻到油漆、皮革、鱼、油的味道——各行各业的味道。还进来了一队炮兵部队的工人，他们一身士兵打扮，由一个下士带领着。他们都迅速坐在各自的长凳上，拿掉下面我们垫脚的木板，立刻埋下头做作业。
Some stepped up to the teachers to ask explanations, with their open copy-books in their hands. I caught sight of that young and well-dressed master "the little lawyer," who had three or four workingmen clustered round his table, and was making corrections with his pen; and also the lame one, who was laughing with a dyer who had brought him a copy-book all adorned with red and blue dyes. My master, who had recovered, and who will return to school Tomorrow, was there also. The doors of the schoolroom were open. I was amazed, when the lessons began, to see how attentive they all were, and how they kept their eyes fixed on their work. Yet the greater part of them, so the head-master said, for fear of being late, had not even been home to eat a mouthful of supper, and they were hungry.
有些人手里拿着翻开的字帖，走上前去请老师解答问题。我看到那个年轻的、穿着讲究的老师“小律师”被三四个工人围着，正在用钢笔批改作业；还有那个跛脚的老师，他正跟那个拿给他一本沾着红色和蓝色染料的习字帖的染工一起笑着。我的老师也在那里，他已经康复了，明天就会回学校。教室的门开着。开始上课时，我惊奇地看到他们是那么地专心致志，眼睛专注地盯着书本。据校长说，他们大部分人为了不迟到，甚至没有回家吃上一口晚饭，正饿着肚子呢。
But the younger ones, after half an hour of school, were falling off the benches with sleep; one even went fast asleep with his head on the bench, and the master waked him up by poking his ear with a pen. But the grown-up men did nothing of the sort; they kept awake, and listened, with their mouths wide open, to the lesson, without even winking; and it made a deep impression on me to see all those bearded men on our benches. We also ascended to the story floor above, and I ran to the door of my schoolroom and saw in my seat a man with a big mustache and a bandaged hand, who might have injured himself while at work about some machine; but he was trying to write, though very, very slowly.
但是那些比较年轻的人，上了半小时的课就在凳子上打瞌睡了；甚至有个人头趴在桌子上睡得很熟，然后老师用钢笔戳了戳他的耳朵，把他弄醒了。但是大人们从不做这样的事，他们很清醒，听课时嘴巴圆张着，甚至不眨一下眼睛。所有那些留着胡子坐在我们的凳子上的人们给我留下了深刻的印象。我们去了楼上，我跑到我那间教室的门口，看到我的座位上坐着一个留着大胡子、手上缠着绷带的人，他可能是在操作什么机器时受的伤；尽管他写得非常慢，他还是努力写着。
But what pleased me most was to behold in the seat of the little mason, on the very same bench and in the very same corner, his father, the mason, as huge as a giant, who sat there all coiled up into a narrow space, with his chin on his fists and his eyes on his book, so absorbed that he hardly breathed. And there was no chance about it, for it was he himself who said to the head-master the first evening he came to the school:— 
但是最让我开心的是看到小石匠的位置上，就是那个角落里的那条长凳上，坐着他巨人一般的石匠父亲。他蜷缩在那个狭小的空间里，拳头托着下巴，眼睛看着课本，全神贯注得连气都不怎么出了。那不是碰巧，因为他第一天晚上来学校时就亲自对校长说：
"Signor Director, do me the favor to place me in the seat of 'my hare's face.'" For he always calls his son so.
“校长阁下，拜托你让我坐在我家‘兔子脸’的位置上。”因为他经常这样叫他的儿子。
My father kept me there until the end, and in the street we saw many women with children in their arms, waiting for their husbands; and at the entrance a change was effected: the husbands took the children in their arms, and the women made them surrender their books and copy-books; and in this wise they proceeded to their homes. For several minutes the street was filled with people and with noise. Then all grew silent, and all we could see was the tall and weary form of the head-master disappearing in the distance.
我父亲一直让我在那里呆到放学。在街上，我们看到很多怀里抱着孩子的妇人在等她们的丈夫；而在进口处，角色换了过来：丈夫们抱着孩子，妇人们则让他们交出课本和习字帖，然后他们就这样朝家走去。几分钟时间里，街上满是人群和喧闹声。然后一切安静了下来，我们只能看到校长高挑而疲惫的身影消失在远处。
