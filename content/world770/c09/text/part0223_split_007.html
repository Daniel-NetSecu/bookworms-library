CHAPTER VIII  

第八章  

"My father has gone," thought Eugenie, who heard all that took place from the head of the stairs. Silence was restored in the house, and the distant rumbling of the carriage, ceasing by degrees, no longer echoed through the sleeping town. At this moment Eugenie heard in her heart, before the sound caught her ears, a cry which pierced the partitions and came from her cousin's chamber. A line of light, thin as the blade of a sabre, shone through a chink in the door and fell horizontally on the balusters of the rotten staircase.
“我父亲走了。”欧仁妮心想，她在楼梯顶部听到了所发生的一切。屋子又恢复了寂静；远处马车的隆隆声渐渐消失了，不再在这个沉睡的城市里回荡了。这时，欧仁妮耳朵还没听到，就先在心里听见了一声哭喊，那哭喊声从她堂弟的卧室穿过隔墙传了过来。一道像马刀刀刃一样细的光从门缝射出来，横照在破旧的楼梯的扶手上。
"He suffers!" she said, springing up the stairs. A second moan brought her to the landing near his room. The door was ajar, she pushed it open. Charles was sleeping; his head hung over the side of the old armchair, and his hand, from which the pen had fallen, nearly touched the floor. The oppressed breathing caused by the strained posture suddenly frightened Eugenie, who entered the room hastily.
“他很难受！”她一边往楼上跑，一边说。听到第二声呻吟时，她已跑到了靠近他房间的楼梯口。门虚掩着，她推开了门。夏尔正睡着，他的头垂在旧扶手椅的一侧，一只手几乎触到了地板，笔已从那只手里掉了下来。这种不舒服的姿势造成他呼吸困难，这让欧仁妮突然害怕起来，她赶紧进了屋子。
"He must be very tired," she said to herself, glancing at a dozen letters lying sealed upon the table. She read their addresses: 
“他一定很累。”她自言自语道，瞥见了桌子上放着的十几封已封好的信。她看看信上的地址：
"To Messrs. Farry, Breilmann, & Co., carriage-makers”; "To Monsieur Buisson, tailor," etc.
“法里、布雷依曼车行收”；“比松裁缝店收”等等。
"He has been settling all his affairs, so as to leave France at once," she thought. Her eyes fell upon two open letters. The words, "My dear Annette," at the head of one of them, blinded her for a moment. Her heart beat fast, her feet were nailed to the floor.
“他一直在处理他的事情，这样就能马上离开法国了。”她想。她的眼睛落在两封打开的信上。信里写道：“我亲爱的安妮特，”一看其中一封的开头，她的两眼瞬间就发黑了。她的心跳加速，脚像被钉在了地板上一样。
"His dear Annette! He loves! He is loved! No hope! What does he say to her?" 
“他亲爱的安妮特！他在恋爱！有人爱着他！没有希望了！他对她说了什么？”
These thoughts rushed through her head and heart. She saw the words everywhere, even on the bricks of the floor, in letters of fire.
这些念头从她的脑海和心里闪过。她到处都能看见这些像火焰一样的字，甚至地板砖上都有。
"Resign him already? No, no! I will not read the letter. I ought to go away—What if I do read it?” 
“就这样放弃他？不，不！我不看这封信。我应该走开——我要是看了会怎样呢？”
She looked at Charles, then she gently took his head and placed it against the back of the chair; he let her do so, like a child which, though asleep, knows its mother's touch and receives, without awaking, her kisses and watchful care. Like a mother Eugenie raised the drooping hand, and like a mother she gently kissed the chestnut hair—"Dear Annette!" a demon shrieked the words in her ear.
她看着夏尔，然后温柔地把他的头托起，并将其靠在椅背上；他任由她摆布着，就像一个孩子，尽管睡着，也知道那是他母亲的爱抚，便继续在梦乡里享受着她的亲吻和小心的呵护。欧仁妮就像母亲一样，拿起他垂下的手，像母亲一样温柔地亲吻着他那栗色的头发——“亲爱的安妮特！”一个魔鬼在她耳边尖声叫着这几个字眼。
"I am doing wrong; but I must read it, that letter," she said. She turned away her head, for her noble sense of honor reproached her. For the first time in her life good and evil struggled together in her heart. Up to that moment she had never had to blush for any action. Passion and curiosity triumphed. As she read each sentence her heart swelled more and more, and the keen glow which filled her being as she did so, only made the joys of first love still more precious.
“我这么做不对，可是我必须看那封信。”她说。她扭过头去，因为她高尚的正义感在谴责她。生平第一次，善与恶一起在她心里挣扎。直到那一刻，她还从来没有做过任何让她脸红的事。强烈的爱和好奇心胜利了。她一句一句读着，情绪越来越激动，看信时溢满全身的炙热只能让这初恋的快乐变得更加珍贵。
My dear Annette,—Nothing could ever have separated us but the great misfortune which has now overwhelmed me, and which no human foresight could have prevented. My father has killed himself; his fortune and mine are irretrievably lost. I am orphaned at an age when, through the nature of my education, I am still a child; and yet I must lift myself as a man out of the abyss into which I am plunged. I have just spent half the night in facing my position.
我亲爱的安妮特——除了我现在遭遇的这巨大不幸——这是任何人都无法预料到的——什么也不能把我们分开。我父亲自杀了，他的财产和我的财产都荡然无存，不可挽回了。我这个年龄就成了孤儿，以我所受的教育性质而言，我这个年纪还算是个孩子，但是我必须像个男子汉一样，从我身陷的深渊中爬起来。我刚花了半个晚上的时间琢磨我的处境。
If I wish to leave France an honest man,—and there is no doubt of that,—I have not a hundred francs of my own with which to try my fate in the Indies or in America. Yes, my poor Anna, I must seek my fortune in those deadly climates. Under those skies, they tell me, I am sure to make it. As for remaining in Paris, I cannot do so. Neither my nature nor my face are made to bear the affronts, the neglect, the disdain shown to a ruined man, the son of a bankrupt! Good God! Think of owing two millions! I should be killed in a duel the first week; therefore I shall not return there. Your love—the most tender and devoted love which ever ennobled the heart of man—cannot draw me back. Alas! My beloved, 
如果我希望以一个正人君子的身份离开法国——这一点是毫无疑问的——去西印度群岛或美洲碰碰运气，我连一百法郎都没有。是的，我可怜的安娜，我必须要在那种恶劣的气候条件下寻找发财的机会。他们告诉我，在那些地方，我肯定能成功。至于说继续留在巴黎，我是做不到的。无论我的本性，还是我的脸面，都受不了别人对一个倾家荡产的人——一个破产者的儿子所表示出的那种轻蔑、冷漠和鄙薄！老天啊！想想吧，竟然欠了两百万！我第一个星期就会在决斗中被人杀死，因此我是不会回去的。你的爱——最温柔、最忠贞的爱，曾经使一个男人的心灵得到升华——也是无法把我拉回去的。唉！我的心上人，
I have no money with which to go to you, to give and receive a last kiss from which I might derive some strength for my forlorn enterprise.
我没钱去找你，好给你最后一吻，或接受你的最后一吻，从亲吻中，我可能会为我那几乎没有成功希望的事业汲取一些力量。
"Poor Charles! I did well to read the letter. I have gold; I will give it to him," thought Eugenie.
“可怜的夏尔！幸亏我读了这封信。我有金子，我会给他的。”欧仁妮心想。
She wiped her eyes, and went on reading.
她擦擦眼睛，继续往下看。
I have never thought of the miseries of poverty. If I have the hundred louis required for the mere costs of the journey, I have not a sou for an outfit. But no, I have not the hundred louis, not even one louis. I don't know that anything will be left after I have paid my debts in Paris. If I have nothing, I shall go quietly to Nantes and ship as a common sailor; and I will begin in the new world like other men who have started young without a sou and brought back the wealth of the Indies. During this long day I have faced my future coolly. It seems more horrible for me than for another, because I have been so petted by a mother who adored me, so indulged by the kindest of fathers, so blessed by meeting, on my entrance into life, with the love of an Anna! The flowers of life are all I have ever known. Such happiness could not last.
我从没想过贫困的悲惨。我即使有了一百金路易用作必不可少的旅行费，也没有一个子儿用来做本钱。可是，没有，我没有那一百个金路易，甚至连一个都没有。我不知道等我把巴黎的债全都还清后，还能剩下什么。如果我身无分文，我会一声不响地去南特，上船当个普通的水手；我会像其他那些年轻时白手起家的人一样，开始在新世界闯荡，从西印度群岛赚回大笔的财富。在这漫长的一天中，我冷静地考虑了我的前途。我的前途似乎比别人的都更可怕，因为我曾被疼爱我的母亲宠爱过，受过最慈祥的父亲的溺爱，刚一踏入社会，又幸运地得到了一位叫安娜的女士的爱情！我所知道的就只是生活中的鲜花。这样的幸福不可能长久。
Nevertheless, my dear Annette, I feel more courage than a careless young man is supposed to feel,—above all a young man used to the caressing ways of the dearest woman in all Paris, cradled in family joys, on whom all things smiled in his home, whose wishes were a law to his father—oh, my father! Annette, he is dead!
可是，我亲爱的安妮特，我感觉有了更多的勇气，超过了一个无忧无虑的年轻人应该有的——尤其是一个习惯了巴黎最迷人的女人的爱抚、享尽家庭幸福的年轻人，他在家里事事顺心，父亲对他则是有求必应——哦，我的父亲呀！安妮特，他死了！
Well, I have thought over my position, and yours as well. I have grown old in twenty-four hours. Dear Anna, if in order to keep me with you in Paris you were to sacrifice your luxury, your dress, your opera-box, we should even then not have enough for the expenses of my extravagant ways of living. Besides, I would never accept such sacrifices. No, we must part now and forever— 
唉，我反复考虑了我的处境，还有你的处境。一天之内，我就变老了。亲爱的安娜，如果让我和你一起留在巴黎，你就要放弃你的奢华、你的衣着、你的歌剧院包厢，我们甚至都没有足够的钱来维持我挥霍的生活。再说，我决不会让你放弃这些的。不，咱们现在就得永别了——
"He gives her up! Blessed Virgin! What happiness!" 
“他放弃了她！圣母玛利亚！太幸福了！”
Eugenie quivered with joy. Charles made a movement, and a chill of terror ran through her. Fortunately, he did not wake, and she resumed her reading.
欧仁妮高兴得直发抖。夏尔动了一下，吓得她浑身一阵寒战。所幸，他没有醒来，于是她接着看下去。
When shall I return? I do not know. The climate of the West Indies ages a European, so they say, especially a European who works hard. Let us think what may happen ten years hence. In ten years your daughter will be eighteen; she will be your companion, your spy. To you society will be cruel, and your daughter perhaps more cruel still. We have seen cases of the harsh social judgment and ingratitude of daughters; let us take warning by them. Keep in the depths of your soul, as I shall in mine, the memory of four years of happiness, and be faithful, if you can, to the memory of your poor friend. I cannot exact such faithfulness, because, do you see, dear Annette, I must conform to the exigencies of my new life; I must take a commonplace view of them and do the best I can. Therefore I must think of marriage, which becomes one of the necessities of my future existence; and I will admit to you that I have found, here in Saumur, in my uncle's house, a cousin whose face, manners, mind, and heart would please you, and who, besides, seems to me— 
我什么时候会回来？我不知道。据他们说，西印度群岛的气候会让一个欧洲人变老，尤其是一个辛苦劳作的欧洲人。让我们想想十年后可能会发生什么吧。十年后，你的女儿就十八岁了，她将成为你的伙伴，你的密探。对你来说，社会将是残酷的，而你的女儿也许更为残酷。严厉的社会评判和女儿们忘恩负义的例子屡见不鲜，我们要引以为戒。请像我一样，把这四年幸福的回忆珍藏在你的心灵深处吧，再有，如果可能的话，一定要将你这可怜的朋友铭记在心，并对他忠诚不渝。你知道吗，亲爱的安妮特，我不能强求这份忠诚，因为我必须适应我新生活中的种种突变；我必须用平常的眼光来看待它们，并尽力而为。所以我必须考虑结婚，这成了我未来生活中一件必须做的事；我要向你承认，就在索米尔这里，在我伯父的家中，我遇到了一位堂姐，她的容貌、举止、头脑和心地都会让你喜欢的，而且在我看来，她似乎——
"He must have been very weary to have ceased writing to her," thought Eugenie, as she gazed at the letter which stopped abruptly in the middle of the last sentence.
“他一定是太累了，才停下笔来。”当欧仁妮看到信的最后一句话戛然停到半中间时，心里这样想。
Already she defended him. How was it possible that an innocent girl should perceive the cold-heartedness evinced by this letter? To young girls religiously brought up, whose minds are ignorant and pure, all is love from the moment they set their feet within the enchanted regions of that passion. They walk there bathed in a celestial light shed from their own souls, which reflects its rays upon their lover; they color all with the flame of their own emotion and attribute to him their highest thoughts. A woman's errors come almost always from her belief in good or her confidence in truth. In Eugenie's simple heart the words, "My dear Annette, my loved one," echoed like the sweetest language of love; they caressed her soul as, in childhood, the divine notes of the Venite adoremus, repeated by the organ, caressed her ear. Moreover, the tears which still lingered on the young man's lashes gave signs of that nobility of heart by which young girls are rightly won. How could she know that Charles, though he loved his father and mourned him truly, was moved far more by paternal goodness than by the goodness of his own heart? Monsieur and Madame Guillaume Grandet, by gratifying every fancy of their son, and lavishing upon him the pleasures of a large fortune, had kept him from making the horrible calculations of which so many sons in Paris become more or less guilty when, face to face with the enjoyments of the world, they form desires and conceive schemes which they see with bitterness must be put off or laid aside during the lifetime of their parents.
她已经在为他辩护了。一个天真无邪的姑娘怎么可能察觉出信中所流露的冷漠无情呢？对于在宗教气氛中长大的少女来说，她们的思想无知而纯洁，一旦涉足那迷人的爱情世界，便把一切都看作爱情了。她们漫步其中，沐浴在天国的光辉里，这圣光从她们的灵魂中散发出来，又映照到她们所爱的人身上；她们用自己感情的火焰点燃一切，并把自己崇高的思想归于爱人的功劳。一个女人的错误几乎总是来自于她对善的信仰或是她对真理的信任。“我亲爱的安妮特，我的心上人”这几个字就像是爱情最甜美的语言，在欧仁妮单纯的内心里回响着；它们轻抚着她的心灵，仿佛儿时听的“来吧，让我们一起赞美”，那美妙的音符被风琴反复弹奏着，悦耳动听。而且，仍旧挂在那个年轻人睫毛上的泪水显示出了那种心灵的高贵，少女们自然而然会为之动情。夏尔尽管爱着他的父亲并真诚地在悼念他，这与其说是出于他的心地善良，倒不如说是为父爱所感动，她又怎么会知道这个呢？纪尧姆·葛朗台先生和夫人总是满足儿子的所有愿望，给他大笔的钱财去随意挥霍，使得夏尔不会去做些可怕的算计，这是巴黎很多的少爷们或多或少都会有的罪孽。那些少爷们在面对花花世界时，他们便产生了欲念，心怀鬼胎。他们痛苦地看到，在父母的有生之年，这些欲念和阴谋都必须推后或暂时放到一边。
The liberality of the father in this instance had shed into the heart of the son a real love, in which there was no afterthought of self-interest.
在这种情况下，父亲的慷慨在儿子的心中撒下了真爱，这真爱中没有任何自私自利、有所保留的念头。
Nevertheless, Charles was a true child of Paris, taught by the customs of society and by Annette herself to calculate everything; already an old man under the mask of youth. He had gone through the frightful education of social life, of that world where in one evening more crimes are committed in thought and speech than justice ever punishes at the assizes; where jests and clever sayings assassinate the noblest ideas; where no one is counted strong unless his mind sees clear: and to see clear in that world is to believe in nothing, neither in feelings, nor in men, nor even in events,—for events are falsified.
可是，夏尔是个真正的巴黎孩子，在社会风气和安妮特亲身的调教下，他凡事都要盘算一番，虽然脸上还洋溢着年轻气，但他已经是个世故的人了。他曾经历过社会生活的可怕教育，在那个社会生活圈子里，一个晚上，在思想和言语上所犯的罪比法官在巡回审判庭所判罚的罪行还要多；打趣和俏皮话在诋毁最高尚的思想；只有目光准确的人才可谓强者：在那个圈子里，目光准确就是一概不信，既不相信感情，也不相信人，甚至也不相信事实——因为事实是捏造的。
There, to "see clear" we must weigh a friend's purse daily, learn how to keep ourselves adroitly on the top of the wave, cautiously admire nothing, neither works of art nor glorious actions, and remember that self-interest is the mainspring of all things here below. After committing many follies, the great lady—the beautiful Annette—compelled Charles to think seriously; with her perfumed hand among his curls, she talked to him of his future position; as she rearranged his locks, she taught him lessons of worldly prudence; she made him effeminate and materialized him,—a double corruption, but a delicate and elegant corruption, in the best taste.
在那个圈子里，要想“目光准确”，我们就必须每天都掂掂朋友钱包的分量，学会如何巧妙地使自己保持上风；还要谨慎小心，不赞赏任何东西，不管是艺术作品，还是值得称颂的行为；并牢记自身的利益是尘世间一切事物的主要动力。在数番风流放纵之后，那个贵妇——美丽的安妮特——迫使夏尔进行了认真的思考；她把散发着香气的手放进他的卷发，跟他谈论着他将来的地位；当她重新梳理他的一绺绺头发时，她教给他精明处世的经验；她使他变得女性化、物质化——这是一种双重的腐蚀，却又是一种细腻而优雅的腐蚀，十分高雅得体。
"You are very foolish, Charles," she would say to him. "I shall have a great deal of trouble in teaching you to understand the world. You behaved extremely ill to Monsieur des Lupeaulx. I know very well he is not an honorable man; but wait till he is no longer in power, then you may despise him as much as you like. Do you know what Madame Campan used to tell us? —'My dears, as long as a man is a minister, adore him; when he falls, help to drag him in the gutter. Powerful, he is a sort of god; fallen, he is lower than Marat in the sewer, because he is living, and Marat is dead. Life is a series of combinations, and you must study them and understand them if you want to keep yourselves always in good position.'” 
“你很傻，夏尔，”她会对他说，“我得费好大劲来教你懂得世道。你对德吕伯克斯先生的态度很糟糕。我很清楚他不是个正人君子，可是你得等到他失势的时候，才可以随心所欲地鄙薄他。你知道康庞夫人是怎么对我们说的？——‘亲爱的，只要一个人还在台上，就敬爱他；等他下了台，就帮着把他拖到阴沟里。有权时，他是个神；倒台时，他比阴沟里的马拉还要低等，因为他还活着，而马拉死了。人生就是一系列的暗码，要想使自己一直处于有利的位置，你就必须研究它们，理解它们。’”
Charles was too much a man of the world, his parents had made him too happy, he had received too much adulation in society, to be possessed of noble sentiments. The grain of gold dropped by his mother into his heart was beaten thin in the smithy of Parisian society; he had spread it superficially, and it was worn away by the friction of life. Charles was only twenty-one years old. At that age the freshness of youth seems inseparable from candor and sincerity of soul. The voice, the glance, the face itself, seem in harmony with the feelings; and thus it happens that the sternest judge, the most sceptical lawyer, the least complying of usurers, always hesitate to admit decrepitude of heart or the corruption of worldly calculation while the eyes are still bathed in purity and no wrinkles seam the brow. Charles, so far, had had no occasion to apply the maxims of Parisian morality; up to this time he was still endowed with the beauty of inexperience. And yet, unknown to himself, he had been inoculated with selfishness. The germs of Parisian political economy, latent in his heart, would assuredly burst forth, sooner or later, whenever the careless spectator became an actor in the drama of real life.
夏尔老于世故，他的父母让他过得太幸福了，在社交中，他又曾受到太多的阿谀奉承，他是不会拥有高尚的情感的。母亲在他心中播下的金粒子已经在巴黎社会的锻造中被敲打得薄之又薄。他把金子撒在表面，而金子在生活的摩擦下被慢慢磨蚀掉了。夏尔只有二十一岁。在这个年纪，青春的朝气似乎和心灵的直率和真诚是分不开的。那声音、那目光、那面容本身似乎都是和感情协调一致的。所以即使是最严厉的法官、最不轻信的律师、最不通融的高利贷者都总是不会贸然相信：一个人的眼睛仍旧明亮清澈，额头也没有一道皱纹，内心却老于世故或是心术不正。迄今为止，夏尔还没有机会去应用巴黎式的道德准则。直至此时，他的美还美在缺乏经验。然而，他不知不觉就已经被灌输了自私的思想。巴黎人政治经济的胚芽在他心里潜伏着，一旦悠然自得的旁观者变成了现实生活戏剧中的演员，迟早那胚芽一定会开花的。
Nearly all young girls succumb to the tender promises such an outward appearance seems to offer: even if Eugenie had been as prudent and observing as provincial girls are often found to be, she was not likely to distrust her cousin when his manners, words, and actions were still in unison with the aspirations of a youthful heart. A mere chance—a fatal chance—threw in her way the last effusions of real feeling which stirred the young man's soul; she heard as it were the last breathings of his conscience. She laid down the letter—to her so full of love—and began smilingly to watch her sleeping cousin; the fresh illusions of life were still, for her at least, upon his face; she vowed to herself to love him always. Then she cast her eyes on the other letter, without attaching much importance to this second indiscretion; and though she read it, it was only to obtain new proofs of the noble qualities which, like all women, she attributed to the man her heart had chosen.
几乎所有的少女都会被这种外表所预示的温柔所迷惑：即使欧仁妮也像外省姑娘一样谨慎，并且善于察言观色，但是在堂弟的举止、言辞及行为还和年轻人内心的憧憬相一致的时候，她是不可能怀疑他的。只是一个偶然的机会——一个致命的机会——让她看到了激荡在那个年轻人心中的最后一次真情流露；她似乎听到了他良心最后的呼吸声。她把那封在她看来情意绵绵的信放下，开始微笑着端详起她熟睡的堂弟；至少在她眼里，对人生充满朝气的幻想依旧还停留在他的脸上；她暗暗发誓要永远爱他。接着，她把目光投向另外一封信，不再觉得这第二次鲁莽之举有多么严重了；尽管她在看着信，但她唯一的目的却是为了找到品格高尚的新证据，像所有女人一样，她认为这是她心仪的男人所具有的品质。
My dear Alphonse,—When you receive this letter I shall be without friends; but let me assure you that while I doubt the friendship of the world, I have never doubted yours. I beg you therefore to settle all my affairs, and I trust to you to get as much as you can out of my possessions. By this time you know my situation. I have nothing left, and I intend to go at once to the Indies. I have just written to all the people to whom I think I owe money, and you will find enclosed a list of their names, as correct as I can make it from memory. My books, my furniture, my pictures, my horses, etc., ought, I think, to pay my debts. I do not wish to keep anything, except, perhaps, a few baubles which might serve as the beginning of an outfit for my enterprise. My dear Alphonse, I will send you a proper power of attorney under which you can make these sales. Send me all my weapons. Keep Briton for yourself; nobody would pay the value of that noble beast, and I would rather give him to you—like a mourning-ring bequeathed by a dying man to his executor. Farry, Breilmann, & Co. built me a very comfortable travelling-carriage, which they have not yet delivered; persuade them to keep it and not ask for any payment on it. If they refuse, do what you can in the matter, and avoid everything that might seem dishonorable in me under my present circumstances. I owe the British Islander six louis, which I lost at cards; don't fail to pay him— 
我亲爱的阿方斯——当你收到这封信时，我已经没有朋友了；可是我向你保证，尽管我怀疑这世上的友谊，却从没有怀疑过你的友情。因此，我求你来料理我的所有事情，并且指望你竭尽所能把我的财物卖个好价。现在，你已经知道了我的处境。我一无所有，打算马上去印度。我已经写信给所有我认为是债主的人，你会看到随信附上的名单，凭记忆我尽量做到准确无误。我想，我的书籍、我的家具、我的画、我的马匹等等，应该可以抵清我的债务。也许，除了一些可以用作我创业本钱的小玩意儿，我不想保留任何东西。我亲爱的阿方斯，我将给你邮寄一份正式授权书，你可以凭此出售这些东西。请将我所有的武器都寄给我。你留着布里顿吧，没有人会为那匹骏马出高价的，所以我宁可把它给了你——好像一个临死之人把纪念死者的戒指赠予遗嘱执行人一样。法里、布雷依曼车行给我造了一量非常舒适的旅行马车，他们还没有送货；请说服他们将车留下，别要我作任何赔付。如果他们拒绝了，尽你所能去处理吧，以我目前的处境，有损名誉之事应尽量避免。我欠那个英国岛民六个金路易，我打牌输的，别忘了还他——
"Dear cousin!" whispered Eugenie, throwing down the letter and running softly back to her room, carrying one of the lighted candles. A thrill of pleasure passed over her as she opened the drawer of an old oak cabinet, a fine specimen of the period called the Renaissance, on which could still be seen, partly effaced, the famous royal salamander. She took from the drawer a large purse of red velvet with gold tassels, edged with a tarnished fringe of gold wire,—a relic inherited from her grandmother. She weighed it proudly in her hand, and began with delight to count over the forgotten items of her little hoard. First she took out twenty portugaises, still new, struck in the reign of John V., 1725, worth by exchange, as her father told her, five lisbonnines, or a hundred and sixty-eight francs, sixty-four centimes each; their conventional value, however, was a hundred and eighty francs apiece, on account of the rarity and beauty of the coins, which shone like little suns. Item, five genovines, or five hundred-franc pieces of Genoa; another very rare coin worth eighty-seven francs on exchange, but a hundred francs to collectors. These had formerly belonged to old Monsieur de la Bertelliere. Item, three gold quadruples, Spanish, of Philip V., struck in 1729, given to her one by one by Madame Gentillet, who never failed to say, using the same words, when she made the gift, "This dear little canary, this little yellow-boy, is worth ninety-eight francs! Keep it, my pretty one, it will be the flower of your treasure.” Item (that which her father valued most of all, the gold of these coins being twenty-three carats and a fraction), a hundred Dutch ducats, made in the year 1756, and worth thirteen francs apiece. Item, a great curiosity, a species of medal precious to the soul of misers,—three rupees with the sign of the Scales, and five rupees with the sign of the Virgin, all in pure gold of twenty-four carats; the magnificent money of the Great Mogul, each of which was worth by mere weight thirty-seven francs, forty centimes, but at least fifty francs to those connoisseurs who love to handle gold.
“亲爱的堂弟！”欧仁妮一边悄声说着，一边扔下信，轻手轻脚地跑回自己的房间，拿起了一支点燃的蜡烛。当她打开一个旧橡木柜的抽屉时，一阵愉悦的兴奋感传遍了她的全身。那柜子是文艺复兴时期的精品，尽管有部分磨损，上面仍旧可以看见著名的蝾螈王室的徽号。她从抽屉里拿出一个带金坠子的红丝绒大钱袋，边上是失去光泽的金线流苏——那是她祖母留下一个遗物。她骄傲地把它放在手里掂掂分量，兴冲冲地开始数起她那很少的私房钱来，她已经忘了数目是多少了。她先拿出二十枚葡萄牙金币，仍旧很新，一七二五年约翰五世在位时铸造，按她父亲告诉她的，兑换率是每枚五葡币，或是每枚一百六十八法郎六十四生丁；不过，因为这些硬币稀少而且漂亮，闪着小太阳似的光泽，它们的常规市价是一枚一百八十法郎。其次，五枚各值一百法郎的热那亚金币，是另一种非常稀罕的硬币，市值八十七法郎，而收藏家出价是一百法郎。这些东西过去都是属于老德拉贝尔特利耶先生的。其次，三枚一七二九年菲利普五世时铸造的西班牙金币，是让蒂耶夫人一枚一枚交给她的，当让蒂耶夫人赠送礼物的时候，总是用相同的字眼说道：“这个可爱的小金丝雀，这个黄澄澄的小东西，值九十八法郎呢！收着它，我的好乖乖，它将来就是你金库里的一宝。”其次（这些硬币中的纯金，二十三克拉挂零，是她父亲最珍视的），一百枚荷兰达克特，一七五六年铸造，每枚值十三法郎。其次，一批罕见的古玩，是守财奴们最珍爱的一种纪念章——三枚卢比刻有天平图案，五枚卢比刻着圣母像，全是二十四克拉的纯金，是莫卧儿大帝时的精美钱币，每一枚仅按重量算就值三十七法郎四十生丁，但对喜欢玩赏金币的行家来说，至少值五十法郎。
Item, the napoleon of forty francs received the day before, which she had forgotten to put away in the velvet purse. This treasure was all in virgin coins, true works of art, which Grandet from time to time inquired after and asked to see, pointing out to his daughter their intrinsic merits,—such as the beauty of the milled edge, the clearness of the flat surface, the richness of the lettering, whose angles were not yet rubbed off.
其次，前一天拿到的四十法郎拿破仑金币，她忘记收拾到丝绒钱袋里了。这些珍宝都是全新的硬币，是真正的艺术品，葛朗台不时会问到，让拿出来看看，并给女儿指出它们内在的优点——比如，抛光边缘的做工多么精美，平滑的表面多么光亮，字体多么饱满，字体笔划的棱角也没有磨掉。
Eugenie gave no thought to these rarities, nor to her father's mania for them, nor to the danger she incurred in depriving herself of a treasure so dear to him; no, she thought only of her cousin, and soon made out, after a few mistakes of calculation, that she possessed about five thousand eight hundred francs in actual value, which might be sold for their additional value to collectors for nearly six thousand.
欧仁妮根本不考虑这些奇珍异品，也没有顾及她父亲对这些东西的迷恋，更没有去想抢走父亲心爱的宝贝会招来什么样的危险；没有，她只想着她的堂弟。在算错几次之后，她很快弄清楚她大约拥有五千八百法郎的实际财产，如果卖给收藏家，还有一定的附加值，价值差不多就是六千法郎。
She looked at her wealth and clapped her hands like a happy child forced to spend its overflowing joy in artless movements of the body. Father and daughter had each counted up their fortune this night,—he, to sell his gold; Eugenie, to fling hers into the ocean of affection. She put the pieces back into the old purse, took it in her hand, and ran upstairs without hesitation. The secret misery of her cousin made her forget the hour and conventional propriety; she was strong in her conscience, in her devotion, in her happiness.
她看着自己的财产，拍起了手，好像一个高兴的孩子必须手舞足蹈才能释放那抑制不住的喜悦。父亲和女儿这晚各自清点了自己的财产——他，要卖他的金子；欧仁妮，要把她的金币投入到爱情的海洋中。她把金币放回了旧钱袋，拿到手中，毫不犹豫地跑上了楼。她堂弟暗中的窘境让她忘记了时间和体统；她的良心、她的献身精神和她的快乐给她壮了胆。

"My cousin, I must beg pardon for a wrong I have done you; but God will pardon me—if you—will help me to wipe it out.”

“是什么事？”夏尔揉了揉眼睛，问道。
"I have read those letters."

夏尔脸红了。

“还有什么？”夏尔问。

欧仁妮半是成年女子，半是小姑娘，从没想过会被人拒绝，可是她的堂弟却一直保持沉默。

她堂弟的迟疑让她感到窘迫，可是他身无分文的窘境在她脑海里变得更加清晰了，她便跪了下来。

夏尔终于能够表达自己的感受了。

“你是什么意思？”她有些害怕，说道。

他走过去，从封套中拿出盒子并打开，让他那快乐的堂姐看一个梳妆箱，其精美的工艺使这些金质装饰品的价值远远超过了它们的重量。

“你正欣赏的这个东西算不得什么，”他说着，按了一个隐藏的弹簧，打开了一个夹层，“这才是我眼里价值连城的东西。”他抽出两幅肖像，是米贝尔夫人的杰作，镶满了珍珠。
"Oh, how beautiful! Is it the lady to whom you wrote that—” 
“哦，太美了！你就是给这位夫人写信吧——”

Hearing the very words she had just used to her cousin now addressed to herself, she turned upon him a look of love, her first look of loving womanhood,—a glance in which there is nearly as much of coquetry as of inmost depth. He took her hand and kissed it.
听到堂弟现在把她刚刚说的话又说给自己听，她深情地朝他看了一眼，那是她用恋爱女子的眼神看的第一眼——这一眼差不多既有妩媚，又有深情。他拉起她的手，吻了一下。
"Angel of purity! Between us two money is nothing, never can be anything. Feeling, sentiment, must be all henceforth." 
“纯洁的天使！钱在我们两人之间是无所谓的，永远都不重要。感觉和情感从今以后就是一切。”

“你像你的母亲——她的声音跟你一样温柔吗？”

“哦！温柔多了——”
"Yes, for you," she said, dropping her eyelids. "Come, Charles, go to bed; I wish it; you must be tired. Good night." She gently disengaged her hand from those of her cousin, who followed her to her room, lighting the way. When they were both upon the threshold,— 
“是的，对你当然是啦，”她说着，垂下了眼睑，“去吧，夏尔，上床睡去吧，我希望你睡一会儿，你肯定累了。晚安。”她轻轻把手从堂弟手里抽出来，堂弟正用灯照着路，跟着她往她的房间走去。他们两人走到了门槛时——
"Ah!" he said, "why am I ruined?" 
“啊！”他说，“为什么我会倾家荡产呢？”
"What matter?—My father is rich; I think so," she answered.
“有什么关系？——我父亲有钱，我想是这样的。”她答道。
"Poor child!" said Charles, making a step into her room and leaning his back against the wall, "if that were so, he would never have let my father die; he would not let you live in this poor way; he would live otherwise himself." 
“可怜的孩子！”夏尔迈步进了她的房间，背靠着墙，说道，“要是那样的话，他决不会让我父亲死的，他也不会让你过得这么穷困，他会过另外一种生活的。”
"But he owns Froidfond." 
“可是他拥有弗罗瓦丰。”
"What is Froidfond worth?" 
“弗罗瓦丰值多少钱？”
"I don't know; but he has Noyers.” 
“我不知道，不过他还有努瓦耶。”
"Nothing but a poor farm!" 
“那只不过是个穷田庄！”
"He has vineyards and fields." 
“他有葡萄园和草场。”
"Mere nothing," said Charles disdainfully. "If your father had only twenty-four thousand francs a year do you suppose you would live in this cold, barren room?" he added, making a step in advance. "Ah! There you will keep my treasures," he said, glancing at the old cabinet, as if to hide his thoughts.
“不值什么钱的，”夏尔鄙夷地说道，“就算你父亲每年只有两万四千法郎，你觉得你会住在这么阴冷寒酸的房子里吗？”他往前迈了一步，接着说道，“啊！你是要把我的宝贝放在那里。”他一边说，一边瞥了一眼那个旧柜子，似乎想掩饰自己的想法。
"Go and sleep," she said, hindering his entrance into the disordered room.
“去睡吧。”她说道，不让他进自己凌乱的房间，。
Charles stepped back, and they bid each other goodnight with a mutual smile.
夏尔退了出去，他们相互微笑着道了晚安。
Both fell asleep in the same dream; and from that moment the youth began to wear roses with his mourning. The next day, before breakfast, Madame Grandet found her daughter in the garden in company with Charles. The young man was still sad, as became a poor fellow who, plunged in misfortune, measures the depths of the abyss into which he has fallen, and sees the terrible burden of his whole future life.
两个人都睡着了，做着同样的梦；从那时起，这个年轻人的丧服上仿佛开始戴上了玫瑰。第二天早饭前，葛朗台太太发现女儿在花园里陪着夏尔。那个年轻人仍旧很悲伤，正如一个坠入不幸的可怜人估量到他掉进的苦海有多深，便明白他整个的未来生活会有可怕的重负那样。
"My father will not be home till dinner-time," said Eugenie, perceiving the anxious look on her mother's face.
欧仁妮觉察到母亲脸上有不安的神情，便说道：“我父亲晚饭时才回家。”
It was easy to trace in the face and manners of the young girl and in the singular sweetness of her voice a unison of thought between her and her cousin. Their souls had espoused each other, perhaps before they even felt the force of the feelings which bound them together. Charles spent the morning in the hall, and his sadness was respected. Each of the three women had occupations of her own. Grandet had left all his affairs unattended to, and a number of persons came on business,—the plumber, the mason, the slater, the carpenter, the diggers, the dressers, the farmers; some to drive a bargain about repairs, others to pay their rent or to be paid themselves for services. Madame Grandet and Eugenie were obliged to go and come and listen to the interminable talk of all these workmen and country folk. Nanon put away in her kitchen the produce which they brought as tribute. She always waited for her master's orders before she knew what portion was to be used in the house and what was to be sold in the market. It was the goodman's custom, like that of a great many country gentlemen, to drink his bad wine and eat his spoiled fruit.
从这个年轻姑娘的面容、举止，以及她声音的异常柔美中，很容易看出她和她堂弟之间有一种思想上的默契。也许他们甚至还没有感觉出把他们连结到一起的那份感情的力量，就已经心心相印了。夏尔整个早晨都呆在大厅里，他暗自忧伤，谁也没去打搅他。三个女人各忙各的事。葛朗台忘了把事情交代好，结果许多人跑来干活——有管子工、砖瓦匠、铺石板的工匠、木匠、挖沟工人、装饰橱窗的人，还有佃户；有的来商谈维修费，有的来交租金，还有的来拿工钱。葛朗台太太和欧仁妮不得不跑来跑去，听所有这些工人和乡下人没完没了的唠叨。娜农把他们拿来抵租的农产品收拾到了厨房。她总要等她的主人发了令，才知道哪些要留在家里用，哪些要拿到市场上去卖。老头的习惯跟很多乡绅一样，就是喝劣质酒，吃烂水果。
Towards five in the afternoon Grandet returned from Angers, having made fourteen thousand francs by the exchange on his gold, bringing home in his wallet good treasury-notes which bore interest until the day he should invest them in the Funds. He had left Cornoiller at Angers to look after the horses, which were well-nigh foundered, with orders to bring them home slowly after they were rested.
下午快五点时，葛朗台从昂热回来了，他用他的金子换了一万四千法郎，用皮夹子揣回了王家债券，在他拿去投资购买公债之前，这些债券一直还有利息。他把科努瓦耶留在昂热照料那几匹差不多快要累死的马，他嘱咐科努瓦耶，等马歇过来之后再慢慢赶回家。
"I have got back from Angers, wife," he said, "I am hungry." 
“我从昂热回来了，太太，”他说，“我饿了。”
Nanon called out to him from the kitchen: 
娜农在厨房冲他大声喊道：
"Haven't you eaten anything since yesterday?” 
“打昨天起您就没吃过东西吧？”
"Nothing," answered the old man.
“什么也没吃。”老头答道。
Nanon brought in the soup. Des Grassins came to take his client's orders just as the family sat down to dinner. Grandet had not even observed his nephew.
娜农端上来汤。就在一家人坐下来要吃晚饭时，德格拉桑来了，要听取他主顾的指示。葛朗台连看都没看他侄子一眼。
"Go on eating, Grandet," said the banker, "we can talk. Do you know what gold is worth in Angers? They have come from Nantes after it? I shall send some of ours." 
“接着吃，葛朗台，”银行家说，“我们能说话。你知道昂热的金价吗？他们从南特赶来要收买呢？我要拿一些我们的金子去抛售。”
"Don't send any," said Grandet, "they have got enough. We are such old friends, I ought to save you from such a loss of time." 
“别去，”葛朗台说，“他们拿的已经够多了。我们是老朋友了，我不能让你白白浪费时间。”
"But gold is worth thirteen francs fifty centimes." 
“可金价是十三法郎五十生丁呢。”
"Say was worth—” 
“应该说‘曾经是’——”
"Where the devil have they got any?" 
“他们究竟是从哪里搞到的？”
"I went to Angers last night," answered Grandet in a low voice.
“我昨晚去昂热了。”葛朗台低声回答。
The banker shook with surprise. Then a whispered conversation began between the two, during which Grandet and des Grassins frequently looked at Charles. Presently des Grassins gave a start of astonishment; probably Grandet was then instructing him to invest the sum which was to give him a hundred thousand francs a year in the Funds.
银行家惊得一哆嗦。接着两个人悄声交谈了一番，其间葛朗台和德格拉桑频频地看着夏尔。不一会儿，德格拉桑显得十分惊诧，大概是葛朗台当时让他投资购买一年十万法郎的公债。
"Monsieur Grandet," said the banker to Charles, "I am starting for Paris; if you have any commissions—” 
“葛朗台先生，”银行家对夏尔说，“我要去巴黎，如果你有事相托——”
"None, monsieur, I thank you," answered Charles.
“没什么事，先生，谢谢您。”夏尔答道。
"Thank him better than that, nephew. Monsieur is going to settle the affairs of the house of Guillaume Grandet." 
“要多谢谢他，侄儿。先生要去处理纪尧姆·葛朗台商号的事情。”
"Is there any hope?" said Charles eagerly.
“有希望吗？”夏尔急切地说。
"What!" exclaimed his uncle, with well-acted pride, "are you not my nephew? Your honor is ours. Is not your name Grandet?" 
“唉！”他伯父装着自豪的样子，感叹道，“你不是我侄儿吗？你的名誉就是我们的名誉。你不是也姓葛朗台吗？”
Charles rose, seized Pere Grandet, kissed him, turned pale, and left the room. Eugenie looked at her father with admiration.
夏尔站起来，抱住葛朗台老头，吻了吻他，然后夏尔脸色变得很苍白，离开了房间。欧仁妮钦佩地看着她的父亲。
"Well, goodby, des Grassins; it is all in your hands. Decoy those people as best you can; lead 'em by the nose.” 
“好吧，再见，德格拉桑，一切都拜托你了。尽可能好好诓诓那些人，牵住他们的鼻子。”
The two diplomatists shook hands. The old cooper accompanied the banker to the front door. Then, after closing it, he came back and plunged into his armchair, saying to Nanon,— 
两个能说会道的人握了握手。老箍桶匠陪着银行家走到前门。接着，他关上门，走回来，一屁股坐进了他的扶手椅，对娜农说——
"Get me some black-currant ratafia.” 
“给我拿点黑藨子果仁甜酒。”
Too excited, however, to remain long in one place, he got up, looked at the portrait of Monsieur de la Bertelliere, and began to sing, doing what Nanon called his dancing steps,— 
但是，他实在是太兴奋了，在一个地方坐不住，便站起身来，看着德拉贝尔特利耶先生的肖像，开始一边做着被娜农叫做舞步的动作，一边唱起来——
"Dans les gardes francaises 
“在法国的近卫团里
J'avais un bon papa.” 
我有一个好爸爸。”
Nanon, Madame Grandet, and Eugenie looked at each other in silence. The hilarity of the master always frightened them when it reached its climax. The evening was soon over. Pere Grandet chose to go to bed early, and when he went to bed, everybody else was expected to go too; like as when Augustus drank, Poland was drunk. On this occasion Nanon, Charles, and Eugenie were not less tired than the master. As for Madame Grandet, she slept, ate, drank, and walked according to the will of her husband. However, during the two hours consecrated to digestion, the cooper, more facetious than he had ever been in his life, uttered a number of his own particular apothegms,—a single one of which will give the measure of his mind. When he had drunk his ratafia, he looked at his glass and said,— 
娜农、葛朗台太太和欧仁妮相互看着，沉默不语。当这位男主人高兴到极点时，那高兴劲儿总是让她们感到害怕。晚会很快结束了。葛朗台老头决定早点上床睡觉，他一睡，其他人都得去睡，如同奥古斯塔斯一喝酒，整个波兰都要醉一样。这时，娜农、夏尔和欧仁妮三人疲惫的程度不亚于这位一家之主。至于葛朗台太太，她睡觉、吃喝，还有走路都是随着丈夫的意愿进行的。但是，在饭后消化的两个小时中，箍桶匠一辈子都没这么滑稽过，说了一大通他自己独有的格言警句——单单其中一句便显出了他的心有几寸几尺。他喝完果仁甜酒后，看着杯子，说道——
"You have no sooner put your lips to a glass than it is empty! Such is life. You can't have and hold. Gold won't circulate and stay in your purse. If it were not for that, life would be too fine.” 
“你的嘴刚放到杯子上，杯子就空了。这就是人生。你不可能有了。金子不会一边流通，一边还呆在你的钱包里。要不是这样的话，生活就太美好了。”
He was jovial and benevolent. When Nanon came with her spinning-wheel, "You must be tired," he said, "put away your hemp." 
他友善快活、仁慈宽厚。当娜农搬着她的纺车进来时，“你一定累了，”他说，“把你的麻收拾起来吧。”
"Ah, bah! Then I shall get sleepy," she answered.
“啊，不行！那样我会瞌睡的。”她答道。
"Poor Nanon! Will you have some ratafia?" 
“可怜的娜农！你要不要喝点果仁甜酒？”
"I won't refuse a good offer; madame makes it a deal better than the apothecaries. What they sell is all drugs.” 
“我不会拒绝一个善意的邀请，太太做的酒比药剂师做的强多了。他们卖的都是药。”
"They put too much sugar," said the master, "you can't taste anything else.” 
“他们放的糖太多，”这位主人说，“你就品不出别的味来了。”
