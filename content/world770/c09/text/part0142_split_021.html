CHAPTER XXII  

第二十二章  

A week is gone; le jour des noces arrived; the marriage was solemnized at St. Jacques; Mdlle. Zoraïde became Madame Pelet, née Reuter; and in about an hour after this transformation 'the happy pair,' as newspapers phrase it, were on their way to Paris, where, according to previous arrangement, the honeymoon was to be spent. The next day I quitted the pensionnat. Myself and my chattels (some books and clothes) were soon transferred to a modest lodging I had hired in a street not far off. In half an hour my clothes were arranged in a commode, my books on a shelf, and the 'flitting' was effected. I should not have been unhappy that day had not one pang tortured me—a longing to go to the Rue Notre Dame aux Neiges, resisted, yet irritated by an inward resolve to avoid that street till such time as the mist of doubt should clear from my prospects.
一个星期过去了，婚期到来了；婚礼在圣雅克教堂隆重举行；佐蕾德小姐变成了珀莱夫人，勒泰氏，这一转变发生大概一个小时之后，正如报纸所说，“那幸福的一对”按照之前的计划踏上了赴往巴黎的行程，他们要在那度蜜月。第二天我搬离了珀莱家。很快我便连人带物（一些书和衣服）搬到我租在不远处一条街上的简陋的房子里。不到半个小时我便把衣服收到了柜子里，把书摆在书架上，“迁徙大业”至此结束。我那天本无烦心之事，但是有一种剧痛折磨着我——我渴望到内日河畔的圣母大街去，但我克制着，心里已经下决心，在前途未卜的阴云散去之前避开那条街；但是我仍很烦躁。
It was a sweet September evening—very mild, very still. I had nothing to do. At that hour I knew Frances would be equally released from occupation. I thought she might possibly be wishing for her master. I knew I wished for my pupil. Imagination began with her low whispers, infusing into my soul the soft tale of pleasures that might be.
那是一个甜蜜的九月份的夜晚——很暖和，很恬静。我没事可做。我知道此时弗朗西斯也已下班回家。我想她也许正想着见到她的老师呢。我知道我也很想见我的学生。想象力开始对我窃窃私语，对我的灵魂讲述着有可能发生的温馨的故事。
'You will find her reading or writing,' said she; 'you can take your seat at her side; you need not startle her peace by undue excitement; you need not embarrass her manner by unusual action or language. Be as you always are; look over what she has written; listen while she reads; chide her, or quietly approve. You know the effect of either system; you know her smile when pleased; you know the play of her looks when roused; you have the secret of awakening what expression you will, and you can choose amongst that pleasant variety. With you she will sit silent as long as it suits you to talk alone; you can hold her under a potent spell. Intelligent as she is, eloquent as she can be, you can seal her lips, and veil her bright countenance with diffidence. Yet, you know, she is not all monotonous mildness; you have seen, with a sort of strange pleasure, revolt, scorn, austerity, bitterness, lay energetic claim to a place in her feelings and physiognomy； you know that few could rule her as you do; you know she might break but never bend under the hand of Tyranny and Injustice but Reason and Affection can guide her by a sign. Try their influence now. Go—they are not passions; you may handle them safely.' 
“你会发现她在读书或者写字，”她说，“你可以在她旁边坐下，无需让过分的激动来扰乱她的平静，也无需让不同寻常的行为或者话语来使她局促不安。而是像一如往常，俯身看她写了什么，侧耳倾听她读书，训斥她，或者无声地表示赞许。你知道这两种方法分别会有什么效果；你了解她高兴时的微笑，了解她激动时的面部表情；你有秘诀能在她的脸上唤起你想要的表情，你也能在她各种令人愉快的表情中选择。如果你在身边她就能够安静地坐着，无论你自顾自说多久都行；你可以用有力的咒语将吸引她。尽管她很聪明，又能言善辩，但是你能封住她的嘴巴，使她不得不害羞地掩饰住开心的表情。但是，你知道，她并不总是那么温柔；你曾见过她脸上出现过的奇怪的开心、反叛、鄙视、严厉、以及痛苦的表情；她的感情和面容都表现出充分的自信。你知道很少有人能像你那样制服她，你知道她宁愿牺牲也绝不向专政和不公低头，但是理智和情感只要稍作示意就可支配她。现在试试它们的影响力吧。去吧——它们不是激情；你可以安全地驾驭它们。”
'I will not go,' was my answer to the sweet temptress. 'A man is master of himself to a certain point, but not beyond it. Could I seek Frances to-night, could I sit with her alone in a quiet room, and address her only in the language of reason and affection?' 
“我不会去的。”我对那位引诱我的甜美妖妇回答说道。“一个人在某种限度内是自己的主人，但超越这个限度就不是了。”我能去找弗朗西斯吗？能和她单独坐在安静的房间里，只用理智和情感的语言与她交谈吗？
'No,' was the brief, fervent reply of that love which had conquered and now controlled me.
“不能。”那位征服我，且此刻控制着我的爱神简短而炽烈地回答道。
Time seemed to stagnate; the sun would not go down; my watch ticked, but I thought the hands were paralysed.
时间似乎停止了，太阳不愿意下山去；我的表滴答作响，但我以为指针已经停了。
'What a hot evening!' I cried, throwing open the lattice for, indeed, I had seldom felt so feverish. Hearing a step ascending the common stair, I wondered whether the locataire, now mounting to his apartments, were as unsettled in mind and condition as I was, or whether he lived in the calm of certain resources and in the freedom of unfettered feelings. What! was he coming in person to solve the problem hardly proposed in inaudible thought? He had actually knocked at the door—at my door—a smart, prompt rap; and almost before I could invite him in he was over the threshold, and had closed the door behind him.
“好热的夜晚哪！”我喊道，推开了格子窗；的确，我很少这样狂躁。听见有上公共楼梯的脚步声，我以为是一个房客正在回自己的房间，他可能如我一样心神不宁，无依无靠，也可能他的生活风平浪静，衣食无忧，自由自在，没有感情的羁绊。什么！难道我那无声的心思刚刚提出问题，他就亲自过来解决？他确实敲门了——敲了我的门——果断而急促；我还没邀请他就跨过门槛，然后随手关上房门。
'And how are you?' asked an indifferent, quiet voice, in the English language, while my visitor, without any sort of bustle or introduction, put his hat on the table and his gloves into his hat, and drawing the only armchair the room afforded a little forward, seated himself tranquilly therein.
“你好吗？”只听有个人用英语冷漠平静地问道，而我的客人既没有寒暄半句也没有通报姓名，便把帽子放在桌上，把手套放入帽子里，向前拉了拉房间里唯一的一把安乐椅，静静地坐了进去。
'Can't you speak?' he inquired in a few moments, in a tone whose nonchalance seemed to intimate that it was much the same thing whether I answered or not. The fact is, I found it desirable to have recourse to my good friends les bésicles, not exactly to ascertain the identity of my visitor—for I already knew him, confound his impudence! —but to see how he looked, to get a clear notion of his mien and countenance. I wiped the glasses very deliberately, and put them on quite as deliberately, adjusting them so as not to hurt the bridge of my nose or get entangled in my short tufts of dun hair. I was sitting in the window-seat, with my back to the light, and I had him vis-Evis—a position he would much rather have had reversed, for at any time he preferred scrutinizing to being scrutinized. Yes, it was he, and no mistake, with his six feet of length arranged in a sitting attitude, with his dark travelling surtout with its velvet collar, his gray pantaloons, his black stock, and his face, the most original one Nature ever modelled, yet the least obtrusively so—not one feature that could be termed marked or odd, yet the effect of the whole unique. There is no use in attempting to describe what is indescribable. Being in no hurry to address him, I sat and stared at my ease.
“你不会说话吗？”过了一会儿他问道，语气中的漠不关心似乎暗示我回答与否都没有什么两样。事实上，我现在很需要我的好朋友——眼镜——的帮助，并不是想完全确定我的客人是谁——因为我认识他，看他那厚颜无耻的样子！——但是我想看看他的样子，想看清他的神态和表情。我很小心地擦了擦眼镜，又同样小心翼翼地戴上，调了一下位置以免碰到鼻梁或者缠住我的褐色短发。我坐在靠窗的座位上，背向着光，正好跟他面对面——他肯定很想和我换位置，因为不论何时他都更喜欢观察别人而不是被别人观察。是的，来人就是他，没错，他那六英尺高的身躯坐在椅子上，身着天鹅绒衣领的深色旅行大衣，一条灰色的裤子，还有黑色领圈，他的脸是大自然所创造出的最新颖的脸，但这种新颖却最不易引人注意——没有哪个五官称得上特别或者奇特，但是总体效果却非同一般。试图描述不可描述的东西毫无用处。我并不急着和他说话，便安然地坐在那里瞪着眼睛。
'Oh, that's your game, is it?' said he at last. 'Well, we'll see which is soonest tired.' And he slowly drew out a fine cigar-case, picked one to his taste, lit it, took a book from the shelf convenient to his hand, then leaning back, proceeded to smoke and read as tranquilly as if he had been in his own room in Grove Street, X—shire, England. I knew he was capable of continuing in that attitude till midnight, if he conceived the whim; so I rose, and taking the book from his hand, I said,— 
“哦，你是要跟我比赛，是吗？”最后他说道，“好的，我们来看看谁先开口。”说着他慢慢地拿出一个精美的香烟盒，抽出一根烟放入嘴里，点燃，又从手边的书架上抽出本书，然后靠到椅子上，继续安安静静地抽烟看书，似乎他是在英国Ｘ镇格罗夫大街他自己的房间里。我知道他只要想出这个古怪的想法，就能这个样子坚持到半夜；于是我站了起来，从他手里拿过书，说道，——
'You did not ask for it, and you shall not have it.' 
“你没有向我要过书，你不应该看。”
'It is silly and dull,' he observed, 'so I have not lost much;' then, the spell being broken, he went on, 'I thought you lived at Pelet's. I went there this afternoon, expecting to be starved to death by sitting in a boarding-school drawing-room, and they told me you were gone—had departed this morning. You had left your address behind you though, which I wondered at; it was a more practical and sensible precaution than I should have imagined you capable of. Why did you leave?' 
“真是愚蠢无聊之极，”他说，“所以我还没有输很多是吧。”就这样尴尬局面被打破了，他接着说道：“我以为你住在珀莱先生那。今天下午我去他那儿，以为要坐在寄宿学校的客厅里饿死，他们告诉我你已经离开了——今天上午走的。但是你离开时留下了地址，我觉得很奇怪，因为这种预防措施很实际、很理智，我还觉得你没那么聪明呢。你为什么要离开？”
'Because M. Pelet has just married the lady whom you and Mr. Brown assigned to me as my wife.' 
“因为珀莱先生刚刚跟那个你和布朗先生都指定给我当妻子的女人结婚了。”
'Oh, indeed!' replied Hunsden, with a short laugh. 'So you've lost both your wife and your place?' 
“哦，真的啊！”亨斯登短笑了一声。“所以说你赔了夫人又折兵了？”
'Precisely so.' 
“正是这样。”
I saw him give a quick, covert glance all round my room; he marked its narrow limits, its scanty furniture. In an instant he had comprehended the state of matters—had absolved me from the crime of prosperity. A curious effect this discovery wrought in his strange mind. I am morally certain that if he had found me installed in a handsome parlour, lounging on a soft couch, with a pretty, wealthy wife at my side, he would have hated me. A brief, cold, haughty visit would in such a case have been the extreme limit of his civilities, and never would he have come near me more so long as the tide of fortune bore me smoothly on its surface; but the painted furniture, the bare walls, the cheerless solitude of my room relaxed his rigid pride, and I know not what softening change had taken place both in his voice and look ere he spoke again.
我看见他偷偷而迅速地四处扫视了一下我的房间；他留意到了狭小的房间和寒酸的家具。他立马就理解了事态——一下子免除了我享乐荣华的罪过。这个发现在他奇特的脑海里产生了古怪的效果。我心里能确定，如果他发现我置身于一个富丽堂皇的客厅，懒洋洋地躺在柔软的沙发上，旁边是漂亮而富有的妻子，他肯定会恨我。那样的话，他最多只会出于礼貌进行一次短暂、冷酷而傲慢的拜访，只要财富之潮将我稳稳地托在其表面，他就绝不会再靠近我；但是那刷过油漆的家具，空荡的墙壁，还有阴郁又孤寂的房间却使他僵硬的自尊心软化了，当他再次说话时，我不知道他的声音和表情都为何变温和了很多。
'You have got another place?' 
“你又找了份工作？”
'No.' 
“没有呢。”
'You are in the way of getting one?' 
“你正在找？”
'No.' 
“也没有。”
'That is bad. Have you applied to Brown?' 
“那很糟糕。你找过布朗先生没？”
'No, indeed.' 
“没有，确实没有。”
'You had better; he often has it in his power to give useful information in such matters.' 
“你最好去找他，他经常有能力在这种事情上给人提供有用的信息。”
'He served me once very well; I have no claim on him, and am not in the humour to bother him again.' 
“他已经很好地帮过我一次了；我没有资格，也不愿意再麻烦他。”
'Oh, if you're bashful, and dread being intrusive, you need only commission me. I shall see him to-night; I can put in a word.
“啊，如果你这么不好意思，害怕麻烦别人，那你只需要委托我就好。我今晚要见他，可以帮你捎句话。
'I beg you will not, Mr. Hunsden; I am in your debt already. You did me an important service when I was at X—; got me out of a den where I was dying. That service I have never repaid, and at present I decline positively adding another item to the account.' 
“求求你不要吧，亨斯登先生，我已经欠您了。在Ｘ镇的时候您就帮过我大忙——帮我脱离虎穴，我当时已然奄奄一息了。那次恩情我尚未回报，现在我着实不想再欠你更多人情了。”
'If the wind sits that way, I'm satisfied. I thought my unexampled generosity in turning you out of that accursed counting-house would be duly appreciated some day. "Cast your bread on the waters, and it shall be found after many days," say the Scriptures. Yes, that's right, lad; make much of me—I'm a nonpareil. There's nothing like me in the common herd. In the meantime, to put all humbug aside and talk sense for a few moments, you would be greatly the better of a situation, and what is more, you are a fool if you refuse to take one from any hand that offers it.' 
“如果你那样想，我就心满意足了。帮你离开那该死的账房是我史无前例的慷慨，我知道这一举动终有一天会得到应有的感激的。圣经上说过，‘将面包丢到水里，很多天后你会找到它的。’是的，那是对的，小伙子，多多依靠我吧，我可是无可匹敌的。在普通人中没有像我这样的。好吧，我们废话少说，讲一会儿有意义的吧，你的处境会大大好转，此外，如果你拒绝任何人提供给你的工作，那你就是个大傻瓜。”
'Very well, Mr. Hunsden. Now you have settled that point, talk of something else. What news from X—?' 
“很好，亨斯登先生。如今你已经解决了这个问题，再讲点别的吧。Ｘ镇有什么新闻吗？”
'I have not settled that point, or at least there is another to settle before we get to X—. Is this Miss Zénobie' (‘Zoraïde,' interposed I)—‘well, Zoraïde—is she really married to Pelet?' 
“我还没解决这个问题呢，至少在我们讲Ｘ镇之前还有一个事情要解决——那个佐诺比小姐（“是佐蕾德”，我更正道）——好吧，佐蕾德，真的嫁给珀莱了吗？”
'I tell you yes; and if you don't believe me, go and ask the curé of St. Jacques.' 
“我都说是了；如果你不相信我，可以去问问圣雅克教堂的牧师。”
'And your heart is broken?' 
“那你心碎了？”
'I am not aware that it is. It feels all right—beats as usual.' 
“我觉得它没碎。它感觉良好——像往常一样跳动。”
'Then your feelings are less superfine than I took them to be. You must be a coarse, callous character to bear such a thwack without staggering under it.' 
“那么你的感情并不像我所想的那么细腻。你肯定是个粗俗麻木的人，受到这么大的打击竟然没有摇晃着倒下。”
'Staggering under it? What the deuce is there to stagger under in the circumstance of a Belgian schoolmistress marrying a French schoolmaster? The progeny will doubtless be a strange hybrid race; but that's their lookout, not mine.' 
“摇晃着倒下？一位比利时女校长嫁给一位法国男校长，我究竟为什么要倒下呢？他们的子孙绝对会是奇怪混血儿，但这是他们担心的事，不是我的。”
'He indulges in scurrilous jests, and the bride was his affianced one!' 
“他竟然说起粗俗的笑话来，那个新娘曾是他的未婚妻呢！”
'Who said so?' 
“谁说的呀？”
'Brown.' 
“布朗。”
'I'll tell you what, Hunsden: Brown is an old gossip.' 
“我告诉你吧，亨斯登，布朗就是一个爱传播谣言的老头子。”
'He is; but in the meantime, if his gossip be founded on less than fact, if you took no particular interest in Miss Zoraïde, why, O youthful pedagogue, did you leave your place in consequence of her becoming Madame Pelet?' 
“他的确是；但与此同时，如果他的小道消息是无风起浪，如果你对佐蕾德小姐毫无兴趣，那么，年轻的教员，为什么她成了珀莱夫人后你就离开那了呢？”
'Because'—I felt my face grow a little hot—'because, in short, Mr. Hunsden, I decline answering any more questions;' and I plunged my hands deep in my breeches pocket.
“那是因为”——我觉得自己的脸有点发烫——“因为，简而言之，亨斯登先生，我拒绝再回答任何问题。”我把双手深深地插进裤袋里。
Hunsden triumphed; his eyes, his laugh announced victory.
亨斯登打了胜仗；他的眼睛，他的笑声都宣布着他的胜利。
'What the deuce are you laughing at, Mr. Hunsden?' 
“你到底在笑什么，亨斯登先生？”
'At your exemplary composure. Well, lad, I'll not bore you. I see how it is. Zoraïde has jilted you—married some one richer, as any sensible woman would have done if she had had the chance.' 
“笑你真是够沉着。好了，小伙子，我不烦你了。我明白是怎么一回事了。佐蕾德抛弃了你——嫁给了一个更有钱的人，任何理智的女人只要有机会都会这么做的。”
I made no reply, I let him think so, not feeling inclined to enter into an explanation of the real state of things, and as little to forge a false account. But it was not easy to blind Hunsden. My very silence, instead of convincing him that he had hit the truth, seemed to render him doubtful about it. He went on,— 
我没有回应，随他怎么去想，我不想去解释事情的真相，更不想编造谎言。但是蒙蔽亨斯登是不容易的。我的沉默，不但没有使他确信已经知道真相，反而似乎使他对此产生怀疑。他接着说道——
'I suppose the affair has been conducted as such affairs always are amongst rational people. You offered her your youth and your talents—such as they are—in exchange for her position and money. I don' t suppose you took appearance, or what is called love, into the account—for I understand she is older than you, and Brown says rather sensible looking than beautiful. She having then no chance of making a better bargain, was at first inclined to come to terms with you; but Pelet, the head of a flourishing school, stepped in with a higher bid. She accepted, and he has got her: a correct transaction—perfectly so—business-like and legitimate. And now we'll talk of something else.' 
“我想这类事情发生在理智的人身上时一般是这样解决的。你提供她青春和才华——这些你都有——用来交换她的地位和金钱。我认为你不会以貌取人，或者看重所谓的爱情，因为我知道她比你年龄大，而且布朗说她虽看上去很聪敏，但并不美丽。她没法要得更多了，所以一开始就和你交往了；但是珀莱作为一所生意兴隆的学校校长，以更高的价钱插了一脚。她接受了他，而他就得到了她。这是一笔正确的交易——完全正确——就像做生意一样，十分正当。那我们现在谈点别的吧。”
'Do,' said I, very glad to dismiss the topic, and especially glad to have baffled the sagacity of my cross-questioner—if indeed, I had baffled it; for though his words now led away from the dangerous point, his eyes, keen and watchful, seemed still preoccupied with the former idea.
“那谈吧。”我很庆幸摆脱了这个话题，尤其庆幸能够抵挡住这位盘问者的精明审问——如果我真的已经挡住了它的话；因为虽然他说的话已经远离了那个危险的话题，但他敏锐机警的眼睛似乎表明他还在思量着之前的问题。
'You want to hear news from X—? And what interest can you have in X—? You left no friends there, for you made none. Nobody ever asks after you, neither man nor woman; and if I mention your name in company, the men look as if I had spoken of Prester John, and the women sneer covertly. Our X— belles must have disliked you. How did you excite their displeasure?' 
“你想听听来自Ｘ镇的消息吗？你对Ｘ镇还有什么牵挂呢？你在那里没有留下朋友，因为你在那里没有交上朋友。无论男女都没有人问过你；如果我在朋友中提及你的名字，男人们看起来就像我在说普雷斯特·约翰那样无所谓，而女人们则偷偷地嘲笑着。我们Ｘ镇的美女们肯定都很不喜欢你。你怎么惹她们不高兴了？”
'I don't know. I seldom spoke to them—they were nothing to me. I considered them only as something to be glanced at from a distance. Their dresses and faces were often pleasing enough to the eye, but I could not understand their conversation, nor even read their countenances. When I caught snatches of what they said, I could never make much of it; and the play of their lips and eyes did not help me at all.' 
“我不知道。我很少跟她们说话——她们对我而言无关紧要。我觉得她们是只可远观的东西。她们的穿着和面孔常使我赏心悦目，但是我不懂她们的对话，甚至也看不懂她们的表情。有时我无意中听到她们的谈话，根本就不解其意；她们双唇和眼睛的动作也没有给我启发。”

“那就是你的过错，不是她们的。Ｘ镇有很多又漂亮又聪明的女人。女人值得任何一个男人与之交谈，我就很喜欢和女人交谈。但是你不论过去还是现在，都没有过愉快的交谈经历，在你身上没有什么能赢得女人的青睐。我曾经见过在满是人的房间里，你只坐在房门旁，俯身倾听着，却不喜欢交谈；一直观察着，却不喜欢主动迎合；聚会开始时显得呆板害羞，聚会中间显得迷惑警惕，聚会结束时又显得疲倦无礼。你觉得这样能够传递愉悦或者引起别人的兴趣吗？不能；如果大家都不喜欢你，那就说明你是咎由自取。”
'Content!' I ejaculated.
“我就愿意这样！”我叫道。
'No, you are not content. You see beauty always turning its back on you; you are mortified, and then you sneer. I verify believe all that is desirable on earth—wealth, reputation, love—will for ever to you be the ripe grapes on the high trellis. “不是的，你不愿意的。看见美女们总是对你毫不理睬，你深感羞辱，然后便嗤之以鼻。我坚定不移地相信世界上所有令人渴望的事情——财富、名誉、爱情——对你而言永远都是高架上熟透的葡萄。你会抬头看它们；它们引诱着你，使你露出贪婪的眼神，但是你却够不到它们。你没有地方找梯子，只得离开，口里还叫着它们是酸的。”
Cutting as these words might have been under some circumstances, they drew no blood now. My life was changed, my experience had been varied since I left X—; but Hunsden could not know this. He had seen me only in the character of Mr. Crimsworth's clerk—a dependant amongst wealthy strangers, meeting disdain with a hard front, conscious of an unsocial and unattractive exterior, refusing to sue for notice which I was sure would be withheld, declining to evince an admiration which I knew would be scorned as worthless. He could not be aware that since then youth and loveliness had been to me everyday objects, that I had studied them at leisure and closely, and had seen the plain texture of truth under the embroidery of appearance; nor could he, keen-sighted as he was, penetrate into my heart, search my brain, and read my peculiar sympathies and antipathies. He had not known me long enough or well enough to perceive how low my feelings would ebb under some influences, powerful over most minds; how high, how fast they would flow under other influences, that perhaps acted with the more intense force on me because they acted on me alone. Neither could he suspect for an instant the history of my communications with Mdlle. Reuter. Secret to him and to all others was the tale of her strange infatuation. Her blandishments, her wiles had been seen but by me, and to me only were they known. but they had changed me, for they had proved that I could impress. A sweeter secret nestled deeper in my heart, one full of tenderness and as full of strength. It took the sting out of Hunsden's sarcasm; it kept me unbent by shame and unstirred by wrath. But, of all this I could say nothing—nothing decisive at least; uncertainty sealed my lips, and during the interval of silence by which alone I replied to Mr. Hunsden, I made up my mind to be for the present wholly misjudged by him, and misjudged I was. He thought he had been rather too hard upon me, and that I was crushed by the weight of his upbraidings; so to reassure me he said doubtless I should mend some day. I was only at the beginning of life yet; and since happily I was not quite without sense, every false step I made would be a good lesson.
有些时候，这些话让我觉得很尖刻，可是这次却没有让我血流不止。自从离开Ｘ镇，我的生活就改变了，我的经历也是五味杂陈，但是亨斯登是不知道这一点的。他只看过作为克里姆斯沃思先生职员的我——那时我在富有的陌生人眼中只是一个仆人，不会在意别人的轻蔑，因为知道自己不善交际，外表毫无魅力，不会去特意吸引注意，因为知道没有人会来关注我，也不会阿谀奉承，因为知道会由于毫无用处而遭人鄙夷。他不可能知道，自那以后，年轻而可爱的姑娘们成了我每天接触的对象，我可以从容而仔细地审视她们，看到她们靓丽外表下的平庸本质；尽管他的目光如此犀利，也无法穿过我的内心，探寻我的大脑，读懂我所特有的同情和反感。他认识我的时间还不够长，或对我不够了解，没办法感知我的感情在某些影响下会退缩脆弱，而在大多人面前又很有力；在某种影响下我感情的潮水会激昂奔腾，或许有一股力量左右着我，它们由于只对我一个人有影响而显得更为有力。他也绝不会想到我和勒泰小姐还有这样一段往事。她对我的痴心对于他，对于其他所有的人来说都是个秘密。她的花言巧语，她的阴谋诡计只有我见过，也只有我知道。但是它们已经改变了我，这是因为它们证明我能够给人留下印象的。我的内心里还住着另一个更为甜蜜的秘密，那是个充满柔情与力量的秘密。它抚平了亨斯登先生冷嘲热讽所带来的刺痛，它使我受到屈辱而不屈服，面对愤慨而不愤怒。但是，对于这一切我什么都不能说——因为什么都没有最终确定下来；正因为不确定，我没有说话，只以沉默回复亨斯登先生，在沉默之中，我下定决心，目前就让他误解我吧，反正我已经被误解了。他以为他对我说得有些重，以为我被他的谴责压垮了；为了安慰我他又确定地说总有一天我会开窍的，说我的人生才刚刚起步，庆幸的是，我又不是不懂情理，我所走错的每一步都会成为很好的教训。
Just then I turned my face a little to the light. The approach of twilight and my position in the window-seat had, for the last ten minutes, prevented him from studying my countenance; as I moved, however, he caught an expression which he thus interpreted,— 
就在那时我的脸稍稍朝光亮转了转。因为已近黄昏，而我的位置又是在靠窗处，所以在刚刚的十分钟内他看不到我的表情；然而当我转过来时，他捕捉到了我的表情，并这样理解它，——

“厄运会像压碎李子一样易如反掌地压碎那头牛；我相信，你一直厄运缠身，你是生来嘴里就含着木匙的，准是这样。”

“一点不错，他老婆回她父亲家了。他的脾气也随着破产偏离了常态。他一直对她不好。我曾跟你说过，总有一天他会像一个暴君一样对她。至于他——”

“那克里姆斯沃思公馆的——家具也卖了吗？”

“所有东西——从大钢琴到擀面杖。”

“那橡木餐厅里的东西呢——它们也卖掉了吗？”

“当然了。为什么把那个房间的沙发和椅子看得比其他房间的更神圣呢？”

“那些画也卖了？”

“什么画啊？据我所知克里姆斯沃思没有什么特别的收藏啊。他没说过他是一位业余收藏家呀。”

“那里有两幅肖像画的，壁炉两边各一张。你不会忘记的，亨斯登先生，你曾注意过那幅夫人的画像——”

“哦，我记起来了！那个面容很清瘦，披着像帷帐一样披肩的贵妇人。哎，它肯定是跟别的东西混在一起卖掉了。如果你很富有的话，也许会把它买下来，因为我记得你说过画的是你母亲。你看现在身无分文就什么也干不成吧。”

这个我懂。“但是肯定的，”我暗想，“我绝不会总是这般穷困潦倒，总有一天我会把它赎回来”——“谁买过去了？你知道吗？”我问道。

Some people, however indifferent they may become after a considerable space of absence, always contrive to leave a pleasant impression just at parting. Not so Hunsden. A conference with him affected one like a draught of Peruvian bark: it seemed a concentration of the specially harsh, stringent, bitter. Whether, Eke bark, it invigorated, I scarcely knew.
一些人不管在久别之后感情多么冷淡，总是会在重逢离开时尽量留下一个好的印象。但是亨斯登不是这样的。和他谈次话就像吞了秘鲁树皮一样：真是又苦又涩，味道很重。不知道它是不是跟金鸡纳树皮一样有可以使人精力充沛的。
A ruffled mind makes a restless pillow. I slept little on the night after this interview. Towards morning I began to doze, but hardly had my slumber become sleep when I was roused from it by hearing a noise in my sitting-room, to which my bedroom adjoined—a step and a shoving of furniture. The movement lasted barely two minutes; with the closing of the door it ceased. I listened: not a mouse stirred. Perhaps I had dreamt it; perhaps a locataire had made a mistake and entered my apartment instead of his own. It was yet but five o'clock; neither I nor the day was wide awake. I turned, and was soon unconscious. When I did rise, about two hours later, I had forgotten the circumstance. The first thing I saw, however, on quitting my chamber, recalled it. Just pushed in at the door of my sitting- room, and still standing on end, was a wooden packing-case—a rough deal affair, wide but shallow. A porter had doubtless shoved it forward, but seeing no occupant of the room, had left it at the entrance.

我很困惑，但是想要搞清楚情况最好的方式就是看看里面。我割断绳子，打开箱子。
Green baize enveloped its contents, sewn carefully at the sides. Boards and baize being at length removed, I lifted from the case a large picture, in a magnificent frame. Leaning it against a chair, in a position where the light from the window fell favourably upon it, I stepped back; already I had mounted my spectacles. A portrait-painter's sky (the most sombre and threatening of welkins) and distant trees of a conventional depth of hue raised in full relief a pale, pensive-looking female face, shadowed with soft dark hair, almost blending with the equally dark clouds; large, solemn eyes looked reflectively into mine; a thin cheek rested on a delicate little hand, a shawl, artistically draped, half hid, half showed a slight figure. A listener (had there been one) might have heard me, after ten minutes' silent gazing, utter the word 'Mother!' I might have said more, but with me the first word uttered aloud in soliloquy rouses consciousness; it reminds me that only crazy people talk to themselves, and then I think out my monologue instead of speaking it. I had thought a long while, and a long while had contemplated the intelligence, the sweetness, and, alas! the sadness also of those fine gray eyes, the mental power of that forehead, and the rare sensibility of that serious mouth, when my glance, travelling downwards, fell on a narrow billet, stuck in the corner of the picture, between the frame and the canvas. 绿色的毛毯包着里面的东西，四周仔细地缝住了。我用袖珍折刀挑开包装线，线松开后，镀金的亮光从逐渐变宽的缝隙处露出来。最后我终于拆下木板，拿掉毛毯，从箱子里拿出一副装在华贵相框里的大画像。我将它靠着一张椅子摆好，这样窗外射来的光线刚好照在画面上，我已经戴上了眼镜，后退几步打量。上面是一位肖像画家渲染的天空（极为昏暗而可怕的苍穹）远处的树木用传统的深色调勾勒而成，衬托出一张苍白而哀愁的女性面孔；她的脸上遮着几缕柔软的黑发，几乎与深色的云朵融为一体，那双严肃的大眼睛反思地看着我的眼睛，消瘦的面颊托在精致的小手上，那条披巾很有艺术感地披在身上，纤细的身躯若隐若现。如果身边有人的话，他可能会听见我在沉默注视了十分钟后说出的“母亲！”二字。我本来还会再多说些话，但是那声自言自语的“母亲”让我一下子清醒了过来，它使我想起只有疯子才会自言自语，于是我思忖着我的独白，而没有说出来。我想了很久，良久地注视着她那美丽的灰色双眸中散发出来的睿智和甜美，还有，哎！还有悲伤，和那前额显示出的精神力量，坚毅的嘴巴显示出的不多的敏感；我的目光向下游移，最后落在一个狭窄的图章上，那图章印在画像的角落处，就在画框与画布之间。于是我才想起来要问：“这幅画是谁送来的？谁会为我着想，将它从克里姆斯沃思公馆的残留物中救出来，现在又将它交给本来的主人保管？”我从箱子内部找到一张便条，上面写着：——
'There is a sort of stupid pleasure in giving a child sweets, a fool his bells, a dog a bone. I would have added five shillings more to my bid if the auctioneer could only have promised me that pleasure.

I muffled the picture in its green baize covering, restored it to the case, and having transported the whole concern to my bedroom, put it out of sight under my bed. My pleasure was now poisoned by pungent pain; I determined to look no more till I could look at my ease. If Hunsden had come in at that moment, I should have said to him, 'I owe you nothing, Hunsden—not a fraction of a farthing; you have paid yourself in taunts.' 
我用那块绿色毛毯又将画像包好，装回箱子里，将整个箱子搬回我的卧室，放在我看不见的床底下。此时，我的欢乐已经被剧痛代替；我决定在能安适悠闲地欣赏它之前，不再看它。如果亨斯登此时进来，我会对他说：“我什么都不欠你，亨斯登——一分钱都不欠你；你已经用讥讽替我还了你的债了。”
Too anxious to remain any longer quiescent, I had no sooner breakfasted than I repaired once more to M. Vandenhuten's, scarcely hoping to find him at home—for a week had barely elapsed since my first call—but fancying I might be able to glean information as to the time when his return was expected. A better result awaited me than I had anticipated; for though the family were yet at Ostend, M. Vandenhuten had come over to Brussels on business for the day. He received me with the quiet kindness of a sincere though not excitable man. I had not sat five minutes alone with him in his bureau before I became aware of a sense of ease in his presence such as I rarely experienced with strangers. I was surprised at my own composure, for, after all, I had come on business to me exceedingly painful—that of soliciting a favour. I asked on what basis the calm rested. I feared it might be deceptive. Ere long I caught a glimpse of the ground, and at once I felt assured of its solidity; I knew where I was.
我十分不安，久久不能平静，一用过早餐便再次造访范登赫顿先生，虽然并未指望能在他家见到他——因为自我第一次拜访后过了还不到一个星期——但是我说不定能打听到他什么时候回来。想不到等待我的是个更好的结果；因为虽然他家人还在奥斯坦德，但是范登赫顿先生那天恰好回布鲁塞尔办事。他平静、真诚而友善地接待了我，尽管他不是那种容易兴奋的人。我在他办公室还没单独坐上五分钟，便感到在他面前很自在，以前跟陌生人在一起时我很少有这种感觉。我很意外自己竟这么镇静，因为毕竟我来这里是要完成一件很痛苦的事——求别人的帮忙。我问自己究竟为什么这么沉着呢。我害怕理由是站不住脚的。不久我就抓到了某种根据，立即觉得它很坚固，我知道自己的位置了。
M. Vandenhuten was rich, respected, and influential; I poor, despised, and powerless. So we stood to the world at large as members of the world's society; but to each other, as a pair of human beings, our positions were reversed. The Dutchman (he was not Flamand, but pure Hollandais) was slow, cool, of rather dense intelligence, though sound and accurate judgment: the Englishman far more nervous, active, quicker both to plan and to practise, to conceive and to realize. The Dutchman was benevolent, the Englishman susceptible; in short, our characters dovetailed, but my mind having more fire and action than his, instinctively assumed and kept the predominance.
范登赫顿先生十分富有，受人尊敬，举足轻重；而我一穷二白，受人鄙视，无权无势。对于这个世界来说我们不过是社会中的两个成员；但是作为一对特定的人，我们彼此的位置相差甚远。这位荷兰人（他不是佛兰芒人，而是纯荷兰人）行动迟缓，十分冷静，拥有敏锐的判断力，而理解力并不缜密：而我这个英国人无论计划还是行动，构思还是实现都更容易神经紧张，积极迅速。这位荷兰人仁慈亲切，而我这个英国人易动感情；简而言之，我们的性格有些相似，但我的头脑更有活力更爱思考，于是便本能地占据并保持着优势。
This point settled, and my position well ascertained, I addressed him on the subject of my affairs with that genuine frankness which full confidence can alone inspire. It was a pleasure to him to be so appealed to; he thanked me for giving him this opportunity of using a little exertion in my behalf. I went on to explain to him that my wish was not so much to be helped as to be put into the way of helping myself; of him I did not want exertion—that was to be my part—but only information and recommendation. Soon after I rose to go. He held out his hand at parting—an action of greater significance with foreigners than with Englishmen. As I exchanged a smile with him, I thought the benevolence of his truthful face was better than the intelligence of my own. Characters of my order experience a balm-like solace in the contact of such souls as animated the honest breast of Victor Vandenhuten.
这一点想明白了，我的位置也就摆得很清楚了；我向他毫不隐瞒地介绍自己的情况，只有满怀信心时我才会这样。他因为我来向他求助而很开心，他感谢我给了他这样一个机会能够为我尽绵薄之力。我接着解释说，我的愿望并不是让他给予很大的帮助，只想请他为我指明自助的方向；我并不想他直接帮我安排——那是我的事情——我只是想让他给一些信息并给以推荐。不久我便起身离开，他伸手表示告别——外国人比英国人更为注重这种礼节。当我向他微笑时，我想他真诚的面孔上显示的善意比我面孔上的智慧好很多。我这种身份的人能和像维克多·范登赫顿这样心胸坦荡的人接触，心里极为安慰。
The next fortnight was a period of many alternations; my existence during its lapse resembled a sky of one of those autumnal nights which are specially haunted by meteors and falling stars. Hopes and fears, expectations and disappointments, descended in glancing showers from zenith to horizon; but all were transient, and darkness followed swift each vanishing apparition. M. Vandenhuten aided me faithfully. He set me on the track of several places, and himself made efforts to secure them for me; but for a long time solicitation and recommendation were vain—the door either shut in my face when I was about to walk in, or another candidate, entering before me, rendered my further advance useless. Feverish and roused, no disappointment arrested me； defeat following fast on defeat served as stimulants to will. I forgot fastidiousness, conquered reserve, thrust pride from me; I asked, I persevered, I remonstrated, I dunned. It is so that openings are forced into the guarded circle where Fortune sits dealing favours round. My perseverance made me known; my importunity made me remarked. I was inquired about; my former pupils' parents, gathering the reports of their children, heard me spoken of as talented, and they echoed the word. The sound, bandied about at random, came at last to ears which, but for its universality, it might never have reached; and at the very crisis when I had tried my last effort, and knew not what to do, Fortune looked in at me one morning, as I sat in drear and almost desperate deliberation on my bedstead, nodded with the familiarity of an old acquaintance—though God knows I had never met her before—and threw a prize into my lap.
接下来的两个星期是充满变数的时期，那一段时间我的生活就像是秋日的夜晚，天空中徘徊着流星和陨石。希望与担心，期望与失望，如阵雨般闪烁着从天顶滑向地面；但是这一切都极为短暂，每次幻象消逝之后，黑暗就立即随之而来。范登赫顿先生全心全意地给予我帮助。他介绍了好几个地方给我，还亲自为我担保；但是长久以来他的恳求与推荐都是徒劳无功的——学校的大门要不是在我走入之前就关上，要不就是有人捷足先登，使得我怎么努力都是无济于事。我激动而愤怒，但是并没有被失望击倒。失败紧跟着失败，我的意志却越挫越勇。我不再吹毛求疵，不再沉默矜持，把骄傲远远甩开；我询问着，坚持着，抗议着，催促着。就这样那守卫森严的圈子被强行打开了，我看见命运之神正坐在里面给予周围之人恩惠。我的坚持不懈使我为人所知晓，我的死缠烂打使我为人所注意。有人开始询问我的事，我之前学生的父母根据孩子们的反映，只要听到有人夸我很能干，他们便附和着。这种赞美之声在周围随意流传着，最后传到一些有权势的人耳中，要不是因为流传广泛，也许永远不会为这些人所知；正当我竭尽全力但仍不知所措的关键时刻，幸运之神在一天上午眷顾了我，当时我正苦闷绝望地坐在床前，她如老朋友般熟悉地向我点头——尽管对天发誓我以前从没见过她——她在我的腿上扔了一个奖品。
In the second week of October 18-- I got the appointment of English professor to all the classes of—College, Brussels, with a salary of three thousand francs per annum, and the certainty of being able, by dint of the reputation and publicity accompanying the position, to make as much more by private lessons. The official notice which communicated this information mentioned also that it was the strong recommendation of M. Vandenhuten, négociant, which had turned the scale of choice in my favour.
一八几几年十月的第二个星期，我得到了一个英语教师的职位，教授布鲁塞尔某某学院所有的班级，年薪三千法郎，凭借这一职位所获得的名声和宣传，可以使我在外面兼职的时候赚更多的钱。告知我这一消息的正式通知还提到，正是商人范登赫顿先生的强有力举荐，才使得入选的天平倾向了我一边。
No sooner had I read the announcement than I hurried to M. Vandenhuten's bureau, pushed the document under his nose, and when he had perused it, took both his hands and thanked him with unrestrained vivacity. My vivid words and emphatic gesture moved his Dutch calm to unwonted sensation. He said he was happy—glad to have served me, but he had done nothing meriting such thanks. He had not laid out a centime, only scratched a few words on a sheet of paper.
我一看完通知便急忙跑到范登赫顿先生的办公室，将聘书推到他的面前，当他细读完之后我便抓住他的双手，难以抑制住兴奋地向他表示感谢。我充满活力的言语和坚决有力的手势感染了这位一向平静的荷兰人，他也不同寻常地激动起来。他说他很开心——很高兴能帮到我，但他说区区小事不足挂齿。他连一个生丁都没花，只是在一张纸上随便写了几句话罢了。

'You have made me quite happy, and in a way that suits me. I do not feel an obligation irksome, conferred by your kind hand; I do not feel disposed to shun you because you have done me a favour. From this day you must consent to admit me to your intimate acquaintance, for I shall hereafter recur again and again to the pleasure of your society.' 
“您让我感到很高兴，这种方式也很适合我。从您这样慷慨的人手中得到援助，我毫不觉得欠着什么令我苦恼的人情，我完全不觉得因为您给予过我恩惠，我就得躲避您。从今天起，您一定得同意让我做您的好友，因为从今往后我会经常看望您的。”
'Ainsi soit-il,' was the reply, accompanied by a smile of benignant content. I went away with its sunshine in my heart.
“这样很好。”他回答说，脸上带着和蔼满足的微笑。我内心充满阳光地微笑着离开了他家。
