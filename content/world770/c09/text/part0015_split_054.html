THE BLIND BOYS.  

盲童  

Thursday, 24th.

星期四，24日

The master is very ill, and they have sent in his stead the master of the fourth grade, who has been a teacher in the Institute for the Blind. He is the oldest of all the instructors, with hair so white that it looks like a wig made of cotton, and he speaks in a peculiar manner, as though he were chanting a melancholy song; but he does it well, and he knows a great deal. No sooner had he entered the schoolroom than, catching sight of a boy with a bandage on his eye, he approached the bench, and asked him what was the matter.
老师病得很厉害，他们请了五年级的老师来代课，他曾经是盲人学校的老师。他是所有教师中最老的一位，头发白得像是一顶棉花做的假发，他讲话的方式很奇特，像在吟唱一首悲伤的歌曲，但他教得很好，并且知识渊博。他一走进教室，看到一个男孩眼睛上缠着绷带，就走到他位置上问他怎么了。
"Take care of your eyes, my boy," he said to him. And then Derossi asked him:— 
“保护好你的眼睛，我的孩子。”他对他说。然后代洛西问他：
"Is it true, sir, that you have been a teacher of the blind?" 
“先生，您真的教过盲人吗？”
"Yes, for several years," he replied. And Derossi said, in a low tone, "Tell us something about it." 
“是啊，教过几年。”他回答。然后代洛西用低沉的音调说：“给我们讲讲吧。”
The master went and seated himself at his table.
老师走过去坐在他的桌子前。
Coretti said aloud, "The Institute for the Blind is in the Via Nizza." 
可莱谛大声说：“盲人学校在维亚尼斯。”
"You say blind—blind," said the master, "as you would say poor or ill, or I know not what. But do you thoroughly comprehend the significance of that word? Reflect a little. Blind! Never to see anything! Not to be able to distinguish the day from night; to see neither the sky, nor sun, nor your parents, nor anything of what is around you, and which you touch; to be immersed in a perpetual obscurity, and as though buried in the bowels of the earth! Make a little effort to close your eyes, and to think of being obliged to remain forever thus; you will suddenly be overwhelmed by a mental agony, by terror; it will seem to you impossible to resist, that you must burst into a scream, that you must go mad or die. But, poor boys! when you enter the Institute of the Blind for the first time, during their recreation hour, and hear them playing on violins and flutes in all directions, and talking loudly and laughing, ascending and descending the stairs at a rapid pace, and wandering freely through the corridors and dormitories, you would never pronounce these unfortunates to be the unfortunates that they are. It is necessary to observe them closely. There are lads of sixteen or eighteen, robust and cheerful, who bear their blindness with a certain ease, almost with hardihood; but you understand from a certain proud, resentful expression of countenance that they must have suffered tremendously before they became resigned to this misfortune.
“你们说盲——盲，”老师说，“而你们应该说可怜、生病，或者，我也不知道怎么说。但是你们完全理解那个词的意义吗？想一下。盲！什么也看不到！分不出白天和黑夜，看不到天空、太阳，还有你们的父母，也看不到你们周围所触碰到的一切；陷于无止境的黑暗之中，就像被埋于土地最深处！稍稍花点儿力气去闭上你们的眼睛，然后想象不得不永远这样下去的情景，你们就会突然被一种精神上的极大痛苦和恐惧所淹没，你们肯定会忍不住而突然哭喊、发疯，或者死去。但是，可怜的孩子们！当你们第一次在他们的休息时间进入盲人学校，听到他们在四处拉着小提琴，吹着长笛，看到他们大声地谈笑，快步上下楼梯，在走廊和寝室自由地漫步时，你们就不会说他们所经历的不幸是不幸了。密切地观察他们是很必要的。那些十六或十八岁的小伙子们很结实，也很快乐、从容，勇敢地忍受他们的失明；但是你看到他们脸上那种骄傲、愤恨的表情就能知道，他们在对这不幸妥协之前肯定遭受了极大的痛苦。
"There are others, with sweet and pallid faces, on which a profound resignation is visible; but they are sad, and one understands that they must still weep at times in secret. Ah, my sons! reflect that some of them have lost their sight in a few days, some after years of martyrdom and many terrible chirurgical operations, and that many were born so,—born into a night that has no dawn for them, that they entered into the world as into an immense tomb, and that they do not know what the human countenance is like. Picture to yourself how they must have suffered, and how they must still suffer, when they think thus confusedly of the tremendous difference between themselves and those who see, and ask themselves, 'Why this difference, if we are not to blame?' 
“还有一些人，从他们可爱、苍白的脸上能看到一种深深的妥协，但是他们很难过，人们能想到他们有时肯定还会暗地里哭泣。啊，我的儿子们！想想看，他们中有些在几天之内就失明了，有些在经历了几年的痛苦和很多可怕的外科手术后失明了，还有很多生下来就是这样——生在一个对他们来说没有黎明的黑夜，他们来到这个世界就像进入了一个巨大的坟墓，他们也不知道人的面孔是什么样子。你们想象一下，当他们疑惑地想着他们自己和那些能看到的人之间的巨大区别，并问自己：‘如果不是我们的错，为什么会有这样的区别？’的时候，他们受到了怎样的折磨，并且他们仍在遭受着怎样的折磨。
"I who have spent many years among them, when I recall that class, all those eyes forever sealed, all those pupils without sight and without life, and then look at the rest of you, it seems impossible to me that you should not all be happy. Think of it! there are about twenty-six thousand blind persons in Italy! Twenty-six thousand persons who do not see the light—do you understand? An army which would employ four hours in marching past our windows." 
“我和他们相处了很多年，当我想起那个班级，那些永远闭着的眼睛，那些没有视觉也没有生命力的学生，然后再看看你们，对我来说，你们所有人都不可能不幸福。想想吧！在意大利有大约两万六千个盲人！两万六千人看不到光亮——你们明白吗？这些人列队走过我们的窗口，要花上四个小时。”
The master paused. Not a breath was audible in all the school. Derossi asked if it were true that the blind have a finer sense of feeling than the rest of us.
老师停了一下。教室里静得连呼吸声都不听到了。代洛西问盲人的感觉是否真的比我们其他人灵敏。
The master said: "It is true. All the other senses are finer in them, because, since they must replace, among them, that of sight, they are more and better exercised than they are in the case of those who see. In the morning, in the dormitory, one asks another, 'Is the sun shining?' and the one who is the most alert in dressing runs instantly into the yard, and flourishes his hands in the air, to find out whether there is any warmth of the sun perceptible, and then he runs to communicate the good news, 'The sun is shining!' From the voice of a person they obtain an idea of his height. We judge of a man's soul by his eyes; they, by his voice. They remember intonations and accents for years. They perceive if there is more than one person in a room, even if only one speaks, and the rest remain motionless. They know by their touch whether a spoon is more or less polished. Little girls distinguish dyed wools from that which is of the natural color. As they walk two and two along the streets, they recognize nearly all the shops by their odors, even those in which we perceive no odor. They spin top, and by listening to its humming they go straight to it and pick it up without any mistake. They trundle hoop, play at ninepins, jump the rope, build little houses of stones, pick violets as though they saw them, make mats and baskets, weaving together straw of various colors rapidly and well—to such a degree is their sense of touch skilled. The sense of touch is their sight. One of their greatest pleasures is to handle, to grasp, to guess the forms of things by feeling them. It is affecting to see them when they are taken to the Industrial Museum, where they are allowed to handle whatever they please, and to observe with what eagerness they fling themselves on geometrical bodies, on little models of houses, on instruments; with what joy they feel over and rub and turn everything about in their hands, in order to see how it is made. They call this seeing!" 
老师说：“是真的。他们的其他所有感官都更灵敏，因为对他们来说，它们必须代替视觉，所以它们比在能看到的人身上得到更多、更好的锻炼。早上，在寝室里，一个人问其他人：‘太阳出来了吗？’衣服穿得最快的一个就马上跑到院子里，手在空中挥舞着，看能不能感受到一丝阳光的温暖，然后跑回去传达好消息：‘太阳出来了！’他们能从一个人的声音得知他的身高。我们评判一个人的心灵是通过他的眼睛，而他们是通过声音。他们能把语调和口音牢记几年。就算房间里只有一个人说话，而其他人一动也不动，他们也能察觉到房间里不止一个人。他们一摸就能知道勺子擦得干净与否。小女孩们能分辨出染过的羊毛和原色的羊毛。他们两个两个走在街上时，能通过气味辨认出几乎所有的商店，甚至包括那些我们在里面根本闻不到气味的商店。他们玩陀螺时，靠听它嗡嗡的声音就能直接走过去，把它分毫不差地捡起来。他们玩滚环、九柱球、跳绳，建石头小房子，摘紫罗兰时就像能看到它们一样，而编席子和篮子时，能把各种颜色的麦秆快速熟练地编织在一起——他们的触觉已经达到了如此熟练的程度。触觉就是他们的视觉。他们最高兴的事情之一就是去触摸、去抓取，通过感受来猜东西的形状。看着他们被带到想摸什么都可以的工业博物馆，看着他们急切地冲向那些几何物体、小模型房子和仪表器械时，很令人感动。他们高兴地把所有东西拿在手里感受着、揉搓着，翻来翻去地看它们是怎么做出来的。他们把它叫做‘看’!"
Garoffi interrupted the teacher to inquire if it was true that blind boys learn to reckon better than others.
卡洛斐打断了老师，问盲童是不是真的比其他人的算术学得好。
The master replied: "It is true. They learn to reckon and to write. They have books made on purpose for them, with raised characters; they pass their fingers over these, recognize the letters and pronounce the words. They read rapidly; and you should see them blush, poor little things, when they make a mistake. And they write, too, without ink. They write on a thick and hard sort of paper with a metal bodkin, which makes a great many little hollows, grouped according to a special alphabet; these little punctures stand out in relief on the other side of the paper, so that by turning the paper over and drawing their fingers across these projections, they can read what they have written, and also the writing of others; and thus they write compositions: and they write letters to each other. They write numbers in the same way, and they make calculations; and they calculate mentally with an incredible facility, since their minds are not diverted by the sight of surrounding objects, as ours are. And if you could see how passionately fond they are of reading, how attentive they are, how well they remember everything, how they discuss among themselves, even the little ones, of things connected with history and language, as they sit four or five on the same bench, without turning to each other, and converse, the first with the third, the second with the fourth, in a loud voice and all together, without losing a single word, so acute and prompt is their hearing.
老师回答：“是的。他们学算术和写字。他们有专门的课本，字是突起的；他们用手指去摸，认出字母，然后读出单词。他们读得很快，当他们出错时，你会看到他们脸羞红了，可怜的小东西。他们也写字，不用墨水。他们用一根金属粗针在一张又厚又硬的纸上写字，扎出很多按特殊字母表分成组的小孔，这些小孔会像浮雕一样显示在纸背面，这样一来他们把纸翻过来并用手指摸摸这些突起，就能读出他们写的东西，也能读出别人写的，他们就这样写作文。他们也互相写信。他们用同样的方法写数字、做算术；他们有着惊人的心算能力，因为他们的思维不会像我们这样被周围看到的东西分心。他们热爱阅读，非常专心，所有东西都记得很牢；他们常常四五个人坐在一张长凳上，独立讨论有关历史和语言的问题——即使很小的孩子也是如此，交谈时不面向对方，第一个跟第三个谈，第二个和第四个说，还有所有人一起大声地交谈，他们的听觉准确、灵敏，从不会漏掉一个单词。要是你们能看到这一切，那该有多好啊。
"And they attach more importance to the examinations than you do, I assure you, and they are fonder of their teachers. They recognize their teacher by his step and his odor; they perceive whether he is in a good or bad humor, whether he is well or ill, simply by the sound of a single word of his. They want the teacher to touch them when he encourages and praises them, and they feel of his hand and his arms in order to express their gratitude. And they love each other and are good comrades to each other. In play time they are always together, according to their wont. In the girls' school, for instance, they form into groups according to the instrument on which they play,—violinists, pianists, and flute-players,—and they never separate. When they have become attached to any one, it is difficult for them to break it off. They take much comfort in friendship. They judge correctly among themselves. They have a clear and profound idea of good and evil. No one grows so enthusiastic as they over the narration of a generous action, of a grand deed." 
“他们比你们更重视考试，我敢保证，并且他们更喜欢老师。他们能通过脚步声和气味认出他们的老师，他们只听到一个词就能察觉到他的心情是好是坏，他是健康还是生病了。当老师鼓励表扬他们的时候，他们想让老师摸摸他们，并且他们会摸着老师的手和胳膊来表示感谢。他们很爱彼此，也是彼此的好伙伴。按照他们的习惯，他们在闲暇时间里总是呆在一起。比如说，在女校里，她们会根据演奏的乐器分成组——拉小提琴的、弹钢琴的，还有吹长笛的——并且她们从不分开。他们一旦和谁黏在一起，就很难把他们分开了。他们在友情中得到很大的安慰。他们彼此之间评判得很准确。他们对善恶有明确而深刻的概念。没有人会像他们那样，在听说一项善举或高尚行为时迸发出那样的热情。”
Votini inquired if they played well.
华梯尼问他们演奏得好不好。
"They are ardently fond of music," replied the master. "It is their delight: music is their life. Little blind children, when they first enter the Institute, are capable of standing three hours perfectly motionless, to listen to playing. They learn easily; they play with fire. When the teacher tells one of them that he has not a talent for music, he feels very sorrowful, but he sets to studying desperately. Ah! if you could hear the music there, if you could see them when they are playing, with their heads thrown back a smile on their lips, their faces aflame, trembling with emotion, in ecstasies at listening to that harmony which replies to them in the obscurity which envelops them, you would feel what a divine consolation is music! And they shout for joy, they beam with happiness when a teacher says to them, "You will become an artist." The one who is first in music, who succeeds the best on the violin or piano, is like a king to them; they love, they venerate him. If a quarrel arises between two of them, they go to him; if two friends fall out, it is he who reconciles them. The smallest pupils, whom he teaches to play, regard him as a father. Then all go to bid him good night before retiring to bed. And they talk constantly of music. They are already in bed, late at night, wearied by study and work, and half asleep, and still they are discussing, in a low tone, operas, masters, instruments, and orchestras. It is so great a punishment for them to be deprived of the reading, or lesson in music, it causes them such sorrow that one hardly ever has the courage to punish them in that way. That which the light is to our eyes, music is to their hearts." 
“他们非常喜欢音乐。”老师回答，“那是他们的快乐：音乐是他们的生命。第一次进学校时，那些小盲童就能完全一动不动地站三个小时听演奏。他们学得很轻松，他们演奏时满怀热情。要是老师跟其中一个人说他没有音乐天赋，他会非常伤心，但他还是会拼命地努力学习。啊！要是你们能听到那里的音乐，看到他们演奏时头往后仰，唇边带着微笑，脸红得像火一般，激动地颤抖着，陶醉地听着那和谐的曲调在笼罩着他们的黑暗中对他们的回应，你们就会感受到音乐真是上天赐予的安慰啊！并且当老师对他们说：‘你会成为一个艺术家。’的时候，他们开心地叫着，面露喜色。音乐得到第一名、小提琴或钢琴演奏得最好的那个人，对他们来说就像是王者，他们热爱并尊重他。要是有两个人吵了起来，他们就会去找他，如果两个朋友闹翻了，他会让他们和好。他会教那些年纪最小的学生们演奏，而他们把他当作父亲。所有人上床之前都去跟他说晚安。他们经常谈论音乐。深夜，他们躺在床上，学习和工作之后已经很疲惫了，而半睡半醒之间还在低声地讨论着歌剧、老师、乐器，还有管弦乐队。对他们来说，不准他们读书或者不准上音乐课就是极大的惩罚，这给他们造成的痛苦会让人从来不敢这样惩罚他们。就像光亮照进了我们的眼睛一样，音乐进入了他们的心灵。”
Derossi asked whether we could not go to see them.
代洛西问我们能否去看看他们。
"Yes," replied the teacher; "but you boys must not go there now. You shall go there later on, when you are in a condition to appreciate the whole extent of this misfortune, and to feel all the compassion which it merits. It is a sad sight, my boys. You will sometimes see there boys seated in front of an open window, enjoying the fresh air, with immovable countenances, which seem to be gazing at the wide green expanse and the beautiful blue mountains which you can see; and when you remember that they see nothing—that they will never see anything—of that vast loveliness, your soul is oppressed, as though you had yourselves become blind at that moment. And then there are those who were born blind, who, as they have never seen the world, do not complain because they do not possess the image of anything, and who, therefore, arouse less compassion. But there are lads who have been blind but a few months, who still recall everything, who thoroughly understand all that they have lost; and these have, in addition, the grief of feeling their minds obscured, the dearest images grow a little more dim in their minds day by day, of feeling the persons whom they have loved the most die out of their memories. One of these boys said to me one day, with inexpressible sadness, 'I should like to have my sight again, only for a moment, in order to see mamma's face once more, for I no longer remember it!' And when their mothers come to see them, the boys place their hands on her face; they feel her over thoroughly from brow to chin, and her ears, to see how they are made, and they can hardly persuade themselves that they cannot see her, and they call her by name many times, to beseech her that she will allow them, that she will make them see her just once. How many, even hard-hearted men, go away in tears! And when you do go out, your case seems to you to be the exception, and the power to see people, houses, and the sky a hardly deserved privilege. Oh! there is not one of you, I am sure, who, on emerging thence, would not feel disposed to deprive himself of a portion of his own sight, in order to bestow a gleam at least upon all those poor children, for whom the sun has no light, for whom a mother has no face!" 
“可以。”老师回答，“但是你们这些男孩现在不能去。你们应该等到你们能完整地体会到这种不幸，能感受到它应得的全部怜悯以后再去。那是个令人心痛的景象，我的孩子们。你们有时会看到男孩们坐在一扇打开的窗户前，一动不动地享受着新鲜的空气，脸上的表情就好像在凝视着你们所能看到的宽广的绿色大地和美丽的青山；当你们想起他们看不到——他们以后也看不到——那极其美好的一切时，你们的灵魂会觉得压抑，就像在那一刻你们自己变成了盲人一样。还有那些生来就失明的人，因为从没看到过这个世界，所以不去抱怨他们对所有东西都没有印象，于是，他们的抱怨就更少。但是有些小伙子刚刚失明几个月，他们仍在回忆一切，他们完全知道他们所失去的一切；另外，他们痛苦地感受到自己的思维正变得模糊，那些最珍贵的画面一天天在他们脑海中慢慢消逝，感受到他们最爱的人从他们的记忆中渐渐消失了。有一天，一个男孩带着无法形容的悲伤对我说：‘我想重新拥有视力，哪怕一会儿，再一次看看我妈妈的脸，因为我已经记不起它了！’当他们的母亲去看他们时，男孩们把手放在母亲的脸上，仔细地感受着她，从额头到下巴，再到耳朵，看它们是什么样的。他们无法让自己相信他们看不到她了，他们还一直喊着她的名字，乞求她允许他们这样做，并乞求她再让他们看她一眼。很多人，就算是铁石心肠的人，也流着泪走开了！你们去过之后，对你们来说，似乎你们的情况变成了例外，能看到人、房子和天空变成了一项难得的特权。哦！我敢肯定，你们从那里回来后，都会乐意分出一部分自己的视力给所有那些可怜的孩子们的，至少让那些看不到太阳的光芒和母亲的面孔的孩子们感到一丝光明！”
