Chapter 15  

第十五章  

Joseph and I had been getting everything ready for my departure for about an hour, when there was a violent ringing at my door.
我和约瑟夫正为出发做着准备，忙了将近一个小时，突然有人猛拉我家的门铃。
'Should I answer it?' said Joseph.
“要开门吗？”约瑟夫问我。
'Yes,' I told him, wondering who could be calling so late, and not daring to hope it was Marguerite.
“开吧。”我对他说，心想这么晚了谁会到我家来，我不敢去奢望会是玛格丽特。
'Sir,' said Joseph when he returned, 'there are two ladies...'
“先生，”约瑟夫回来说，“是两位太太。”
'It's us, Armand,' cried a voice which I recognized as belonging to Prudence.
“是我们，阿尔芒。”一个声音嚷道，我听出是普鲁登斯。
I emerged from my bedroom.
我走出卧室。
Prudence was standing and gazing about her at the few curios dotted around my drawing-room; Marguerite was sitting on the sofa, occupied by her thoughts. When I entered, I went to her, knelt before her, took both her hands and, in a voice touched with emotion, I said: Forgive me.'
普鲁登斯在那边站着，盯着我客厅中零星摆放着的几件古董，玛格丽特则坐在沙发上想着什么。我一进去就径直向她走去，跪在她面前握住她的双手，激动地说：“原谅我吧。”
'She kissed me on the brow and said: That's the third time I've forgiven you.'
她吻了吻我的额头，对我说：“这已经是我第三次原谅您了。”
'I was going to go away tomorrow.'
“我本打算明天就走的。”
'How can my visit change your mind? I haven't come here to stop you leaving Paris. I came because I haven' t had time all day to reply to your letter, and I didn't want to leave you with the impression that I was cross with you. Even so, Prudence didn't want me to come: she said I might be in your way.'
“我的来访怎么能改变您的决定呢？我不是来阻止您离开巴黎的。我来是因为我一整天都没时间给您回信，又不想让您认为我是生您的气了。即便这样，普鲁登斯还不让我来呢，她说我也许会打搅到您的。”
'You! In my way, Marguerite! But how?'
“您！打扰我？玛格丽特！怎么会呢！”
'Why, you could have had a woman here,' answered Prudence, 'and it wouldn't have been very funny for her to see another two turning up.'
“当然啦！要是您这儿有一个女人，”普鲁登斯回答说，“让她看见又来了两个，这可不是什么有趣的事。”
While Prudence was making this remark, Marguerite watched me closely.
普鲁登斯说这些话的时候，玛格丽特一直在紧紧注视着我。
'My dear Prudence,' I replied, 'you're talking nonsense.'
“我亲爱的普鲁登斯，”我回答说，“您简直是在胡言乱语。”
'You've got a very nice apartment,' answered Prudence. Mind if I take a look at the bedroom?'
“您的房间可真是不错，”普鲁登斯说道。“介意我参观一下您的卧室吗？”
'Not at all.'
“一点儿也不。”
Prudence went off into my bedroom, not so much to see inside as to cover up her unfortunate remark and to leave Marguerite and me alone together.
普鲁登斯走进我的卧室，她倒并非真要参观一下，而是要弥补她刚刚说过的蠢话，给我和玛格丽特留一个独处的空间。
'Why did you bring Prudence with you?' I said.
“您怎么会带普鲁登斯一起来呢？”我问她。
'Because she was with me at the theatre, and because I wanted to have someone to see me home when I left here.'
“因为她刚刚陪我看戏去了，再说我离开这里的时候也想有个人陪我回家。”
'Couldn't I have done it?'
“难道我不可以吗？”
'Yes. But apart from the fact that I didn't want to disturb you, I was quite certain that when you got to my door you would ask if you could come up and, since I couldn't let you, I didn't want you to go away feeling you had any right to blame me for refusing you anything.'
“当然可以。但是一方面我不想麻烦您，另一方面，我敢肯定您到了我家门口就会要求上去坐坐，而我又不能答应您，我可不想因为我的拒绝而使您在离开时又多了一个埋怨我的理由。”
'And why couldn't you let me come up?'
“那么您为什么不能让我上去呢？”
'Because I'm being watched very closely, and because the least hint of suspicion could do me a great deal of harm.'
“因为我受到了严密的监视，最细微的怀疑也会对我造成很大的危害。”
'Is that the only reason?'
“这是唯一的原因吗？”
'If there was another, I would tell you what it was; we' ve got past the stage of having secrets from each other.'
“如果还有别的原因，我会告诉您的，我们之间早就过了互相隐藏秘密的阶段了。”
'Listen, Marguerite, I'm not going to make any bones about what I want to say to you. Tell me, do you love me a little?'
“玛格丽特，我不想跟您拐弯抹角地说话。告诉我，您有一丁点儿爱我吗？”
'A great deal.'
“我非常爱您。”
'Then why did you deceive me?'
“那您为什么要欺骗我呢？”
'My dear, if I were the Duchess of This or That, if I had two hundred thousand livres a year, if I were your mistress and had another lover besides you, then you'd have every right to ask why I deceive you. But I am Mademoiselle Marguerite Gautier, I have debts of forty thousand and not a penny behind me, and I spend a hundred thousand francs a year: your question is out of order and my answer irrelevant.'
“亲爱的，假如我是某某公爵夫人，假如我有二十万利弗尔的年金，假如我在做您情妇以后又有了其他情人，您尽可以来问我为什么要欺骗您。但我是玛格丽特·戈蒂埃小姐，我担负着四万法郎的债务，我没有一点儿财产，我每年还要花掉十万法郎；所以您的问题根本毫无意义，我回答您也只是白费力气。”
'You're quite right,' I said letting my head fall on to Marguerite's knees, 'but I do love you, to distraction.'
“您说得很对，”我把头枕在玛格丽特的膝盖上说，“但是我真的爱您，爱得近乎疯狂。”
'Well, my dear, you should have loved me a little less or understood me a little better. Your letter hurt me very deeply. If I'd been free to choose, then in the first place I would never have seen the Count the day before yesterday, or, if I had, I would have come to beg you for the forgiveness which you asked of me a few moments ago and, from that moment on, I would have had no other lover but you. There was a moment when I thought I could indulge myself and be really happy for those six months. You would have none of it; you just had to know how I was going to manage it—good heavens! It was easy enough to guess. The sacrifice I was going to have to make if it was to be possible, was much greater than you think. I could have told you: "I need twenty thousand francs." You were in love with me, you would have raised it some how, though there was a risk that one day you'd be sorry you'd done so and blame me. I chose to owe you nothing; you didn't understand my delicacy, for delicacy it is. Girls of my sort, at least those of us who still have some feelings left, take words and things further and deeper than other women. I repeat: coming from Marguerite Gautier, the means which she found of repaying her debts without asking you for the money it took, was an act of great delicacy of which you should now take advantage without another word. If you met me today for the first time, you'd be only too delighted with the promises I'd make you, and you wouldn't ask questions about what I did the day before yesterday. Sometimes, we have no choice but to buy gratifications for the soul at some cost to the body, and it hurts all the more when those gratifications subsequently elude us.'
“我亲爱的，您就少爱我一些，或者多了解我一些。您的信让我非常伤心。如果我可以选择，我前天绝不会接待伯爵，就算接待了他，我也会来请求您的原谅，就像刚才您向我请求原谅那样，而且从那刻起，除您之外我不会再有任何情人了。有一阵子我以为自己能放松下来好好享受六个月的快乐日子。但您却不愿意；您非要知道我用的是什么方法，天哪！这难道还用猜吗？我这样做所付出的代价远比您想象的大得多。我本可以对您说：‘我需要两万法郎。’您现在正爱着我，兴许能筹到这笔钱，但以后可能您就会后悔并埋怨我害您这么做。我情愿不欠您任何东西；您不懂得我对您的体贴，因为它太过微妙。我们这样的女人，至少是那些还留有感情的，所说的话、所做的事都有深刻的含义，胜过其他女人。我再对您重复一遍，对玛格丽特·戈蒂埃来说，不去向您要钱而自己找办法还清债务是对您的体贴，您应该一声不响地接受。如果您今天才认识我，您会对我答应您的事感到非常高兴，也不会盘问我前天到底干了什么。有些时候为了获取精神上的满足，我们必须付出肉体上的代价，但当精神上的满足也离我们远去之后，我们就会更加痛苦。”
'I heard and saw Marguerite with admiration. When I reflected that this marvelous creature, whose feet I once had longed to kiss, should consent to give me a place in her thoughts and a role in her life, and when I thought that I was still not content with what she was giving me, I asked myself whether man's desire has any limits at all if, though satisfied as promptly as mine had been, it can still aspire to something more.
我怀着赞赏的心倾听着玛格丽特的话语，凝望着她的脸庞。我心想，过去我只渴望能亲吻这个绝代佳人的脚，现在她竟让我了解她真实的想法，并且在她的生活中占据了一席之地，而我对此却仍不满足。我不禁自问，人的欲望是否有止境？尽管已经得到满足，我却还想要更多。
'It's true,' she went on, 'we creatures of chance have weird desires and unimaginable passions. Sometimes we give ourselves for one thing, sometimes for another. There are men who could ruin themselves and get nowhere with us; there are others who can have us for a bunch of flowers. Our hearts are capricious: it's their only diversion and their only excuse. I gave myself to you more quickly than I ever did to another man, I swear. Why? Because when you saw me coughing blood, you took me by the land, because you wept, because you are the only human being who ever felt sorry for me. I'm now going to tell you something silly. Once I had a little dog who used to look at we with sad eyes when I coughed: he was the only living creature I have ever loved.
“就是这样，”她接着说，“我们这些受命运摆布的人总会有一些古怪的愿望和不可思议的感情。有些时候我们为一件事情而委身于人，有些时候又是为了其他的事情。有些人为我们倾家荡产，却一无所获；有些人只用一束鲜花就可以拥有我们。我们总是这样心血来潮，这是我们仅有的消遣和借口。我发誓我从没像答应您那么快地答应过其他任何男人。为什么呢？因为那天我咳血的时候，您握住我的手，因为您为我流下了眼泪，因为您是唯一一个真正同情我的人。现在我告诉您一件很可笑的事。从前我有一只小狗，每当我咳嗽的时候，它总会用哀伤的眼神望着我；它是我唯一喜欢过的动物。
'When he died, I cried more than after my mother's death. Mind you, she did spend twelve years of her life beating me. Well, from the start, I loved you as much as my dog. If men only knew what can be had with just one tear, they would be better loved and we should ruin fewer of them.
“当它死的时候，我哭得比母亲去世时还要伤心。您要知道，我整整挨了母亲十二年的打骂。就这样，我从一开始就爱上了您，就像我之前爱我的小狗一样。如果男人明白用一滴眼泪可以换到些什么的话，他们就能获得更多的爱，为我们破产的人也会少些。
'Your letter gave you away: it showed me that you didn't understand the workings of the heart, and it injured you more in the love I had for you than anything else you could have done. It was jealousy, of course, but a sarcastic, haughty kind of jealousy. I was feeling miserable when I got the letter. I was counting on seeing you at midday, on having lunch with you, hoping the sight of you would chase away a thought I kept having which, before I knew you, never bothered me in the least.
“您的来信暴露了真实的您：它告诉我您并不明白心灵的奥妙所在，在我对您的爱中，它对您的伤害比您能做出的其他任何事情都更大。这当然是嫉妒，但却是可笑而又粗鄙的嫉妒。收到您的信时，我感到万分痛苦。我原指望中午能见到您，和您一起吃午饭，希望见到您我就能抹掉那些一直纠缠着我的想法，而在认识您之前，这些东西我从来都不放在心上。
'Then again,' continued Marguerite, 'you were the only person with whom I'd sensed from the first I could think and speak freely. People who congregate around girls like me can gain a great deal by paying close attention to the slightest words we say, and by drawing conclusions from our most insignificant actions. Naturally, we have no friends, we have egotistical lovers who spend their fortunes not on us, as they claim, but on their vanity.
“而且，”玛格丽特继续说道，“您是唯一一个让我一开始就感觉在您面前可以毫无顾虑、畅所欲言的人。那些喜欢在像我这样的姑娘身边转来转去的人总爱对我们所讲的东西追根究底，想在我们那些最微不足道的举动中找出些所以然。我们自然没有什么朋友，我们有的只是一群自私自利的情人，他们声称为我们挥霍了他们的财产，其实只是为了自己的虚荣心。
For men like these, we have to be cheerful when they are happy, hale and hearty when they decide they want supper, and as cynical as they are. We are not allowed to have feelings, for fear of being jeered at and losing our credibility.
对于这些人，在他们开心的时候，我们也要跟着开心；在他们想吃夜宵的时候，我们也要变得胃口大开；在他们疑神疑鬼的时候，我们也要疑神疑鬼。我们不能有自己的感情，否则就会遭到嘲笑，毁掉自己的名声。
Our lives are no longer our own. We aren't human beings, but things. We rank first in their pride, and last in their good opinion. We have women friends, but they are friends like Prudence—yesterday's kept women who still have expensive tastes which their age prevents them from indulging. So they become our friends, or rather associates. Their friendship may verge on the servile, but it is never disinterested. They'll never give you a piece of advice unless there's money in it. They don't care if we've got ten lovers extra as long as they get a few dresses or a bracelet out of them and can drive about every now and then in our carriages and sit in our boxes at the theatre. They end up with the flowers we were given the night before, and they borrow our Indian shawls. They never do us a good turn, however trifling, without making sure they get paid twice what their trouble was worth. You saw as much yourself the evening Prudence brought me the six thousand francs which I'd asked her to go and beg from the Duke; she borrowed five hundred francs which she'll never give back, or else she'll pay it off in hats that will never get taken out of their boxes.
我们的生命早已不属于自己。我们不再是人，而是物品。当他们想要满足自己的自尊心时，总会先想到我们，但他们却从没正眼看过我们。我们有不少女朋友，但她们都是像普鲁登斯那样的朋友，她们过去也是妓女，习惯了挥霍，但现在人老了，没法再放纵了。于是，她们成了我们的朋友，或者说，合伙人。她们的友情有时甚至就像奴役的关系，但这可不是白做的。除非有钱可赚，否则她们不会给你任何建议。她们不会在乎我们是否有十几个情人，只要能从他们那里搞到几件裙子首饰，或者能经常坐着我们的马车出去逛逛，又或者能坐在我们的包厢里看戏。她们拿走我们前夜用过的花，借用我们的开司米披肩。不管是多么小的事情，如果她们没法得到双倍于她们辛劳的谢礼，她们是绝对不会帮我们忙的。那天晚上您也亲眼看到了，普鲁登斯替我从公爵那要来了六千法郎，却向我借走了五百法郎，那些钱她是永远不会还的，顶多还给我几顶帽子，这也绝不会是她们自己的东西。
So we can have, or rather I had, only one hope of happiness: and this was, sad as I sometimes am and ill as I am always, to find a man of sufficiently rare qualities who would never ask me to account for my actions, and be the lover of my wilder fancies more than the lover of my body . I found this man in the Duke, but the Duke is old and old age neither shields nor consoles. I'd thought I could settle for the life he made for me. But it was no use. I was dying of boredom, and I felt that if I was going to be destroyed, then I might as well jump into the flames as choke on the fumes.
因此我们只能希望，或者说我曾经希望，拥有一种幸福：像我这种偶尔情绪低落，又经常体弱多病的人，这唯一的幸福就是找到具有十分罕见品质的男人，他不过问我的生活，爱我疯狂的想法胜过爱我的身体。我曾经在公爵身上找到过这样的品质，但是公爵已经老了，他既不能保护我，又无法给我安慰。我本以为我能够按照他为我安排的那样生活。但是我做不到。我快要无聊死了，我想如果我要死掉，我宁愿跳到火中烧死，让烟呛死。
Then I met you. You were young, passionate, happy, and I tried to turn you into the man I had cried out for in my crowded but empty life. What I loved in you was not the man you were but the man you could be. You refuse to accept the part; you reject it as unworthy of you; you are a commonplace lover. Just do what the others do: pay me and let's not talk about it any more.'
然后我遇见了您。您是那么年轻、热情、开朗，我试着将您变成我这拥挤却又空虚的生命中迫切想要找到的那个人。我真正爱的，不是现在的您，而是您将会变成的那个人。但您拒绝接受这个角色；您觉得这有失您的身份；您不过是个普通的情人。您还是像别人那样付钱给我吧，我们也别再谈这些事了。”
Marguerite, tried by this long confession, settled back into the sofa and, to check a mild fit of coughing, put her handkerchief to her lips and even wiped her eyes.
说完这段长长的表白之后，玛格丽特疲惫地倚在沙发上，她努力忍住阵阵轻微的咳嗽，把手绢按在嘴唇上，甚至擦了擦眼睛。
'Forgive me, forgive me,' I murmured, 'I knew all this, but I wanted to hear you say it, my darling Marguerite. Let's forget the rest. Let's just remember one thing: we belong to one another, we are young and we are in love.
“原谅我，原谅我，”我喃喃地说，“其实这一切我早已明白，但我还是想听您把这些讲出来，我亲爱的玛格丽特。让我们把其他的都忘了吧。我们只需要记住一件事，那就是我们是属于彼此的，我们还很年轻，我们深深相爱。
Marguerite, do with me what you will. I am your slave, your dog. But, in the name of God, tear up the letter I wrote you and don't let me go away tomorrow. It would kill me.'
玛格丽特，随便您怎么处置我都可以。我是您的奴隶，您的小狗。但看在上帝的份上，把我写给您的那封信撕掉吧，明天别让我走。否则我会死的。”
'Marguerite withdrew the letter from the bodice of her dress and, as she handed it back to me, said with a smile of infinite sweetness:
玛格丽特把那封信从她裙子的口袋里掏出来，递还给我，带着无限甜蜜的微笑对我说：
Here, I was bringing it back to you.'
“在这儿呢，我本来就是来把它还给你的。”
I tore up the letter and, with tears in my eyes, kissed the hand which held it.
我把信撕成了碎片，含着眼泪亲吻她递给我信的那只手。
At this juncture, Prudence reappeared.
这时普鲁登斯又再次出现了。
'Oh, Prudence, can you guess what he wants me to do?' said Marguerite.
“啊，普鲁登斯，您知道他想要我做什么吗？”玛格丽特问。
'To forgive him.'
“让您原谅他。”
'That's right.'
“正是这样。”
'And have you?'
“您原谅他了吗？”
'I can't do otherwise. But there's something else he wants.'
“我别无选择啊。但他还有一个请求。”
'What's that?'
“什么请求？”
'He wants to come and have supper with us.'
“他想来和我们一起吃夜宵。”
'And are you going to say yes? '
“您会同意吗？”
'What do you think?'
“您说呢？”
'I think you're a couple of children without an ounce of common sense between you. But I also think that I'm ravenous, and the sooner you do say yes, the sooner we'll have supper.'
“我看你们就是两个幼稚的孩子，你俩之间没有常理可言。但我也已经饿得不行了，您越早同意，我们就能越早吃上夜宵。”
'Come on, then,' said Marguerite, 'we can all fit into my carriage. By the way,' she added, turning to me, 'Nanine will have gone to bed, so you'll have to open the door. Take my key, and try not to lose it again.'
“那就走吧，”玛格丽特说，“我的马车可以把我们都装下。对了，”她又转身对我说，“纳尼娜可能已经睡觉了，一会儿还是您去开门好了。拿好我的钥匙，别再弄丢了啊。”
I kissed Marguerite until she had no breath left.
我深深地吻着玛格丽特，几乎让她透不过气来。
Thereupon, Joseph came in.
这时候约瑟夫进来了。
'Sir,' he said with the air of a man terribly pleased with himself, 'the trunks are packed.'
“先生，”他带着对自己极度满意的神情说，“行李都收拾好了。”
'All of them? '
“全部都收拾好了吗？”
'Yes, sir.'
“是的，先生。”
'Well, unpack them. I'm not leaving.'
“那么，再打开吧。我不走了。”
