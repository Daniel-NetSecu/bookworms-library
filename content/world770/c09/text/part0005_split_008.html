IX  

九  

I left Venice the next morning, as soon as I learned that the old lady had not succumbed, as I feared at the moment, to the shock I had given her—the shock I may also say she had given me. How in the world could I have supposed her capable of getting out of bed by herself? I failed to see Miss Tita before going; I only saw the donna, whom I entrusted with a note for her younger mistress. In this note I mentioned that I should be absent but for a few days. I went to Treviso, to Bassano, to Castelfranco; I took walks and drives and looked at musty old churches with ill-lighted pictures and spent hours seated smoking at the doors of cafes, where there were flies and yellow curtains, on the shady side of sleepy little squares. In spite of these pastimes, which were mechanical and perfunctory, I scantily enjoyed my journey: there was too strong a taste of the disagreeable in my life. I had been devilish awkward, as the young men say, to be found by Miss Bordereau in the dead of night examining the attachment of her bureau; and it had not been less so to have to believe for a good many hours afterward that it was highly probable I had killed her. In writing to Miss Tita I attempted to minimize these irregularities; but as she gave me no word of answer I could not know what impression I made upon her. It rankled in my mind that I had been called a publishing scoundrel, for certainly I did publish and certainly I had not been very delicate. There was a moment when I stood convinced that the only way to make up for this latter fault was to take myself away altogether on the instant; to sacrifice my hopes and relieve the two poor women forever of the oppression of my intercourse. Then I reflected that I had better try a short absence first, for I must already have had a sense (unexpressed and dim) that in disappearing completely it would not be merely my own hopes that I should condemn to extinction. It would perhaps be sufficient if I stayed away long enough to give the elder lady time to think she was rid of me. That she would wish to be rid of me after this (if I was not rid of her) was now not to be doubted: that nocturnal scene would have cured her of the disposition to put up with my company for the sake of my dollars. I said to myself that after all I could not abandon Miss Tita, and I continued to say this even while I observed that she quite failed to comply with my earnest request (I had given her two or three addresses, at little towns, post restante) that she would let me know how she was getting on. I would have made my servant write to me but that he was unable to manage a pen. It struck me there was a kind of scorn in Miss Tita's silence (little disdainful as she had ever been), so that I was uncomfortable and sore. I had scruples about going back and yet I had others about not doing so, for I wanted to put myself on a better footing. The end of it was that I did return to Venice on the twelfth day; and as my gondola gently bumped against Miss Bordereau's steps a certain palpitation of suspense told me that I had done myself a violence in holding off so long.
我听说老太太并没有像我担心的那样，被我惊吓致死，就立刻在第二天早上离开了威尼斯——她当时也让我同样惊怕。我怎么可能会想到，她竟然能自己从床上爬起来？我离开之前没能见到蒂塔小姐，我只看到了小女仆，我委托她把一张便条转交给年轻一点儿的女主人。在便条中，我说我会离开数日。我去了特雷维索、巴萨诺、卡斯特尔弗朗科。我或是步行，或是开车，观看陈腐的老教堂，里面有光线阴暗的绘画。我花上好几个小时坐在咖啡店门外抽烟。咖啡店里有很多苍蝇，有黄色的幕墙，坐落在小广场昏昏欲睡的背阳一面。除了这些老套敷衍的消遣之外，我几乎体会不到旅行的乐趣：我强烈地感到自己的生活太不顺心了。用年轻人的话来说，我真是笨手笨脚得过分，漆黑的夜里，居然会被博尔德罗小姐发现我察看她书桌的物件。而事后过了很多个小时我不得不认为，我很有可能会导致她的死亡。这同样让我很难堪。在给蒂塔小姐写信的时候，我尽可能地对这些意外事件轻描淡写，但是，她一个字也没有回我，所以我也不知道她是怎样看我的。我心里很愤慨，自己被称为出版界的恶棍，因为我的确出版作品，而且人品也的确不高雅。有时候，我相信唯一能弥补我最后犯下的这个错误的方法就是让我自己立即消失，放弃我的希望，使这两位可怜的女士永远免受我的打扰之苦。然后我又想，我最好先试试短暂地消失，因为我已经意识到（这感觉说不出来，模模糊糊的），如果完全消失，那么我的希望就被判了死刑了。如果我消失足够长的时候，让老太太以为她已经摆脱我了，那也就够了。在那件事情之后，她现在毫无疑问地想摆脱我（如果我没有摆脱她的话）：那天晚上的一幕也许会治好她爱钱的毛病，也就是，为了我的钱而允许我和她们住在一起。我对自己说，毕竟，我不能放弃蒂塔小姐。我继续这样告诉自己，尽管我发现她根本没有好好配合我诚挚的要求（我给她两三个地址，在小镇上，办的存局侯领），告诉我她到底怎么样了。我本来要让我的仆人写信给我的，但是他不会写字。恐怕蒂塔小姐的缄默是一种轻蔑的表示（她以前几乎从没表现出轻蔑），于是我感到不舒服和心痛。回去，我有顾忌；不回去，也有顾忌。这是因为我想让自己的情形变好一点儿。最后，我在第二十天回到了威尼斯。而当我的船慢慢地，摇摇晃晃地碰到博尔德罗小姐家的台阶时，我悬着的心扑扑跳着，告诉自己，离开这么长时间，对自己太残酷了。
I had faced about so abruptly that I had not telegraphed to my servant. He was therefore not at the station to meet me, but he poked out his head from an upper window when I reached the house. "They have put her into the earth, la vecchia," he said to me in the lower hall, while he shouldered my valise; and he grinned and almost winked, as if he knew I should be pleased at the news.
我如此突然地回来，甚至没有给我的仆人打个电报。所以他没有来车站接我，但是，我到家的时候，他从楼上的窗子探出了头来。“她们已经安葬她了，那个老太太。”他扛着我的旅行箱在楼下大厅里告诉我。他咧嘴笑着，几乎在眨眼睛，就像知道我听到这消息会很高兴似的。
"She's dead!" I exclaimed, giving him a very different look.
“她死了！”我叫道，脸色大变。
"So it appears, since they have buried her." 
“看起来是，因为他们已经把她埋了。”
"It's all over? When was the funeral?” 
“就这么死了？什么时候举行的葬礼？”
"The other yesterday. But a funeral you could scarcely call it, signore; it was a dull little passeggio of two gondolas. Poveretta!" the man continued, referring apparently to Miss Tita. His conception of funerals was apparently that they were mainly to amuse the living.
“前天。但那简直不能叫葬礼，先生。送葬队伍很寒碜，只有两艘平底小船。真可怜！”他继续说道，当然指的是蒂塔小姐。很明显，他概念里的葬礼主要是用来安慰生者的。
I wanted to know about Miss Tita—how she was and where she was—but I asked him no more questions till we had got upstairs. Now that the fact had met me I took a bad view of it, especially of the idea that poor Miss Tita had had to manage by herself after the end. What did she know about arrangements, about the steps to take in such a case? Poveretta indeed! I could only hope that the doctor had given her assistance and that she had not been neglected by the old friends of whom she had told me, the little band of the faithful whose fidelity consisted in coming to the house once a year. I elicited from my servant that two old ladies and an old gentleman had in fact rallied round Miss Tita and had supported her (they had come for her in a gondola of their own) during the journey to the cemetery, the little red-walled island of tombs which lies to the north of the town, on the way to Murano. It appeared from these circumstances that the Misses Bordereau were Catholics, a discovery I had never made, as the old woman could not go to church and her niece, so far as I perceived, either did not or went only to early mass in the parish, before I was stirring. Certainly even the priests respected their seclusion; I had never caught the whisk of the curato's skirt. That evening, an hour later, I sent my servant down with five words written on a card, to ask Miss Tita if she would see me for a few moments. She was not in the house, where he had sought her, he told me when he came back, but in the garden walking about to refresh herself and gathering flowers. He had found her there and she would be very happy to see me.
我想知道蒂塔小姐的情况——她怎么样了，她在什么地方——但是直到我们上楼，我也没有问他别的问题。既然知道了实情，我认为事情很糟糕，尤其是想到蒂塔小姐在姑妈死了之后，要独自料理后事。她懂什么安排？她哪知道处理这种事情时要采取什么步骤啊？的确很可怜！我只能希望医生可以协助她，也希望她没有被那些老朋友们忽视。那些人，她曾经告诉过我，那群忠实的朋友，每年要来这里一次，以示忠诚。我从仆人那里听说。两位老太太和一位先生的确陪在蒂塔小姐身边，在送至墓地的过程中支持着她（他们是坐着自己的平底船来的），墓地在一个红墙围起来的小岛上，上面全是坟墓，坐落在小镇的北面，在通往穆拉诺的路上。从这些情况来看，博尔德罗小姐是天主教徒，这是我从没发现过的事情。因为老太太没有办法去教堂礼拜，而她的侄女，据我观察，在我开始打扰她们之前，就没有去礼拜了，或是只参加教区的早弥撒。很明显，甚至是教士，都尊重她们与世隔绝的生活。我连神父袍的影子都没有看到过。那天晚上，一个小时以后，我让仆人带一张卡到楼下去，写了五个词，问问蒂塔小姐可不可以同我见上一面。仆人回来告诉我，说他去找她的时候，她不在房内，而是在花园里散步，放松自己，采撷鲜花。他在那里找到了她，她说她非常高兴能见到我。
I went down and passed half an hour with poor Miss Tita. She had always had a look of musty mourning (as if she were wearing out old robes of sorrow that would not come to an end), and in this respect there was no appreciable change in her appearance. But she evidently had been crying, crying a great deal—simply, satisfyingly, refreshingly, with a sort of primitive, retarded sense of loneliness and violence. But she had none of the formalism or the self-consciousness of grief, and I was almost surprised to see her standing there in the first dusk with her hands full of flowers, smiling at me with her reddened eyes. Her white face, in the frame of her mantilla, looked longer, leaner than usual. I had had an idea that she would be a good deal disgusted with me—would consider that I ought to have been on the spot to advise her, to help her; and, though I was sure there was no rancor in her composition and no great conviction of the importance of her affairs, I had prepared myself for a difference in her manner, for some little injured look, half-familiar, half-estranged, which should say to my conscience, "Well, you are a nice person to have professed things!" But historic truth compels me to declare that Tita Bordereau's countenance expressed unqualified pleasure in seeing her late aunt's lodger. That touched him extremely, and he thought it simplified his situation until he found it did not. I was as kind to her that evening as I knew how to be, and I walked about the garden with her for half an hour. There was no explanation of any sort between us; I did not ask her why she had not answered my letter. Still less did I repeat what I had said to her in that communication; if she chose to let me suppose that she had forgotten the position in which Miss Bordereau surprised me that night and the effect of the discovery on the old woman I was quite willing to take it that way: I was grateful to her for not treating me as if I had killed her aunt.
我下了楼，和可怜的蒂塔小姐呆了半个小时。她总是看起来有一种腐朽的哀悼神情（就像她穿了一件旧丧服，穿上就没个完），就这点来说，她的样貌没什么可喜的变化。但是她很明显哭过，哭得很厉害——彻底、酣畅、痛快，带着一种原生的、迟钝的孤独感和激烈感。但是，她不管是形式上还是意识上，都没有悲伤的感觉，而我几乎是很惊讶地看着她站在暮色初现的花园里，手里满是鲜花，用红红的眼睛笑盈盈地看着我。她白皙的脸，裹在她的头纱里面，显得比平日更加瘦长。我有种想法，认为她一定非常讨厌我——她可能认为我理应留在现场，给她出谋献策，帮助她。虽然我肯定她没有怨恨人的特质，也没有表现出她的事情有多么重要，我还是作好了准备，面对她不同的态度，比如小小的受伤的表情，半是熟悉，半是陌生的表情，那会告诉我的良心说：“哼，你是个好人，有正经事要做！”但是事实却让我不得不说，蒂塔·博尔德罗的脸色表明了一种见到她已故姑妈的房客时的绝对喜悦之情。这让他极受触动，他认为这表明自己的情形好转了，直到他发现实情并非如此。我那天晚上用一切我能想到的方法对她友好，和她在花园里走了半个小时。我们两人之间没有任何的解释。我没有问她为什么不回我的信。而我更是没有向她提及那场对话。如果她选择让我以为她已经忘记了那天晚上博尔德罗小姐那让我惊讶的阵势，和这个老妇人发现我的时候的样子，那么我乐于如此：我很高兴她没有把我当成杀害了她姑妈的人来对待。
We strolled and strolled and really not much passed between us save the recognition of her bereavement, conveyed in my manner and in a visible air that she had of depending on me now, since I let her see that I took an interest in her. Miss Tita had none of the pride that makes a person wish to preserve the look of independence; she did not in the least pretend that she knew at present what would become of her. I forbore to touch particularly on that, however, for I certainly was not prepared to say that I would take charge of her. I was cautious; not ignobly, I think, for I felt that her knowledge of life was so small that in her unsophisticated vision there would be no reason why—since I seemed to pity her—I should not look after her. She told me how her aunt had died, very peacefully at the last, and how everything had been done afterward by the care of her good friends (fortunately, thanks to me, she said, smiling, there was money in the house; and she repeated that when once the Italians like you they are your friends for life); and when we had gone into this she asked me about my giro, my impressions, the places I had seen. I told her what I could, making it up partly, I am afraid, as in my depression I had not seen much; and after she had heard me she exclaimed, quite as if she had forgotten her aunt and her sorrow, "Dear, dear, how much I should like to do such things—to take a little journey!” It came over me for the moment that I ought to propose some tour, say I would take her anywhere she liked; and I remarked at any rate that some excursion—to give her a change—might be managed: we would think of it, talk it over. I said never a word to her about the Aspern documents; asked no questions as to what she had ascertained or what had otherwise happened with regard to them before Miss Bordereau's death. It was not that I was not on pins and needles to know, but that I thought it more decent not to betray my anxiety so soon after the catastrophe. I hoped she herself would say something, but she never glanced that way, and I thought this natural at the time. Later, however, that night, it occurred to me that her silence was somewhat strange; for if she had talked of my movements, of anything so detached as the Giorgione at Castelfranco, she might have alluded to what she could easily remember was in my mind. It was not to be supposed that the emotion produced by her aunt's death had blotted out the recollection that I was interested in that lady's relics, and I fidgeted afterward as it came to me that her reticence might very possibly mean simply that nothing had been found. We separated in the garden (it was she who said she must go in); now that she was alone in the rooms I felt that (judged, at any rate, by Venetian ideas) I was on rather a different footing in regard to visiting her there. As I shook hands with her for goodnight I asked her if she had any general plan—had thought over what she had better do. "Oh, yes, oh, yes, but I haven't settled anything yet," she replied quite cheerfully. Was her cheerfulness explained by the impression that I would settle for her?
我们不停散步，我们之间没有什么交流。只是我认识到她的丧亲之痛，这表现在我的态度和她对我明显的依靠上，因为我让她感觉到，我是在意她的。蒂塔小姐没有那种骄傲，那种隐藏对他人的依赖的骄傲。她一点儿也不装作知道自己将来会是什么情况。然而，我对于此事避而不谈，因为我确实没有准备说我要照顾她。我很谨慎，我认为这并不卑劣，因为我感觉她对于生活的了解甚少，在她天真的观念里，她想不出来为什么——既然我看起来很同情她——我不照顾她。她告诉我她姑妈是怎么死的，临终时很安详，告诉我她在好朋友的帮助下，她的一切后事是怎样办的（幸运的是，因为我，她笑着说，家里有钱办这事。而且她一再说，一旦意大利人喜欢你，他们就是一辈子的朋友）。我们聊完了这些，她问起我的旅行，我的印象，我去过的地方。我尽可能地告诉她，编造了一部分经历。恐怕我当时情绪低落，也没看什么。她听完我的话，惊呼起来，好像已经忘记了她的姑妈和她的悲伤：“天哪，天哪，我是多么想要做这样的事情啊——来一场小小的旅行！”我那时回过神，想到应该提议去旅行，告诉她我可以带她去任何她想去的地方。而且我还说，至少，远足——让她换个心情——是可以安排的：我们可以考虑考虑，讨论一番。关于阿斯本的文稿，我一句也没有向她提起，没有问她打探到什么消息，或是在博尔德罗小姐死前，这些文稿发生的任何别的事情。并不是我不着急忙慌地想要知道，而是我认为这次大变故刚发生不久，不那么快泄露自己的焦虑更加得体。我希望她本人能说些什么，但是她完全没有那样做，而我那个时候觉得这是很自然的。然而，那天晚上晚一些的时候，我想到，她的沉默是古怪的，因为如果她已经提到了我的旅行，提到像卡斯特尔弗朗科的吉奥尔若里的画那么不相关的事情，那么她可以很容易地就想到我心里在想的这件事。姑妈之死给她带来的情感上冲击，不应该会让她忘记了我还惦记着她姑妈的遗物。我事后坐立难安，因为我想，她的沉默不语很可能只是说明她什么也没有找到。我们在花园里分手（是她说她必须要进屋去）。既然她独自一人在房内，我觉得（无论如何，按威尼斯的方式来判断）我得以一种相当不同的立场去看她。当我和她握手道晚安，我问她有没有什么大体的计划——有没有想过她最好能做些什么。“哦，是的，哦，是的，但是我还什么都没有定下来。”她兴致勃勃地说。她这快活的样子是不是可以解释为：我会替她安排？
I was glad the next morning that we had neglected practical questions, for this gave me a pretext for seeing her again immediately. There was a very practical question to be touched upon. I owed it to her to let her know formally that of course I did not expect her to keep me on as a lodger, and also to show some interest in her own tenure, what she might have on her hands in the way of a lease. But I was not destined, as it happened, to converse with her for more than an instant on either of these points. I sent her no message; I simply went down to the sala and walked to and fro there. I knew she would come out; she would very soon discover I was there. Somehow I preferred not to be shut up with her; gardens and big halls seemed better places to talk. It was a splendid morning, with something in the air that told of the waning of the long Venetian summer; a freshness from the sea which stirred the flowers in the garden and made a pleasant draught in the house, less shuttered and darkened now than when the old woman was alive. It was the beginning of autumn, of the end of the golden months. With this it was the end of my experiment—or would be in the course of half an hour, when I should really have learned that the papers had been reduced to ashes. After that there would be nothing left for me but to go to the station; for seriously (and as it struck me in the morning light) I could not linger there to act as guardian to a piece of middle-aged female helplessness. If she had not saved the papers wherein should I be indebted to her? I think I winced a little as I asked myself how much, if she HAD saved them, I should have to recognize and, as it were, to reward such a courtesy. Might not that circumstance after all saddle me with a guardianship? If this idea did not make me more uncomfortable as I walked up and down it was because I was convinced I had nothing to look to. If the old woman had not destroyed everything before she pounced upon me in the parlor she had done so afterward.
第二天早上，我很高兴我们忽略了这些实际的问题，因为这给了我一个借口马上再见她一面。有个非常实际的问题要谈谈。我应当正式告诉她，我当然不希望她把我当成一个寄宿者留下。而且，我也应该对她本人的房权表现出兴趣，她手上的权力到底可以允许她怎么出租这个房子。但是，凑巧的是，这两点中的任意一点都只能和她谈上一小会儿。我没有派人送信去，我只是下楼走到正厅里，在那里来来回回地走着。我知道她会出来的，她很快就会发现我在那里。不知何故，我不愿意和她单独关在一个房间里。花园和大厅看起来是比较好的聊天场所。这是个美妙的早晨，空气中某些东西表明悠长的威尼斯之夏正在渐渐消减。一缕来自海上的清新气息搅扰着花园里的鲜花，使得房间里形成了一阵怡人的气流。比起老太太在世的时候，百叶窗关得没有以前那样严实了，房间也没有那么黑了。这是金色夏日的结束，也是秋日的开始。我的试验就这样结束了——也许半个小时内就结束了。那时我就会真正发现，那些文稿早已经化为灰烬了。在那以后，除了往车站走，我真的没什么可做的了。因为，老实说（就像我在晨光中想到的一样）我不能留在这里，扮演一位无助的中年女人的守护神。如果她都没有救下来那些文稿，那我又亏欠她什么呢？当我问自己，如果她真的救了这些文稿，我到底该怎么感谢她，可以说，还要因此还她这个大人情的时候，我想我还是有点儿退缩。那样的情况会不会最终要我承担保护她的责任？如果这个想法没有让我难受得踱来踱去的话，那是因为我让自己相信，我没什么好期盼的。就算之前老太太没有把一切都毁掉，那么她在会客室抓住我之后，她也会那样做的。
It took Miss Tita rather longer than I had expected to guess that I was there; but when at last she came out she looked at me without surprise. I said to her that I had been waiting for her, and she asked why I had not let her know. I was glad the next day that I had checked myself before remarking that I had wished to see if a friendly intuition would not tell her: it became a satisfaction to me that I had not indulged in that rather tender joke. What I did say was virtually the truth—that I was too nervous, since I expected her now to settle my fate.
蒂塔小姐猜出了我在那里，花的时间比我想象的更长。但当她最终出来的时候，她看着我并不觉得惊讶。我对她说我在等她，她问我为什么不告诉她。第二天我很高兴当时管住了自己，没有对她说，我想看看朋友间的直觉能不能让她感觉到我在那里：我很满意自己没有一时冲动，讲出那个敏感的笑话。我所说的，完全是实情——我太紧张了，因为我现在期待她来决定我的命运。
"Your fate?" said Miss Tita, giving me a queer look; and as she spoke I noticed a rare change in her. She was different from what she had been the evening before—less natural, less quiet. She had been crying the day before and she was not crying now, and yet she struck me as less confident. It was as if something had happened to her during the night, or at least as if she had thought of something that troubled her—something in particular that affected her relations with me, made them more embarrassing and complicated. Had she simply perceived that her aunt's not being there now altered my position?
“你的命运？”蒂塔小姐说道，怪怪地看着我。而在她讲话的时候，我注意到她有个不寻常的变化。她和前一个晚上不一样了——不那么自然，也不那么平静。她头一天一直在哭，但现在没哭了，但我觉得她仍然缺乏信心。好像晚上有什么事情发生在她身上，或者，至少好像是她想起来什么困扰她的事情——一些影响到她和我的关系的特别的事情。这些事让我们的关系变得尴尬和复杂。她是不是完全意识到，如今她的姑妈不在了，我的立场也就变了？
"I mean about our papers. ARE there any? You must know now." 
“我说的是我们的文稿。现在还有吗？你现在一定知道了。”
"Yes, there are a great many; more than I supposed." I was struck with the way her voice trembled as she told me this.
“是的，很多文稿，比我想象的还多。”她对我说话的时候，声音在发抖，我吓了一跳。
"Do you mean that you have got them in there—and that I may see them?” 
“你是说，你已经拿到他们了——我可以看到它们了？”
"I don't think you can see them," said Miss Tita with an extraordinary expression of entreaty in her eyes, as if the dearest hope she had in the world now was that I would not take them from her. But how could she expect me to make such a sacrifice as that after all that had passed between us? What had I come back to Venice for but to see them, to take them? My delight in learning they were still in existence was such that if the poor woman had gone down on her knees to beseech me never to mention them again I would have treated the proceeding as a bad joke. "I have got them but I can't show them," she added.
“我觉得你不能看它们。”蒂塔小姐说道，眼神里有一种极不寻常的哀求，好像现在对于她来说，最珍贵的愿望就是我不要从她手里把它们拿走。但是，在我们之间经历了这一切之后，她怎么可能指望我做出这样的牺牲？要不是为了看到它们，拿走它们，我回威尼斯来干什么？我得知它们还在的时候是那么高兴，以至于就算这个可怜的女人跪下求我再也别提它们，我也会把这个过程当成一个恶俗的笑话，不予理睬。“我得到了它们，但是我不能给别人看。”她补充说。
"Not even to me? Ah, Miss Tita!" I groaned, with a voice of infinite remonstrance and reproach.
“连我也不行吗？哦，蒂塔小姐！”我呻吟道，声音里有无限的抗议和责备。
She colored, and the tears came back to her eyes; I saw that it cost her a kind of anguish to take such a stand but that a dreadful sense of duty had descended upon her. It made me quite sick to find myself confronted with that particular obstacle; all the more that it appeared to me I had been extremely encouraged to leave it out of account. I almost considered that Miss Tita had assured me that if she had no greater hindrance than that—! "You don't mean to say you made her a deathbed promise? It was precisely against your doing anything of that sort that I thought I was safe. Oh, I would rather she had burned the papers outright than that!” 
她脸色一变，眼里又有泪水了。我看出，她的立场让自己有种痛苦的感觉，因为一种令人苦闷的责任感降临到她身上。我很厌恶面对这样的障碍。它越发让我觉得自己受到鼓舞，要克服这个障碍。我几乎相信，蒂塔小姐向我明确地表明，她最大的阻碍莫过于此——！“你不是说，你在她临终的时候许下了什么诺言吧？我恰恰也反对你作出任何这类事情，我以为我可以让人放心的。哎，我宁愿她当场把文稿烧了，也不愿这样的事情发生。”
"No, it isn't a promise," said Miss Tita.
“不，那不是个承诺。”蒂塔小姐说。
"Pray what is it then?" 
“天哪，那到底是什么事？”
She hesitated and then she said, "She tried to burn them, but I prevented it. She had hid them in her bed." 
她犹豫了，然后说：“她想烧了它们，但是我阻止了。她以前把它们藏在她床上。”
"In her bed?" 
“床上？”
"Between the mattresses. That's where she put them when she took them out of the trunk. I can't understand how she did it, because Olimpia didn't help her. She tells me so, and I believe her. My aunt only told her afterward, so that she shouldn't touch the bed—anything but the sheets. So it was badly made," added Miss Tita simply.
“在床垫之间。她把它们从箱子里拿出来之后，就藏在那里了。我不知道她是怎么做到的，因为欧林匹亚没有帮过她。她是这样告诉我的，而我相信她。姑妈也是事后告诉她的，这样，她就不能碰床了——除了床单。所以，她的床整理得很糟糕。”蒂塔小姐简单地说道。
"I should think so! And how did she try to burn them?" 
“我早该想到！那她是怎么试图烧掉它们的？”
"She didn't try much; she was too weak, those last days. But she told me—she charged me. Oh, it was terrible! She couldn't speak after that night; she could only make signs.” 
“她没怎么尝试，最后那几天，她太虚弱了。但是她告诉我——她让我来做。哎，太糟糕了！那天晚上之后，她就没法说话，只会叹气了。”
"And what did you do?" 
“那你做了什么？”
"I took them away. I locked them up." 
“我拿走了它们。我把它们锁起来了。”
"In the secretary?" 
“在写字台里？”
"Yes, in the secretary," said Miss Tita, reddening again.
“是的，在写字台里。”蒂塔小姐说，脸又红了。
"Did you tell her you would burn them?" 
“你告诉她你要烧了它们？”
"No, I didn't—on purpose.” 
“不，我没有——故意的。”
"On purpose to gratify me?" 
“目的是要让我满意？”
"Yes, only for that." 
“是的，就为了这个。”
"And what good will you have done me if after all you won't show them?” 
“最终，如果你不肯给我看，那你这样对我有什么好处？”
"Oh, none; I know that—I know that.” 
“哦，没什么好处。我知道那一点——我知道那一点。”
"And did she believe you had destroyed them?" 
“那她相信你把它们毁掉了吗？”
"I don't know what she believed at the last. I couldn't tell—she was too far gone.” 
“我不知道她最终有没有相信。我说不出来——她就快死了。”
"Then if there was no promise and no assurance I can't see what ties you.” 
“那么，如果既没有承诺，也没有保证不能给我看，是什么束缚了你。”
"Oh, she hated it so—she hated it so! She was so jealous. But here's the portrait—you may have that," Miss Tita announced, taking the little picture, wrapped up in the same manner in which her aunt had wrapped it, out of her pocket.
“啊，她痛恨那样——她痛恨那样！她太猜忌了。但是画像在这里——你可以拿这个。”蒂塔小姐宣布道，拿出那个小图片，像她姑妈那样把它包着，从口袋里拿出来。
"I may have it—do you mean you give it to me?" I questioned, staring, as it passed into my hand.
“我可以拿这个——你是说你把它给我？”当它被递到我手上的时候，我盯着她问道。
"Oh, yes." 
“哦，是的。”
"But it's worth money—a large sum.” 
“但是，这个很值钱——一大笔钱。”
"Well!" said Miss Tita, still with her strange look.
“是的！”蒂塔小姐说，表情仍很奇怪。
I did not know what to make of it, for it could scarcely mean that she wanted to bargain like her aunt. She spoke as if she wished to make me a present. "I can't take it from you as a gift," I said, "and yet I can't afford to pay you for it according to the ideas Miss Bordereau had of its value. She rated it at a thousand pounds.” 
我不知道怎么看待这个问题，因为这几乎不可能是表示，她想像她姑妈那样和我做笔交易。她说得像是想把它作为礼物送给我一样。“我不能把它当礼物拿走。”我说，“而且，依据博尔德罗小姐给它定的价，我负担不起。她给它定了一千英镑。”
"Couldn't we sell it?" asked Miss Tita.
“我们不能卖了它吗？”蒂塔小姐说。
"God forbid! I prefer the picture to the money." 
“天理不容啊！我宁可要画，不要钱。”
"Well then keep it." 
“那你留着它吧。”
"You are very generous." 
“你真慷慨。”
"So are you." 
“你也是。”
"I don't know why you should think so," I replied; and this was a truthful speech, for the singular creature appeared to have some very fine reference in her mind, which I did not in the least seize.
“我不知道你为什么会这样想。”我回答道。这话说得情真意切，因为这个人看起来心里有个很好的证据，而我根本不知道。
"Well, you have made a great difference for me," said Miss Tita.
“啊，你对我来说很重要。”蒂塔小姐说。
I looked at Jeffrey Aspern's face in the little picture, partly in order not to look at that of my interlocutress, which had begun to trouble me, even to frighten me a little—it was so self-conscious, so unnatural.
我看着这张小小图片中杰弗里·阿斯本的脸，一部分是因为，这样就不必看和我谈话的这个女人的脸，那张脸已经开始困扰我，甚至让我有点儿害怕了——它是那么不自在，那么不自然。
I made no answer to this last declaration; I only privately consulted Jeffrey Aspern's delightful eyes with my own (they were so young and brilliant, and yet so wise, so full of vision); I asked him what on earth was the matter with Miss Tita.
对于她最后说的这个话，我没有回应。我只是私下和杰弗里·阿斯本明亮的眼睛交汇（那双眼睛是如此年轻，如此明亮，却是如此睿智，充满远见）。我问他蒂塔小姐到底是出了什么事情。
He seemed to smile at me with friendly mockery, as if he were amused at my case. I had got into a pickle for him—as if he needed it! He was unsatisfactory, for the only moment since I had known him.
他似乎微笑地看着我，带着一种友善的嘲弄，仿佛他也觉得这个情况很好玩。我为了他，陷入了两难的境地——就像他需要这样似的！这是我认识他以来，他唯一的一次令人不满意。
Nevertheless, now that I held the little picture in my hand I felt that it would be a precious possession. "Is this a bribe to make me give up the papers?" I demanded in a moment, perversely. "Much as I value it, if I were to be obliged to choose, the papers are what I should prefer. Ah, but ever so much!" 
然而，既然我手里握着这个小画像，我就觉得自己拥有个珍贵的物件。“这是在收买我，让我放弃文稿吗？”片刻后，我固执地问道，“就我自己估量，如果我必须要选择的话，我更愿意要文稿。啊，我太想要文稿了！”
"How can you choose—how can you choose?" Miss Tita asked, slowly, lamentably.
“你怎么能选择——你怎么能选择？”蒂塔小姐缓慢地问道，语气哀伤。
"I see! Of course there is nothing to be said, if you regard the interdiction that rests upon you as quite insurmountable. In this case it must seem to you that to part with them would be an impiety of the worst kind, a simple sacrilege!" 
“我知道了！如果你把你身上的这条禁令看作是不可逾越的，那当然就没什么可说的了。这样的话，你一定认为，交出文稿就是最糟糕的亵渎，简直是悖理逆天的行为！”
Miss Tita shook her head, full of her dolefulness. "You would understand if you had known her. I'm afraid," she quavered suddenly—”I'm afraid! She was terrible when she was angry.” 
蒂塔小姐摇摇头，满是哀伤。“如果你了解她的话，你会理解的。我害怕，”她突然颤抖地说——“我害怕！她生气的时候是很恐怖的。”
"Yes, I saw something of that, that night. She was terrible. Then I saw her eyes. Lord, they were fine!" 
“是的，那天晚上，我见识了那种恐怖。她很可怕。然后我看见了她的眼睛。天哪，它们真犀利呀！”
"I see them—they stare at me in the dark!" said Miss Tita.
“我看见它们了——它们在黑暗中注视着我！”蒂塔小姐说。
"You are nervous, with all you have been through." 
“经历了这么多事情，你很紧张。”
"Oh, yes, very—very!” 
“哦，是的，非常——非常！”
"You mustn't mind; that will pass away," I said, kindly. Then I added, resignedly, for it really seemed to me that I must accept the situation, "Well, so it is, and it can't be helped. I must renounce.” 
“你不用介意。它们会过去的。”我友善地说。看来我必须要接受这个情形了，于是我无可奈何地说：“好，就这样吧。事情不能回转。我必须放弃了。”
Miss Tita, at this, looking at me, gave a low, soft moan, and I went on: "I only wish to heaven she had destroyed them; then there would be nothing more to say. And I can't understand why, with her ideas, she didn't.” 
听我这样说，蒂塔小姐看着我，发出了一声低软的呻吟。我接着说：“我只想祈求老天，让她把它们都毁掉，这样就没有什么好说的啦。我真不明白，她这样有主意的人，居然没有这样做。”
"Oh, she lived on them!" said Miss Tita.
“啊，她就靠它们而活！”蒂塔小姐说。
"You can imagine whether that makes me want less to see them," I answered, smiling. "But don't let me stand here as if I had it in my soul to tempt you to do anything base. Naturally you will understand if I give up my rooms. I leave Venice immediately.” And I took up my hat, which I had placed on a chair. We were still there rather awkwardly, on our feet, in the middle of the sala. She had left the door of the apartments open behind her but she had not led me that way.
“你可以想象，这会不会让我更不想看到它们，”我笑着回答，“但是，别让我站在这里，就好像骨子里想引诱你做什么龌龊的事情似的。如果我空出房间来，你自然就会明白。我马上离开威尼斯。”于是我拿起挂在椅子上的帽子。我们很尴尬地站在那里，在正厅中间。她身后的房间门敞开着，但她也没有引我往那里走。
A kind of spasm came into her face as she saw me take my hat. "Immediately—do you mean today?” The tone of the words was tragical—they were a cry of desolation.
她看见我拿起帽子的时候，脸上有点儿痉挛。“马上——你是说今天？”这句话的语气是悲伤的——它们是一种哀号。
"Oh, no; not so long as I can be of the least service to you." 
“哦，不。只要还能为你尽最后一点儿力，我都不急着走。”
"Well, just a day or two more—just two or three days," she panted. Then controlling herself, she added in another manner, "She wanted to say something to me—the last day—something very particular, but she couldn't.” 
“那，就多一两天吧——就两三天。”她喘着气。然后，她控制住自己，换了另一种方式接着说：“她想告诉我些事——最后那一天——一些非常特别的事，但是她没能够。”
"Something very particular?" 
“非常特别的事？”
"Something more about the papers." 
“更多关于文稿的事。”
"And did you guess—have you any idea?” 
“那你猜出来了吗——你有什么想法吗？”
"No, I have thought—but I don't know. I have thought all kinds of things.” 
“没有，我想过——但我不知道。我想过各种各样的事情。”
"And for instance?" 
“举个例来说？”
"Well, that if you were a relation it would be different." 
“嗯，如果你变成了一位亲人的话，那就不一样了。”
"If I were a relation?" 
“如果我是亲人？”
"If you were not a stranger. Then it would be the same for you as for me. Anything that is mine—would be yours, and you could do what you like. I couldn't prevent you—and you would have no responsibility.” 
“如果你不是个外人。那么，对你就和对我是一样的。所有我的东西——会是你的，你想怎么做都行。我不能阻止你——你也不用负什么责任。”
She brought out this droll explanation with a little nervous rush, as if she were speaking words she had got by heart. They gave me an impression of subtlety and at first I failed to follow.
她有点儿紧张地飞快讲出这闹剧般的解释，就像是在背诵一样。这给我一种精明的印象，一开始，我没能听懂。
But after a moment her face helped me to see further, and then a light came into my mind. It was embarrassing, and I bent my head over Jeffrey Aspern's portrait. What an odd expression was in his face! "Get out of it as you can, my dear fellow!" I put the picture into the pocket of my coat and said to Miss Tita, "Yes, I'll sell it for you. I shan't get a thousand pounds by any means, but I shall get something good.” 
但是过了一会儿，她的脸色让我又清楚了一些，然后突然心里就明白了。这真尴尬。我低头看着杰弗里·阿斯本的画像。他脸上的表情多诡异啊！“尽力脱身，我亲爱的朋友！”我把画像放到我外套的口袋里，对蒂塔小姐说：“好的，我会替你卖了它。我一定卖不到一千英镑，但是，我能卖个好价钱。”
She looked at me with tears in her eyes, but she seemed to try to smile as she remarked, "We can divide the money." 
她双眼含泪看着我，但是她说话的时候似乎努力想微笑：“钱我们可以分。”
"No, no, it shall be all yours." Then I went on, "I think I know what your poor aunt wanted to say. She wanted to give directions that her papers should be buried with her." 
“不，不，那全是你的，”然后我接着说，“我想，我知道你那可怜的姑妈想说什么。她想要求那些文稿和她一起被埋起来。”
Miss Tita appeared to consider this suggestion for a moment; after which she declared, with striking decision, "Oh no, she wouldn't have thought that safe!” 
蒂塔小姐看起来考虑了一下这个建议。然后她坚定地回答：“哦，不，她会觉得那样不安全！”
"It seems to me nothing could be safer." 
“在我看来，没什么比这更安全。”
"She had an idea that when people want to publish they are capable—” And she paused, blushing.
“她认为，如果人们想要出版，他们能——”她停下来，脸红了。
"Of violating a tomb? Mercy on us, what must she have thought of me!" 
“能盗墓？我的天哪，她都是怎么看待我的啊！”
"She was not just, she was not generous!" Miss Tita cried with sudden passion.
“她不公正，她也不大度！”蒂塔小姐突然激动地叫道。
The light that had come into my mind a moment before increased. "Ah, don't say that, for we ARE a dreadful race.” Then I pursued, "If she left a will, that may give you some idea." 
刚刚我心里闪现的观点变得越来越明白了。“哦，别那样说，因为我们的确是一群糟糕的人。”然后我继续说道，“如果她留下了什么遗嘱，你可能就会有主意了。”
"I have found nothing of the sort—she destroyed it. She was very fond of me," Miss Tita added incongruously. "She wanted me to be happy. And if any person should be kind to me—she wanted to speak of that.” 
“我没找到那类东西——她毁掉了。她很喜欢我。”蒂塔小姐很突兀地说。“她希望我幸福。如果有人对我很体贴——她想要讲的就是这件事。”
I was almost awestricken at the astuteness with which the good lady found herself inspired, transparent astuteness as it was and sewn, as the phrase is, with white thread. "Depend upon it she didn't want to make any provision that would be agreeable to me.” 
我对她的机智几乎充满敬畏。她被激发出的这点儿机智明显得，就像俗话说的那样，让人一眼看穿。“照这样看，她不会想做出任何让我称心如意的安排。”
"No, not to you but to me. She knew I should like it if you could carry out your idea. Not because she cared for you but because she did think of me," Miss Tita went on with her unexpected, persuasive volubility. "You could see them—you could use them.” 
“不，不是称你的心，而是合我心意。她知道，如果你能落实你的计划，我会很高兴的。不是因为她喜欢你，而是因为她关心我。”蒂塔小姐继续用她令人意外的，让人信服的方式流利地说着。“你可以看它们——你可以使用它们。”
She stopped, seeing that I perceived the sense of that conditional—stopped long enough for me to give some sign which I did not give.
她停顿一下，看看我是不是能理解到这个条件意味着什么——停了足够久，好让我做出我尚未做出的某种表示。
She must have been conscious, however, that though my face showed the greatest embarrassment that was ever painted on a human countenance it was not set as a stone, it was also full of compassion.
然而，她一定意识到我的脸上，尽管表现出人类容貌所能表达的最为难的表情，但它毕竟不像石头，也充满了同情。
It was a comfort to me a long time afterward to consider that she could not have seen in me the smallest symptom of disrespect.
很久之后，我认为她没有从我的表情里看到一点点不恭敬的态度，我感到很安慰。
"I don't know what to do; I'm too tormented, I'm too ashamed!" she continued with vehemence.
“我不知道做什么。我备受折磨，我太羞愧了！”她很激烈地继续说。
Then turning away from me and burying her face in her hands she burst into a flood of tears. If she did not know what to do it may be imagined whether I did any better. I stood there dumb, watching her while her sobs resounded in the great empty hall.
然后她把头转向一边，把脸埋在手里，突然泪流成河。你可以想象，如果她不知道怎么办，我也不比她更清楚。我麻木地站在那里，看着她啜泣，哭声在空旷的大厅里回响。
In a moment she was facing me again, with her streaming eyes. "I would give you everything—and she would understand, where she is—she would forgive me!” 
过了一会儿，她用泪汪汪的眼睛再次看着我。“我可以给你所有的东西——而她会理解的，在她安息的地方——她会原谅我的！”
"Ah, Miss Tita—ah, Miss Tita," I stammered, for all reply. I did not know what to do, as I say, but at a venture I made a wild, vague movement in consequence of which I found myself at the door.
“哦，蒂塔小姐——哦，蒂塔小姐。”我只能结结巴巴地回答。正如我所说，我不知道该做什么，但是我胡乱地做了个率性而下意识的动作，结果就发现自己站在了门口。
I remember standing there and saying, "It wouldn't do—it wouldn't do!"pensively, awkwardly, grotesquely, while I looked away to the opposite end of the sala as if there were a beautiful view there.
我记得自己站在那里，说：“这不行——这不行！”语气焦虑，笨拙又古怪。而同时，我转眼看着正厅的另一面，就像那里有美丽的风景一样。
The next thing I remember is that I was downstairs and out of the house. My gondola was there and my gondolier, reclining on the cushions, sprang up as soon as he saw me. I jumped in and to his usual "Dove commanda?" I replied, in a tone that made him stare, "Anywhere, anywhere; out into the lagoon!" 
我记得，接下来我就下了楼，出了门。我的平底小船和船夫都在那里，他靠在垫子上，一看见我就跳了起来。我跳上船，他照旧问我：“去哪里？”我回答的语气让他傻了眼：“随便，随便，去泻湖！”
He rowed me away and I sat there prostrate, groaning softly to myself, with my hat pulled over my face. What in the name of the preposterous did she mean if she did not mean to offer me her hand? That was the price—that was the price! And did she think I wanted it, poor deluded, infatuated, extravagant lady?
他划船载着我，我沮丧地坐着，拿帽子遮住了脸，轻声地对自己叹息。真荒谬，如果不是想嫁给我，那她是什么意思？那就是代价——那就是代价！难道她以为我愿意吗？这个可怜的，误会我的，痴迷的，又放肆的小姐。
My gondolier, behind me, must have seen my ears red as I wondered, sitting there under the fluttering tenda, with my hidden face, noticing nothing as we passed—wondered whether her delusion, her infatuation had been my own reckless work.
我的船夫在我身后，他肯定看到我耳朵都红了。那时我坐在飞舞的帘子下面，遮着脸，我们路过的地方我什么也没有注意到——想知道她的错觉，她对我的迷恋是不是因为我轻率的话。
Did she think I had made love to her, even to get the papers? I had not, I had not; I repeated that over to myself for an hour, for two hours, till I was wearied if not convinced. I don't know where my gondolier took me; we floated aimlessly about in the lagoon, with slow, rare strokes.
她是不是以为，我为了得到文稿，竟然肯向她示爱？我没有，我没有。我一遍一遍对自己这样说了一个小时，两个小时，直到我就算没被说服，也感到累了为止。我不知道船夫会怎么看我。我们漫无目的地在泻湖上漂着，慢慢地，懒懒地划着船。
At last I became conscious that we were near the Lido, far up, on the right hand, as you turn your back to Venice, and I made him put me ashore. I wanted to walk, to move, to shed some of my bewilderment.
最后，我意识到，我们都快到利多岛了，背对威尼斯的话，它在右手边的远处。我叫他让我上岸。我想走一走，动一动，摆脱掉一点儿我的困惑。
I crossed the narrow strip and got to the sea beach—I took my way toward Malamocco. But presently I flung myself down again on the warm sand, in the breeze, on the coarse dry grass. It took it out of me to think I had been so much at fault, that I had unwittingly but nonetheless deplorably trifled.
我穿过了一条窄窄的地方，来到海滩上——我朝着马拉莫科走去。但是不久，我又一次敞开地躺倒在温暖的沙滩上，在微风里，躺在粗糙的干草上。我精疲力竭地想到自己犯了多大的错，虽是无意，却也可悲地愚弄了人家的感情。
But I had not given her cause—distinctly I had not. I had said to Mrs. Prest that I would make love to her; but it had been a joke without consequences and I had never said it to Tita Bordereau.
我没有给她理由——我肯定没有。我是对普雷斯顿夫人说过，我可能会向蒂塔小姐求爱，但那只是个没有付诸行动的笑话，而我从来没有对蒂塔·博尔德罗本人说过。
I had been as kind as possible, because I really liked her; but since when had that become a crime where a woman of such an age and such an appearance was concerned?
我对她尽可能地温柔，因为我真的喜欢她。但是，从什么时候开始，关心一个这种年纪，如此长相的女人，变成了一种罪过？
I am far from remembering clearly the succession of events and feelings during this long day of confusion, which I spent entirely in wandering about, without going home, until late at night; it only comes back to me that there were moments when I pacified my conscience and others when I lashed it into pain.
我简直记不清楚那漫长而混乱的一天中接连发生的事情，我也不清楚自己的感受是什么。我整天四处游荡，没有回家，直到晚上很晚。我只记得，有时候，我安慰自己的良知；其他时候，我又痛斥它。
I did not laugh all day—that I do recollect; the case, however it might have struck others, seemed to me so little amusing.
我整天没有笑——我记得我没有笑。那件事，不管多么能打动其他人，似乎没什么令我开心的。
It would have been better perhaps for me to feel the comic side of it. At any rate, whether I had given cause or not it went without saying that I could not pay the price. I could not accept. I could not, for a bundle of tattered papers, marry a ridiculous, pathetic, provincial old woman.
要是我能领会到这件事情滑稽的一面，我也许会好受一些。无论如何，不管我有没有给她理由，我都毫无疑问地不会买账。我不能接受。我不能，为了一堆破烂文稿，娶一个可笑可怜又土气的老女人。
It was a proof that she did not think the idea would come to me, her having determined to suggest it herself in that practical, argumentative, heroic way, in which the timidity however had been so much more striking than the boldness that her reasons appeared to come first and her feelings afterward.
很明显，她不认为我会有这样的想法：她决定用一种实际、善辩而勇敢的方式告诉我。她的这种方式，羞怯比大胆要明显得多，看起来，理性先行，她的感情是其后的。
As the day went on I grew to wish that I had never heard of Aspern's relics, and I cursed the extravagant curiosity that had put John Cumnor on the scent of them.
随着那一天慢慢消磨，我开始希望自己从来没有听说过阿斯本的遗物。我诅咒那过分的好奇心，它让约翰·卡姆诺嗅到了文稿的气味。
We had more than enough material without them, and my predicament was the just punishment of that most fatal of human follies, our not having known when to stop.
没有这些文稿，我们的材料也够多的了。而我的困境，是人类最致命的荒诞行为应得的惩罚，我们不懂何时收手。
It was very well to say it was no predicament, that the way out was simple, that I had only to leave Venice by the first train in the morning, after writing a note to Miss Tita, to be placed in her hand as soon as I got clear of the house; for it was a strong sign that I was embarrassed that when I tried to make up the note in my mind in advance (I would put it on paper as soon as I got home, before going to bed), I could not think of anything but "How can I thank you for the rare confidence you have placed in me?" 
要说这不是个困境，也是合理的，因为解救的方法很简单。那就是，我只需要坐明天早上第一班火车离开威尼斯。之前给蒂塔小姐写个便条，我一整理好房间就马上放到她手里。因为当我提前下定决心打算写这个便条，就强烈地表明我尴尬的感觉（我可以一回家马上写下来，然后再睡觉），除了“我该如何感谢你对我的信任？”之外，我想不出别的可写。
That would never do; it sounded exactly as if an acceptance were to follow.
这肯定不行。因为这话听起来简直像接下来就会接受她的求婚似的。
Of course I might go away without writing a word, but that would be brutal and my idea was still to exclude brutal solutions.
我当然可以一走了之，不留下半句话，但是这样太残忍了，而我仍然摒弃残忍的解决方案。
As my confusion cooled I was lost in wonder at the importance I had attached to Miss Bordereau's crumpled scraps; the thought of them became odious to me, and I was as vexed with the old witch for the superstition that had prevented her from destroying them as I was with myself for having already spent more money than I could afford in attempting to control their fate.
当我的混乱冷却下来，我迷失在猜测中。我在想，我曾多么看重博尔德罗小姐那堆皱巴巴的文稿啊。现在想到它们都让我觉得可憎。我对这个老巫婆的执迷很恼怒，因为这让她不能毁掉文稿；我对自己同样很生气，因为我花了负担不起的钱，试图操纵文稿的命运。
I forget what I did, where I went after leaving the Lido and at what hour or with what recovery of composure I made my way back to my boat.
我忘记了自己在离开利多岛之后，做了什么，去了哪里，在什么时间，在风度恢复到何种程度的情况下，才回头往船上走。
I only know that in the afternoon, when the air was aglow with the sunset, I was standing before the church of Saints John and Paul and looking up at the small square-jawed face of Bartolommeo Colleoni, the terrible condottiere who sits so sturdily astride of his huge bronze horse, on the high pedestal on which Venetian gratitude maintains him.
我只知道，下午的时候，当夕阳映红了天空，我站在圣约翰与圣保罗教堂，仰望巴托洛米奥·科里奥尼那张长着方下巴的小脸。这个可怕的名将稳坐他那巨大的青铜大马，高居基座之上，那威尼斯人用感激之情托起的基座。
The statue is incomparable, the finest of all mounted figures, unless that of Marcus Aurelius, who rides benignant before the Roman Capitol, be finer: but I was not thinking of that; I only found myself staring at the triumphant captain as if he had an oracle on his lips.
这座雕像无可匹敌，是所有骑马的雕像中最好的，除非罗马朱庇特神殿前那骑着马、宽厚仁慈的马库斯·奥里利厄斯的雕像是比它更好的佳作：但我想的不是这点，我只是发现自己站着，盯着这位胜利的将军，就像他嘴上说出了什么圣言似的。
The western light shines into all his grimness at that hour and makes it wonderfully personal.
那时，西下的阳光照亮了他冷峻的轮廓，让雕像栩栩如生。
But he continued to look far over my head, at the red immersion of another day—he had seen so many go down into the lagoon through the centuries—and if he were thinking of battles and stratagems they were of a different quality from any I had to tell him of.
但是他仍旧越过我的头，看得很远，看着又一天浸没在一片绯红之中——经过了好几百年，他很多次看到斜阳沉入泻湖——就算他在想着战争和谋略，在性质上，也和我想告诉他的谋略相去甚远。
He could not direct me what to do, gaze up at him as I might.
我可以凝望着他，他却不能指导我该怎么做。
Was it before this or after that I wandered about for an hour in the small canals, to the continued stupefaction of my gondolier, who had never seen me so restless and yet so void of a purpose and could extract from me no order but "Go anywhere—everywhere—all over the place"?
也不知道是在此之前还是之后，我在小运河里徘徊了一个小时。这让我的船夫仍然很惊讶，他从来没有看见我这样不安，这样漫无目的，也没有给他任何安排，只是说：“随便哪里——每个地方——绕着这个地方就行。”
He reminded me that I had not lunched and expressed therefore respectfully the hope that I would dine earlier.
他提醒我说，我还没有吃午饭，因此恭敬地表明，他希望我能早点儿吃晚饭。
He had had long periods of leisure during the day, when I had left the boat and rambled, so that I was not obliged to consider him, and I told him that that day, for a change, I would touch no meat.
白天，当我离开小船，四处漫游，他有大把时间休息，所以我没有义务考虑他，于是我告诉他，那一天，换换口味，我不沾肉。
It was an effect of poor Miss Tita's proposal, not altogether auspicious, that I had quite lost my appetite.
这都是可怜的蒂塔小姐的求婚导致的。一点儿也不幸运，搞得我胃口全无。
I don't know why it happened that on this occasion I was more than ever struck with that queer air of sociability, of cousinship and family life, which makes up half the expression of Venice.
我不知道怎么会发生这样的情况，对于这表现了威尼斯一半特色的，怪异的交际方式，姑侄关系，和家庭生活，我比以往任何时候都觉得惊奇。
Without streets and vehicles, the uproar of wheels, the brutality of horses, and with its little winding ways where people crowd together, where voices sound as in the corridors of a house, where the human step circulates as if it skirted the angles of furniture and shoes never wear out, the place has the character of an immense collective apartment, in which Piazza San Marco is the most ornamented corner and palaces and churches, for the rest, play the part of great divans of repose, tables of entertainment, expanses of decoration.
这里没有街道，没有车辆，没有呼啸而过的车轮，没有粗野的马匹，蜿蜒的小道挤满了人。这里的声音听起来像是在房间的走道上，人们来来回回地走着，就像在绕着家具的各个角落一样走着，鞋子永远磨不破。这地方有着巨大的集体公寓的特征，在这里，圣马克广场是装点得最隆重的地方，而剩下的那些宅邸和教堂成了大型休息室，休闲桌成了宽敞的点缀物。
And somehow the splendid common domicile, familiar, domestic, and resonant, also resembles a theater, with actors clicking over bridges and, in straggling processions, tripping along fondamentas.
不知怎么的，壮观的公共住区，亲近，居家，又怀旧，也像是歌剧院一样，演员们在桥上发出咔哒声，散漫地沿着桥基行进着。
As you sit in your gondola the footways that in certain parts edge the canals assume to the eye the importance of a stage, meeting it at the same angle, and the Venetian figures, moving to and fro against the battered scenery of their little houses of comedy, strike you as members of an endless dramatic troupe.
人行道某些部分顺着运河伸展开来，当你坐在平底小船里面，船和路角度一致的时候，这些部分看起来像舞台一样重要。这些威尼斯人，来来往往于他们戏剧般的小房子构成的破败景象之中，看起来让人觉得他们是一群没完没了的话剧表演队的成员。
I went to bed that night very tired, without being able to compose a letter to Miss Tita. Was this failure the reason why I became conscious the next morning as soon as I awoke of a determination to see the poor lady again the first moment she would receive me?
我那天晚上极其疲惫地睡了，没有能够给蒂塔小姐写一封信。我没写信。难道因为这样，导致我第二天早上一醒来就决定要马上再见一次这位可怜的小姐，一旦她愿意见我？
That had something to do with it, but what had still more was the fact that during my sleep a very odd revulsion had taken place in my spirit.
那算是一部分原因，但是最重要的原因是，在我睡觉的时候，我的想法发生了巨大的变化。
I found myself aware of this almost as soon as I opened my eyes; it made me jump out of my bed with the movement of a man who remembers that he has left the house door ajar or a candle burning under a shelf. Was I still in time to save my goods?
我一睁开眼睛就意识到这一点。这让我从床上一跃而起，那动作就像一个人想起来自己忘了关房门，或是把蜡烛放在架子下面燃着忘了灭一样。我还有没有时间拯救我的宝贝？
That question was in my heart; for what had now come to pass was that in the unconscious cerebration of sleep I had swung back to a passionate appreciation of Miss Bordereau's papers. They were now more precious than ever, and a kind of ferocity had come into my desire to possess them.
这个问题在我心里纠结，因为现在重要的事情是，在我睡觉的时候，我无意识的思考让我又重新回到对博尔德罗小姐的文稿的热切欣赏上来。它们现在比什么东西都要珍贵，一种强烈的欲望让我想要拥有它们。
The condition Miss Tita had attached to the possession of them no longer appeared an obstacle worth thinking of, and for an hour, that morning, my repentant imagination brushed it aside.
蒂塔小姐开出的占有它们的条件，看起来完全是不值得考虑的障碍。那天早上，花了一个小时，我反悔的想法把这个障碍排除到一边了。
It was absurd that I should be able to invent nothing; absurd to renounce so easily and turn away helpless from the idea that the only way to get hold of the papers was to unite myself to her for life. I would not unite myself and yet I would have them.
真荒唐，我竟然什么也说不出来；真荒唐，我居然这么快就放弃了，还绝望地否定了这个主意，以为我唯一能得到这些文稿的方法，就是让我自己和她共同生活。我不会和她结合，而且我要得到文稿。
I must add that by the time I sent down to ask if she would see me I had invented no alternative, though to do so I had had all the time that I was dressing. This failure was humiliating, yet what could the alternative be?
我必须要再说一句，在我派人下楼去请问她愿不愿意见我的时候，我还没有想好对策，虽然我把全部穿衣服的时间都用来思考这个问题。这个失误是很丢人的，那有什么解决的办法呢？
Miss Tita sent back word that I might come; and as I descended the stairs and crossed the sala to her door—this time she received me in her aunt's forlorn parlor—I hoped she would not think my errand was to tell her I accepted her hand. She certainly would have made the day before the reflection that I declined it.
蒂塔小姐回话说我可以下去，而当我下了楼梯，穿过正厅来到她的门前——这一次，她是在她姑妈废弃的会客室见我的——我希望她认为我这次来是为了告诉她，我接受她。她当然理解我前一天的反应是对她的拒绝。
As soon as I came into the room I saw that she had drawn this inference, but I also saw something which had not been in my forecast. Poor Miss Tita's sense of her failure had produced an extraordinary alteration in her, but I had been too full of my literary concupiscence to think of that.
我一进房间，就知道她有这样的推论，但是我也看到了我先前没有预料到的事情。可怜的蒂塔小姐，她的失败感极大地改变了她，但是我太过专注于自己的文学邪念而没有考虑过那一点。
Now I perceived it; I can scarcely tell how it startled me. She stood in the middle of the room with a face of mildness bent upon me, and her look of forgiveness, of absolution, made her angelic.
现在我发觉了。我简直说不出它有多么令我震惊。她站在屋子中间，一张温和的脸低头看着我。她原谅的神情，宽恕的样子，让她看起来像个天使。
It beautified her; she was younger; she was not a ridiculous old woman.
那让她变美了，年轻了。她不是一个荒谬可笑的老女人了。
This optical trick gave her a sort of phantasmagoric brightness, and while I was still the victim of it I heard a whisper somewhere in the depths of my conscience: "Why not, after all—why not?” 
这种视觉上的幻像让她有了一种幻影般的光辉，尽管自己仍然是个受害者，我还是听到我意识深处的某个地方一阵悄声低语：“为什么不呢，既然如此——为什么不？”
It seemed to me I was ready to pay the price. Still more distinctly however than the whisper I heard Miss Tita's own voice. I was so struck with the different effect she made upon me that at first I was not clearly aware of what she was saying; then I perceived she had bade me goodbye—she said something about hoping I should be very happy.
看起来，我已经准备要付出这个代价了。我听到了蒂塔小姐本人的声音，这个声音可比我意识中的悄悄话要明显得多了。她给我的感觉如此不同，我惊讶得一开始根本没有意识到她在说什么。然后我发现她在和我道别——她说希望我非常幸福之类的话。
"Goodbye—goodbye?" I repeated with an inflection interrogative and probably foolish.
“再见——再见？”我用询问和也许很愚蠢的语气重复她的话。
I saw she did not feel the interrogation, she only heard the words; she had strung herself up to accepting our separation and they fell upon her ear as a proof.
我发现她并没有感觉到询问的语气，她只听见了这些词语。她费了很大的力气来接受我们的分裂，她用耳朵来证明这分离。
"Are you going today?" she asked. "But it doesn't matter, for whenever you go I shall not see you again. I don't want to.” And she smiled strangely, with an infinite gentleness. She had never doubted that I had left her the day before in horror. How could she, since I had not come back before night to contradict, even as a simple form, such an idea? And now she had the force of soul—Miss Tita with force of soul was a new conception—to smile at me in her humiliation.
“你打算今天走？”她问道，“但是这没关系了，因为不论你什么时候走，我都不会再见你了。我不想再见你。”然后她古怪地笑着，无限温柔。她从来没有怀疑我昨天是极其厌恶地离开她的。既然我没有在晚上之前回来，做个简单的表示，她怎么会否认这个想法呢？现在，她有了魄力——蒂塔小姐有了魄力是个新的概念——在她的屈辱中对我微笑。
"What shall you do—where shall you go?" I asked.
“你会做什么——你会去哪里？”我问。
"Oh, I don't know. I have done the great thing. I have destroyed the papers.” 
“啊，我不知道。我做了重要的事情。我把文稿毁了。”
"Destroyed them?" I faltered.
“毁了它们？”我声音颤抖。
"Yes; what was I to keep them for? I burned them last night, one by one, in the kitchen." 
“是的，我留着它们有什么用？我昨天晚上把它们烧了，一点儿一点儿地烧了，在厨房里。”
"One by one?" I repeated, mechanically.
“一点儿一点儿地？”我机械地重复道。
"It took a long time—there were so many.” The room seemed to go round me as she said this, and a real darkness for a moment descended upon my eyes.
“花了很长时间——有好多文稿。”在她讲着这些话的时候，房间似乎开始在围着我旋转，一时间，一片黑暗真真实实地降临我的眼前。
When it passed Miss Tita was there still, but the transfiguration was over and she had changed back to a plain, dingy, elderly person.
黑暗过去，蒂塔小姐还在那里，但是她的“变身”结束了，她又变回了一个平凡邋遢的中年人。
It was in this character she spoke as she said, "I can't stay with you longer, I can't;" and it was in this character that she turned her back upon me, as I had turned mine upon her twenty-four hours before, and moved to the door of her room.
她这样的人说话就有这样的特征，她说：“我再也受不了你了，我受不了。”她一边说着这些话，一边转身背对着我，就像我二十四小时以前，转身对着她，离开她的房间一样。
Here she did what I had not done when I quitted her—she paused long enough to give me one look. I have never forgotten it and I sometimes still suffer from it, though it was not resentful.
在这里，她做了我当时离开她的时候没做的事情——她停留足够久之后，看了我一眼。我永远也忘不了那一眼，虽然它不带恨意，但我有时还是会受它影响。
No, there was no resentment, nothing hard or vindictive in poor Miss Tita; for when, later, I sent her in exchange for the portrait of Jeffrey Aspern a larger sum of money than I had hoped to be able to gather for her, writing to her that I had sold the picture, she kept it with thanks; she never sent it back.
不，蒂塔小姐没有恨意，没有刁难，没有报复心理。因为，后来，当我寄了一大笔钱给她作为交换杰弗里·阿斯本画像的代价，那笔钱比我原本希望能为她筹得的要多，并写信告诉她我把画卖了的时候，她感激地收下了，没有退回来。
I wrote to her that I had sold the picture, but I admitted to Mrs. Prest, at the time (I met her in London, in the autumn), that it hangs above my writing table.
我写信告诉她说画像卖了，但是我对普雷斯顿夫人坦白地说，那个时候（我和她秋天的时候在伦敦见了面），画像挂在我写字台的上方。
When I look at it my chagrin at the loss of the letters becomes almost intolerable.
当我看着它的时候，我还是感觉到失去文稿的那份几乎难以抑制的懊恼。
