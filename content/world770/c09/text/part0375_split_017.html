XVIII  

第十八章  

Among the persons assembled on the 18th of August at twelve o'clock on the platform at the railway station was Litvinov. Not long before, he had seen Irina: she was sitting in an open carriage with her husband and another gentleman, somewhat elderly. She caught sight of Litvinov, and he perceived that some obscure emotion flitted over her eyes; but at once she hid herself from him with her parasol.
八月十八日中午十二点，李特维诺夫站在人潮涌动的火车站月台上。不久前，他还看见了伊琳娜：她和她的丈夫坐在一辆敞篷马车上，旁边还坐着一个上了年纪的先生。她看到了李特维诺夫，他也发现了她眼里闪过某种隐晦的情感，但是她马上就用阳伞遮住了自己，不让他看见。
A strange transformation had taken place in him since the previous day—in his whole appearance, his movements, the expression of his face; and indeed he felt himself a different man. His self-confidence had vanished, and his peace of mind had vanished, too, and his respect for himself; of his former spiritual condition nothing was left. Recent ineffaceable impressions obscured all the rest from him. Some sensation unknown before had come, strong, sweet—and evil; the mysterious guest had made its way to the innermost shrine and taken possession and lain down in it, in silence, but in all its magnitude, like the owner in a new house. Litvinov was no longer ashamed, he was afraid; at the same time a desperate hardihood had sprung up in him; the captured, the vanquished know well this mixture of opposing feelings; the thief, too, knows something of it after his first robbery. Litvinov had been vanquished, vanquished suddenly... and what had become of his honesty?  The train was a few minutes late. Litvinov's suspense passed into agonizing torture; he could not stop still in one place, and, pale all over, moved about jostling in the crowd. "My God," he thought, "if I only had another twenty-four hours."... The first look at Tanya, the first look of Tanya... that was what filled him with terror... that was what he had to live through directly... And afterwards? Afterwards... come, what may come!... He now made no more resolutions, he could not answer for himself now. His phrase of yesterday flashed painfully through his head… And this was how he was meeting Tanya… A prolonged whistle sounded at last, a heavy momentarily increasing rumble was heard, and, slowly rolling around a bend in the line, the train came into sight. The crowd hurried to meet it, and Litvinov followed it, dragging his feet like a condemned man. Faces, ladies' hats began to appear out of the carriages, at one window a white handkerchief gleamed… Kapitolina Markovna was waving to him.... It was over; she had caught sight of Litvinov and he recognized her. The train stood still; Litvinov rushed to the carriage door, and opened it; Tatyana was standing near her aunt, smiling brightly and holding out her hand.
自前一天起，李特维诺夫发生了一种奇怪的变化——他的整个面貌、他的举止、他的面部表情都发生了变化；说真的，他觉得自己是另外一个人了。他的自信心消失了，平和的心态也不见了，连他的自尊心都不在了。他以前的精神面貌荡然无存。最近形成的那些永生难忘的印象给他的一切蒙上了一层阴影。出现了一种前所未有的情感，是那样强烈、幸福——而邪恶。那位诡秘的客人潜入了心里最深处的圣地，并占领了那里，躺在里面。虽然悄无声息，但却又极其堂而皇之，俨然新房的主人。李特维诺夫不再感到羞愧，而是害怕起来；同时，一种视死如归的胆识在他胸中涌动。凡是俘虏、被征服者都很了解这种对立、复杂的情感，盗贼第一次盗窃后也会明白这种感受。李特维诺夫被征服了，就这样突然间被征服了……然而他的诚实去哪里了呢？火车晚来了几分钟。李特维诺夫的踌躇犹豫现在已经转变成痛苦的折磨，他无法平静地站在原地，整张脸苍白，在人群里挤来挤去。“我的天哪。”他想，“如果我还能有一天一夜的时间那该多好。”……他看塔尼娅的第一眼，塔尼娅看他的第一眼……他满心恐惧的就是这个……他不得不克服的就是这个……那以后呢？以后……以后随他去吧！现在他再也无法作出决定了。他都无法对自己负责了。昨天说过的话闪过他的脑海，令他痛苦……他就是以这种状态去见塔尼娅的……火车持续的汽笛声终于呼啸而来，接着，瞬间听到了越来越响亮的、沉闷的轰隆声，火车沿着铁轨缓缓地蜿蜒而至，进入人们的视野。人群忙涌上前去迎接，李特维诺夫也跟在人群后面，像定了罪的囚犯一样拖着两条腿。只见一张张脸和女士的帽子开始从车厢里往外探，一条白色的手绢闪现在其中一个窗口……卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜正在向他挥动着手绢……完了；她已经看见了李特维诺夫，他也认出了她。火车停稳了；李特维诺夫冲到火车车厢门口，打开了车门。塔尼娅站在她的姑妈旁边，灿烂地笑着，并向他伸出了手。
He helped them both to get out, uttered a few words of welcome, unfinished and confused, and at once bustled about, began taking their tickets, their traveling bags, and rugs, ran to find a porter, called a fly; other people were bustling around them. He was glad of their presence, their fuss, and loud talk. Tatyana moved a little aside, and, still smiling, waited calmly for his hurried arrangements to be concluded. Kapitolina Markovna, on the other hand, could not keep still; she could not believe that she was at last at Baden.
他把她们两位扶下车，断断续续、迷迷糊糊地说了几句欢迎的话，然后马上就开始忙碌起来：帮她们拿票、旅行包和毛毯，跑去找搬运工，并且还叫了马车。四周的人们也在忙碌着。有他们的在场，有他们的忙乱和高声谈话，他感到很高兴。塔吉娅娜往边上走了一点，仍旧带着微笑，平静地等待他完成那些仓促的安排。另一方面，卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜却无法平静下来，她仍不能相信自己终于到了巴登。
She suddenly cried, "But the parasols? Tanya, where are our parasols?" all unconscious that she was holding them fast under her arm; then she began taking a loud and prolonged farewell of another lady with whom she had made friends on the journey from Heidelberg to Baden. This lady was no other than our old friend Madame Suhantchikov. She had gone away to Heidelberg to do obeisance to Gubaryov, and was returning with "instructions." Kapitolina Markovna wore a rather peculiar striped mantle and a round traveling hat of a mushroom-shape, from under which her short white hair fell in disorder; short and thin, she was flushed with traveling and kept talking Russian in a shrill and penetrating voice... She was an object of attention at once.
她忽然惊呼道：“阳伞呢？塔尼娅，我们的阳伞呢？”她完全没有意识到伞正被她紧紧地夹在腋下。接着她开始高声和另外一个女士久久地告别。她们是在从海德堡来巴登旅途中结识的。这位女士不是别人，正是我们的老朋友苏汉契科娃女士。她刚去了海德堡拜访古巴廖夫，并且带回来一些“指示”。卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜披着十分怪异的条纹披肩，还戴着一顶蘑菇型的圆旅行帽，帽子下面灰白的短发十分凌乱。瘦矮的她因为旅途奔波，脸上显得一阵潮红。她一直用刺耳而又具有穿透力的声音讲着俄语……她马上就成为了人们注意的焦点。
Litvinov at last put her and Tatyana into a fly, and placed himself opposite them. The horses started. Then followed questionings, renewed handshaking, interchanging of smiles and welcomes… Litvinov breathed freely; the first moment had passed off satisfactorily. Nothing in him, apparently, had struck or bewildered Tanya; she was smiling just as brightly and confidently, she was blushing as charmingly, and laughing as goodnaturedly. He brought himself at last to take a look at her; not a stealthy cursory glance, but a direct steady look at her, hitherto his own eyes had refused to obey him. His heart throbbed with involuntary emotion: the serene expression of that honest, candid face gave him a pang of bitter reproach. "So you are here, poor girl," he thought, "you whom I have so longed for, so urged to come, with whom I had hoped to spend my life to the end, you have come, you believed in me... while I... while I...." Litvinov's head sank; but Kapitolina Markovna gave him no time for musing; she was pelting him with questions.
李特维诺夫最后将她和塔吉娅娜送上马车，自己则坐在她们对面。马车出发了。接下来他们开始嘘寒问暖，再次握手、相互微笑、表示欢迎……李特维诺夫松了口气。最初的时刻已经令人满意地过去了。显然，他身上并没有什么东西令塔尼娅吃惊或困惑，她笑得依旧那么灿烂自信，她脸红起来依旧那么迷人，她大笑时还是那么亲切。他最终决定还是让自己看她一眼，但并不是偷偷的匆匆一瞥，而是直接的长时间凝视，可是他的眼睛却不听他的使唤。他的心因这种无意识的情感跳动着而疼痛：那诚实坦率、平和宁静的面容使他感到一阵痛苦的斥责。“您终于来了，可怜的女孩。”他想，“我本来是多么盼着您来，多么迫切地想见到您，希望与您共度余生啊。现在您来了，您信任我……而我……而我却……”李特维诺夫低下头来，但是卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜可不给时间让他思考，不停地问他问题。
"What is that building with columns? Where is it the gambling's done? Who is that coming along? Tanya, Tanya, look, what crinolines! And who can that be? I suppose they are mostly French creatures from Paris here? Mercy, what a hat! Can you get everything here just as in Paris? But, I expect, everything's awfully dear, eh? Ah, I've made the acquaintance of such a splendid, intellectual woman! You know her, Grigory Mihalitch; she told me she had met you at some Russian's, who's a wonderfully intellectual person, too. She promised to come and see us. How she does abuse all these aristocrats—it's simply superb! What is that gentleman with gray moustaches? The Prussian king? Tanya, Tanya, look, that's the Prussian king. No? not the Prussian king, the Dutch ambassador, did you say? I can't hear, the wheels rattle so. Ah, what exquisite trees!” 
“那个带圆柱子的建筑是什么啊？人们在哪赌博啊？那个走过来的人是啊？塔尼娅，塔尼娅，快看，多漂亮的裙子啊！那个人会是谁呢?我想他们多半是来自巴黎的法国人吧？谢谢（法语）！多么漂亮的帽子啊！这里也同巴黎一样，能买到所有的东西吗?但是我想，这里的东西都非常贵吧，嗯？啊，我居然认识这样一位漂亮聪明的女人！您认识她的，格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇。她告诉我她在某个也非常聪明的俄国人家里见过您。她承诺会过来看我们。她把那些贵族们骂得可惨啦——真是骂得太好了！那个留着花白胡子的先生是谁?是普鲁士国王吗？塔尼娅！塔尼娅！快看！那是普鲁士国王啊！不是？那不是普鲁士国王？您说那是荷兰大使？我听不见您在说什么，车轮声太响了。啊！多么漂亮的树啊！”
"Yes, exquisite, aunt," Tanya assented, "and how green everything is here, how bright and gay! Isn't it, Grigory Mihalitch?" 
“是的,很漂亮，姑妈。”塔尼娅赞同地说，“这里一切都是那样葱翠，那样光鲜亮丽、赏心悦目！是吧，格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇？”
"Oh, very bright and gay"... he answered through his teeth. The carriage stopped at last before the hotel. Litvinov conducted the two travelers to the room taken for them, promised to come back within an hour, and went to his own room. Directly he entered it, he fell again under the spell which had been lulled for a while. Here, in that room, since the day before, Irina reigned supreme; everything was eloquent of her, the very air seemed to have kept secret traces of her visit… Again Litvinov felt himself her slave. He drew out her handkerchief, hidden in his bosom, pressed it to his lips, and burning memories flowed in subtle poison through his veins. He realized that there was no turning back, no choosing now; the sorrowful emotion aroused in him by Tatyana melted away like snow in the fire, and remorse died down... died down so completely that his uneasiness even was soothed, and the possibility—present to his intellect—of hypocrisy no longer revolted him.... Love, Irina's love, that was now his truth, his bond, his conscience… The sensible Litvinov did not even ponder how to get out of a position, the horror and hideousness of which he bore lightly, as if it did not concern him.
“哦，是很光鲜亮丽、赏心悦目……”他从牙缝里挤出这句话。马车最终在旅馆前停了下来。李特维诺夫把她们带到为她们订好的房间里，承诺一个小时后回来找他们，接着就去自己的房间了。他刚一进房间，平静了一会儿的那种感觉再一次迷住了他。在这里，就是这个房间里，从前一天起，伊琳娜就占据着至高无上的地位。房间里的一切都好似在拥戴着她，连屋子里的空气都似乎保留着她来访的隐秘痕迹……李特维诺夫又一次觉得自己成了她的奴仆。他取出藏在胸口的伊琳娜的手绢，将它紧紧地贴在双唇上。灼热的回忆带着不易察觉的毒药在他的血管里流动。他意识到现在已经没有退路了，也没得选择了。由塔吉娅娜引起的悲伤情绪在他身体里就像烈火中的雪一般融化了，悔恨也逐渐消失……消失地如此彻底以至于就连他的不安也得以缓和，在他脑海里闪过的其他任何可能的虚伪也不再令他厌恶了……爱，伊琳娜的爱，现在那便是他的真理，他的约定，他的良心……理智的李特维诺夫甚至从未想过如何摆脱这种处境，这种他稍稍有点厌倦的可怕处境，好像事不关己似的。
The hour had not yet passed when a waiter came to Litvinov from the newly arrived ladies; they begged him to come to them in the public drawing-room. He followed the messenger, and found them already dressed and in their hats; They both expressed a desire to go out at once to see Baden, as the weather was so fine. Kapitolina Markovna, especially, seemed burning with impatience; she was quite cast down when she heard that the hour of the fashionable promenade before the Konversation Hall had not yet arrived. Litvinov gave her his arm, and the ceremony of sight-seeing began. Tatyana walked beside her aunt, looking about her with quiet interest; Kapitolina Markovna pursued her inquiries. The sight of the roulette, the dignified croupiers, whom—had she met them in any other place—she would certainly have taken for ministers, the quickly moving scoops, the heaps of gold and silver on the green cloth, the old women gambling, and the painted cocottes reduced Kapitolina Markovna to a sort of speechless stupor; she altogether forgot that she ought to feel moral indignation, and could only gaze and gaze, giving a start of surprise at every new sight.... The whiz of the ivory ball into the bottom of the roulette thrilled her to the marrow of her bones, and it was only when she was again in the open air that, drawing a long breath, she recovered energy enough to denounce games of chance as an immoral invention of aristocracy. A fixed, unpleasant smile had made its appearance on Litvinov's lips; he had spoken abruptly and lazily, as though he were annoyed or bored… But now he turned round towards Tatyana, and was thrown into secret confusion; she was looking attentively at him, with an expression as though she were asking herself what sort of an impression was being made on her. He made haste to nod his head to her, she responded with the same gesture, and again looked at him questioningly, with a sort of strained effort, as though he were standing much farther off than he really was. Litvinov led his ladies away from the Konversation Hall, and passing the "Russian tree," under which two Russian ladies were already sitting, he went towards Lichtenthaler Alice. He had hardly entered the avenue when he saw Irina in the distance.
还未到达约定时间，新来的两位女士就派了一位侍者过来找李特维诺夫，她们请求他去公共会客厅找她们。他跟着这位报信的侍者来到大厅，发现她们已经换好了衣服，戴好了帽子。看样子她们都很想立马出去看一看巴登，因为天气非常好。尤其是卡托里娜·马尔科夫娜，一副迫不及待的样子。听说晤谈厅前的时髦舞会还未到时间举行，她感到十分扫兴。李特维诺夫向她伸出手，观光仪式就这样开始了。塔吉娅娜走在她姑妈旁边，安静而饶有兴致地看着周围的景色，卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜还是问个没完没了。赌场的轮盘赌具，还有那些威严的赌场工作人员——如果是在别处见到他们的话———她肯定会把他们当成政府部长，飞速运转的小铲子，翠绿色桌布上成堆的金币和银币，参赌的老妇人以及抹着浓妆的妓女，这一切都使得卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜目瞪口呆，一阵恍惚。她完全忘记了自己应该感到愤慨，只是一个劲地睁大眼睛看着，每看到一个新场景就大吃一惊……在轮转赌具底部嗖嗖响着的象牙骰子吓得她缩成一团，重新到了户外之后才长长地吸了口气。她又恢复了力气，足以抨击这种靠运气的赌博游戏是贵族们不道德的发明。此时李特维诺夫嘴角浮现了一丝僵硬不悦的微笑，说话唐突慵懒，似乎有点恼怒或厌烦……但这时候，他转向了塔吉娅娜，陷入了莫名的困惑中；她正专注地看着他，脸上的表情好像是在问自己给他留下了怎样的印象。见此状，他急忙向她点头，她也点头回应，但还是以一种询问的表情地看着他，还带着一丝紧张，似乎他站得比实际离她要更远一些。李特维诺夫将她们从晤谈厅带出来，经过“俄国树”，树下已经坐着两位俄国姑娘，然后朝李赫顿泰勒林阴道走去。他刚走进这个林阴道，就远远地看见了伊琳娜。
She was walking towards him with her husband and Potugin. Litvinov turned white as a sheet; he did not slacken his pace, however, and when he was on a level with her, he made a bow without speaking. She too bowed to him, politely, but coldly, and taking in Tatyana in a rapid glance, she glided by... Ratmirov lifted his hat high, Potugin muttered something.
她和她的丈夫以及波图金正向他走过来。李特维诺夫顿时脸色惨白，但是他并没有放慢脚步。走到她跟前的时候，他默默地向她鞠了个躬。她也很礼貌但冷冷地向他鞠了个躬，匆匆地瞟了一眼塔吉娅娜后就迅速走开了……拉特米罗夫高高地举起了帽子，波图金正在喃喃说着什么。
"Who is that lady?" Tatyana asked suddenly. Till that instant she had hardly opened her lips.
“那位女士是谁？”塔吉娅娜忽然问。在这之前她都没怎么张过嘴。
"That lady?" repeated Litvinov, "that lady? That is a Madame Ratmirov." 
“那位女士？”李特维诺夫重复了她的话，“那位女士，是吗？那是拉特米罗夫夫人。”
"Is she Russian?" 
“她是俄国人吗？”
"Yes."  
“是的。”
"Did you make her acquaintance here?" 
“您是在这里认识她的吗？”
"No; I have known her a long while." 
“不是的，我认识她很久了。”
"How beautiful she is!" "Did you notice her dress?" put in Kapitolina Markovna. "Ten families might live for a whole year on the cost of her lace alone. Was that her husband with her?" she inquired turning to Litvinov.
“她真漂亮！”“您注意到她的裙子了吗？”卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜插话道，“仅那些花边就抵得上十个家庭一年的生活费了。和她在一起的是她的丈夫吗？” 她转身问李特维诺夫。
"Yes." 
“是的。”
"He must be awfully rich, I suppose?" 
“我想，他肯定非常有钱吧？”
"Really I don't know; I don't think so." 
“说真的，我不知道，但我并不认为他很有钱。”
"What is his rank?" 
“他是什么官衔？”
"He's a general." 
“他是一位将军。”
"What eyes she has!" said Tatyana, "and what a strange expression in them: pensive and penetrating at the same time… I have never seen such eyes." 
“她的眼睛可真漂亮！”塔吉娅娜说道，“他们的表情好奇怪啊：既忧郁同时又很敏锐……我从未见过这样的眼睛。”
Litvinov made no answer; he fancied that he felt again Tatyana's questioning glance bent on his face, but he was wrong, she was looking at her own feet, at the sand of the path. "Mercy on us! Who is that fright?" cried Kapitolina Markovna suddenly, pointing to a low, jaunting-car in which a red-haired pug-nosed woman lay lolling impudently, in an extraordinarily gorgeous costume and lilac stockings.
李特维诺夫没有回答，他想塔吉娅娜应该又在疑惑地盯着他的脸，但是他想错了，她只是低着头看着自己的脚，看着路上的沙石。“我的老天啊！那个丑女人是谁啊？”卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜突然大叫道，她指向一辆低矮的双轮马车：一位红头发，塌鼻子的女人，衣着格外艳丽，还穿着一双紫色的袜子，懒洋洋、无所顾忌地躺在车上。
"That fright! why, that's the celebrated Ma'mselle Cora." 
“丑女人！啊，她是赫赫有名的科拉小姐。”
"Who?" 
“您说谁？”
"Ma'mselle Cora... a Parisian... notoriety." 
“科拉小姐……她是巴黎人……是个名人。”
"What? That pug? Why, but she's hideous!” 
“什么？这只哈巴狗吗？不会吧，她好丑啊！”
"It seems that's no hindrance." 
“看来这并不能妨碍她什么。”
Kapitolina Markovna could only lift her hands in astonishment.
卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜只能诧异地摊开手。
"Well, this Baden of yours!" she brought out at last. "Can one sit down on a seat here? I'm rather tired."  
“好吧，你们巴登可真是怪！”她最后说了句，“我能在这里的位子上坐坐吗？我很累了。”
"Of course you can, Kapitolina Markovna… That's what the seats are put here for." 
“当然可以，卡比托里娜·马尔科夫……椅子放在这里就是让人坐的啊。”
"Well, really, there's no knowing! But there in Paris, I'm told, there are seats, too, along the boulevards; but it's not proper to sit on them." 
“嗯，真的，谁知道啊！但是在巴黎，别人告诉我街边也有椅子，但是坐在那些椅子上不怎么体面。”
Litvinov made no reply to Kapitolina Markovna; only at that moment he realized that two paces away was the very spot where he had had that explanation with Irina, which had decided everything. Then he recalled that he had noticed a small rosy spot on her cheek to-day….
李特维诺夫并没有回应卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜。只是，就在这时，他意识到两步开外就是那个他向伊琳娜作过解释，决定了一切的地方。于是他又回想起，他注意到今天伊琳娜的脸上泛起了小小的一片红晕……
Kapitolina Markovna sank down on to the seat, Tatyana sat down beside her. Litvinov remained on the path; between Tatyana and him—or was it only his fancy?—something seemed to have happened ... unconsciously and gradually.
卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜坐了那椅子上，塔吉娅娜坐在她旁边。李特维诺夫仍旧站在路上。在他与塔吉娅娜之间，有什么事情似乎已经不知不觉地逐渐发生……或者，这只是他的幻想吗？
"Ah, she's a wretch, a perfect wretch!” Kapitolina Markovna declared, shaking her head commiseratingly; "why, with the price of her get-up, you could keep not ten, but a hundred families. Did you see under her hat, on her red hair, there were diamonds? Upon my word, diamonds in the day-time!” 
“哎呀！她可真是个小丑，十足的小丑！”卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜大声说，一边还同情地摇了摇头，“哎，真是的！她的一身衣服所值的钱能养活的可不仅仅是十个家庭，而是一百个家庭。你们注意到她帽子下红头发上的珠宝了吗？我的意思是，大白天的还戴着那么多的宝石！”
"Her hair's not red," remarked Litvinov; "she dyes it red—that's the fashion now." 
“她的头发本来不是红色的。”李特维诺夫说道，“她把它染红了——现在流行这个。”
Again Kapitolina Markovna could only lift her hands; she was positively dumbfounded. "Well," she said at last, "where we were, in Dresden, things had not got to such a scandalous pitch, yet. It's a little further from Paris, anyway, that's why. Don't you think that's it, Grigory Mihalitch, eh?" 
卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜还是只能举起手，完全目瞪口呆了。“好吧，” 她终于说道，“在我们德累斯顿，倒还不至于有这样荒唐至极的事情。无论怎样，这里离巴黎还是要更远些。您不认为这样吗，格里高利·米哈伊维奇？”
"Don't I think so?" answered Litvinov. While he thought to himself, "What on earth is she talking of?" "I? Of course... of course..." 
“我吗？”李特维诺夫答道。但是他心里却想着，“她到底在谈论什么呢？”“我？我当然也这样认为了……当然了……”
But at this point the sound of slow footsteps was heard, and Potugin approached the seat. "Good-morning, Grigory Mihalitch," he began, smiling and nodding.
但正在此时，缓缓的脚步声又响传来了，波图金正向着椅子走来。“您好！格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇。”他开始说道，并微笑着点点头。
Litvinov grasped him by the hand at once.
李特维诺夫马上就抓住了他的手。
"Good-morning, good-morning, Sozont Ivanitch. I fancy I passed you just now with ... just now in the avenue?" 
“您好，您好！索松特·伊凡内奇！我想我们刚刚碰过面……就刚刚在林阴道上？”
"Yes, it was me." 
“是的，那是我。”
Potugin bowed respectfully to the ladies sitting on the seat.
波图金很恭敬地向坐在凳子上的两位女士鞠了一躬。
"Let me introduce you, Sozont Ivanitch. Old friends and relatives of mine, who have only just arrived in Baden. Potugin, Sozont Ivanitch, a countryman of ours, also staying in Baden." 
“我来向您介绍一下，索松特·伊凡内奇。她们是我的老朋友和亲戚，刚来巴登。波图金·索松特·伊凡内奇，我们的老乡，也住在巴登。”
Both ladies rose a little. Potugin renewed his bows. "It's quite a levée here," Kapitolina Markovna began in a delicate voice; the kind-hearted old lady was easily intimidated, but she tried before all to keep up her dignity. "Every one regards it as an agreeable duty to stay here." 
两位女士都稍稍起身。波图金再次向她们鞠躬行礼。“这里的名流可真不少啊。”卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜细声说道。这位好心的老太太本是非常容易受惊的，但是在大家面前她努力维持她高贵的形象。“大家把呆在这里看成是一种令人愉快的职责。”
"Baden is an agreeable place, certainly," answered Potugin, with a sidelong look at Tatyana; "a very agreeable place, Baden." 
“当然了，巴登确实是个令人愉快的地方。”波图金回答说道，同时瞟了瞟塔吉娅娜，“巴登的确是个很令人愉快的地方。”
"Yes; but it's really too aristocratic, so far as I can form an opinion. You see we have been staying all this time in Dresden... a very interesting town; but here there's positively a levée." 
“是的，但是这里的贵族气息太浓了，至少我是这么认为。您知道，我们一直都是呆在德累斯顿……那是个有趣的小镇，但是这里确实是名流荟萃。
"She's pleased with the word," thought Potugin. "You are perfectly right in that observation," he said aloud; "but then the scenery here is exquisite, and the site of the place is something one cannot often find. Your fellow-traveler, especially, is sure to appreciate that. Are you not, madam?" he added, addressing himself this time directly to Tatyana.
“她很喜欢说‘名流’这个词。”波图金想。“您说得对极了。”他大声说道，“但这里的景色很美丽，这种地方可不好找啊。尤其是您的同伴，肯定会欣赏这一点的。是吗，夫人？”他又说道，这一次他直视着塔吉娅娜。
Tatyana raised her large, clear eyes to Potugin. It seemed as though she were perplexed. What was wanted of her, and why had Litvinov introduced her, on the first day of her arrival, to this unknown man, who had, though, a kind and clever face, and was looking at her with cordial and friendly eyes.
塔吉娅娜抬起明亮清澈的大眼睛看着波图金。她似乎显得有些困惑。他想要她做什么呢，为什么李特维诺夫在她们来的第一天就把她介绍给这个陌生人？虽然他有着一张亲切、睿智的脸，看她的眼神也很热情友好。
"Yes," she said at last, "it's very nice here." 
“是的。”她最终说道，“这里非常漂亮。”
"You ought to visit the old castle," Potugin went on; "I especially advise a drive to—" "The Saxon Switzerland—" Kapitolina Markovna was beginning. The blare of wind instruments floated up the avenue; it was the Prussian military band from Rastadt (in 1862 Rastadt was still an allied fortress), beginning its weekly concert in the pavilion. Kapitolina Markovna got up. "The music!" she said; "the music à la Conversation!... We must go there. It's four o'clock now... isn't it? Will the fashionable world be there now?" "Yes," answered Potugin: "this is the most fashionable time, and the music is excellent." 
“您应该去参观古堡。”波图金继续说道，“我要特别向您推荐，驾车去——”“撒克逊时期瑞士的那些——”卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜开始说了起来。此时林阴道里响起了声势浩大的管弦乐声。乐声来自拉施塔特（1862年拉施塔特还是联邦的一个城堡)的普鲁士军队。他们在帐篷里举行的每周一次的音乐会开始了。卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜站了起来。“音乐！”她说道，“晤谈厅的音乐！……我们得去那里看看。现在四点钟了……不是吗？那里现在应该是一个时尚界了吧？”“是的。”波图金回答说，“现在是最时尚的时刻了，而且音乐非常棒。”
"Well, then, don't let us linger. Tanya, come along." 
“嗯，那我们就别再磨蹭了。塔尼娅，我们走吧。”
"You allow me to accompany you?" asked Potugin, to Litvinov's considerable astonishment; it was not possible for it even to enter his head that Irina had sent Potugin.
“我可以陪你们一快去吗？”波图金问。李特维诺夫相当惊讶，他根本不可能想到是伊琳娜让波图金过来的。
Kapitolina Markovna simpered.
卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜傻笑着。
"With the greatest pleasure—M'sieu... M'sieu —" 
“万分荣幸——先生……先生——”
"Potugin," he murmured, and he offered her his arm.
“波图金。”他轻声提示道，并向她伸出一只手。
Litvinov gave his to Tatyana, and both couples walked towards the Konversation Hall.
李特维诺夫也挽住塔吉娅娜。这两对男女一同向晤谈厅走去。
Potugin went on talking with Kapitolina Markovna. But Litvinov walked without uttering a word; yet twice, without any cause, he smiled, and faintly pressed Tatyana's arm against his. There was a falsehood in those demonstrations, to which she made no response, and Litvinov was conscious of the lie. They did not express a mutual confidence in the close union of two souls given up to one another; they were a temporary substitute—for words which he could not find. That unspoken something which was beginning between them grew and gained strength. Once more Tatyana looked attentively, almost intently, at him.
波图金继续和卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜聊着天。但是李特维诺夫一言不发地走着，但是无缘无故地笑了两次，并且用手臂将塔吉娅娜的手微微夹紧。对这种虚假的举动，她也没作出什么反应，而李特维诺夫也意识到自己的虚伪。这个举动并没有表现出两个灵魂彼此心心相印的相互信任，它只是李特维诺夫一时语塞时的临时替代品而已。他们之间那种无声的隔阂，开始渐渐滋长并加强了。塔吉娅娜又一次仔细地近乎全神贯注地看着他。
It was the same before the Konversation Hall at the little table round which they all four seated themselves, with this sole difference, that, in the noisy bustle of the crowd, the clash and roar of the music, Litvinov's silence seemed more comprehensible. Kapitolina Markovna became quite excited; Potugin hardly had time to answer her questions, to satisfy her curiosity. Luckily for him, there suddenly appeared, in the mass of moving figures the lank person and everlastingly leaping eyes of Madame Suhantchikov. Kapitolina Markovna at once recognized her, invited her to their table, made her sit down, and a hurricane of words arose. Potugin turned to Tatyana, and began a conversation with her in a soft, subdued voice, his face bent slightly down towards her with a very friendly expression; and she, to her own surprise, answered him easily and freely; she was glad to talk with this stranger, this outsider, while Litvinov sat immovable as before, with the same fixed and unpleasant smile on his lips.
他们四个坐在晤谈大厅前的小圆桌时情形也一模一样，唯一的不同就是这里有熙熙攘攘的人群，以及嘈杂喧闹的音乐，所以李特维诺夫的沉默似乎更加容易理解了。卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜变得十分兴奋。波图金几乎来不及回答她的问题来满足她的好奇心了。幸好，在涌动的人流中，出现了身材瘦小的苏汉契科娃女士和她老是转来转去的眼睛。卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜，一下就认出了她，邀请她坐到他们的桌子旁边，然后开始口若悬河了。波图金转过身来开始与塔吉娅娜聊起天来，他压低着声音，很温柔，脸微微地凑向她，表情十分友好，而塔吉娅娜也是轻松自如地回应着他（这一点连她自己都觉得惊讶）。她很乐意跟这个陌生人、外人谈话，而李特维诺夫则如先前一样坐在那里一动也不动，唇边还是带着那种一成不变的不悦的笑容。
Dinner-time came at last. The music ceased, the crowd thinned. Kapitolina Markovna parted from Madame Suhantchikov on the warmest terms. She had conceived an immense respect for her, though she did say afterwards to her niece, that "this person is really too severe; but then she does know everything and everybody; and we must really get sewing-machines directly the wedding festivities are over." Potugin took leave of them; Litvinov conducted his ladies home. As they were going into the hotel, he was handed a note; he moved aside and hurriedly tore open the envelope. On a tiny scrap of vellum paper were the following words, scribbled in pencil: "Come to me this evening at seven, for one minute, I entreat you.—Irina." Litvinov thrust the note into his pocket, and, turning round, put on his smile again... to whom? why? Tatyana was standing with her back to him. They dined at the common table of the hotel. Litvinov was sitting between Kapitolina Markovna and Tatyana, and he began talking, telling anecdotes and pouring out wine for himself and the ladies, with a strange, sudden joviality. He conducted himself in such a free and easy manner, that a French infantry officer from Strasbourg, sitting opposite, with a beard and moustaches à laNapoleon III., thought it admissible to join in the conversation, and even wound up by a toast à la santé des belles Moscovites! After dinner, Litvinov escorted the two ladies to their room, and after standing a little while at the window with a scowl on his face, he suddenly announced that he had to go out for a short time on business, but would be back without fail by the evening. Tatyana said nothing; she turned pale and dropped her eyes. Kapitolina Markovna was in the habit of taking a nap after dinner; Tatyana was well aware that Litvinov knew of this habit of her aunt's; she had expected him to take advantage of it, to remain with her, for he had not been alone with her, nor spoken frankly to her, since her arrival. And now he was going out! What was she to make of it? And, indeed, his whole behavior all along… Litvinov withdrew hurriedly, not waiting for remonstrances; Kapitolina Markovna lay down on the sofa, and with one or two sighs and groans, fell into a serene sleep; while Tatyana moved away into a corner, and sat down in a low chair, folding her arms tightly across her bosom.
午餐时间终于到了。音乐停止了，人群也渐渐散去。卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜十分热烈地和苏汉契科娃女士道别。她对苏汉契科娃怀着极大的敬意，虽然后来她的确跟她的侄女说“苏汉契科娃这个人真的太严肃了，但她也确实无所不知、无人不识。我们真的必须在婚礼过后马上买一台缝纫机。”波图金告辞了；李特维诺夫也两位女士带回去了。他们一进旅馆，就有人递给了李特维诺夫一封信，他走到一边，匆忙地撕开信封。在一小片牛皮纸上，用铅笔潦草地写着以下这些话：“今晚七点来我这里，就呆一分钟，我求您了。 ——伊琳娜。”李特维诺夫将纸条塞进口袋，转身，又笑了……他在对谁笑？为何而笑？塔吉娅娜背对着他站在那里。他们在旅馆的公共餐桌上吃了午餐。李特维诺夫坐在卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜和塔吉娅娜之间，令人感到奇怪的是，他忽然来了兴致，开始聊起天来，一边说着奇闻轶事，一边为自己和身边的两位女士倒酒。他是那样地轻松自如，就连坐在他对面的那位来自斯特拉斯堡、留着拿破仑三世.式小胡子的法国步兵军官都认为可以加入他们的谈话了，甚至还举杯祝两位莫斯科美女身体安康！吃完饭，李特维诺夫陪两位女士回房后，在窗边愁容满面地站了一会儿，忽然宣布说自己有事要出去一会儿，但晚上一定会回来。塔吉娅娜什么也没有说，脸色变得有点苍白，低下了头。卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜习惯在午后小憩一会儿，塔吉娅娜很清楚李特维诺夫知道姑妈的这个习惯，她本来期待着他会好好利用这个时机，留下来陪她，因为自从她到这里来，他就一直没有和她单独呆过，也没有向她袒露过心事。而现在他竟然要出去！她怎么能够明白他这样的举动呢？而且，一路上他的行为实在是……李特维诺夫没等对方表示抗议就匆匆离开了。卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜躺在沙发上，叹了叹气，呻吟了几下就安详地睡着了。塔吉娅娜走到一个角落，在一张低矮的椅子上坐了下来，手臂紧紧地交叉抱在胸前。
