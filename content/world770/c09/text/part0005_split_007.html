VIII  

八  

As it turned out the precaution had not been needed, for three hours later, just as I had finished my dinner, Miss Bordereau's niece appeared, unannounced, in the open doorway of the room in which my simple repasts were served.
结果，给她预示根本是没有必要的，因为三个小时后，我刚刚吃完晚饭，博尔德罗小姐的侄女就现身了。她没有预先通知我，就出现在我平时简单用餐的房间那敞开的房门口。
I remember well that I felt no surprise at seeing her; which is not a proof that I did not believe in her timidity.
我记得很清楚，看到她时，我一点儿也不惊讶，这不是说我就相信她为人不羞怯。
It was immense, but in a case in which there was a particular reason for boldness it never would have prevented her from running up to my rooms.
她是个很害羞的人，但是如果有什么特别的原因需要她很大胆，她也绝对不会因为害羞就不跑上我的房间来。
I saw that she was now quite full of a particular reason; it threw her forward—made her seize me, as I rose to meet her, by the arm.
我看她这时有某个足够充分的原因。这使得她横冲直撞——让她在我起身迎接她的时候，抓住了我的手臂。
"My aunt is very ill; I think she is dying!" 
“我姑妈病得很重。我想她快死了！”
"Never in the world," I answered bitterly. "Don't you be afraid!” 
“绝不可能，”我痛苦地说，“你别怕！”
"Do go for a doctor—do, do! Olimpia is gone for the one we always have, but she doesn't come back; I don't know what has happened to her. I told her that if he was not at home she was to follow him where he had gone; but apparently she is following him all over Venice. I don't know what to do—she looks so as if she were sinking.” 
“请务必找个医生——务必，务必！欧林匹亚已经去找我们常找的那个医生去了，但是她还没有回来，我不知道她到底出什么事情了。我告诉她，如果医生不在家，那她要跟到他去的地方，但是很明显，她正在满威尼斯地寻找他。我不知道怎么办——她看起来快不行了。”
"May I see her, may I judge?" I asked.
“我能看看她吗？让我诊断一下行吗？”我问。
"Of course I shall be delighted to bring someone; but hadn't we better send my man instead, so that I may stay with you?” 
“当然，我会很乐意能带个医生去见她，但是，我们最好让仆人去做这事，这样我就可以呆在你身边，行吗？”
Miss Tita assented to this and I dispatched my servant for the best doctor in the neighborhood. I hurried downstairs with her, and on the way she told me that an hour after I quitted them in the afternoon Miss Bordereau had had an attack of "oppression," a terrible difficulty in breathing.
蒂塔小姐表示同意，我就派了仆人去请附近最好的医生。我和她急冲冲地下了楼。在路上，她告诉我，下午，在我离开一个小时以后，博尔德罗小姐感觉“憋气”，呼吸很困难。
This had subsided but had left her so exhausted that she did not come up: she seemed all gone.
症状虽然减轻了，却让她很疲惫，她恢复不过来了：她看起来已经完全失去知觉了。
I repeated that she was not gone, that she would not go yet; whereupon Miss Tita gave me a sharper sidelong glance than she had ever directed at me and said, "Really, what do you mean? I suppose you don't accuse her of making believe!” I forget what reply I made to this, but I grant that in my heart I thought the old woman capable of any weird maneuver.
我又说了一遍，她没有去世，她还不会去世。此时蒂塔小姐从眼角犀利地看了我一眼，她从来没有这样有针对性地对待我，她说：“真的？你什么意思？我想，你不是在说她是假装的吧！”我忘了我是怎么回答她的，但是我心里觉得这个老太太什么怪事都做得出来。
Miss Tita wanted to know what I had done to her; her aunt had told her that I had made her so angry. I declared I had done nothing—I had been exceedingly careful; to which my companion rejoined that Miss Bordereau had assured her she had had a scene with me—a scene that had upset her.
蒂塔小姐想知道，我到底对她做了什么。她姑妈告诉她，我让她很生气。我澄清说，我什么也没有做——我一直非常小心。而我的同伴则告诉我，说博尔德罗小姐确定地告诉她，她和我有点儿争吵——那争吵让她非常生气。
I answered with some resentment that it was a scene of her own making—that I couldn't think what she was angry with me for unless for not seeing my way to give a thousand pounds for the portrait of Jeffrey Aspern. "And did she show you that?
我愤愤地说，那个争吵也是她编的——除了我不知趣，没有花一千磅买下杰弗里·阿斯本的画像外，我想不出她有什么事情要和我生气的。“她把那个给你看了？
Oh, gracious—oh, deary me!" groaned Miss Tita, who appeared to feel that the situation was passing out of her control and that the elements of her fate were thickening around her.
噢，天哪——哦，我的天哪！”蒂塔小姐呻吟道，看起来，她觉得情形渐渐失控，决定她命运的那些因素正变得越来越复杂了。
I said that I would give anything to possess it, yet that I had not a thousand pounds; but I stopped when we came to the door of Miss Bordereau's room.
我回答她说，我为了得到它，什么都愿意付出，但是我就是没有那一千英镑。但是，当我们来到博尔德罗小姐房门的时候，我停了下来。
I had an immense curiosity to pass it, but I thought it my duty to represent to Miss Tita that if I made the invalid angry she ought perhaps to be spared the sight of me. "The sight of you? Do you think she can SEE?" my companion demanded almost with indignation.
我很好奇，很想进去，但我认为我有义务向蒂塔小姐表明，如果我让博尔德罗小姐无谓地生气，那她应该不想见我。“看见你？你认为她看得见东西？”我的同伴说道，几乎是带着一种愤怒的口吻。
I did think so but forbore to say it, and I softly followed my conductress.
我的确是这样想的，但忍住了没说出来，我只是顺从地跟着给我带路的女人。
I remember that what I said to her as I stood for a moment beside the old woman's bed was, "Does she never show you her eyes then? Have you never seen them?" 
我还记得，当我在老太太床前站了一会儿时，我对她说的话是：“那她从来没有给你看她的眼睛？你从没见过她的眼睛吗？”
Miss Bordereau had been divested of her green shade, but (it was not my fortune to behold Juliana in her nightcap) the upper half of her face was covered by the fall of a piece of dingy lacelike muslin, a sort of extemporized hood which, wound round her head, descended to the end of her nose, leaving nothing visible but her white withered cheeks and puckered mouth, closed tightly and, as it were consciously. Miss Tita gave me a glance of surprise, evidently not seeing a reason for my impatience. "You mean that she always wears something? She does it to preserve them." 
博尔德罗小姐的绿罩子已经被摘去了，但是（看见朱莉安娜戴着睡帽的样子绝对不是我的福气）她脸的上半部分盖着落下来的一块邋遢的薄棉布，像蕾丝一样的布，看起来是临时包上去的，绕过她的头，一直搭到鼻尖上，这样，她的脸上除了苍白干瘪的脸颊，皱巴巴好像故意紧闭的嘴之外，什么也看不见。蒂塔小姐惊讶地扫了我一眼，显然不知道我为什么会不耐烦。“你是说，她为什么总是戴着个东西？她这样是为了保护眼睛。”
"Because they are so fine?" 
“因为它们太美了？”
"Oh, today, today!" And Miss Tita shook her head, speaking very low. "But they used to be magnificent!" 
“哦，如今不行了，如今不行了！”蒂塔小姐摇摇头，声音低沉。“但它们以前真的很美！”
"Yes indeed, we have Aspern's word for that.” 
“是的，的确是，我们有阿斯本的诗歌为证。”
And as I looked again at the old woman's wrappings I could imagine that she had not wished to allow people a reason to say that the great poet had overdone it. But I did not waste my time in considering Miss Bordereau, in whom the appearance of respiration was so slight as to suggest that no human attention could ever help her more.
当我再次看着这位老妇人的裹头布，我可以想象得到，她不希望给人机会，说这位伟大的诗人言过其实。但是我也没有浪费时间去考虑博尔德罗小姐。看起来她的呼吸非常微弱，仿佛人们无论怎么努力都于事无补了。
I turned my eyes all over the room, rummaging with them the closets, the chests of drawers, the tables. Miss Tita met them quickly and read, I think, what was in them; but she did not answer it, turning away restlessly, anxiously, so that I felt rebuked, with reason, for a preoccupation that was almost profane in the presence of our dying companion.
我两眼再次各处扫视这个房间，盯着衣橱，五斗柜，和桌子看。蒂塔小姐很快和我的眼神相遇。我想，她读出了里面的含义。但是她没有回应我，马上不安地把眼神转开。我感到自己被拒绝了，但被拒是有原因的，因为，在一个将死的人面前，这样做简直是一种亵渎。
All the same I took another look, endeavoring to pick out mentally the place to try first, for a person who should wish to put his hand on Miss Bordereau's papers directly after her death. The room was a dire confusion; it looked like the room of an old actress. There were clothes hanging over chairs, odd-looking shabby bundles here and there, and various pasteboard boxes piled together, battered, bulging, and discolored, which might have been fifty years old.
尽管如此，作为一个希望在博尔德罗小姐去世后，立即得到文稿的人，我还是又看了看，试图在心里找出先下手的地方。这个地方让人很困惑，它看起来像是一个年老女演员的房间。有好些衣服挂在椅子上，怪模怪样的破旧包袱到处都是，各种纸板箱子堆在一起，压破的，鼓出来的，褪色的，大概都有五十年历史了。
Miss Tita after a moment noticed the direction of my eyes again and, as if she guessed how I judged the air of the place (forgetting I had no business to judge it at all), said, perhaps to defend herself from the imputation of complicity in such untidiness: "She likes it this way; we can't move things. There are old bandboxes she has had most of her life.” Then she added, half taking pity on my real thought, "Those things were THERE." And she pointed to a small, low trunk which stood under a sofa where there was just room for it.
蒂塔小姐过了一阵又看着我的眼睛，就像是在猜测我会怎样评价这个地方（她忘记了我完全没有权利要评价这个地方）。可能是为了替自己辩解，以免被人指责这么杂乱她也有责任，她说：“她喜欢这样。我们什么东西也不准动。这些旧盒子已经跟了她大半生了。”然后她又说，一半出于照顾我的真实想法：“那些东西以前就放在那里。”她指着一个又矮又小的木头箱子说，搁在沙发下面，那里刚刚好放进去这个箱子。
It appeared to be a queer, superannuated coffer, of painted wood, with elaborate handles and shriveled straps and with the color (it had last been endued with a coat of light green) much rubbed off. It evidently had traveled with Juliana in the olden time—in the days of her adventures, which it had shared. It would have made a strange figure arriving at a modern hotel.
它看起来像是个古怪过时的箱子，油漆过的木头，考究的把手，褶皱的皮带（最外面一层上了浅绿色），颜色大部分都磨掉了。它明显随着以前的朱莉安娜一起旅行——那是她冒险的旅程，它也同她分享这些旅程。要是到了一个现代的旅馆，这个东西一定是个奇怪的物件。
"WERE there—they aren't now?" I asked, startled by Miss Tita's implication.
“以前在——现在不在吗？”我问她，惊讶于蒂塔小姐的暗示。
She was going to answer, but at that moment the doctor came in—the doctor whom the little maid had been sent to fetch and whom she had at last overtaken.
她正准备回答的时候，医生走了进来——这个大夫是那个小个子女仆请来的，她最终还是赶上了。
My servant, going on his own errand, had met her with her companion in tow, and in the sociable Venetian spirit, retracing his steps with them, had also come up to the threshold of Miss Bordereau's room, where I saw him peeping over the doctor's shoulder.
我的仆人去办自己的差事，在路上遇见了女仆和医生，也和他们一块回来了，体现出了亲切的威尼斯人精神。他也来到了博尔德罗小姐房间的门槛前，我看他正越过医生的肩膀往里窥视。
I motioned him away the more instantly that the sight of his prying face reminded me that I myself had almost as little to do there—an admonition confirmed by the sharp way the little doctor looked at me, appearing to take me for a rival who had the field before him.
我示意他麻利点儿离开。他那张窥视的脸立即让我想到自己在这里呆着也没什么必要——这个身材矮小的医生警告地、犀利地看着我，好像把我当成了一个挡在他地盘上的敌人。
He was a short, fat, brisk gentleman who wore the tall hat of his profession and seemed to look at everything but his patient. He looked particularly at me, as if it struck him that I should be better for a dose, so that I bowed to him and left him with the women, going down to smoke a cigar in the garden.
他是个矮胖子，行动却很轻快。他戴着医生的高帽子，看起来除了病人，别的什么他都在看。他牢牢地注视着我，就像是认为我最好也服一剂药似的。于是，我向他鞠了个躬，留下他和女士们，就下楼到花园里抽支烟。
I was nervous; I could not go further; I could not leave the place. I don't know exactly what I thought might happen, but it seemed to me important to be there. I wandered about in the alleys—the warm night had come on—smoking cigar after cigar and looking at the light in Miss Bordereau's windows.
我感到紧张。我不能走得更远，也不能离开这个地方。我不确定，自己到底以为会发生什么事，但是，留在这里看起来对我很重要。我在小径里徘徊——暖夜已然降临——一支接着一支地抽着雪茄，注视着博尔德罗小姐窗户的灯光。
They were open now, I could see; the situation was different. Sometimes the light moved, but not quickly; it did not suggest the hurry of a crisis. Was the old woman dying, or was she already dead? Had the doctor said that there was nothing to be done at her tremendous age but to let her quietly pass away; or had he simply announced with a look a little more conventional that the end of the end had come?
我可以看见窗子现在打开了。情形不一样了。有时候，灯光会移动，但是不快，表明没什么紧急的危机发生。老太太快死了吗？还是她已经死了？医生是不是说，在她这样的高龄，做什么都没有用了，就让她安静地去吧。还是说他直接用多少已经形成了习惯的神情，宣布临终的最后一刻已经到来了？
Were the other two women moving about to perform the offices that follow in such a case? It made me uneasy not to be nearer, as if I thought the doctor himself might carry away the papers with him. I bit my cigar hard as it came over me again that perhaps there were now no papers to carry!
那另外两个女人是不是就要来回奔忙，料理后事呢？没呆在就近的地方让我很不安，我好像在担心医生会自己拿走文稿似的。我使劲咬着雪茄，那时心里又一次想到，也许现在根本就没什么文稿可拿了！
I wandered about for an hour—for an hour and a half. I looked out for Miss Tita at one of the windows, having a vague idea that she might come there to give me some sign. Would she not see the red tip of my cigar moving about in the dark and feel that I wanted eminently to know what the doctor had said? I am afraid it is a proof my anxieties had made me gross that I should have taken in some degree for granted that at such an hour, in the midst of the greatest change that could take place in her life, they were uppermost also in Miss Tita's mind.
我那样徘徊了一个小时左右——一个半小时。我期望能在某个窗口看见蒂塔小姐，隐隐约约希望她能出来，给我一点儿信号。难道她看不见我的雪茄烟红红的烟头在黑暗中来回移动吗？她难道没有感觉到，我极度想知道医生到底说了什么吗？恐怕这证明，我的焦虑让我变得可鄙。在这样的时刻，我还理所当然地认为，在她生命中发生最大变故的时候，我所担心的也是蒂塔小姐最在意的。
My servant came down and spoke to me; he knew nothing save that the doctor had gone after a visit of half an hour. If he had stayed half an hour then Miss Bordereau was still alive: it could not have taken so much time as that to enunciate the contrary.
我的仆人下楼来和我说话。他什么也不知道，只知道医生诊病用了半个小时，已经离开了。如果他呆了半个小时，那么博尔德罗小姐还活着。要证明她还活着的话，就不用呆那么长的时间了。
I sent the man out of the house; there were moments when the sense of his curiosity annoyed me, and this was one of them. He had been watching my cigar tip from an upper window, if Miss Tita had not; he could not know what I was after and I could not tell him, though I was conscious he had fantastic private theories about me which he thought fine and which I, had I known them, should have thought offensive.
我打发他出门去了。有时候，这个人的好奇心着实恼人，现在正是这样的时刻。要是蒂塔小姐没有看，他倒是从上面的窗户看见我点着的雪茄烟头。他不知道我在寻找什么，我也不能告诉他，虽然我意识到，他私底下对我有些猜测，也认为这些想法不错，但我，如果对他的猜测了解得更清楚的话，我会觉得这些猜测很无礼。
I went upstairs at last but I ascended no higher than the sala. The door of Miss Bordereau's apartment was open, showing from the parlor the dimness of a poor candle. I went toward it with a light tread, and at the same moment Miss Tita appeared and stood looking at me as I approached.
最后，我还是上了楼，但是只走到正厅。博尔德罗小姐的房门是开着的，会客室透出一点儿微弱的烛光。我轻轻地走过去，这时，蒂塔小姐出现了，站着看我靠近。
"She's better—she's better," she said, even before I had asked. "The doctor has given her something; she woke up, came back to life while he was there. He says there is no immediate danger." 
“她好些了——她好些了。”她说。我还没有开口问。“医生给她用了些药。她醒了，医生还在这里的时候她就醒了。他说暂时没什么危险。”
"No immediate danger? Surely he thinks her condition strange!" 
“暂时没危险？他肯定是认为她的情况很不寻常咯！”
"Yes, because she had been excited. That affects her dreadfully." 
“是的，因为她受了刺激。那严重地影响到了她。”
"It will do so again then, because she excites herself. She did so this afternoon." 
“这事还会再发生，因为她自己刺激了自己。她今天下午就是这样的。”
"Yes; she mustn't come out any more," said Miss Tita, with one of her lapses into a deeper placidity.
“是的，她一定不能再出房门。”蒂塔小姐说，变得更为平静了。
"What is the use of making such a remark as that if you begin to rattle her about again the first time she bids you?" 
“你说这些有什么用，如果她下一次一吩咐你，你就又要推着她，吱嘎吱嘎地到处走。”
"I won't—I won't do it any more.” 
“我不会——我再也不会了。”
"You must learn to resist her," I went on.
“你必须学会违背她。”我接着说。
"Oh, yes, I shall; I shall do so better if you tell me it's right.” 
“哦，是的，我会。如果你告诉我这是正确的，我会做得更好些。”
"You mustn't do it for me; you must do it for yourself. It all comes back to you, if you are frightened.” 
“你绝不要为了我这么做，你必须要为了自己这么做。要是你害怕，事情又会和从前一样。”
"Well, I am not frightened now," said Miss Tita cheerfully. "She is very quiet." 
“嗯，我现在不怕，”蒂塔小姐振奋地说，“她很安静。”
"Is she conscious again—does she speak?” 
“她现在恢复知觉了吗——她说话了吗？”
"No, she doesn't speak, but she takes my hand. She holds it fast.” 
“没有，她没有说话，但是她抓住我的手。她抓得牢牢的。”
"Yes," I rejoined, "I can see what force she still has by the way she grabbed that picture this afternoon. But if she holds you fast how comes it that you are here?" 
“是的，”我说，“我知道她的力度，下午她抓着那张图像的时候，就是那样的。但是，如果她牢牢抓住你，你怎么会在这里呢？”
Miss Tita hesitated a moment; though her face was in deep shadow (she had her back to the light in the parlor and I had put down my own candle far off, near the door of the sala), I thought I saw her smile ingenuously. "I came on purpose—I heard your step.” 
蒂塔小姐犹豫了一下。虽然她的脸藏在深深的阴影里（她把她的蜡烛放在了会客室，而我也早就放下了我的蜡烛，放在正厅旁边的房间里），我想我看见了她天真的微笑。“我特意出来的——我听见你的脚步声了。”
"Why, I came on tiptoe, as inaudibly as possible." 
“啊？我蹑手蹑脚地走，几乎听不见声音啊。”
"Well, I heard you," said Miss Tita.
“嗯，我听见了你的声音。”蒂塔小姐说。
"And is your aunt alone now?" 
“你姑妈现在单独一人吗？”
"Oh, no; Olimpia is sitting there." 
“哦，没有，欧林匹亚坐在她旁边的。”
On my side I hesitated. "Shall we then step in there?" And I nodded at the parlor; I wanted more and more to be on the spot.
我犹豫了一下。“那我们能到里面去吗？”我朝会客室点点头，我希望能尽量呆在现场。
"We can't talk there—she will hear us.” 
“我们在那儿不能说话——她会听见的。”
I was on the point of replying that in that case we would sit silent, but I was too conscious that this would not do, as there was something I desired immensely to ask her. So I proposed that we should walk a little in the sala, keeping more at the other end, where we should not disturb the old lady.
我准备回答说，那样的话，我们就安静地坐着好了。但是我太清楚了，这样是行不通的，因为我非常想要问她一些事。于是我建议我们可以在正厅走走，到正厅另外一头去，这样就不会打扰老太太了。
Miss Tita assented unconditionally; the doctor was coming again, she said, and she would be there to meet him at the door.
蒂塔小姐无条件地同意了。医生又快要来了，她说，她最好在门口接他。
We strolled through the fine superfluous hall, where on the marble floor—particularly as at first we said nothing—our footsteps were more audible than I had expected.
我们在华丽而多余的正厅里漫步，在大理石楼板上——尤其是我们一开始什么也没有说——我们的脚步声比我预料的还要响亮。
When we reached the other end—the wide window, inveterately closed, connecting with the balcony that overhung the canal—I suggested that we should remain there, as she would see the doctor arrive still better.
当我们到了正厅的那一头——那宽大的窗子经年累月地关着，它连着阳台，阳台上可以俯瞰运河——我提议我们该呆在这里，这样她就可以更好地注意到医生的到来。
I opened the window and we passed out on the balcony. The air of the canal seemed even heavier, hotter than that of the sala. The place was hushed and void; the quiet neighborhood had gone to sleep. A lamp, here and there, over the narrow black water, glimmered in double; the voice of a man going homeward singing, with his jacket on his shoulder and his hat on his ear, came to us from a distance.
我打开窗，穿行到阳台上。运河的湿气更重了，比正厅的空气还要热。这里沉寂而空旷，附近的地方安静地沉睡着。一盏盏灯，星星点点，在暗色的水面上漂着，闪着重重微光。一个赶着回家的人在唱歌，外套搭在他的肩上，帽子挂在耳朵上，从远处向我们划来。
This did not prevent the scene from being very comme il faut, as Miss Bordereau had called it the first time I saw her. Presently a gondola passed along the canal with its slow rhythmical plash, and as we listened we watched it in silence.
这并没有妨碍这个不错的场景，“不错”这个词是我第一次见博尔德罗小姐的时候，听她这样说的。很快，一艘平底小船很有节奏地拍打着水面，划过运河，我们静静地听着，看着。
It did not stop, it did not carry the doctor; and after it had gone on I said to Miss Tita: "And where are they now—the things that were in the trunk?” 
它没有停下来，也没有带来医生。它离开之后，我对蒂塔小姐说：“它们现在在哪里——以前在木箱子里的那些东西？”
"In the trunk?" 
“木箱子里？”
"That green box you pointed out to me in her room. You said her papers had been there; you seemed to imply that she had transferred them." 
“在她房间里，你指给我看的绿色箱子。你说她的文稿以前就在那里。你似乎在说那些东西她已经转移到别的地方去了。”
"Oh, yes; they are not in the trunk," said Miss Tita.
“哦，是，它们现在不在箱子里。”蒂塔小姐说。
"May I ask if you have looked?" 
“请问，你看过吗？”
"Yes, I have looked—for you.” 
“是的，我看过——为了你。”
"How for me, dear Miss Tita? Do you mean you would have given them to me if you had found them?" I asked, almost trembling.
“怎样为了我，亲爱的蒂塔小姐？你是说，你要是找着了，会把它们给我？”我几乎是颤抖着在问。
She delayed to reply and I waited. Suddenly she broke out, "I don't know what I would do—what I wouldn't!” 
她缓了一下，而我等着。突然她脱口而出：“我不知道我该做什么——不该做什么！”
"Would you look again—somewhere else?” 
“你再找找行吗——别的地方？”
She had spoken with a strange unexpected emotion, and she went on in the same tone: "I can't—I can't—while she lies there. It isn't decent.” 
她说话的时候带着一种陌生的、突发的情绪，她继续那样说道：“我不能——我不能——她就躺在那里。这不光彩。”
"No, it isn't decent," I replied gravely. "Let the poor lady rest in peace." And the words, on my lips, were not hypocritical, for I felt reprimanded and shamed.
“是，的确不光彩，”我低落地回答，“就让可怜的老人安静地休息吧。”我嘴里说的这些话不是虚伪的，因为我也感到受了谴责，很惭愧。
Miss Tita added in a moment, as if she had guessed this and were sorry for me, but at the same time wished to explain that I did drive her on or at least did insist too much: "I can't deceive her that way. I can't deceive her—perhaps on her deathbed.” 
蒂塔小姐好像猜到了这一点，替我难过，但是同时又希望能向我解释，是我催她催得紧，至少是敦促得太多了。所以过了一会儿，她补充道：“我不能这样欺骗她。我不能骗她——她可能就要去世了。”
"Heaven forbid I should ask you, though I have been guilty myself!" 
“要是我叫你去骗她，天也不容我，尽管我也是有罪之人！”
"You have been guilty?" 
“你有罪？”
"I have sailed under false colors." I felt now as if I must tell her that I had given her an invented name, on account of my fear that her aunt would have heard of me and would refuse to take me in. I explained this and also that I had really been a party to the letter written to them by John Cumnor months before.
“我隐瞒了自己的身份。”我觉得现在我必须告诉她一切，告诉她我用了假冒的名字，因为我怕她姑妈会听说过我，拒绝让我住进来。我解释了这点，也告诉她，几个月前，约翰·卡姆诺写给他们的信，我也有份参与。
She listened with great attention, looking at me with parted lips, and when I had made my confession she said, "Then your real name—what is it?” She repeated it over twice when I had told her, accompanying it with the exclamation "Gracious, gracious!" Then she added, "I like your own best." 
她极认真地听着，张大嘴看着我。当我和盘托出，她问：“那你的真名——是什么？”当我告诉她的时候，她重复说了两次，还一边叹道：“天哪，天哪！”然后她说：“我比较喜欢你的本名。”
"So do I," I said, laughing. "Ouf! it's a relief to get rid of the other.” 
“我也是，”我笑着答道，“啊！抛掉另一个名字真让人轻松。”
"So it was a regular plot—a kind of conspiracy?” 
“那就是说，这是个有计划的密谋——一种阴谋？”
"Oh, a conspiracy—we were only two," I replied, leaving out Mrs. Prest of course.
“哦，一个阴谋——我们只有两个人。”我说，当然没有提普雷斯顿夫人的名字。
She hesitated; I thought she was perhaps going to say that we had been very base. But she remarked after a moment, in a candid, wondering way, "How much you must want them!" 
她犹豫了一下，我以为她可能要说，我们很龌龊。但是她过了一会儿，用一种坦率，诧异的口吻说：“你们是多么想得到它们啊！”
"Oh, I do, passionately!" I conceded, smiling. And this chance made me go on, forgetting my compunction of a moment before. "How can she possibly have changed their place herself? How can she walk? How can she arrive at that sort of muscular exertion? How can she lift and carry things?" 
“哦，我的确是，非常想！”我笑着坦白。这个机会让我继续说下去，忘记了刚刚自己受到的谴责。“她怎么可能自己把东西换个地方呢？她怎么走路？她哪能有那样大的力气。她怎么能把东西提起来搬走？”
"Oh, when one wants and when one has so much will!" said Miss Tita, as if she had thought over my question already herself and had simply had no choice but that answer—the idea that in the dead of night, or at some moment when the coast was clear, the old woman had been capable of a miraculous effort.
“啊，当一个人下定决心，意志坚决的时候，是办得到的！”蒂塔小姐说。像她把我的问题在自己心里想了一遍，除了这个答案，没有别的选择似的——那就是，在死寂的夜里，或是海岸天气晴朗的某个时候，这个老妇人能拥有奇迹般的力量。
"Have you questioned Olimpia? Hasn't she helped her—hasn't she done it for her?" I asked; to which Miss Tita replied promptly and positively that their servant had had nothing to do with the matter, though without admitting definitely that she had spoken to her. It was as if she were a little shy, a little ashamed now of letting me see how much she had entered into my uneasiness and had me on her mind. Suddenly she said to me, without any immediate relevance: "I feel as if you were a new person, now that you have got a new name." 
“你问过欧林匹亚吗？她没有帮她吗——她难道没有帮过她？”我问。这个问题蒂塔小姐很快回答说她们的仆人很明显与此事毫无关系，虽然没有明确承认她曾经嘱咐过女仆。看起来，她有点儿害羞，有点羞愧于让我了解她分担了多少我的不安，把我放在她心上。突然，她语无伦次地对我说：“既然你有个新名字，我觉得你是个不同的人了。”
"It isn't a new one; it is a very good old one, thank heaven!” 
“这不是个新名字，这是个很好的旧名字，谢天谢地！”
She looked at me a moment. "I do like it better." 
她看了我一会儿。“我真的更喜欢这个名字。”
"Oh, if you didn't I would almost go on with the other!” 
“哦，如果你不喜欢的话，我几乎只能继续用另一个名字了！”
"Would you really?" 
“你真的会这样吗？”
I laughed again, but for all answer to this inquiry I said, "Of course if she can rummage about that way she can perfectly have burnt them." 
我又笑了，但为了完全回答她的询问，我说：“要是她能那样翻箱倒柜地整理东西，她一定也可以烧了它们，那我当然得用假名字了。”
"You must wait—you must wait," Miss Tita moralized mournfully; and her tone ministered little to my patience, for it seemed after all to accept that wretched possibility. I would teach myself to wait, I declared nevertheless; because in the first place I could not do otherwise and in the second I had her promise, given me the other night, that she would help me.
“你必须等着——你必须等着。”蒂塔小姐伤心地告诫我。她的语调几乎不能让我耐住性子，因为这看起来像是终究要接受那种不幸的可能性。然而，我教自己学会等待，我这样告诉自己，因为首先，我没有别的办法，其次，我得到了她的保证，就是之前的那个晚上，她答应要帮我。
"Of course if the papers are gone that's no use," she said; not as if she wished to recede, but only to be conscientious.
“如果文稿没有了，那当然就没什么用了。”她说。这倒不是说她想退却，只是想表明自己是尽责的。
"Naturally. But if you could only find out!" I groaned, quivering again.
“那是自然。但是只要你找到了，那就不同了！”我呻吟地说道，又开始颤抖了。
"I thought you said you would wait." 
“我想你说过你会等的。”
"Oh, you mean wait even for that?" 
“啊，你是说，连这个也要等？”
"For what then?" 
“等什么？”
"Oh, nothing," I replied, rather foolishly, being ashamed to tell her what had been implied in my submission to delay—the idea that she would do more than merely find out. I know not whether she guessed this; at all events she appeared to become aware of the necessity for being a little more rigid.
“哦，没什么。”我傻乎乎地回答。羞于告诉她，我之所以肯等——是因为我认为，她不光要找到文稿，她还可以做得更多。我不知道她到底猜到没有。无论如何，她看起来意识到性子变得刚烈一点儿是必要的了。
"I didn't promise to deceive, did I? I don't think I did.” 
“我没有承诺要骗她，是吧？我认为我没有。”
"It doesn't much matter whether you did or not, for you couldn't!” 
“你有没有承诺不重要，因为你不会欺骗她！”
I don't think Miss Tita would have contested this event had she not been diverted by our seeing the doctor's gondola shoot into the little canal and approach the house. I noted that he came as fast as if he believed that Miss Bordereau was still in danger. We looked down at him while he disembarked and then went back into the sala to meet him. When he came up however I naturally left Miss Tita to go off with him alone, only asking her leave to come back later for news.
我想，蒂塔小姐不会就这个话题进行辩论了，因为她已经注意到医生的平底船飞快地驶入这条小运河，就快到家了。我认为他来得这么快，是因为博尔德罗小姐仍然处在危险期。我们在楼上看着他下船，然后我们回到正厅接他。然而，他上来之后，我自然要离开，让蒂塔小姐和他单独进去。我向她告假，说过一会儿回来听消息。
I went out of the house and took a long walk, as far as the Piazza, where my restlessness declined to quit me. I was unable to sit down (it was very late now but there were people still at the little tables in front of the cafes); I could only walk round and round, and I did so half a dozen times. I was uncomfortable, but it gave me a certain pleasure to have told Miss Tita who I really was.
我出了房间，走了很久，走到了大广场，但我的不安并没有消失。我没法坐下来（已经很晚了，但是咖啡馆前的小桌子上还有人），我只能一圈一圈地走，走了六七圈。我很不舒服。但是，因为告诉了蒂塔小姐我的真实身份，我也感到一种愉快。
At last I took my way home again, slowly getting all but inextricably lost, as I did whenever I went out in Venice: so that it was considerably past midnight when I reached my door. The sala, upstairs, was as dark as usual and my lamp as I crossed it found nothing satisfactory to show me.
最后，我又往回走，慢慢地变得彻底迷失了，就像我每次去威尼斯都会做的这样：于是，我到家的时候，已经过了午夜了。楼上的正厅，和往常一样黑，我拿着灯穿过正厅时没有发现什么令人满意的事情。
I was disappointed, for I had notified Miss Tita that I would come back for a report, and I thought she might have left a light there as a sign. The door of the ladies' apartment was closed; which seemed an intimation that my faltering friend had gone to bed, tired of waiting for me.
我很失望，因为我告诉过蒂塔小姐我会回来听情况，于是我认为她会在房里留个灯什么的，当作记号。女士们房门紧闭。这似乎在暗示我，我那个跌跌撞撞的朋友已经上床睡觉了，懒得等我了。
I stood in the middle of the place, considering, hoping she would hear me and perhaps peep out, saying to myself too that she would never go to bed with her aunt in a state so critical; she would sit up and watch—she would be in a chair, in her dressing gown.
我站在厅中间，思考着，希望她能听见我，或许往外探一眼。我对自己说，她姑妈在这么重要的关头，她肯定不会睡觉的。她会坐着看护她——她一定穿着起居服坐在椅子上。
I went nearer the door; I stopped there and listened. I heard nothing at all and at last I tapped gently. No answer came and after another minute I turned the handle.
我朝门走近了些。我停在那里听了听。什么也没有听到，最后我轻轻地敲了一下。里面没有回应，再等了一分钟后，我转下了门把手。
There was no light in the room; this ought to have prevented me from going in, but it had no such effect.
房间里没有灯，这本该阻止我继续走进去，但是它却产生不了这样的效果。
If I have candidly narrated the importunities, the indelicacies, of which my desire to possess myself of Jeffrey Aspern's papers had rendered me capable I need not shrink from confessing this last indiscretion. I think it was the worst thing I did; yet there were extenuating circumstances. I was deeply though doubtless not disinterestedly anxious for more news of the old lady, and Miss Tita had accepted from me, as it were, a rendezvous which it might have been a point of honor with me to keep. It may be said that her leaving the place dark was a positive sign that she released me, and to this I can only reply that I desired not to be released.
如果我坦率地说出了我的要求，我的粗俗，我就不需要再隐藏我这最后的卑劣想法。我的这些想法都来自我想要占有杰弗里·阿斯本的文稿，为了这些文稿我什么事情都干得出来。我认为这是我最想要干的事情。而且有些情况是情有可原的。我非常想要知道老太太更多的消息，尽管我很明显也不是没有私心的。而且，蒂塔小姐基本上同意和我会面，所以我要信守的承诺，信誉攸关。但是，可以说是她把这地方弄得这么黑，这绝对是她给我的信号，表明我可以不再遵守那个诺言了。
The door of Miss Bordereau's room was open and I could see beyond it the faintness of a taper.
博尔德罗小姐的房门开着，透过门可以看见昏暗的一点儿灯光。
There was no sound—my footstep caused no one to stir. I came further into the room; I lingered there with my lamp in my hand. I wanted to give Miss Tita a chance to come to me if she were with her aunt, as she must be. I made no noise to call her; I only waited to see if she would not notice my light.
没有声音——我的脚步声没有影响到任何人。我朝里面走去，我手里拿着灯，在房里逗留着。我想给蒂塔小姐一个机会来找我，如果她这会儿在她姑妈房间的话，她也应该在那里。我没有出声叫她，我只是在等她，看她有没有注意到我的灯光。
She did not, and I explained this (I found afterward I was right) by the idea that she had fallen asleep. If she had fallen asleep her aunt was not on her mind, and my explanation ought to have led me to go out as I had come.
她没有，而我的解释是（我后来发现我是对的）因为她睡着了。如果她睡着了，她就没有把她姑妈放在心上，这个解释应该让我离开房间，就像我进来那样。
I must repeat again that it did not, for I found myself at the same moment thinking of something else. I had no definite purpose, no bad intention, but I felt myself held to the spot by an acute, though absurd, sense of opportunity. For what I could not have said, inasmuch as it was not in my mind that I might commit a theft. Even if it had been I was confronted with the evident fact that Miss Bordereau did not leave her secretary, her cupboard, and the drawers of her tables gaping. I had no keys, no tools, and no ambition to smash her furniture.
我要再说一次，那并没有起这样的作用，因为我发现自己在此刻想的是别的事情。我没有特定的目的，也没有坏的意图，但是我觉得自己呆在现场，有种虽然荒唐但是却清晰的机会感。我说不出口，但是在那个时候，我心里的确想的是，我也许要当个小偷。就算是当小偷，我也遇到了明显的麻烦，那就是博尔德罗小姐并没有让她的写字台，橱柜，五斗橱和桌子等等开条缝隙。我没有钥匙，没有工具，也没打算要砸烂她的家具。
Nonetheless it came to me that I was now, perhaps alone, unmolested, at the hour of temptation and secrecy, nearer to the tormenting treasure than I had ever been. I held up my lamp, let the light play on the different objects as if it could tell me something. Still there came no movement from the other room. If Miss Tita was sleeping she was sleeping sound. Was she doing so—generous creature—on purpose to leave me the field? Did she know I was there and was she just keeping quiet to see what I would do—what I COULD do? But what could I do, when it came to that? She herself knew even better than I how little.
然而，我还是认为，我现在也许是独自一人，没有什么麻烦事。此时此刻充满了诱惑与秘密，我前所未有地接近那折磨人的珍宝。我举着灯，让它照着不同的地方，就好像这也能告诉我一些事情。这时，另一间房仍然没有什么动静。如果蒂塔小姐睡了的话，她一定睡得很沉。她是不是——她真是个慷慨的人——故意给我留个空间？她是不是知道我在那里，所以她保持安静，想看看我要做什么——我能做什么？如果真的是那样，我又能做什么呢？她比我更清楚，我这样做的可能性有多小。
I stopped in front of the secretary, looking at it very idiotically; for what had it to say to me after all?
我在写字台前停下来，傻傻地看着它，看看它到底能告诉我什么呢？
In the first place it was locked, and in the second it almost surely contained nothing in which I was interested.
首先，它是锁着的。其次，里面肯定没有什么东西是我感兴趣的。
Ten to one the papers had been destroyed; and even if they had not been destroyed the old woman would not have put them in such a place as that after removing them from the green trunk—would not have transferred them, if she had the idea of their safety on her brain, from the better hiding place to the worse. The secretary was more conspicuous, more accessible in a room in which she could no longer mount guard. It opened with a key, but there was a little brass handle, like a button, as well; I saw this as I played my lamp over it.
文稿有十分之一的可能性是被毁了，然而就算是没有被毁了，这个老太太也不会把它们从绿箱子里头拿出来之后，放在这样一个地方——如果她考虑到文稿的安全，那么她也不会把它们从一个较好的隐蔽处转移到一个较差的地点。书桌呆在一个她无法防范的房间里，显得越发明显，越容易接近。写字台要用钥匙打开，但是上面也有个铜制小把手，又像个小扣子。我把灯照到它上面的时候，看到了这点。
I did something more than this at that moment: I caught a glimpse of the possibility that Miss Tita wished me really to understand. If she did not wish me to understand, if she wished me to keep away, why had she not locked the door of communication between the sitting room and the sala? That would have been a definite sign that I was to leave them alone. If I did not leave them alone she meant me to come for a purpose—a purpose now indicated by the quick, fantastic idea that to oblige me she had unlocked the secretary. She had not left the key, but the lid would probably move if I touched the button. This theory fascinated me, and I bent over very close to judge.
那时，我做的事情不止这些：我窥探到蒂塔小姐可能真正想要我明白的事情。如果她不希望我明白，如果她希望我离开，那她为什么不锁上会客室的门？这会客室就在起居室和正厅之间。那会是个清楚的迹象，表明我不能打扰她们。如果我要来打扰她们，她要我找个原因——这个原因就是我马上想到的奇妙想法：她为了方便我，她把写字台的锁打开了。她没有留下钥匙，但是如果我一按按钮，罩子就会打开。这个想法吸引了我，我弯下腰，靠近了判断。
I did not propose to do anything, not even—not in the least—to let down the lid; I only wanted to test my theory, to see if the cover WOULD move.
我不打算做什么事情，甚至没有——一点儿也没有——放下罩子。我只想试试看我的猜测对不对，看看这个罩子到底会不会动。
I touched the button with my hand—a mere touch would tell me; and as I did so (it is embarrassing for me to relate it), I looked over my shoulder. It was a chance, an instinct, for I had not heard anything. I almost let my luminary drop and certainly I stepped back, straightening myself up at what I saw.
我用手碰了一下按钮——只需要轻轻一按就知道。而我在这么做的时候（这样做让我很尴尬），我回头从肩膀看过去。这是个偶然，也是种本能，因为我之前什么也没有听见。我差点儿把手里的灯掉下来，而我也确实后退了一步，我看见的东西让我挺直了身体。
Miss Bordereau stood there in her nightdress, in the doorway of her room, watching me; her hands were raised, she had lifted the everlasting curtain that covered half her face, and for the first, the last, the only time I beheld her extraordinary eyes. They glared at me, they made me horribly ashamed.
穿着睡衣的博尔德罗小姐站起来了，站在她房间的门道，看着我。她的手抬了起来。她把她永远罩在眼前，遮住她半张脸的罩子摘掉了。这是第一次，最后一次，也是唯一一次我看见她不寻常的眼睛。它们盯着我，让我又惊恐又羞愧。
I never shall forget her strange little bent white tottering figure, with its lifted head, her attitude, her expression; neither shall I forget the tone in which as I turned, looking at her, she hissed out passionately, furiously: 
我永远忘不了她那怪异矮小的身形，忘不了她佝偻着身体，白发苍苍，蹒跚不已，还有她仰着的头，她的态度，她的表情。我也忘不了她说话的语气。当我转过去，看着她，她愤怒地，用了极大的力气嘶哑地说：
"Ah, you publishing scoundrel!" 
“哼，你这个出版界的恶棍！”
I know not what I stammered, to excuse myself, to explain; but I went toward her, to tell her I meant no harm. She waved me off with her old hands, retreating before me in horror; and the next thing I knew she had fallen back with a quick spasm, as if death had descended on her, into Miss Tita's arms.
我不知道自己嘟囔着给自己找了什么借口来解释。但是我朝她走过去，告诉她我没有恶意。她挥挥苍老的手叫我住嘴，憎恨地从我面前离开。我知道接下来的事情，就是她一阵痉挛，倒下去了，就好像死亡突然降临她身上一样，她倒在了蒂塔小姐的怀里。
