XX  

第二十章  

"Why did I say that to her?" Litvinov thought the next morning as he sat in his room at the window.
“我为什么要对她说那些话呢？”第二天清晨，李特维诺夫坐在自己房间的窗边思考着。
He shrugged his shoulders in vexation: he had said that to Tatyana simply to cut himself off all way of retreat. In the window lay a note from Irina: she asked him to see her at twelve. Potugin's words incessantly recurred to his mind, they seemed to reach him with a faint ill-omened sound as of a rumbling underground. He was angry with himself, but could not get rid of them anyhow. Some one knocked at the door.
他苦恼地耸耸肩：他之所以对塔吉娅娜说那些话只是为了斩断自己所有的退路罢了。窗台上放着伊琳娜的信：她要他十二点钟的时候去见她。波图金的话不断地在他脑子里浮现着，像从地下隆隆传来的微弱的、不祥的声音。他气自己，但却不能摆脱这种声音。有人在敲门。
"Wer da?" asked Litvinov.
“谁啊？”李特维诺夫问。
"Ah! you're at home! open!" he heard Bindasov's hoarse bass.
“啊，您在家，开门呀！”门外传来宾达索夫低沉沙哑的声音。
The door handle creaked.
只听见门把手咯吱咯吱响了。
Litvinov turned white with exasperation.
李特维诺夫气得脸色发白。
"I'm not at home," he declared sharply.
“我不在家！”他厉声吼道。
"Not at home? That's a good joke!” 
“不在家？真是开玩笑！”
"I tell you—not at home, get along." 
“我说了——我不在家，滚吧！”
"That's civil! And I came to ask you for a little loan," grumbled Bindasov.
“您真有礼貌啊！我过来向您借一点点钱。”宾达索夫咕哝着说道。
He walked off, however, tramping on his heels as usual.
但他还是走开了，像平时一样，鞋子踩得咚咚直响。
Litvinov was all but dashing out after him, he felt such a longing to throttle the hateful ruffian. The events of the last few days had unstrung his nerves; a little more, and he would have burst into tears. He drank off a glass of cold water, locked up all the drawers in the furniture, he could not have said why, and went to Tatyana's.
李特维诺夫差点就要追出去了，他真是恨不得掐死这个可恶的无赖！这几日发生的种种事情，使得他神经衰弱。只要再加上点什么，他就哭出来了。他喝了一整杯凉开水，锁上了家具上所有的抽屉，他也说不出为什么这么做，接着就去塔吉娅娜房间了。
He found her alone. Kapitolina Markovna had gone out shopping. Tatyana was sitting on the sofa, holding a book in both hands. She was not reading it and scarcely knew what book it was. She did not stir, but her heart was beating quickly in her bosom, and the little white collar round her neck quivered visibly and evenly.
他发现她独自一人。卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜出去购物了。塔吉娅娜坐在沙发上，双手捧着一本书。她没在看书，而且也不知道手中是一本什么书。她并没有动，但是她的心却在胸中猛烈地跳动着，脖子上小小的白色衣领明显且有规律地颤动着。
Litvinov was confused… However, he sat down by her, said good-morning, smiled at her; she, too, smiled at him without speaking. She had bowed to him when he came in, bowed courteously, not affectionately, and she did not glance at him. He held out his hand to her; she gave him her chill fingers, but at once freed them again, and took up the book. Litvinov felt that to begin the conversation with unimportant subjects would be insulting Tatyana; she after her custom made no demands, but everything in her said plainly, "I am waiting, I am waiting." ... He must fulfill his promise. But though almost the whole night he had thought of nothing else, he had not prepared even the first introductory words, and absolutely did not know in what way to break this cruel silence.
李特维诺夫对此感到很疑惑……然而，他还是坐在她身边，向她道了一声早安，对她笑了笑，她也向他微笑着，但没有说话。当他进来的时候，她向他礼貌地鞠了一躬，并不是很热情，而且也没有看他。他向她伸出了手，她也伸出自己冰凉的手，但是马上又抽了回来，拿起了书本。李特维诺夫觉得以一些无关痛痒的事来开始他们之间的谈话是对塔吉娅娜的侮辱。像往常一样，塔吉娅娜并没提任何要求，但她内心的声音分明在叫着，“我在等待，在等待……”他必须得履行自己的诺言。然而整个晚上，他都没有想过其他事情。他根本就没准备好该从何说起，而且他完全不知道该如何打破这令人痛苦的沉默。
"Tanya," he began at last, "I told you yesterday that I have something important to say to you. I am ready, only I beg you beforehand not to be angry against me, and to rest assured that my feelings for you..." 
“塔尼娅。”他最终开始说道，“昨天我对您说我有重要事情跟您讲。我准备好了，只是我恳求您不要生我的气，并相信我对您的感情……”
He stopped. He caught his breath. Tatyana still did not stir, and did not look at him; she only clutched the book tighter than ever.
他停了下来。喘了一口气。塔吉娅娜依然没有动，也没有看他，只是把手中的书握得更紧了。
"There has always been," Litvinov went on, without finishing the sentence he had begun, "there has always been perfect openness between us; I respect you too much to be a hypocrite with you; I want to prove to you that I know how to value the nobleness and independence of your nature, even though... though of course..." 
“我们一直……”李特维诺夫上一句还没有说完，就继续说道，“我们之间一直是极其坦诚相待的，我对您是如此尊重，因此不可能对您虚伪。我想向您证明，我知道该怎样珍惜您的高贵与性格独立，虽然……虽然当然……”
"Grigory Mihalitch," began Tatyana in a measured voice while a deathly pallor overspread her whole face, "I will come to your assistance, you no longer love me, and you don't know how to tell me so." 
“格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇。”塔吉娅娜慎重地说道，整张脸苍白如死灰，“我帮您说吧，您已不再爱我了，而且您不知道该如何跟我说。”
Litvinov involuntarily shuddered.
李特维诺夫不由地打了个冷战。
"Why?"... he said, hardly intelligibly, "why could you suppose?... I really don't understand..." 
“您为什么这样说？……” 他说，声音几乎听不清，“您为什么会这样想？……我真是不明白……”
"What! isn't it the truth? Isn't it the truth?—tell me, tell me." 
“什么！这难道不是事实吗？不是事实吗？您说啊！您说啊！”
Tatyana turned quite round to Litvinov; her face, with her hair brushed back from it, approached his face, and her eyes, which for so long had not looked at him, seemed to penetrate into his eyes.
塔吉娅娜猛地转过身面对着李特维诺夫，她的头发掠过脸颊向后一甩，触到了他的面颊。还有她的眼睛，好久没看李特维诺夫的那双眼睛，此刻像是要穿透他的眼睛一般。
"Isn't it the truth?" she repeated.
“这难道不是事实吗？”她又问了一遍。
He said nothing, did not utter a single sound. He could not have lied at that instant, even if he had known she would believe him, and that his lie would save her; he was not even able to bear her eyes upon him. Litvinov said nothing, but she needed no answer, she read the answer in his very silence, in those guilty downcast eyes—and she turned away again and dropped the book… She had been still uncertain till that instant, and Litvinov understood that; he understood that she had been still uncertain—and how hideous, actually hideous was all that he was doing.
他什么也没说，就连一个声音也发不出来。在那一刻他不可能再骗她了，即使他知道她仍然会相信他，而且谎言可以拯救她受伤的心，他甚至承受不了她的目光。李特维诺夫什么都没有说，但是她不需要任何回答。她已经在他的沉默中，在他低垂愧疚的眼神中找到了答案——她再次转开了身，丢掉了书……其实在这一刻她还是没有确定，李特维诺夫也明白这点，他明白她还是不肯定——他的所作所为是多么丑陋、多么丑陋啊。
He flung himself on his knees before her.
他一下子跪在了她面前。
"Tanya," he cried, "if only you knew how hard it is for me to see you in this position, how awful to me to think that it's I... I! My heart is torn to pieces, I don't know myself, I have lost myself, and you, and everything... Everything is shattered, Tanya, everything! Could I dream that I... I should bring such a blow upon you, my best friend, my guardian angel?... Could I dream that we should meet like this, should spend such a day as yesterday!..." 
“塔尼娅！”他大声说道，“我多希望您知道我看到您这样的处境心里有多痛苦，想到这一切都是我造成的我有多恐惧，我……我的心已撕碎了，我不懂我自己了，我已失去自我，失去了您，失去了一切……一切都粉碎了！塔尼娅，所有的一切！我真是做梦都没想到……我会给您带来这样的打击，这位我最好的朋友，守护着我的天使……我真是无法想象我们会以这样的方式见面，会度过像昨天那样的一天！”
Tatyana was trying to get up and go away. He held her back by the border of her dress.
塔吉娅娜想起身离开。他抓着她的衣角将她拉回来。
"No, listen to me a minute longer. You see I am, on my knees before you, but I have not come to beg your forgiveness; you cannot, you ought not to forgive me. I have come to tell you that your friend is ruined, that he is falling into the pit, and would not drag you down with him… But save me... no! even you cannot save me. I should push you away, I am ruined, Tanya, I am ruined past all help." 
“别走，听我多说一小会儿。您也看到我现在跪在您面前，但是我并不是在乞求您的原谅，您不能，您也不应该原谅我。我是来告诉您，您的朋友已经完蛋了，他正在坠入一个深渊，他不想把您也拖下去……但您要拯救我……不！连您也不能救我了。我应该把您推开，我已经堕落了，塔尼娅，我已经无可救药了。”
Tatyana looked at Litvinov. "You are ruined?" she said, as though not fully understanding him. "You are ruined?" 
塔吉娅娜看着李特维诺夫。“您完蛋了？”她说道，像是没有完全听明白似的。“您完蛋了？”
"Yes, Tanya, I am ruined. All the past, all that was precious, everything I have lived for up till now, is ruined for me; everything is wretched, everything is shattered, and I don't know what awaits me in the future. You said just now that I no longer loved you… No, Tanya, I have not ceased to love you, but a different, terrible, irresistible passion has come upon me, has overborne me. I fought against it while I could...." 
“是的，塔尼娅，我完蛋了。我过去所拥有的一切，所有珍贵的东西以及现在一直赖以生存的一切都被我毁灭了。一切都在我眼前坍塌，一切都在我眼前崩溃，我不知道未来还有什么在等待着我。您刚刚说我已经不再爱您了……不是这样的，塔尼娅，我一直都没有停止爱您，但是有一种异样的、可怕的、令人无法抗拒的情感向我逼近，并俘虏了我。我尽全力与之搏斗……”
Tatyana got up, her brows twitched, her pale face darkened. Litvinov, too, rose to his feet.
塔吉娅娜站起来了，紧皱着眉头，苍白的脸变得阴暗起来。李特维诺夫也站起了来了。
"You love another woman," she began, "and I guess who she is.... We met her yesterday, didn't we? ... Well, I see what is left for me to do now. Since you say yourself this passion is unalterable”... (Tatyana paused an instant, possibly she had still hoped Litvinov would not let this last word pass unchallenged, but he said nothing), "it only remains for me to give you back... your word." Litvinov bent his head, as though submissively receiving a well-deserved blow.
“您爱上了另外一个女人，”她开始说道，“让我来猜猜那个人是谁……”“我们昨天见过她的，是吗？嗯，我明白现在我该做些什么了。既然您说这种情感不会改变……”（塔吉娅娜停顿了一会儿，也许她仍希望李特维诺夫不会让她的最后这句话畅通无阻地说出来，但是他什么都没说。）“现在我只能……奉还您的诺言。”李特维诺夫低下了头，像是很顺从地在接受应有的惩罚。
"You have every right to be angry with me," he said. "You have every right to reproach me for feebleness... for deceit." 
“您完全可以生我的气。”他说，“您完全可以责备我的软弱……我对您的欺骗。”
Tatyana looked at him again.
塔吉娅娜又看了他一眼。
"I have not reproached you, Litvinov, I don't blame you. I agree with you: the bitterest truth is better than what went on yesterday. What sort of a life could ours have been now!” 
“我未曾责备过您，李特维诺夫，我也不会怪您。我很赞同您的做法：再残酷的事实也好过昨天所发生的一切。不然的话，我们现在的生活会是什么样！”
"What sort of a life will mine be now!" echoed mournfully in Litvinov's soul.
“我的生活会是什么样子！”这句话在李特维诺夫内心悲哀地回响着。
Tatyana went towards the door of the bedroom.
塔吉娅娜走向卧室的门。
"I will ask you to leave me alone for a little time, Grigory Mihalitch—we will see each other again, we will talk again. All this has been so unexpected I want to collect myself a little... leave me alone... spare my pride. We shall see each other again." And uttering these words, Tatyana hurriedly withdrew and locked the door after her.
“我请您让我单独呆一会儿，格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇，我们会再见面的，以后再谈吧。这一切都太出乎我的意料，我必须好好冷静一下……让我单独呆着……给我留点尊严。我们还会再见面的。”说完这些，塔吉亚娜迅速地跑开了，并锁上了身后的门。
Litvinov went out into the street like a man dazed and stunned; in the very depths of his heart something dark and bitter lay hid, such a sensation must a man feel who has murdered another; and at the same time he felt easier as though he had at last flung off a hated load. Tatyana's magnanimity had crushed him, he felt vividly all that he had lost ... and yet? with his regret was mingled irritation; he yearned towards Irina as to the sole refuge left him, and felt bitter against her. For some time Litvinov's feelings had been every day growing more violent and more complex; this complexity tortured him, exasperated him, he was lost in this chaos. He thirsted for one thing; to get out at last on to the path, whatever it might be, if only not to wander longer in this incomprehensible half-darkness. Practical people of Litvinov's sort ought never to be carried away by passion, it destroys the very meaning of their lives… But nature cares nothing for logic, our human logic; she has her own, which we do not recognize and do not acknowledge till we are crushed under its wheel.
李特维诺夫茫然而又不知所措地走在大街上。在他的内心的最深处有着一种阴暗而又痛苦的感觉，这种感觉就像谋杀一个人后的感觉一样。同时他又感到了一种轻松，像是终于卸下了心中一个可恨的包袱。塔吉娅娜的大度让他心痛万分，他真切地感觉到他正在失去的一切……然而这又能怎样呢？带着万分懊悔和恼怒之情，他苦苦思念着伊琳娜，似乎她就是他唯一的避难处，却又痛恨着她。此后的一段时间，李特维诺夫的内心变得日益激烈和复杂。这种复杂的情感折磨着他，激怒着他，他在混乱中迷失了自己。他唯一渴望的是：最终找到一条路，不管这是一条什么样的路，只要不在这种迷茫和半明半暗中徘徊就行了。像李特维诺夫这类务实的人本来任何时候都不应该被情感冲昏头脑的，因为这样会毁掉他们生活的意义……但是人性才不管什么逻辑——人类的逻辑呢。她有着她自己的一套逻辑，尽管直到我们被这种逻辑的车轮压碎的那一刻，我们才会意识到她并认可她。
On parting from Tatyana, Litvinov held one thought in his mind, to see Irina; he set off, indeed, to see her. But the general was at home, so at least the porter told him, and he did not care to go in, he did not feel himself capable of hypocrisy, and he moved slowly off towards the Konversation Hall. Litvinov's incapacity for hypocrisy was evident that day to both Voroshilov and Pishtchalkin, who happened to meet him; he simply blurted out to the former that he was empty as a drum; to the latter that he bored every one to extinction; it was lucky indeed that Bindasov did not come across him; there would certainly have been a "grosser Scandal." Both the young men were stupefied; Voroshilov went so far as to ask himself whether his honor, as an officer did not demand satisfaction? But like Gogol's lieutenant, Pirogov, he calmed himself with bread and butter in a café. Litvinov caught sight in the distance of Kapitolina Markovna running busily from shop to shop in her striped mantle… He felt ashamed to face the good, absurd, generous old lady. Then he recalled Potugin, their conversation yesterday… Then something was wafted to him, something intangible and unmistakable: if a falling shadow shed a fragrance, it could not be more elusive, but he felt at once that it was Irina near him, and in fact she appeared a few paces from him, arm-in-arm with another lady; their eyes met at once. Irina probably noticed something peculiar in the expression of Litvinov's face; she stopped before a shop, in which a number of tiny wooden clocks of Black Forest make were exhibited, and summoning him by a motion of her head, she pointed to one of these clocks, and calling upon him to admire a charming clock-face with a painted cuckoo above it, she said, not in a whisper, but as though finishing a phrase begun, in her ordinary tone of voice, much less likely to attract the attention of outsiders, "Come in an hour's time, I shall be alone." But at this moment the renowned lady-killer Monsieur Verdier swooped down upon her, and began to fall into ecstasies over the color, feuille morte, of her gown and the low-crowned Spanish hat she wore tilted almost down to her eyebrows… Litvinov vanished in the crowd.
与塔吉娅娜分别之后，李特维诺夫头脑中只有一个念头，那就是去见伊琳娜。事实上，他真得出发去见她了。但是那个将军在家——至少那个守门人是这样说的——于是他不想再进去了，因为他觉得自己不善于惺惺作态，于是他慢慢地走向晤谈厅。李特维诺夫的不善掩饰在他偶遇沃罗希洛夫和夏尔金的那天体现无遗。他直接就冲着沃罗希洛夫说他空虚得像一面鼓；又对另外一个人说他无聊得要死。幸好，宾达索夫没有碰到他，不然肯定会有一出好戏。两位年轻人都惊呆了。沃罗希洛夫甚至在想，他的官员尊荣难道还不足以令人满意？但是他像果戈理所刻画的庇罗戈夫中尉一样，跑到咖啡厅吃了些三明治以让自己平静下来。李特维诺夫远远地看到卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜披着她的那个条纹披肩，忙碌地在各个商店跑进跑出……他觉得自己愧对这位善良、滑稽而慷慨的老太太。接着，他想起了他昨天和波图金的谈话……此时，他明显地感觉到一种不可捉摸却又确凿无疑的东西向他飘荡过来：如果说那是由飘落下来的影子散发出来的清香，那就太诡异了，但是他马上感觉到是伊琳娜向他走过来了。果不其然，离他几步之遥的地方，伊琳娜挽着另外一位女士的手出现了。他们的目光立刻交汇在一起。伊琳娜很可能注意到了李特维诺夫的面部表情有些异样。她在一家展示着许多黑森林树木制造的小木钟的商店前止步了，向他点头示意，唤他来看其中那个钟面上画着布谷鸟的漂亮的闹钟。与此同时，她并没有压低声音，而是以平常的、更不大可能引起他人注意的语气对他说：“一个小时后来见我，那个时候只有我一个人。”但正是在这个时候，那位著名的女性杀手维尔第先生突然到来，开始滔滔不绝地赞赏她衣服颜色以及头上歪戴着的那顶几乎低至眉间的西班牙式王冠帽。李特维诺夫消失在人群之中。
