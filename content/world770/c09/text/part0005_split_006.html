VII  

七  

The fear of what this side of her character might have led her to do made me nervous for days afterward.
接下来的好几天，我都很担心，怕她性格的这一面会导致她做出什么事来。
I waited for an intimation from Miss Tita; I almost figured to myself that it was her duty to keep me informed, to let me know definitely whether or no Miss Bordereau had sacrificed her treasures.
我等蒂塔小姐给我带来什么消息。我几乎认定，她有责任随时通知我，让我确切地了解博尔德罗小姐有没有牺牲掉她的财富。
But as she gave no sign, I lost patience and determined to judge so far as was possible with my own senses.
但是她毫无动静，我不耐烦了起来，决定尽量靠自己的感觉去判断。
I sent late one afternoon to ask if I might pay the ladies a visit, and my servant came back with surprising news. Miss Bordereau could be approached without the least difficulty; she had been moved out into the sala and was sitting by the window that overlooked the garden.
一天傍晚，我派人询问是不是可以去拜访女士们，我的仆人回来告诉我一条惊人的消息。博尔德罗小姐允许我见她，一点儿没有为难。她已经出来了，在正厅里，坐在可以俯瞰外面花园的那扇窗边。
I descended and found this picture correct; the old lady had been wheeled forth into the world and had a certain air, which came mainly perhaps from some brighter element in her dress, of being prepared again to have converse with it.
我下楼来，发现这情形正合我意。这个老妇人的生活已经向前推进，进入了这个世界，还带着某种气息，准备再度同世界打交道，这个大概主要是来自于她礼服裙上亮丽的元素。
It had not yet, however, begun to flock about her; she was perfectly alone and, though the door leading to her own quarters stood open, I had at first no glimpse of Miss Tita.
但是，世界还没有准备好要集结在她四周，尽管那扇通往她的角落的门已经打开了，她也是孤独的，我一开始没有看到蒂塔小姐。
The window at which she sat had the afternoon shade and, one of the shutters having been pushed back, she could see the pleasant garden, where the summer sun had by this time dried up too many of the plants—she could see the yellow light and the long shadows.
她坐的窗边，下午正好背阴，其中一扇百叶窗被拉开了，她能看见令人愉悦的花园。此时的花园，夏日的艳阳已经烤蔫了太多的植物——她可以看见黄色的光线和长长的阴影。
"Have you come to tell me that you will take the rooms for six months more?" she asked as I approached her, startling me by something coarse in her cupidity almost as much as if she had not already given me a specimen of it.
“你是不是来告诉我，你还要再租六个月？”我走近的时候她问我。她带点粗俗的贪婪让我很是惊讶，仿佛她从来没有让我见识过这点似的。
Juliana's desire to make our acquaintance lucrative had been, as I have sufficiently indicated, a false note in my image of the woman who had inspired a great poet with immortal lines; but I may say here definitely that I recognized after all that it behooved me to make a large allowance for her.
朱莉安娜试图把我们之间的结识当成有利可图的机会。既然我的想法得到了足够的证实，这让我对这个曾经给伟大诗人以灵感，写出不朽诗歌的女人，产生了负面的印象。但是我还是要说，我最终认识到，我理应给她一大笔补贴。
It was I who had kindled the unholy flame; it was I who had put into her head that she had the means of making money.
正是我，点燃了这把邪恶之火；也是我，让她认为她有办法弄到钱。
She appeared never to have thought of that; she had been living wastefully for years, in a house five times too big for her, on a footing that I could explain only by the presumption that, excessive as it was, the space she enjoyed cost her next to nothing and that small as were her revenues they left her, for Venice, an appreciable margin.
她似乎从来没有想过这件事。她多年来一直过得铺张浪费，住在对她来说大了五倍的地方。她所依靠的，我只能靠猜测来解释：她住的地方尽管极大，却几乎不花她什么钱。尽管她收入甚少，对于在威尼斯的生活来说，也算是可观的了。
I had descended on her one day and taught her to calculate, and my almost extravagant comedy on the subject of the garden had presented me irresistibly in the light of a victim.
某一天，我突然造访，教她算计。我在花园这个问题上，进行了夸张喜剧的陈述，使自己不可避免地看起来像个牺牲品。
Like all persons who achieve the miracle of changing their point of view when they are old she had been intensely converted; she had seized my hint with a desperate, tremulous clutch.
和所有到了晚年才奇迹般转变观念的人那样，她完全改变了。她颤抖着，拼死抓住我提供的机会。
I invited myself to go and get one of the chairs that stood, at a distance, against the wall (she had given herself no concern as to whether I should sit or stand); and while I placed it near her I began, gaily, 
我自己走过去，找了张远远地靠在墙边的椅子（她也不关心我是坐着还是站着）。当我把椅子放在她旁边，开心地说：
"Oh, dear madam, what an imagination you have, what an intellectual sweep! I am a poor devil of a man of letters who lives from day to day. How can I take palaces by the year? My existence is precarious. I don't know whether six months hence I shall have bread to put in my mouth. I have treated myself for once; it has been an immense luxury. But when it comes to going on—!” 
“哦，亲爱的夫人，你的想象力真丰富，见解真透彻啊！我不过是个天天混日子的文人，一个可怜虫。我怎么能够在这样的大宅里住上整年呢？我的生活是很不稳定的。我都不知道，六个月后我还有没有钱吃饭。我好好地享受了一回，那是莫大的奢侈。但说到续租——!"
"Are your rooms too dear? If they are, you can have more for the same money," Juliana responded. "We can arrange, we can combinare, as they say here." 
“你房间的租金太贵？如果是这样，那么同样的金额，你可以多住几间。”朱莉安娜小姐回应道。“我们可以安排，我们可以合并，就像本地人说的那样。”
"Well yes, since you ask me, they are too dear," I said. "Evidently you suppose me richer than I am." 
“哦，是的，既然你问我，它们的确太贵了，”我说，“很明显，你认为我是富有的人，其实不是。”
She looked at me in her barricaded way. "If you write books don't you sell them?” 
她看着我，眼神像是穿越了重重障碍。“你写的书，你难道不卖吗？”
"Do you mean don't people buy them? A little—not so much as I could wish. Writing books, unless one be a great genius—and even then!—is the last road to fortune. I think there is no more money to be made by literature.” 
“你是说，人们为什么不买它们？买一点儿——没有我期望的那么多。写书，除非是伟大的天才——就算是天才又怎样！——绝不是致富之路。我觉得文学再也赚不了什么钱了。”
"Perhaps you don't choose good subjects. What do you write about?" Miss Bordereau inquired.
“说不定是你选材不对。你都写什么？”博尔德罗小姐问。
"About the books of other people. I'm a critic, an historian, in a small way.” I wondered what she was coming to.
“写关于他人的书。我是个评论家，稍稍也算是个历史学家。”我不知道她会怎么想。
"And what other people, now?" 
“那么，现在告诉我，是些什么其他人呢？”
"Oh, better ones than myself: the great writers mainly—the great philosophers and poets of the past; those who are dead and gone and can't speak for themselves.” 
“啊，比我厉害的人物：主要是伟大的作家——哲学大家和从前的诗人。那些业已去世的，不能为自己说话的人。”
"And what do you say about them?" 
“那你怎么写他们？”
"I say they sometimes attached themselves to very clever women!" I answered, laughing.
“我写他们有时候会爱上非常聪明的女人！”我笑着说。
I spoke with great deliberation, but as my words fell upon the air they struck me as imprudent. However, I risked them and I was not sorry, for perhaps after all the old woman would be willing to treat. It seemed to be tolerably obvious that she knew my secret: why therefore drag the matter out?
我的话是经过了思量的，但是当这些话说出口的时候，我还是觉得鲁莽。但是，我并不后悔冒这个险，因为，也许这个老妇人毕竟是愿意回报我的。看起来，她已经知道我的秘密了，这相当明显了：那为什么要拖拖沓沓呢？
But she did not take what I had said as a confession; she only asked: "Do you think it's right to rake up the past?” 
但她没有把我的话当成是自白。她只是问：“你觉得旧事重提合适吗？”
"I don't know that I know what you mean by raking it up; but how can we get at it unless we dig a little? The present has such a rough way of treading it down.” 
“我不知道自己是不是理解你所说的旧事重提，但是不挖掘挖掘，我们怎么知道呢？如今的世界正粗鲁地踏平了这些旧事。”
"Oh, I like the past, but I don't like critics," the old woman declared with her fine tranquility.
“噢，我喜欢往事，但我不喜欢批评家。”这个老女人优雅平静地宣布。
"Neither do I, but I like their discoveries." 
“我也不喜欢，但我喜欢他们的发现。”
"Aren't they mostly lies?” 
“他们大多数时候不都是说谎吗？”
"The lies are what they sometimes discover," I said, smiling at the quiet impertinence of this. "They often lay bare the truth." 
“说谎的，是他们有时候发现的东西，”我说道，笑着回应她无礼的话，“他们常常不过是把真相曝光而已。”
"The truth is God's, it isn't man's; we had better leave it alone. Who can judge of it—who can say?” 
“真相只有天知道，不是人知道的。我们最好别去打扰这些真相。谁能有定论——谁敢说？”
"We are terribly in the dark, I know," I admitted; "but if we give up trying, what becomes of all the fine things? What becomes of the work I just mentioned, that of the great philosophers and poets? It is all vain words if there is nothing to measure it by." 
“我们毫不知情，这我知道，”我承认，“但是如果我们放弃尝试，那些美好的事情将会变成什么样？我刚刚提到的那些作品将会变成什么样子？那些伟大的哲学家和诗人的成果。如果没有什么可以用来判断他们，这些不过是毫无意义的文字而已。”
"You talk as if you were a tailor," said Miss Bordereau whimsically; and then she added quickly, in a different manner, "This house is very fine; the proportions are magnificent. Today I wanted to look at this place again. I made them bring me out here. When your man came, just now, to learn if I would see you, I was on the point of sending for you, to ask if you didn't mean to go on. I wanted to judge what I'm letting you have. This sala is very grand," she pursued, like an auctioneer, moving a little, as I guessed, her invisible eyes. "I don't believe you often have lived in such a house, eh?” 
“你说话像个裁缝，”博尔德罗小姐怪里怪气地说着，然后又迅速补充一句，语气完全不同了，“这宅子很不错，面积很大。今天，我想再到处看看这个地方。我就让人把我推出来了。刚才你的仆人来，问我愿不愿意见你，我其实正准备叫人找你，问你愿不愿继续住下去。我也想合计一下，到底要租多少房间给你。这个正厅很宏大。”她试探着说，像个拍卖员，也许像我猜测的那样，稍稍转动她遮起来的眼睛。“我想，你也不常住在这样的宅子里，是吧？”
"I can't often afford to!" I said.
“我常常负担不起这样的地方！”我说。
"Well then, how much will you give for six months?" 
“那好吧，六个月，你打算给多少？”
I was on the point of exclaiming—and the air of excruciation in my face would have denoted a moral face—”Don't, Juliana; for HIS sake, don't!” But I controlled myself and asked less passionately: "Why should I remain so long as that?" 
我简直要喊出声了——我脸上冷酷的表情也许表明了一种内心的想法——“不，朱莉安娜，看在他的面子上，不要这样！”但我控制住了自己，语气淡了下来：“我住那么久做什么呢？”
"I thought you liked it," said Miss Bordereau with her shriveled dignity.
“我以为你喜欢这里。”博尔德罗小姐用她那邹巴巴的威严说道。
"So I thought I should." 
“我也以为我喜欢这里。”
For a moment she said nothing more, and I left my own words to suggest to her what they might. I half-expected her to say, coldly enough, that if I had been disappointed we need not continue the discussion, and this in spite of the fact that I believed her now to have in her mind (however it had come there) what would have told her that my disappointment was natural.
有一阵，她无话可说，而我让我说的话向她表达出它们该表达的意思了。我有点儿希望她开口，用冷冰冰的语气，说如果我这么失望，那么我们就没有再讨论下去的必要了。这是很自然的事情，尽管我知道她现在已经想到了（不管她是怎么想到的）我失望的原因了。
But to my extreme surprise she ended by observing: "If you don't think we have treated you well enough perhaps we can discover some way of treating you better.” 
但是，让我极其吃惊的是，她最后竟说：“如果你觉得我们对你不够好，也许我们可以找到其他办法，对你好些。”
This speech was somehow so incongruous that it made me laugh again, and I excused myself by saying that she talked as if I were a sulky boy, pouting in the corner, to be "brought round." 
这话不知道为什么这么别扭，让我又笑了起来。我请她原谅，说她把我说得像是个躲在角落里撅嘴生闷气的孩子，被“哄得回心转意”似的。
I had not a grain of complaint to make; and could anything have exceeded Miss Tita's graciousness in accompanying me a few nights before to the Piazza?
我无可抱怨。有什么比得过蒂塔小姐那天晚上在大广场上和我一起时所表现出的亲切？
At this the old woman went on: "Well, you brought it on yourself!" And then in a different tone, "She is a very nice girl." 
这个老妇人接着我的话继续说：“嗯，这可是你自己说的！”然后，她又换了个语气，“她是个好姑娘。”
I assented cordially to this proposition, and she expressed the hope that I did so not merely to be obliging, but that I really liked her. Meanwhile I wondered still more what Miss Bordereau was coming to.
对于这个说法，我由衷地赞同。她表示，希望我不只是礼貌性地这样做，而是出自我对她的真心喜欢。同时，我在猜测博尔德罗小姐还有什么想要说的。
"Except for me, today," she said, "she has not a relation in the world." Did she by describing her niece as amiable and unencumbered wish to represent her as a parti?
“如今，除了我，”她说，“她在这世上没有别的亲人了。”她把自己的侄女说得性情温和，没有家庭拖累，难道是希望我追求她？
It was perfectly true that I could not afford to go on with my rooms at a fancy price and that I had already devoted to my undertaking almost all the hard cash I had set apart for it. My patience and my time were by no means exhausted, but I should be able to draw upon them only on a more usual Venetian basis.
我的确是没有办法负担价格这么昂贵的房租，我几乎已经把为这项工作而存起来的现款全部用在这上面了。我的耐性和时间倒是一点儿也没有耗尽，但是，我只能用一种威尼斯式的标准来慢慢地取用它们。
I was willing to pay the venerable woman with whom my pecuniary dealings were such a discord twice as much as any other padrona di casa would have asked, but I was not willing to pay her twenty times as much.
其实我愿意付钱给这位身体羸弱的老太太，以解决我和她这场不协调的金钱纠纷。这个价格是其他女房东要价的两倍，尽管如此，我不打算付给她二十倍的价格。
I told her so plainly, and my plainness appeared to have some success, for she exclaimed, "Very good; you have done what I asked—you have made an offer!” 
我直接告诉了她。我的直率看来奏效了，因为她大声说：“很好，你已经做了我要求的事情——你给了个数目！”
"Yes, but not for half a year. Only by the month." 
“是，但不是按半年付。我只按月付。”
"Oh, I must think of that then." She seemed disappointed that I would not tie myself to a period, and I guessed that she wished both to secure me and to discourage me; to say severely, "Do you dream that you can get off with less than six months? Do you dream that even by the end of that time you will be appreciably nearer your victory?" 
“哦，那我得考虑一下。”她看起来很失望，我没有把自己住的时间固定下来。我猜她既希望留住我，又想为难我。她本想严厉地说：“你还想六个月内就离开吗？你还梦想，六个月之后，自己就能离胜利更近了？”
What was more in my mind was that she had a fancy to play me the trick of making me engage myself when in fact she had annihilated the papers. There was a moment when my suspense on this point was so acute that I all but broke out with the question, and what kept it back was but a kind of instinctive recoil (lest it should be a mistake), from the last violence of self-exposure.
我想得更多的是，她有心戏弄我，让我全心投入，但是实际上，她却把文稿毁掉了。有时候，我的疑惑是那么强烈，我几乎要脱口问出声了，但是一种本能的畏缩心态让我控制住了自己（除非我做错了），最终没有很粗暴地把自己暴露出来。
She was such a subtle old witch that one could never tell where one stood with her. You may imagine whether it cleared up the puzzle when, just after she had said she would think of my proposal and without any formal transition, she drew out of her pocket with an embarrassed hand a small object wrapped in crumpled white paper. She held it there a moment and then she asked, "Do you know much about curiosities?" 
她是个如此敏感的老巫婆，和她打交道，别人心里总是没底。你可以想象一下，难题到底解决了没有：那时，她刚说了要考虑我的提议，也没有正式地改变话题，她就突然用一只不灵巧的手从口袋里掏出一个用皱巴巴白纸包着的小东西。她在手里拿了一阵，然后问道：“你懂这些稀奇的玩意吗？”
"About curiosities?" 
“稀奇的玩意？”
"About antiquities, the old gimcracks that people pay so much for today. Do you know the kind of price they bring?" 
“古玩。现在的人愿意花很多钱买这种小东西。你知道他们出多高的价吗？”
I thought I saw what was coming, but I said ingenuously, "Do you want to buy something?" 
我觉得自己看到了眉目，但是我还是很机灵地说：“您想要买什么东西？”
"No, I want to sell. What would an amateur give me for that?" She unfolded the white paper and made a motion for me to take from her a small oval portrait. I possessed myself of it with a hand of which I could only hope that she did not perceive the tremor, and she added, "I would part with it only for a good price." 
“不是，我想要卖。那么一个业余的古玩爱好者会给我多少钱？”她展开白纸，示意我从她手里拿一个椭圆的小画像。我单手拿过来，希望她不要发现我的颤抖。然后她又说：“出价合适的话我就舍得割爱。”
At the first glance I recognized Jeffrey Aspern, and I was well aware that I flushed with the act. As she was watching me however I had the consistency to exclaim, "What a striking face! Do tell me who it is." 
我一眼就认出了杰弗里·阿斯本，我也清楚地意识到自己激动得脸红了起来。既然她在看着我，我还是稳住了，惊呼道：“多么引人注目的脸！请务必告诉我他是谁。”
"It's an old friend of mine, a very distinguished man in his day. He gave it to me himself, but I'm afraid to mention his name, lest you never should have heard of him, critic and historian as you are. I know the world goes fast and one generation forgets another. He was all the fashion when I was young.” 
“他是我的一位老朋友，在他年轻的时候，是个非常杰出的人。他亲自把这个画像给我，但是我不敢说出他的名字。不然，尽管你是批评家和历史学家，你可能却从没有听说过他。我知道世界变化快，一代人会很快忘掉另一代人。我年轻的时候，他非常地著名。”
She was perhaps amazed at my assurance, but I was surprised at hers; at her having the energy, in her state of health and at her time of life, to wish to sport with me that way simply for her private entertainment—the humor to test me and practice on me.
我说得那么笃定，她可能觉得很惊人，但我更惊讶于她说的话。以她的精力，她的健康状况和她的高龄，她竟然仅仅为了她私人的娱乐，想和我开玩笑——还有闲情考验我和戏耍我。
This, at least, was the interpretation that I put upon her production of the portrait, for I could not believe that she really desired to sell it or cared for any information I might give her. What she wished was to dangle it before my eyes and put a prohibitive price on it. "The face comes back to me, it torments me," I said, turning the object this way and that and looking at it very critically.
至少，这解释了她给我看这个画像的原因。因为，我没有真的相信她想卖掉它，她也不关心我会给她怎样的信息。她希望的是，拿它在我眼前晃一晃，然后给出个让人望而却步的价格。“我想起这张脸了，这太让我激动了。”我说。我把这小物件翻来覆去，仔仔细细地看。
It was a careful but not a supreme work of art, larger than the ordinary miniature and representing a young man with a remarkably handsome face, in a high-collared green coat and a buff waistcoat. I judged the picture to have a valuable quality of resemblance and to have been painted when the model was about twenty-five years old. There are, as all the world knows, three other portraits of the poet in existence, but none of them is of so early a date as this elegant production.
这个作品画得很精细，但是不算上乘，比一般的微型作品大，画出了一位年轻男士非常英俊的脸，他身着一件高领的绿色外套和一件浅黄色马甲。我认为这幅画画得很像，画中人当时应该是二十五岁的样子。全世界都知道，现存的诗人画像还有另外三幅，但是没有哪一幅记录下了诗人年轻时的风采。
"I have never seen the original but I have seen other likenesses," I went on. "You expressed doubt of this generation having heard of the gentleman, but he strikes me for all the world as a celebrity. Now who is he? I can't put my finger on him—I can't give him a label. Wasn't he a writer? Surely he's a poet.” I was determined that it should be she, not I, who should first pronounce Jeffrey Aspern's name.
“我没有看过原图，但是我看见过其他相似的图，”我接着说，“你怀疑这代人有没有听说过这位先生，但据我所知，他是一位全世界闻名的人。那他是谁？我不能指出他是谁——我不能确定他。他是个作家？他肯定是位诗人。”我决定要让她，而不是我来先说出杰弗里·阿斯本的名字。
My resolution was taken in ignorance of Miss Bordereau's extremely resolute character, and her lips never formed in my hearing the syllables that meant so much for her. She neglected to answer my question but raised her hand to take back the picture, with a gesture which though ineffectual was in a high degree peremptory. "It's only a person who should know for himself that would give me my price," she said with a certain dryness.
我的决定完全忽视了博尔德罗小姐那坚定无比的个性。从她的口中我始终没有听到任何音节，说明那个画像对她多么有意义。她不回答我的问题，但是伸出手要拿回那个画像，虽然没有成功，却显得非常专断高傲。她干巴巴地说：“只有识货的人才给得出我心仪的价格。”
"Oh, then, you have a price?" I did not restore the precious thing; not from any vindictive purpose but because I instinctively clung to it. We looked at each other hard while I retained it.
“喔，那么，你心里有价位咯？”我没有把这珍贵的东西还回去。不是出于报复，而是本能地想要抓住它。我手里扣着这个东西的时候，我们都紧盯着对方。
"I know the least I would take. What it occurred to me to ask you about is the most I shall be able to get." 
“我知道我愿意接受的最低价格。我想要问你的是，我最高可以得到什么样的价位。”
She made a movement, drawing herself together as if, in a spasm of dread at having lost her treasure, she were going to attempt the immense effort of rising to snatch it from me. I instantly placed it in her hand again, saying as I did so, "I should like to have it myself, but with your ideas I could never afford it." 
她动了一下，把身体挺起来。好像处在一阵担心失去宝贝的恐惧中一样，她似乎打算用力站起身，从我手中抢走它。我马上把东西放回她手里，同时说道：“我自己也想要，但是根据你的说法，我恐怕负担不起。”
She turned the small oval plate over in her lap, with its face down, and I thought I saw her catch her breath a little, as if she had had a strain or an escape. This however did not prevent her saying in a moment, "You would buy a likeness of a person you don't know, by an artist who has no reputation?” 
她把这个椭圆形的肖像倒扣着放在膝上。我听见她喘了一口气，就像是过度劳累或是成功逃脱之后那样。但是，她停了一会儿，说：“你会买一个毫无名气的画家为一个你不知道的人所画的像吗？”
"The artist may have no reputation, but that thing is wonderfully well painted," I replied, to give myself a reason.
“那个画家也许没有名气，但是这画却画得不错。”我回答着，给自己找了个原因。
"It's lucky you thought of saying that, because the painter was my father.” 
“你真走运，想到这个理由，因为这个画家是我父亲。”
"That makes the picture indeed precious!" I exclaimed, laughing; and I may add that a part of my laughter came from my satisfaction in finding that I had been right in my theory of Miss Bordereau's origin.
“这使得这幅画更值钱了！”我笑着赞叹道。我笑，还有另一个原因，那就是，我发现我猜对了博尔德罗小姐的出身，这让我很高兴。
Aspern had of course met the young lady when he went to her father's studio as a sitter. I observed to Miss Bordereau that if she would entrust me with her property for twenty-four hours I should be happy to take advice upon it; but she made no answer to this save to slip it in silence into her pocket.
阿斯本肯定是去她爸爸那里画像时认识了这位年轻女士的。我对博尔德罗小姐说，如果她把这宝贝交给我二十四小时，那我就会很愿意向人请教一下。但是，她没有回答，只是安静地把它放回了自己的口袋。
This convinced me still more that she had no sincere intention of selling it during her lifetime, though she may have desired to satisfy herself as to the sum her niece, should she leave it to her, might expect eventually to obtain for it. "Well, at any rate I hope you will not offer it without giving me notice," I said as she remained irresponsive. "Remember that I am a possible purchaser." 
这使我确信，她没有真的打算在自己在世的时候卖掉它们，但她可能想知道，要是她把这留给她侄女，她最终可以换得多大一笔钱。“好，无论如何我希望你不要还没通知我就卖了它。”我说。她还是一言不发。“希望你记住，我也是个可能的买主。”
"I should want your money first!" she returned with unexpected rudeness; and then, as if she bethought herself that I had just cause to complain of such an insinuation and wished to turn the matter off, asked abruptly what I talked about with her niece when I went out with her that way in the evening.
“你得先给钱！”她回答，惊人的粗暴。接着，她好像认为我可能会因为她这番讥诮话心里不快，所以打算抹过这件事。于是她突然问起我那天晚上和她侄女出去时，聊了些什么话题。
"You speak as if we had set up the habit," I replied. "Certainly I should be very glad if it were to become a habit. But in that case I should feel a still greater scruple at betraying a lady's confidence.” 
“你说得好像我们已经养成了习惯似的，”我回答道，“要是那成了一种习惯，我一定会很高兴的。但是那样的话，我更不愿意泄露一位女士的秘密了。”
"Her confidence? Has she got confidence?" 
“她的秘密？她有秘密？”
"Here she is—she can tell you herself," I said; for Miss Tita now appeared on the threshold of the old woman's parlor. "Have you got confidence, Miss Tita? Your aunt wants very much to know." 
“哦，她来了——她可以亲自告诉你。”我说。因为这时候，蒂塔小姐已经出现在老太太会客室的门口了。“你有什么秘密吗？蒂塔小姐。你的姑妈非常想知道。”
"Not in her, not in her!" the younger lady declared, shaking her head with a dolefulness that was neither jocular nor affected. "I don't know what to do with her; she has fits of horrid imprudence. She is so easily tired—and yet she has begun to roam—to drag herself about the house.” And she stood looking down at her immemorial companion with a sort of helpless wonder, as if all their years of familiarity had not made her perversities, on occasion, any more easy to follow.
“我不告诉她，不告诉她！”这位年轻的女士宣布道。可怜巴巴地摇着头，既不诙谐，也不做作。“我都不知道拿她怎么办，她一阵阵地冒失得叫人害怕。她很容易感觉疲倦——然而她还开始四处闲逛——自己在房子周围转转。”而她低头看着这位年老的同伴，带着无助茫然的神情，就像这么多年亲密的接触，并没有使对方的反复无常在某些时候变得比较容易接受。
"I know what I'm about. I'm not losing my mind. I daresay you would like to think so," said Miss Bordereau with a cynical little sigh.
“我知道我在做什么。我没有发疯。我敢说你会这样以为。”博尔德罗小姐轻声叹了口气，嘲笑地说。
"I don't suppose you came out here yourself. Miss Tita must have had to lend you a hand," I interposed with a pacifying intention.
“我想你不是自己出来的吧。蒂塔小姐一定帮了你一把。”我说道，起个和解之心。
"Oh, she insisted that we should push her; and when she insists!" said Miss Tita in the same tone of apprehension; as if there were no knowing what service that she disapproved of her aunt might force her next to render.
“哦，她坚持要我们推她出来。而她如果坚持要做什么事情，那就得照办！”蒂塔小姐用同样忧伤的语调说，就像完全不知道接下来，她姑妈将会要她做什么她不乐意的事情似的。
"I have always got most things done I wanted, thank God! The people I have lived with have humored me," the old woman continued, speaking out of the gray ashes of her vanity.
“大部分事情还能照着我的意图去做，真是谢天谢地！和我朝夕相处的人全都迁就我。”老太太接着说，带了点儿她残存的自负。
"I suppose you mean that they have obeyed you." 
“我猜你是想说，他们全都服从你。”
"Well, whatever it is, when they like you." 
“哦，随便吧。当他们喜欢你，就会那样。”
"It's just because I like you that I want to resist," said Miss Tita with a nervous laugh.
“正是因为我喜欢你，我才要违背你的意愿。”蒂塔小姐紧张地笑笑说。
"Oh, I suspect you'll bring Miss Bordereau upstairs next to pay me a visit," I went on; to which the old lady replied: "Oh, no; I can keep an eye on you from here!" 
“哦，我想你下次会送博尔德罗小姐上楼，到我那里看看。”我说。老太太听完之后回应说：“哦，不。我在这里就看得到你了！”
"You are very tired; you will certainly be ill tonight!" cried Miss Tita.
“你很累了，你今晚一定会觉得不舒服的！”蒂塔小姐叫道。
"Nonsense, my dear; I feel better at this moment than I have done for a month. Tomorrow I shall come out again. I want to be where I can see this clever gentleman." 
“无所谓，亲爱的，我一个月以来，现在感觉最好。我明天还要出来。我想呆在可以看到这位聪明的先生的地方。”
"Shouldn't you perhaps see me better in your sitting room?" I inquired.
“也许，在你的起居室见我更好？”我问道。
"Don't you mean shouldn't you have a better chance at me?" she returned, fixing me a moment with her green shade.
“你是说你就更有机会可以看清楚我了？”她回答，用她绿色的罩子盯着我看了一会儿。
"Ah, I haven't that anywhere! I look at you but I don't see you.” 
“噢，我哪有那种想法！我看着你，但是却从没有看清楚你。”
"You excite her dreadfully—and that is not good," said Miss Tita, giving me a reproachful, appealing look.
“你让她太激动了——那可不好。”蒂塔小姐说，又是祈求，又是责备地看着我。
"I want to watch you—I want to watch you!" the old lady went on.
“我想看住你——我想看住你！”老太太继续说。
"Well then, let us spend as much of our time together as possible—I don't care where—and that will give you every facility.” 
“那好，那我们就尽可能多花时间呆在一起——我不在乎在哪里——只要你方便就行。”
"Oh, I've seen you enough for today. I'm satisfied. Now I'll go home.” 
“啊，我今天见你的时间已经够了。我很满意。现在我要回去了。”
Miss Tita laid her hands on the back of her aunt's chair and began to push, but I begged her to let me take her place.
蒂塔小姐把手放在她姑妈的轮椅后面，开始推椅子，但我请她让我代替她做这个。
"Oh, yes, you may move me this way—you shan't in any other!” 
“噢，对了，你可以这样推我——但是别的方式就不行！”
Miss Bordereau exclaimed as she felt herself propelled firmly and easily over the smooth, hard floor. Before we reached the door of her own apartment she commanded me to stop, and she took a long, last look up and down the noble sala.
博尔德罗小姐感叹道，因为她感觉自己被稳稳当当地，轻松地推过了平滑而坚硬的地板。在我们到达她房门之前，她要我停下来，然后她长长地，告别般地上下打量了这个宏大的正厅。
"Oh, it's a magnificent house!" she murmured; after which I pushed her forward. When we had entered the parlor Miss Tita told me that she should now be able to manage, and at the same moment the little red-haired donna came to meet her mistress. Miss Tita's idea was evidently to get her aunt immediately back to bed. I confess that in spite of this urgency I was guilty of the indiscretion of lingering; it held me there to think that I was nearer the documents I coveted—that they were probably put away somewhere in the faded, unsociable room.
“嗯，这真是个宏伟的宅子！”她低声说。然后，我又推着她朝前走。当我们进了会客室，蒂塔小姐告诉我，她现在可以自己来了。同时，红头发的小女仆也来迎见她的女主人。蒂塔小姐想要立即让她的姑妈回到床上去。我承认，尽管她很急迫，我还是有种很轻率、想要留下的罪恶感。这让我认为，我离自己觊觎的那些文稿更加接近了——又猜想它们可能被收在这个陈旧孤寂的房间里的某个地方。
The place had indeed a bareness which did not suggest hidden treasures; there were no dusky nooks nor curtained corners, no massive cabinets nor chests with iron bands.
这个房间看起来光秃秃的，没什么地方像是可以藏珍宝的。没什么灰扑扑的隐蔽处，或是用帘子遮起来的角落，没有大柜子，也没有铁条箍起来的箱子。
Moreover it was possible, it was perhaps even probable that the old lady had consigned her relics to her bedroom, to some battered box that was shoved under the bed, to the drawer of some lame dressing table, where they would be in the range of vision by the dim night lamp.
而且，很可能，极有可能这个老太太把她的遗物放在她的卧室里，放在某个旧箱子里，推到床底下，或是放在某个缺了腿的梳妆台的抽屉里，那里正好在昏暗夜灯的照射范围之内。
Nonetheless I scrutinized every article of furniture, every conceivable cover for a hoard, and noticed that there were half a dozen things with drawers, and in particular a tall old secretary, with brass ornaments of the style of the Empire—a receptacle somewhat rickety but still capable of keeping a great many secrets. I don't know why this article fascinated me so, inasmuch as I certainly had no definite purpose of breaking into it; but I stared at it so hard that Miss Tita noticed me and changed color.
然而，我审视了每一件家具，每一处适宜隐藏的盖子。我注意到，有五六件家具有抽屉，尤其是一个高高的旧写字台，上面有黄铜的装饰，是帝国式的——看起来是个摇摇晃晃的容器，但是仍然装得下不少的秘密。我不知道为什么这个家具这么吸引我，因为我的确没有打定主意要撬开它。但是我这么瞪大眼睛盯着它，让蒂塔小姐注意到了我，她脸色也变了。
Her doing this made me think I was right and that wherever they might have been before the Aspern papers at that moment languished behind the peevish little lock of the secretary. it was hard to remove my eyes from the dull mahogany front when I reflected that a simple panel divided me from the goal of my hopes; but I remembered my prudence and with an effort took leave of Miss Bordereau. To make the effort graceful I said to her that I should certainly bring her an opinion about the little picture.
她的举动让我相信我是对的，不管阿斯本文稿之前在什么地方，此刻，它一定就在这张写字台恼人的小锁后搁置着。我难以把眼光从这桃木桌面移开，我在想，不过是一块木板，就把我和我希望的目标隔开了。但是我意识到自己该谨慎，于是强迫自己离开了博尔德罗小姐的房间。为了让自己的努力显得优雅一点儿，我对她说，我一定会为那张小画问点儿意见的。
"The little picture?" Miss Tita asked, surprised.
“小画？”蒂塔小姐惊讶地问。
"What do YOU know about it, my dear?" the old woman demanded. "You needn't mind. I have fixed my price.” 
“你知道什么啊，亲爱的？”老太太说，“你别管。我已经定了价了。”
"And what may that be?" 
“那定价是多少？”
"A thousand pounds." 
“一千英镑。”
"Oh Lord!" cried poor Miss Tita irrepressibly.
“哦，主啊！”蒂塔小姐忍不住惊呼起来。
"Is that what she talks to you about?" said Miss Bordereau.
“你们谈的就是这个吗？”博尔德罗小姐问。
"Imagine your aunt's wanting to know!” 
“想想看，你的姑妈多想知道！”
I had to separate from Miss Tita with only those words, though I should have liked immensely to add, "For heaven's sake meet me tonight in the garden!” 
我只能说这些来和蒂塔小姐道别，虽然我非常想补充一句：“老天在上，今晚到园子里见我吧！”
