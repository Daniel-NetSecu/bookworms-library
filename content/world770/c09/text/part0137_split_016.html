CHAPTER XVII  

第十七章  

A week passed, and no news arrived of Mr. Rochester: ten days, and still he did not come. Mrs. Fairfax said she should not be surprised if he were to go straight from the Leas to London, and thence to the Continent, and not show his face again at Thornfield for a year to come; he had not unfrequently quitted it in a manner quite as abrupt and unexpected. When I heard this, I was beginning to feel a strange chill and failing at the heart. I was actually permitting myself to experience a sickening sense of disappointment; but rallying my wits, and recollecting my principles, I at once called my sensations to order; and it was wonderful how I got over the temporary blunder—how I cleared up the mistake of supposing Mr. Rochester's movements a matter in which I had any cause to take a vital interest. Not that I humbled myself by a slavish notion of inferiority: on the contrary, I just said— 
一周过去了，罗切斯特先生仍然毫无音讯。十天过去了，他还没回来。费尔法克斯太太说即使他从里斯直接去了伦敦，然后又去欧洲大陆，一年都不回到桑菲尔德，她也不会觉得奇怪。他让人无法预料的突然离开，这种事并不是不常发生。我听她这样说，开始感到一种奇怪的凉意，心里一阵失落。事实上，我是在放纵自己沉浸于病态的失望中，但是我重振我的智慧，回忆起我的原则，立即把感情恢复了秩序。真是神奇，我现在可以很好地纠正一时的过失，可以澄清我的错误认识，即认为罗切斯特先生的行踪关系到我的切身利益。我并不是身怀奴性而自卑，相反，我只说——
"You have nothing to do with the master of Thornfield, further than to receive the salary he gives you for teaching his protegee, and to be grateful for such respectful and kind treatment as, if you do your duty, you have a right to expect at his hands. Be sure that is the only tie he seriously acknowledges between you and him; so don't make him the object of your fine feelings, your raptures, agonies, and so forth. He is not of your order: keep to your caste, and be too self-respecting to lavish the love of the whole heart, soul, and strength, where such a gift is not wanted and would be despised.” 
“你与桑菲尔德的主人毫无关系，你只是他被监护人的教师，因此接受他的薪水，对这种恭敬善意的待遇心存感激。你履行了职责就有权利得到这些。一定要清楚这是他唯一真正承认的和你之间的联系。所以不要把他当作你爱慕、欢喜和痛苦等等感情的对象。他不属于你的阶层，所以好好呆在你的阶层中，要自尊自爱，不要把整颗心的爱、灵魂和精力都浪费在不被需要，甚至会被鄙视的地方。”
I went on with my day's business tranquilly; but ever and anon vague suggestions kept wandering across my brain of reasons why I should quit Thornfield; and I kept involuntarily framing advertisements and pondering conjectures about new situations: these thoughts I did not think check; they might germinate and bear fruit if they could.
我每天平静地做自己的事，但是脑子里时不时地会萦绕模糊的想法，考虑我应该离开桑菲尔德的原因。我甚至会无意识地构思求职广告，猜测着新的工作。我不想对这些想法加以控制，它们很可能会发芽、甚至结果。
Mr. Rochester had been absent upwards of a fortnight, when the post brought Mrs. Fairfax a letter.
邮递员送了一封信给费尔法克斯太太，这时罗切斯特先生已经出门两周多了。
"It is from the master," said she, as she looked at the direction. "Now I suppose we shall know whether we are to expect his return or not." 
“是主人写的，”她看着信封上的内容说道。“我想现在我们就能知道是不是该等他回来了。”
And while she broke the seal and perused the document, I went on taking my coffee (we were at breakfast): it was hot, and I attributed to that circumstance a fiery glow which suddenly rose to my face. Why my hand shook, and why I involuntarily spilt half the contents of my cup into my saucer, I did not choose to consider.
她拆开信封后仔细看信，我继续喝咖啡（当时我们在吃早饭）咖啡很烫，我认为那是我脸上突然感到一阵火热的原因。我的手为什么发抖，我为什么不自觉地把一半咖啡洒到了碟子中，我决定不去深究。
"Well, I sometimes think we are too quiet; but we run a chance of being busy enough now: for a little while at least," said Mrs. Fairfax, still holding the note before her spectacles.
“嗯，我有时觉得我们这儿太安静了，但现在我们有机会忙碌起来啦，至少得有一段时间啦。”费尔法克斯太太说，仍把那封信举在眼镜前。
Ere I permitted myself to request an explanation, I tied the string of Adele's pinafore, which happened to be loose: having helped her also to another bun and refilled her mug with milk, I said, nonchalantly— 
我允许自己像她寻求解释之前，先把阿德拉碰巧松散的围嘴系好，又给她拿一个面包，把她的杯子重新加满牛奶。之后，我若无其事地说——
"Mr. Rochester is not likely to return soon, I suppose?" 
“我想罗切斯特先生不会很快回来吧？”
"Indeed he is—in three days, he says: that will be next Thursday; and not alone either. I don't know how many of the fine people at the Leas are coming with him: he sends directions for all the best bedrooms to be prepared; and the library and drawing rooms are to be cleaned out; I am to get more kitchen hands from the George Inn, at Millcote, and from wherever else I can; and the ladies will bring their maids and the gentlemen their valets: so we shall have a full house of it.” And Mrs. Fairfax swallowed her breakfast and hastened away to commence operations.
“他就要回来了——三天后。他说他下周四回来，而且不是独自一人。我不知道有多少贵人从里斯随他到来，他指示要把家里所有最好的卧室都准备好，还要清理书房和客厅。我要从米尔科特的乔治旅馆，还有我能找到的其他地方再雇几名厨师。到访的女士们会带上她们的女仆，先生们也会带上他们的随从，我们整座房子都会人满为患的。”费尔法克斯太太匆忙吃完早饭，就急急地去开始工作了。
The three days were, as she had foretold, busy enough. I had thought all the rooms at Thornfield beautifully clean and well arranged; but it appears I was mistaken. Three women were got to help; and such scrubbing, such brushing, such washing of paint and beating of carpets, such taking down and putting up of pictures, such polishing of mirrors and lustres, such lighting of fires in bedrooms, such airing of sheets and featherbeds on hearths, I never beheld, either before or since. Adele ran quite wild in the midst of it: the preparations for company and the prospect of their arrival seemed to throw her into ecstasies. She would have Sophie to look over all her "toilettes," as she called frocks; to furbish up any that were "passees," and to air and arrange the new. For herself, she did nothing but caper about in the front chambers, jump on and off the bedsteads, and lie on the mattresses and piled up bolsters and pillows before the enormous fires roaring in the chimneys. From school duties she was exonerated: Mrs. Fairfax had pressed me into her service, and I was all day in the storeroom, helping (or hindering) her and the cook; learning to make custards and cheesecakes and French pastry, to truss game and garnish desert dishes.
就像她说的一样，这三天的确非常忙碌。我曾以为桑菲尔德所有的房间都装饰华美、一尘不染、秩序井然——但我显然错了。新雇了三名女人来帮忙。这种热火朝天的情景是我此前之后都没见过。擦洗，刷洗，清洗漆过的家具，击打地毯以祛除灰尘，摘摘挂挂图画，擦洗镜子和灯架，在房间里生上火，在火炉上烘干床单和羽毛床垫等等。阿德拉在忙碌的人群中疯也似地来回穿梭，迎接客人的准备工作和宾客登堂的展望似乎让她陷入狂喜。她让索菲检查一下她所有的衣服，翻新破旧的，把新的晒一下并整理好。她自己无所事事，只是在前面的各个卧室里跳来跳去，在床架上跳上跳下，躺在床垫上和摞起来的长枕和靠枕上，熊熊大火在烟囱里烧得呼呼作响。她不用上课了，因为费尔法克斯太太要求我也帮忙干活，我整天都在储藏室里，给她和厨师帮忙（或帮倒忙），学做蛋奶冻、酪饼和法式糕点，以及捆扎野味，点缀甜点等。
The party was expected to arrive on Thursday afternoon, in time for dinner at six. During the intervening period I had no time to nurse chimeras; and I believe I was as active and gay as anybody—Adele excepted. Still, now and then, I received a damping check to my cheerfulness; and was, in spite of myself, thrown back on the region of doubts and portents, and dark conjectures. This was when I chanced to see the third storey staircase door (which of late had always been kept locked) open slowly, and give passage to the form of Grace Poole, in prim cap, white apron, and handkerchief; when I watched her glide along the gallery, her quiet tread muffled in a list slipper; when I saw her look into the bustling, topsy-turvy bedrooms,—just say a word, perhaps, to the charwoman about the proper way to polish a grate, or clean a marble mantelpiece, or take stains from papered walls, and then pass on. She would thus descend to the kitchen once a day, eat her dinner, smoke a moderate pipe on the hearth, and go back, carrying her pot of porter with her, for her private solace, in her own gloomy, upper haunt. Only one hour in the twenty-four did she pass with her fellow servants below; all the rest of her time was spent in some low ceiled, oaken chamber of the second storey: there she sat and sewed—and probably laughed drearily to herself,—as companionless as a prisoner in his dungeon.
按计划，参加聚会的人会在周四下午到达，正好赶上六点的晚宴。在此之前，我没有时间胡思乱想；而且我觉得我当时像其他人一样积极开心——除了阿德拉。不过，有时我审视自己的兴奋，仍感到失落，不可控制地重新陷入到疑虑、凶兆和黑暗猜测的旋涡中。有一次我偶然看到三楼楼梯处的门（最近常常紧锁着)慢慢打开了，格雷斯·普尔从中走出，整洁的帽子，白色围裙，拿着手绢。我看着她穿着一双布边拖鞋走在走廊里，悄然无声。我看到她往喧闹混乱的卧室里看时嘴里说了什么，或许她在教打杂的女佣应该怎么样擦壁炉，或怎么样清扫大理石壁炉台，或怎么样去除墙纸上的污点，说完她又往前走。她每天下楼到厨房来一次，吃午饭并在火炉边吸上一袋不多不少的烟，然后又回到楼上去，带着一罐黑啤酒，这是她独自一人在楼上阴森住处的个人慰藉。她每天二十四小时只有一个小时是和其他仆人在一起，其他时间都在三楼一间低矮的橡木房间里度过。她坐在那儿缝缝补补——或许还恐怖地对着自己大笑——就像地牢里的犯人一样孤独无伴。
The strangest thing of all was, that not a soul in the house, except me, noticed her habits, or seemed to marvel at them: no one discussed her position or employment; no one pitied her solitude or isolation. I once, indeed, overheard part of a dialogue between Leah and one of the charwomen, of which Grace formed the subject. Leah had been saying something I had not caught, and the charwoman remarked— 
最奇怪的是，房子里除了我之外没有一个人注意到她的习惯或对之感到奇怪。没有人议论她的地位或是她的工作职责。没有人可怜她的寂寞与孤单。我一次偶然听到了利厄和一位打杂女工的部分谈话，她们在谈论格雷斯。我没听到利厄说了什么，但听到了那个女仆说——
"She gets good wages, I guess?" 
“我猜她的工资不菲？”
"Yes," said Leah; "I wish I had as good; not that mine are to complain of,—there's no stinginess at Thornfield; but they're not one fifth of the sum Mrs. Poole receives. And she is laying by: she goes every quarter to the bank at Millcote. I should not wonder but she has saved enough to keep her independent if she liked to leave; but I suppose she's got used to the place; and then she's not forty yet, and strong and able for anything. It is too soon for her to give up business." 
“是，”利厄说，“我希望我也有她那么多的工资，我并不是抱怨我的少——桑菲尔德并不吝啬，但是我的工资还不到普尔夫人的五分之一。她还在存钱呢，每个季度都会到米尔科特的银行去。我觉得她一定已经攒了不少钱，就算要离开这儿也能独立生活了。但我觉得她已经习惯这个地方了，况且她还不到四十岁，身体强壮，什么都干得来。她现在放弃这差事还太早。”
"She is a good hand, I daresay," said the charwoman.
“我敢说她一定对工作非常拿手。”那个女佣说。
"Ah! —she understands what she has to do,—nobody better," rejoined Leah significantly; "and it is not every one could fill her shoes—not for all the money she gets.” 
“噢！——她知道自己该做什么——没人比她强，”利厄意味深长地回答说，“不是任何人都能代替她的职责——即使拿她那么多工资也不行。
"That it is not!" was the reply. "I wonder whether the master—” 
“当然不能！”那人应和着。“我很好奇主人会不会——”
The charwoman was going on; but here Leah turned and perceived me, and she instantly gave her companion a nudge.
那个女佣正继续说下去，但利厄转身看到了我，立即用胳膊推了那个女佣一下。
"Doesn't she know?" I heard the woman whisper.
“她不知道？”我听到那个女佣低声问。
Leah shook her head, and the conversation was of course dropped. All I had gathered from it amounted to this,—that there was a mystery at Thornfield; and that from participation in that mystery I was purposely excluded.
利厄摇了摇头，当然谈话也终止了。我从中得到的信息是桑菲尔德有一个秘密，而且我被故意排除在这个秘密之外。
Thursday came: all work had been completed the previous evening; carpets were laid down, bed hangings festooned, radiant white counterpanes spread, toilet tables arranged, furniture rubbed, flowers piled in vases: both chambers and saloons looked as fresh and bright as hands could make them. The hall, too, was scoured; and the great carved clock, as well as the steps and banisters of the staircase, were polished to the brightness of glass; in the dining room, the sideboard flashed resplendent with plate; in the drawing room and boudoir, vases of exotics bloomed on all sides.
星期四到了。一切工作都已经在前一天晚上完成了。地毯已经铺上，床上的帐幔已经装饰完毕，雪白色的床罩已经铺上，梳妆台已经布置完毕，家具已经擦洗一新，花儿也已经插入了花瓶。卧室和客厅看起来都像人力所及的那般焕然一新。大厅也已经擦洗过了；巨大的石雕钟表和楼梯以及栏杆都擦得像玻璃一样发亮；餐室里，餐具橱里的盘子光彩夺目；客厅和化妆室里，具有异国情调的花儿四处绽放。
Afternoon arrived: Mrs. Fairfax assumed her best black satin gown, her gloves, and her gold watch; for it was her part to receive the company,—to conduct the ladies to their rooms, &c. Adele, too, would be dressed: though I thought she had little chance of being introduced to the party that day at least. However, to please her, I allowed Sophie to apparel her in one of her short, full muslin frocks. For myself, I had no need to make any change; I should not be called upon to quit my sanctum of the schoolroom; for a sanctum it was now become to me,—"a very pleasant refuge in time of trouble." 
下午终于到了。费尔法克斯太太穿上了她最好的黑色缎子长袍，戴上她的手套和金表，因为她的工作是要接待客人——引导女士们到她们的房间去之类的。阿德拉也被盛装打扮，虽然我觉得她在聚会上亮相的机会很小，至少在那天。但是为了让她高兴，我让索菲给她穿上了一件宽松的短平纹细布外衣。至于我，我不需要有什么改变。应该没人会叫我离开教室。现在教室就像属于我私人一样——“受到困扰时一个完美的避难所”。
It had been a mild, serene spring day—one of those days which, towards the end of March or the beginning of April, rise shining over the earth as heralds of summer. It was drawing to an end now; but the evening was even warm, and I sat at work in the schoolroom with the window open.
那是一个温和安静的春日——正当三月底或四月初，阳光照耀大地，像是在揭开夏季的序曲。已经夕阳西下，但夜晚也很温暖。我坐在教室里工作，窗户打开着。
"It gets late," said Mrs. Fairfax, entering in rustling state. "I am glad I ordered dinner an hour after the time Mr. Rochester mentioned; for it is past six now. I have sent John down to the gates to see if there is anything on the road: one can see a long way from thence in the direction of Millcote.” She went to the window. "Here he is!" said she. "Well, John" (leaning out), "any news?" 
“天色晚了，”费尔法克斯说着走进来，长袍沙沙作响。“还好我安排晚餐时间比罗切斯特先生吩咐的时间推迟一个小时。现在都已经六点多了。我已经派约翰下去到大门口看看大道上有没有什么动静，从那儿可以往米尔科特方向看很远。”她走到窗户前。“他在那儿呢！”她说，“嗯，约翰”（她身体往外倾着）“看到什么了吗？”
"They're coming, ma'am," was the answer. "They'll be here in ten minutes." 
“夫人，他们来啦！”约翰回答道，“他们十分钟后就能到这儿。”
Adele flew to the window. I followed, taking care to stand on one side, so that, screened by the curtain, I could see without being seen.
阿德拉飞奔到窗前。我跟着她，小心地站在一边。在窗帘的掩护下，我可以看到外面，而外面的人却看不到我。
The ten minutes John had given seemed very long, but at last wheels were heard; four equestrians galloped up the drive, and after them came two open carriages. Fluttering veils and waving plumes filled the vehicles; two of the cavaliers were young, dashing looking gentlemen; the third was Mr. Rochester, on his black horse, Mesrour, Pilot bounding before him; at his side rode a lady, and he and she were the first of the party. Her purple riding habit almost swept the ground, her veil streamed long on the breeze; mingling with its transparent folds, and gleaming through them, shone rich raven ringlets.
约翰说的十分钟显得特别漫长，但是现在至少可以听到车轮的声音。四个人骑着马飞驰而来，后面跟着两辆敞篷马车。马车里面纱飘荡，羽毛摇曳。骑马的人中有两个是年轻潇洒的绅士。第三个是罗切斯特先生，他骑着他的黑马梅斯罗，派洛特在前面跳跃着。和罗切斯特先生并排前行的是一位女士，他们跑在所有人的前面。她紫色的骑马装几乎拖到了地上；长长的面纱在微风中飘扬；浓密乌黑的卷发在面纱透明的褶皱间若隐若现，相映生辉。
"Miss Ingram!" exclaimed Mrs. Fairfax, and away she hurried to her post below.
“英格拉姆小姐！”费尔法克斯太太叫喊道，并慌忙跑下去履行职责。
The cavalcade, following the sweep of the drive, quickly turned the angle of the house, and I lost sight of it. Adele now petitioned to go down; but I took her on my knee, and gave her to understand that she must not on any account think of venturing in sight of the ladies, either now or at any other time, unless expressly sent for: that Mr. Rochester would be very angry, &c. "Some natural tears she shed" on being told this; but as I began to look very grave, she consented at last to wipe them.
马队顺着弯曲的道路，瞬间转过屋角，从我的视线中消失了。阿德拉当时恳请我允许她下楼去，但是我把她抱到我腿上，让她理解不管是现在还是别的时候，她都不能以任何理由出现在那些女士面前，除非有人明确地让她去，否则罗切斯特先生会非常生气的，等等。她听到这些话“本能地洒下几滴泪水”，但是我的表情开始变得很严肃，她最终愿意擦干泪水。
A joyous stir was now audible in the hall: gentlemen's deep tones and ladies' silvery accents blent harmoniously together, and distinguishable above all, though not loud, was the sonorous voice of the master of Thornfield Hall, welcoming his fair and gallant guests under its roof. Then light steps ascended the stairs; and there was a tripping through the gallery, and soft cheerful laughs, and opening and closing doors, and, for a time, a hush.
大厅里传来一阵欢快的说话声，绅士们低沉的声音和女士们银铃般的声音和谐地交错在一起。但有一个不大却与众不同又铿锵有力的声音，那是桑菲尔德大厅的主人正在门前欢迎他美丽潇洒的客人。过了一会儿，轻快的脚步迈上了楼梯，而后轻盈地穿过走廊，柔和愉快的笑声和一阵开门关门声，接着是一片寂静。
"Elles changent de toilettes," said Adele; who, listening attentively, had followed every movement; and she sighed.
“她们在换衣服，”阿德拉说，她认真地听着下面的动静，听着每一个声响，叹了一口气。
"Chez maman," said she, "quand il y avait du monde, je le suivais partout, au salon et e leurs chambres; souvent je regardais les femmes de chambre coiffer et habiller les dames, et c'etait si amusant: comme cela on apprend.” 
“在我妈妈家，”她说，“当有很多人时，我可以到处跟着她们，到客厅和她们的房间去。我常常看女仆给女士们梳头穿衣，这非常有趣，因为我学到了很多东西。”
"Don't you feel hungry, Adele?" 
“阿德拉，你不饿吗？”
"Mais oui, mademoiselle: voile cinq ou six heures que nous n'avons pas mange." 
“小姐，我饿了。我们已经有五六个小时没吃东西了。”
"Well now, while the ladies are in their rooms, I will venture down and get you something to eat." 
“好吧，那我就趁女士们还都在她们的房间里，冒昧下去给你拿点东西吃吧。”
And issuing from my asylum with precaution, I sought a backstairs which conducted directly to the kitchen. All in that region was fire and commotion; the soup and fish were in the last stage of projection, and the cook hung over her crucibles in a frame of mind and body threatening spontaneous combustion. In the servants' hall two coachmen and three gentlemen's gentlemen stood or sat round the fire; the abigails, I suppose, were upstairs with their mistresses; the new servants, that had been hired from Millcote, were bustling about everywhere. Threading this chaos, I at last reached the larder; there I took possession of a cold chicken, a roll of bread, some tarts, a plate or two and a knife and fork: with this booty I made a hasty retreat. I had regained the gallery, and was just shutting the backdoor behind me, when an accelerated hum warned me that the ladies were about to issue from their chambers. I could not proceed to the schoolroom without passing some of their doors, and running the risk of being surprised with my cargo of victualage; so I stood still at this end, which, being windowless, was dark: quite dark now, for the sun was set and twilight gathering.
我小心地从我的“避难所”里出来，找到一个直通厨房的后楼梯。厨房里一片火光，喧喧嚷嚷的。汤和鱼马上就可以出锅了，厨师在坩埚前弯着腰，像是身心时刻都有自动燃烧起来的危险一样。仆人的大厅里，两个车夫和三位绅士的随从围着火炉或站或坐；我猜侍女们都在楼上陪着她们的女主人；从米尔科特新雇来的仆人在到处忙乱地跑着。我好不容易穿过混乱的人群，走到了食橱边。我拿了一只冷冻鸡，一个面包卷，一些馅饼，一两个盘子和一副刀叉。拿着这些战利品，我急忙往后退。我到了走廊里，正关上后门，突然一阵愈来愈响的曲调警示我女士们就要从她们的房间里出来了。我要去教室一定会经过她们的门口，而且要冒着撞见她们，我手中的满满的食物让她们大吃一惊的危险。因此我就在这头儿静静地站着，这儿没有窗户，很暗。现在太阳落山，夜幕降临使这儿更暗了。
Presently the chambers gave up their fair tenants one after another: each came out gaily and airily, with dress that gleamed lustrous through the dusk. For a moment they stood grouped together at the other extremity of the gallery, conversing in a key of sweet subdued vivacity: they then descended the staircase almost as noiselessly as a bright mist rolls down a hill. Their collective appearance had left on me an impression of highborn elegance, such as I had never before received.
现在她们一个个从卧室里出来了，轻快活泼，礼服在暮色中闪闪发光。她们在走廊的另一头围在一起，抑制着兴奋，声音甜美。然后她们顺着楼梯走下，安静地像一团明亮的雾滑下山坡一样。她们的集体亮相让我深深感到了她们出身高贵的优雅，我从未见过的优雅。
I found Adele peeping through the schoolroom door, which she held ajar. "What beautiful ladies!" cried she in English. "Oh, I wish I might go to them! Do you think Mr. Rochester will send for us by-and-bye, after dinner?” "No, indeed, I don't; Mr. Rochester has something else to think about. Never mind the ladies tonight; perhaps you will see them tomorrow: here is your dinner.” 
我发现阿德拉把教室的门半开着，透过门缝往外偷看。“多漂亮的女士呀！”她用英语大叫着。“噢，我要也能和她们一起就好了！你觉得晚宴过后罗切斯特先生会派人叫我们去吗？”“不，真的，我觉得不会。罗切斯特先生有别的事要考虑。今天别再想那些女士了，也许你明天可以看到她们。给你晚餐。”
She was really hungry, so the chicken and tarts served to divert her attention for a time. It was well I secured this forage, or both she, I, and Sophie, to whom I conveyed a share of our repast, would have run a chance of getting no dinner at all: every one downstairs was too much engaged to think of us. The dessert was not carried out till after nine and at ten footmen were still running to and fro with trays and coffee cups. I allowed Adele to sit up much later than usual; for she declared she could not possibly go to sleep while the doors kept opening and shutting below, and people bustling about. Besides, she added, a message might possibly come from Mr. Rochester when she was undressed; "et alors quel dommage!" 
她真的饿了，所以鸡肉和馅饼暂时分散了她的注意力。幸亏我刚去弄了食物，否则她和我还有索菲——我把我们的饭分给了索菲一些——很有可能吃不到晚饭，楼下的人都忙得顾不上想起我们。甜点在九点后才上。十点时，仆人还端着托盘和咖啡杯跑来跑去。我允许阿德拉比往常晚睡，因为她说楼下的门开开关关，人声鼎沸，她不可能睡得着。另外，她还说如果罗切斯特先生派人送来口信而她已经换了衣服，“该有多遗憾呀！”
I told her stories as long as she would listen to them; and then for a change I took her out into the gallery. The hall lamp was now lit, and it amused her to look over the balustrade and watch the servants passing backwards and forwards. When the evening was far advanced, a sound of music issued from the drawing room, whither the piano had been removed; Adele and I sat down on the top step of the stairs to listen. Presently a voice blent with the rich tones of the instrument; it was a lady who sang, and very sweet her notes were. The solo over, a duet followed, and then a glee: a joyous conversational murmur filled up the intervals. I listened long: suddenly I discovered that my ear was wholly intent on analysing the mingled sounds, and trying to discriminate amidst the confusion of accents those of Mr. Rochester; and when it caught them, which it soon did, it found a further task in framing the tones, rendered by distance inarticulate, into words.
只要她愿意听故事，我就讲给她听。然后我变换了一下方式，带她到走廊里去。大厅里灯火通明，阿德拉很开心地靠在栏杆上看仆人来回穿行。夜深了，音乐声从客厅里传出，钢琴之前就移到了那里。阿德拉和我坐在楼梯顶上听着。一个声音与钢琴圆润的声音交错在一起，是一个女士在唱歌，声音非常甜美。独唱后，一曲二重奏接上，接着是一首无伴奏的重唱曲。换曲时的空暇充满了愉快低沉的谈话声。我一直听着，突然我发现自己完全将听力集中于分析混在一起的声音，想从中分辨出罗切斯特先生的声音。不久，我就听出了他的声音，但由于离得太远了，我还要艰难地从他含糊不清的调子中猜出歌词。
The clock struck eleven. I looked at Adele, whose head leant against my shoulder; her eyes were waxing heavy, so I took her up in my arms and carried her off to bed. It was near one before the gentlemen and ladies sought their chambers.
钟表敲响了十一点。我看了看倚在我肩膀上的阿德拉，她的眼皮越来越下垂，于是我把她抱起来，送到床上。快到凌晨一点时，绅士们和女士们才开始回到他们的卧室。
The next day was as fine as its predecessor: it was devoted by the party to an excursion to some site in the neighbourhood. They set out early in the forenoon, some on horseback, the rest in carriages; I witnessed both the departure and the return. Miss Ingram, as before, was the only lady equestrian; and, as before, Mr. Rochester galloped at her side; the two rode a little apart from the rest. I pointed out this circumstance to Mrs. Fairfax, who was standing at the window with me— 
第二天和前一天一样晴朗。来宾们借此良日要到附近的某个地方去远足。他们上午很早就出发了，一些人骑着马，其他的坐在车里。我看着他们出发，又看着他们返回。英格拉姆小姐像之前一样，是唯一一位女骑手，罗切斯特先生也像之前一样在她旁边策马奔驰。两人与其他人有一些距离。我指着这个情景对和我一起站在窗户边的费尔法克斯太太说——
"You said it was not likely they should think of being married," said I, "but you see Mr. Rochester evidently prefers her to any of the other ladies." 
“你说他们不太可能会结婚，”我说，“但是你看，与其他女士相比，罗切斯特先生明显更喜欢她。”
"Yes, I daresay: no doubt he admires her.” 
“是呀，我敢说他无疑很仰慕她。”
"And she him," I added; "look how she leans her head towards him as if she were conversing confidentially; I wish I could see her face; I have never had a glimpse of it yet." "You will see her this evening," answered Mrs. Fairfax. "I happened to remark to Mr. Rochester how much Adele wished to be introduced to the ladies, and he said: 'Oh! let her come into the drawing room after dinner; and request Miss Eyre to accompany her.'" 
“而且她对他也一样，”我接着说，“看她把头凑到他跟前，好像秘密地说些什么；我要能看到她的脸就好了，我一眼还没见过她呢。”“你今晚就能见到她啦。”费尔法克斯太太回答说。“我碰巧跟罗切斯特先生说了阿德拉有多希望见到那些女士们，他说：‘哦，让她晚饭后来客厅吧，让爱小姐陪她来。’”
"Yes; he said that from mere politeness: I need not go, I am sure," I answered.
“嗯，他一定是出于礼貌才那样说，我确定我不需要去。”我回答说。
"Well, I observed to him that as you were unused to company, I did not think you would like appearing before so gay a party—all strangers; and he replied, in his quick way—'Nonsense! If she objects, tell her it is my particular wish; and if she resists, say I shall come and fetch her in case of contumacy.'" 
“哎，我告诉他你不太习惯在人群里出现，我觉得你不会喜欢参加这样热闹的宴会——全是陌生人。但他像往常一样快速的回了一句——‘胡说！如果她不愿来，就告诉她这是我特别要求的；如果她还拒绝，告诉她要是她一直倔强下去我会自己去叫她的。’”
"I will not give him that trouble," I answered. "I will go, if no better may be; but I don't like it. Shall you be there, Mrs. Fairfax?" 
“我不会让他那么麻烦的。”我回答，“如果只能这样，我会去的；但是我不喜欢。你会去吗，费尔法克斯太太？”
"No; I pleaded off, and he admitted my plea. I'll tell you how to manage so as to avoid the embarrassment of making a formal entrance, which is the most disagreeable part of the business. You must go into the drawing room while it is empty, before the ladies leave the dinner table; choose your seat in any quiet nook you like; you need not stay long after the gentlemen come in, unless you please: just let Mr. Rochester see you are there and then slip away—nobody will notice you.” 
“不会，我恳请他不要让我去，他同意了。我告诉你如何避免一次正式入场的尴尬，那是最让人不自在的地方。你一定要在女士们离开晚餐桌，客厅里没人的时候进去，选一个你喜欢的角落坐下。绅士们进去后一会儿你就可以走了，除非你还想呆在那儿。只要让罗切斯特先生看到你去了就行，然后就偷偷溜走——没有人会注意到你的。”
"Will these people remain long, do you think?" 
“你觉得这些人会在这儿常住吗？”
"Perhaps two or three weeks, certainly not more. After the Easter recess, Sir George Lynn, who was lately elected member for Millcote, will have to go up to town and take his seat; I daresay Mr. Rochester will accompany him: it surprises me that he has already made so protracted a stay at Thornfield.” 
“也许两三周，肯定不能再长了。复活节假期后，最近刚当选为米尔科特议员的乔治·林恩爵士要去赴任。我敢说罗切斯特先生一定会陪他去。他在桑菲尔德呆这么长时间已经让我觉得奇怪了。”
It was with some trepidation that I perceived the hour approach when I was to repair with my charge to the drawing room. Adele had been in a state of ecstasy all day, after hearing she was to be presented to the ladies in the evening; and it was not till Sophie commenced the operation of dressing her that she sobered down. Then the importance of the process quickly steadied her, and by the time she had her curls arranged in well smoothed, drooping clusters, her pink satin frock put on, her long sash tied, and her lace mittens adjusted, she looked as grave as any judge. No need to warn her not to disarrange her attire: when she was dressed, she sat demurely down in her little chair, taking care previously to lift up the satin skirt for fear she should crease it, and assured me she would not stir thence till I was ready. This I quickly was: my best dress (the silver-gray one, purchased for Miss Temple's wedding, and never worn since) was soon put on; my hair was soon smoothed; my sole ornament, the pearl brooch, soon assumed. We descended.
我意识到我要到客厅去完成任务的时间渐渐逼近，心里一阵焦虑。阿德拉听到晚上可以见那些女士们的消息后，一整天都很兴奋，直到索菲开始给她穿衣打扮，她才平静下来。穿衣打扮这个过程的重要性很快使她平静了，她的卷发很顺地排在一起，向下垂着。穿上了粉红色的绸缎外套，系上了长长的腰带，戴好了蕾丝手套，一切准备停当后，她看起来像法官一样严肃。毋庸提醒她不要弄乱了装束，因为她穿好后，就端庄地坐在她的小椅子上；坐下之前还小心地掀起绸缎裙子，怕压出褶来。而且她还向我保证在我准备好之前，她一定会安静地呆着。我迅速地穿上了我最好的裙子（一件银灰色的裙子，是为出席坦普尔小姐的婚礼而买的，但那次之后就没再穿过）；很快地梳好头，我唯一的首饰——一个珍珠胸针也很快戴好。我们下楼了。
Fortunately there was another entrance to the drawing room than that through the saloon where they were all seated at dinner. We found the apartment vacant; a large fire burning silently on the marble hearth, and wax candles shining in bright solitude, amid the exquisite flowers with which the tables were adorned. The crimson curtain hung before the arch: slight as was the separation this drapery formed from the party in the adjoining saloon, they spoke in so low a key that nothing of their conversation could be distinguished beyond a soothing murmur.
幸运的是，我们不用穿过宾客正在用餐的餐厅，因为客厅还有另外一个入口。客厅里空无一人。熊熊火光正在大理石壁炉里安静地燃烧着，蜡烛孤寂地闪着光亮，周围是用来装饰桌子的花朵，精致美丽。拱门前悬挂着深红色的帷幔，把这个房间与旁边的餐厅隔开，虽然隔层不厚，但客人们的声音很低，所以他们的谈话听起来只是柔和的低喃声，一个字也听不清。
Adele, who appeared to be still under the influence of a most solemnising impression, sat down, without a word, on the footstool I pointed out to her. I retired to a window seat, and taking a book from a table near, endeavoured to read. Adele brought her stool to my feet; ere long she touched my knee.
阿德拉看起来依然神情严肃，我指着一个脚凳，让她坐在那儿，她一言不发地坐下了。我坐在一个靠窗的位子上，从旁边的桌子上取了一本书，努力看进去。阿德拉把她的凳子搬到我跟前坐下。不久，她碰了一下我的膝盖。
"What is it, Adele?" 
“阿德拉，怎么啦？”
"Est-ce que je ne puis pas prendrie une seule de ces fleurs magnifiques, mademoiselle? Seulement pour completer ma toilette.” 
“小姐，我可以从那些漂亮的花儿中拿一朵吗？这样我的装束就更美了。”
"You think too much of your 'toilette,' Adele: but you may have a flower.” And I took a rose from a vase and fastened it in her sash. She sighed a sigh of ineffable satisfaction, as if her cup of happiness were now full. I turned my face away to conceal a smile I could not suppress: there was something ludicrous as well as painful in the little Parisienne's earnest and innate devotion to matters of dress.
“阿德拉，你太注意你的打扮啦，但是你可以要一朵花。”我从花瓶里取出一支玫瑰花，系在她的腰带上。她舒了口气，有种不易察觉的满足感，就像她的幸福之杯现在已经满了一样。我转过脸，想要掩饰我的忍俊不禁，这个法国小姑娘对装束诚挚、与生俱来的钟爱有点可笑，又有点可怜。
A soft sound of rising now became audible; the curtain was swept back from the arch; through it appeared the dining room, with its lit lustre pouring down light on the silver and glass of a magnificent dessert service covering a long table; a band of ladies stood in the opening; they entered, and the curtain fell behind them.
这时，一阵离席的柔声传来，拱门上的帷幔拉开了，餐厅出现在眼前，长桌上摆满丰盛的甜点，屋里的亮光映照在银器和玻璃杯上。一群女士站在门口，她们进来了，帷幔在她们身后落下。
There were but eight; yet, somehow, as they flocked in, they gave the impression of a much larger number. Some of them were very tall; many were dressed in white; and all had sweeping amplitude of array that seemed to magnify their persons as a mist magnifies the moon. I rose and curtseyed to them: one or two bent their heads in return; the others only stared at me.
她们只有八位，但不知为何，她们簇拥进来时，看起来好像远远不止八人。她们中有些人身材高挑，大都身着白色衣服。她们的晚服都摆幅较大，这样使整个人也显得大了一些，就像月亮在迷雾中显得大了一些一样。我起身向她们行礼，有一两位女士对我点头以示回礼，其他的只是盯着我看。
They dispersed about the room, reminding me, by the lightness and buoyancy of their movements, of a flock of white plumy birds. Some of them threw themselves in half reclining positions on the sofas and ottomans: some bent over the tables and examined the flowers and books: the rest gathered in a group round the fire: all talked in a low but clear tone which seemed habitual to them. I knew their names afterwards, and may as well mention them now.
她们在房间里分散开来，轻盈欢快的动作让我想起了一群白羽翩翩的鸟儿。她们有些半倚在沙发或软垫椅子上，有些俯身看桌子上的花和书，其余人围着火炉坐在一起谈论，声音低却清晰。她们似乎对此习以为常。我后来才知道她们的名字，但现在可以先提一下。
First, there was Mrs. Eshton and two of her daughters. She had evidently been a handsome woman, and was well preserved still. Of her daughters, the eldest, Amy, was rather little: naive, and childlike in face and manner, and piquant in form; her white muslin dress and blue sash became her well. The second, Louisa, was taller and more elegant in figure; with a very pretty face, of that order the French term minois chiffone: both sisters were fair as lilies.
首先是埃希顿太太和她的两个女儿。显然，她以前一定很漂亮，现在也风韵犹存。她的大女儿埃米身材娇小，从相貌和举止上看都很幼稚、天真，非常活泼开朗。她白色的平纹细布裙子和蓝色的腰带相得益彰。小女儿路易莎，身材更高，也更优雅，容貌秀美，正如法语中所说的“少女娇媚的脸庞”，两姐妹都像百合一样俊俏。
Lady Lynn was a large and stout personage of about forty, very erect, very haughty looking, richly dressed in a satin robe of changeful sheen: her dark hair shone glossily under the shade of an azure plume, and within the circlet of a band of gems.
林恩女士大约四十岁，身材高大肥胖，身板笔直，看起来很傲慢。她身着盛装，穿了一件大放异彩的绸缎礼服。她头上戴了一个宝石头箍，暗色的头发在一支天蓝色羽毛的掩映下闪闪发亮。
Mrs. Colonel Dent was less showy; but, I thought, more ladylike. She had a slight figure, a pale, gentle face, and fair hair. Her black satin dress, her scarf of rich foreign lace, and her pearl ornaments, pleased me better than the rainbow radiance of the titled dame.
登特上校太太没那么引人注目，但我觉得她更贤淑高贵。她身材瘦小，脸色苍白文雅，发色金黄。她黑色的绸缎裙子，鲜艳、具有异国风情的蕾丝披肩以及她的珍珠饰物，比那位有爵位的贵妇彩虹般光彩照人的形象更让我感到愉悦。
But the three most distinguished—partly, perhaps, because the tallest figures of the band—were the Dowager Lady Ingram and her daughters, Blanche and Mary. They were all three of the loftiest stature of women. The Dowager might be between forty and fifty: her shape was still fine; her hair (by candlelight at least) still black; her teeth, too, were still apparently perfect. Most people would have termed her a splendid woman of her age: and so she was, no doubt, physically speaking; but then there was an expression of almost insupportable haughtiness in her bearing and countenance. She had Roman features and a double chin, disappearing into a throat like a pillar: these features appeared to me not only inflated and darkened, but even furrowed with pride; and the chin was sustained by the same principle, in a position of almost preternatural erectness. She had, likewise, a fierce and a hard eye: it reminded me of Mrs. Reed's; she mouthed her words in speaking; her voice was deep, its inflections very pompous, very dogmatical,—very intolerable, in short. A crimson velvet robe, and a shawl turban of some gold wrought Indian fabric, invested her (I suppose she thought) with a truly imperial dignity.
但是，最与众不同的三个人——也许是因为她们是这些人中个头最高的——是遗孀贵妇英格拉姆太太和她的女儿布兰奇和玛丽。她们三个算是女士中个子最高的了。英格拉姆太太大约四五十岁，身材还很苗条，头发（至少在烛光下）依然乌黑，牙齿显然还很完美。大多数人会觉得她是这个年龄中的大美女。无疑，从外形来看，她确实很美。但她的举止神情中有一些让人难以忍受的傲慢。她的面容酷似罗马人，双下巴连向柱子似的脖颈。在我看来，她这副嘴脸不仅因为骄傲显得臃肿、暗淡，而且还皱纹满面。同样，她骄傲挺起的下巴挺直地有些不可思议。她的眼睛同样尖刻严厉，让我想起了里德太太的眼眸。她说话时总是装腔作势，音调深沉，语句夸张，口气专横——一句话，让人无法忍受。深红色的天鹅绒袍子和由含金的印度织物制成的披肩式头巾为她注入了（我想她这么觉得）一种真正的皇家尊贵。
Blanche and Mary were of equal stature,—straight and tall as poplars. Mary was too slim for her height, but Blanche was moulded like a Dian. I regarded her, of course, with special interest. First, I wished to see whether her appearance accorded with Mrs. Fairfax's description; secondly, whether it at all resembled the fancy miniature I had painted of her; and thirdly—it will out!—whether it were such as I should fancy likely to suit Mr. Rochester's taste.
布兰奇和玛丽体型相当——像白杨一样高大挺直。玛丽有些偏瘦，但布兰奇却宛如月亮女神戴安。我看着她，当然，带着更特殊的兴趣。第一，我想看看她的相貌是否与费尔法克斯太太的描述相符；第二，我想看看她是否与我为她画的精美画像相像；第三——就要知道真相了——她是否像我想的那样符合罗切斯特先生的品位。
As far as person went, she answered point for point, both to my picture and Mrs. Fairfax's description. The noble bust, the sloping shoulders, the graceful neck, the dark eyes and black ringlets were all there;—but her face? Her face was like her mother's; a youthful unfurrowed likeness: the same low brow, the same high features, the same pride. It was not, however, so saturnine a pride! she laughed continually; her laugh was satirical, and so was the habitual expression of her arched and haughty lip.
就外表来看，她的相貌与我笔下的画像和费尔法克斯太太的描述都很相符。丰满的胸部，瘦削的肩头，优雅的脖颈，深色的眼睛和乌黑的发卷，样样具备——但是，她的脸庞呢？她的脸像她母亲的一样，只是更年轻，没有皱纹而已。同样低低的额头，同样的高调的五官，同样的傲气。然而，她的傲气却并不沉默寡言！她不停地笑着，笑声里夹杂着讽刺，她习惯性弯起高傲的嘴唇，这个表情也同样带着讽刺的意味。
Genius is said to be self-conscious. I cannot tell whether Miss Ingram was a genius, but she was self-conscious—remarkably self-conscious indeed. She entered into a discourse on botany with the gentle Mrs. Dent. It seemed Mrs. Dent had not studied that science: though, as she said, she liked flowers, "especially wild ones;" Miss Ingram had, and she ran over its vocabulary with an air. I presently perceived she was (what is vernacularly termed) TRAILING Mrs. Dent; that is, playing on her ignorance—her TRAIL might be clever, but it was decidedly not good-natured. She played: her execution was brilliant; she sang: her voice was fine; she talked French apart to her mamma; and she talked it well, with fluency and with a good accent.
人们常说天才是自知自觉的。我不确定英格拉姆小姐是否是个天才，但她是自知自觉的——非常自知自觉。她与文雅的登特太太谈起了植物学。登特太太似乎没有研究过这门学问，但她说自己喜欢花，“特别是野花。”英格拉姆小姐却学过，她卖弄地讲起植物学的词汇。我发现她（用地方话说）正对登特太太“围追堵截”，即玩弄她的无知——也许她的做法很聪明，但肯定没安好心。她弹琴，技法精湛；她唱歌，音色优美；她单独给她母亲讲法语，讲得很好，流利且口音纯正。
Mary had a milder and more open countenance than Blanche; softer features too, and a skin some shades fairer (Miss Ingram was dark as a Spaniard)—but Mary was deficient in life: her face lacked expression, her eye lustre; she had nothing to say, and having once taken her seat, remained fixed like a statue in its niche. The sisters were both attired in spotless white.
玛丽比布兰奇面相更温柔也更开朗，她五官的轮廓更柔和，肤色也更白（英格拉姆小姐的肤色黑得像西班牙人）——但是，玛丽不够有生机，面无表情，眼神呆滞。她不善言谈，坐下后就像壁龛里的一尊雕像一样纹丝不动。两姐妹都穿的一身雪白。
And did I now think Miss Ingram such a choice as Mr. Rochester would be likely to make? I could not tell—I did not know his taste in female beauty. If he liked the majestic, she was the very type of majesty: then she was accomplished, sprightly. Most gentlemen would admire her, I thought; and that he DID admire her, I already seemed to have obtained proof: to remove the last shade of doubt, it remained but to see them together.
现在我还觉得英格拉姆小姐是罗切斯特先生会选择的对象吗？我不知道——我不知道他在女性美方面的品位。如果他喜欢高贵，那么她恰好属于这一类——而且她颇有才艺，活泼可爱。我想大部分绅士都对她心怀仰慕，罗切斯特先生也的确仰慕她，我似乎已经找到了证据，要消除最后一片疑云，只需看看他们在一起的样子了。
You are not to suppose, reader, that Adele has all this time been sitting motionless on the stool at my feet: no; when the ladies entered, she rose, advanced to meet them, made a stately reverence, and said with gravity— 
读者朋友，你可不能认为阿德拉此时正一动不动地坐在我脚旁的凳子上。不是的，女士们进来时，她起身迎上去，恭敬地行了个礼，一本正经地说——
"Bon jour, mesdames." 
“女士们好。”
And Miss Ingram had looked down at her with a mocking air, and exclaimed, "Oh, what a little puppet!" 
英格拉姆小姐低头嘲讽地望着她，喊道：“噢，小玩偶！”
Lady Lynn had remarked, "It is Mr. Rochester's ward, I suppose—the little French girl he was speaking of.” 
林恩女士说：“我想这个小东西就是罗切斯特先生的被监护对象——他提到过这个法国小姑娘。”
Mrs. Dent had kindly taken her hand, and given her a kiss.
登特太太善意地拉起她的手，吻了一下。
Amy and Louisa Eshton had cried out simultaneously—"What a love of a child!" 
埃米和路易莎·埃希顿异口同声地惊叫——“多可爱的小孩！”
And then they had called her to a sofa, where she now sat, ensconced between them, chattering alternately in French and broken English; absorbing not only the young ladies' attention, but that of Mrs. Eshton and Lady Lynn, and getting spoilt to her heart's content.
然后，她们就让她坐到沙发上，她现在就在那儿，坐在她们中间，交替着用法语和结结巴巴的英语唠叨呢。她不仅吸引了年轻女士的注意力，而且引起了埃希顿太太和林恩女士的注意，心里溢满了受宠的满足。
At last coffee is brought in, and the gentlemen are summoned. I sit in the shade—if any shade there be in this brilliantly lit apartment; the window curtain half hides me. Again the arch yawns; they come. The collective appearance of the gentlemen, like that of the ladies, is very imposing: they are all costumed in black; most of them are tall, some young. Henry and Frederick Lynn are very dashing sparks indeed; and Colonel Dent is a fine soldierly man. Mr. Eshton, the magistrate of the district, is gentlemanlike: his hair is quite white, his eyebrows and whiskers still dark, which gives him something of the appearance of a "pere noble de theatre." Lord Ingram, like his sisters, is very tall; like them, also, he is handsome; but he shares Mary's apathetic and listless look: he seems to have more length of limb than vivacity of blood or vigour of brain.
终于，咖啡端进来了，绅士们聚集到了一起。我坐在阴影下——如果这个灯火明亮的房间里有阴影的话。窗帘遮住了我的半个身子。拱门的帷幔再次掀起，他们进来了。和先前女士们进来时一样，绅士们的集体亮相也气势恢宏。他们都身着黑装，多数都身材高大，有的还很年轻。亨利和弗雷德里克·林恩的确是非常浮华的花花公子，登特上校有着杰出军人的气质。这个地区的官员埃希顿先生很有绅士修养。他的头发已经花白，但眉毛和胡须还是黑的，这使他看起来有点像“剧院里尊贵的老人”。英格拉姆勋爵像他的姐妹一样，个子很高，也很帅气。但是，他的神情和玛丽一样冷漠、无精打采。似乎他虽四肢修长，却缺乏血气方刚的生命力或脑力。
And where is Mr. Rochester?
罗切斯特先生在哪呢？
He comes in last: I am not looking at the arch, yet I see him enter. I try to concentrate my attention on those netting needles, on the meshes of the purse I am forming—I wish to think only of the work I have in my hands, to see only the silver beads and silk threads that lie in my lap; whereas, I distinctly behold his figure, and I inevitably recall the moment when I last saw it; just after I had rendered him, what he deemed, an essential service, and he, holding my hand, and looking down on my face, surveyed me with eyes that revealed a heart full and eager to overflow; in whose emotions I had a part. How near had I approached him at that moment! What had occurred since, calculated to change his and my relative positions? Yet now, how distant, how far estranged we were! So far estranged, that I did not expect him to come and speak to me. I did not wonder, when, without looking at me, he took a seat at the other side of the room, and began conversing with some of the ladies. "To put Adele to bed: it is past her bedtime.” No sooner did I see that his attention was riveted on them, and that I might gaze without being observed, than my eyes were drawn involuntarily to his face; I could not keep their lids under control: they would rise, and the irids would fix on him. I looked, and had an acute pleasure in looking,—a precious yet poignant pleasure; pure gold, with a steely point of agony: a pleasure like what the thirst perishing man might feel who knows the well to which he has crept is poisoned, yet stoops and drinks divine draughts nevertheless.
后来，他终于来了。我并没有看向拱门，但我知道他进来了。我努力把精力集中于针孔，我正编织的网眼钱包——我希望心里想的只有手里的活计，放在腿上的珠子和银线。然而，我清楚地看到了他，自然而然地回忆起我上次见到他时的情景。就在我给了他，他所谓的重要帮助后，他握着我的手，低头看着我的脸，注视着我，眼中流露出满得要溢出的深情，我当时也沉浸在这种感情里。那一刻，我离他有多近呀！但之后又发生了什么，让他和我的相对位置发生了变化？而现在，我们之间又是多么生疏，多么冷淡！这种遥远的距离感使我不期望他走过来与我交谈。我并不奇怪，他看都没看我一眼，就在房间的另一边坐下，开始与一些女士们谈话。“带阿德拉去上床，过了她睡觉的时间了。”我一看到他全身心地聊着，知道可以不引起注意地注视他，我的目光就本能地落到他的脸上。我无法控制，眼皮抬起，眼珠就定格在了他的身上。我看着他，从中体会到了敏锐的快感——一种珍贵而尖刻的快感，如纯金一般，又有着尖刻的痛苦，这种快感就像一个行将渴死的人，虽然他明知自己爬向的是下了毒的泉水，但还是会义无反顾地俯身畅饮这神圣之水。
Most true is it that "beauty is in the eye of the gazer." My master's colourless, olive face, square, massive brow, broad and jetty eyebrows, deep eyes, strong features, firm, grim mouth,—all energy, decision, will,—were not beautiful, according to rule; but they were more than beautiful to me; they were full of an interest, an influence that quite mastered me,—that took my feelings from my own power and fettered them in his. I had not intended to love him; the reader knows I had wrought hard to extirpate from my soul the germs of love there detected; and now, at the first renewed view of him, they spontaneously arrived, green and strong! He made me love him without looking at me.
“情人眼里出西施”，的确如此。主人那苍白的、橄榄色的脸，又大又方的额头，宽阔浓黑的眉毛，深沉的眼睛，凸显的五官，坚挺冷酷的嘴——充满了能量、果断、意志——按一般的标准，他的面貌并不帅气；但在我看来，他已经超越了帅气，成了一种可以将我控制的兴趣和影响力——使我无力掌控自己的感情，而是为他的力量所羁绊。我不想爱上他，读者都知道我已经努力将对他的爱之萌芽驱逐出我的灵魂，但现在，当我再见他时的第一眼，那些嫩芽便自动出现，又绿又茁壮！他无须看我一眼，就使我爱上了他。
I compared him with his guests. What was the gallant grace of the Lynns, the languid elegance of Lord Ingram,—even the military distinction of Colonel Dent, contrasted with his look of native pith and genuine power? I had no sympathy in their appearance, their expression: yet I could imagine that most observers would call them attractive, handsome, imposing; while they would pronounce Mr. Rochester at once harsh featured and melancholy looking. I saw them smile, laugh—it was nothing; the light of the candles had as much soul in it as their smile; the tinkle of the bell as much significance as their laugh. I saw Mr. Rochester smile:—his stern features softened; his eye grew both brilliant and gentle, its ray both searching and sweet. He was talking, at the moment, to Louisa and Amy Eshton. I wondered to see them receive with calm that look which seemed to me so penetrating: I expected their eyes to fall, their colour to rise under it; yet I was glad when I found they were in no sense moved. "He is not to them what he is to me," I thought: "he is not of their kind. I believe he is of mine;—I am sure he is—I feel akin to him—I understand the language of his countenance and movements: though rank and wealth sever us widely, I have something in my brain and heart, in my blood and nerves, that assimilates me mentally to him. Did I say, a few days since, that I had nothing to do with him but to receive my salary at his hands? Did I forbid myself to think of him in any other light than as a paymaster? Blasphemy against nature! Every good, true, vigorous feeling I have gathers impulsively round him. I know I must conceal my sentiments: I must smother hope; I must remember that he cannot care much for me. For when I say that I am of his kind, I do not mean that I have his force to influence, and his spell to attract; I mean only that I have certain tastes and feelings in common with him. I must, then, repeat continually that we are for ever sundered:—and yet, while I breathe and think, I must love him.” 
我将他与其他客人比较。与他天真的本性和真正的力量相比，林恩兄弟的殷勤优雅，英格拉姆勋爵的儒雅之气——甚至登特上校独特的军人气质又有什么呢？我丝毫不为他们的外貌，他们的表情所动，但是我可以想象大多数在场的人都会认为他们很有魅力、帅气、不可抗拒，而会立即认为罗切斯特先生粗犷忧郁。我看着他们轻轻微笑、放声大笑——这些都无所谓；烛光的灵魂都可与他们的微笑相比，叮当的铃声也充满他们大笑的意味。我看着罗切斯特微笑——他坚硬的脸柔和了，眼睛闪动着亮光，更温柔了，眼神敏锐而甜美。他正在和路易莎和埃米·埃希顿讲话。她们平静地看着那张让我穿透我心扉的脸，这让我有些不解。我以为会看到她们眼睛低垂，脸颊羞红，但我很高兴发现她们根本无动于衷。“她们对他的感觉和我对他的感觉不同，”我想，“他和她们不是同一类人。我相信并确定他和我是同一类人——他让我感到亲切——我懂他的一颦一笑和一举一动。虽然我们的等级和财富相差甚远，但我的心中和思想中，血液中和神经中有一种东西让我在精神上将我与他同化。我几天前不是说过，除了接受他的薪水外与他毫不相干吗？我不是让自己只把他当作我的雇主，禁止对他有别的想法了吗？真是亵渎天性！我所有美好的、真实的、强烈的感情都不可抵挡地围绕在他周围。我知道我必须隐藏自己的感情，必须抹杀希望；必须记住他不会那么在乎我。因为我说我们是同一类人时，并不是指我也有他的影响力和吸引人的魅力。我只是想说我们的某些品位和感情相似。所以，我必须不停地重复，我们永远都是被隔开的——但是，只要我还活着，还能思考，我就一定爱他。”
Coffee is handed. The ladies, since the gentlemen entered, have become lively as larks; conversation waxes brisk and merry. Colonel Dent and Mr. Eshton argue on politics; their wives listen. The two proud dowagers, Lady Lynn and Lady Ingram, confabulate together. Sir George—whom, by-the-bye, I have forgotten to describe,—a very big, and very fresh looking country gentleman, stands before their sofa, coffee cup in hand, and occasionally puts in a word. Mr. Frederick Lynn has taken a seat beside Mary Ingram, and is showing her the engravings of a splendid volume: she looks, smiles now and then, but apparently says little. The tall and phlegmatic Lord Ingram leans with folded arms on the chair back of the little and lively Amy Eshton; she glances up at him, and chatters like a wren: she likes him better than she does Mr. Rochester. Henry Lynn has taken possession of an ottoman at the feet of Louisa: Adele shares it with him: he is trying to talk French with her, and Louisa laughs at his blunders. With whom will Blanche Ingram pair? She is standing alone at the table, bending gracefully over an album. She seems waiting to be sought; but she will not wait too long: she herself selects a mate.
咖啡端上来了。绅士们进来后，女士们开始变得如百灵鸟般活跃，谈话更活泼愉快。登特上校和埃希顿先生在争论政治问题，他们的太太在听着。林恩太太和英格拉姆太太这两位高傲的遗孀正在互诉心声。乔治爵士——顺便说一下，我忘了描述他了——是一位身材高大、精神饱满的乡绅。他站在沙发前，手里端了一杯咖啡，不时插上一两句话。弗雷德里克·林恩先生在玛丽·英格拉姆身边坐下，正向她展示一本装帧精美的书上的图画。她看着，时而微笑，但显然说话不多。高大冷淡的英格拉姆勋爵双臂交叉抱在胸前，倚着埃米·埃希顿的椅背。娇小活泼的埃米正看着他，像鹪鹩一样喋喋不休。与罗切斯特先生相比，她更喜欢英格拉姆勋爵。亨利·林恩在路易莎脚旁坐下，与阿德拉共用一个脚凳。他正试着用法语与阿德拉交谈，一出错就遭到路易莎的嘲笑。布兰奇·英格拉姆和谁在一起呢？她正独自站在桌子前，优雅地俯身看一个相册。她似乎在等别人主动找她，但不愿久等，就为自己选了个伴儿。
Mr. Rochester, having quitted the Eshtons, stands on the hearth as solitary as she stands by the table: she confronts him, taking her station on the opposite side of the mantelpiece.
罗切斯特先生和埃希顿们谈话结束后，独自站在壁炉旁，像布兰奇一样无人陪伴。于是，她过去与他搭话，站到壁炉的另一边。
"Mr. Rochester, I thought you were not fond of children?" 
“罗切斯特先生，我以为你不喜欢孩子呢？”
"Nor am I." 
“我不喜欢。”
"Then, what induced you to take charge of such a little doll as that?" (pointing to Adele). "Where did you pick her up?" 
“那么你为什么要照顾那个小玩偶呢？”（她指着阿德拉。）“你从哪儿捡的？”
"I did not pick her up; she was left on my hands." 
“不是捡的，别人把她交给了我。”
"You should have sent her to school." 
“你应该把她送学校去。”
"I could not afford it: schools are so dear.” 
“我付不起费用，学费太贵了。”
"Why, I suppose you have a governess for her: I saw a person with her just now—is she gone? Oh, no! there she is still, behind the window curtain. You pay her, of course; I should think it quite as expensive,—more so; for you have them both to keep in addition.” 
“呃，我想你给她请了家庭教师。我刚才看到她身边有个人——她走了吗？噢，没有！她还在那儿呢，在窗帘后面。你当然要付给她工资，我想这样同样很贵——甚至花得更多，因为你还要养活她们两个人。”
I feared—or should I say, hoped?—the allusion to me would make Mr. Rochester glance my way; and I involuntarily shrank farther into the shade: but he never turned his eyes.
我怕——或者该说，我希望？——布兰奇提到我后，罗切斯特先生会往我这边看，就不自觉地向阴影处挪了挪，但他根本没有转移视线。
"I have not considered the subject," said he indifferently, looking straight before him.
“我还没考虑过这个问题。”他漠然地说，直直地望着前方。
"No, you men never do consider economy and common sense. You should hear mama on the chapter of governesses: Mary and I have had, I should think, a dozen at least in our day; half of them detestable and the rest ridiculous, and all incubi—were they not, mama?” 
“对，你们男人从来不考虑花费和常理。你应该听我母亲是如何看待家庭教师问题的，我和玛丽，我想，一共请了至少一打家庭教师，一半都很令人讨厌，剩下的都很不可理喻。她们都是妖魔——是不是，妈妈？”

“你刚刚说什么，我的宝贝？”
The young lady thus claimed as the dowager's special property, reiterated her question with an explanation.
这位被遗孀称作自己专有财产的年轻小姐，又重复了一遍自己的问题，还解释了一番。

登特太太俯身在这位虔诚的夫人耳边轻声说了些什么。根据后面的回答，我想登特太太是在提醒她，房间里有一位就来自她正诅咒的人群。

"Donna Bianca, if you command it, I will be." 
“比央卡小姐，只要您下令，我就唱。”

“那好，阁下。我下令你清一清你的肺和其他发音器官，因为我要它们为我这个女王效劳。”
"Who would not be the Rizzio of so divine a Mary?" 
“有这样一位神圣的女王玛丽，谁不愿意当里丘呢？”

“里丘有什么了不起！”她叫道，甩着满头的卷发向钢琴走去。“我觉得提琴手戴维一定是个平淡的家伙；我更喜欢皮肤黝黑的博斯·威尔。我认为，一个男人若没有一点邪恶，那他就一无是处。不管历史如何评判詹姆斯·赫伯恩，我自有主张。他正是那种我愿意以身相许的野性、凶狠、恶棍式的英雄。”
"Gentlemen, you hear! Now which of you most resembles Bothwell?" cried Mr. Rochester.
“听听，绅士们！你们谁最像博斯·威尔？”罗切斯特先生大喊道。

"On my honour, I am much obliged to you," was the reply.
“啊，我的荣幸，我对您感激不尽。”罗切斯特先生答道。
Miss Ingram, who had now seated herself with proud grace at the piano, spreading out her snowy robes in queenly amplitude, commenced a brilliant prelude; talking meantime. She appeared to be on her high horse tonight; both her words and her air seemed intended to excite not only the admiration, but the amazement of her auditors: she was evidently bent on striking them as something very dashing and daring indeed.
英格拉姆小姐带着女王的气势拎起她雪白的裙子，优雅骄傲地在钢琴前坐下，开始了一曲技艺高超的序曲，同时还讲着话。她今晚显得趾高气扬，言语和气场不仅志在赢得听众的赞美，而且还要让他们感到震惊。显然，她要用自己的活泼和勇气来感染他们。
"Oh, I am so sick of the young men of the present day!" exclaimed she, rattling away at the instrument. Creatures so absorbed in care about their pretty faces, and their white hands, and their small feet; as if a man had anything to do with beauty! As if loveliness were not the special prerogative of woman—her legitimate appanage and heritage! I grant an ugly WOMAN is a blot on the fair face of creation; but as to the GENTLEMEN, let them be solicitous to possess only strength and valour: let their motto be:—Hunt, shoot, and fight: the rest is not worth a fillip. Such should be my device, were I a man.” 
“噢，现在的年轻人真让我厌烦！”她弹着琴大喊道。“可怜软弱的东西，不能离开爸爸庄园大门一步，没有妈妈的准许和保护也不行！他们把全部精力都放到保养漂亮的脸蛋、白嫩的双手、小巧的双脚，好像男人和美有什么关系一样！好像可爱不是女人的专权——她合法的领域和遗产一样！我承认一个丑陋的女人是造物主完美脸上的一个污点，但是让男士就只热衷于拥有力量与勇气吧，让打猎、射击、搏斗成为他们的座右铭吧，其他的都微不足道。我要是男人，就会谨遵这些规则。”

I will suffer no competitor near the throne; I shall exact an undivided homage: his devotions shall not be shared between me and the shape he sees in his mirror. Mr. Rochester, now sing, and I will play for you.” 
她停顿了一下，没有人插话，就又接着说：“我要是结婚的话，我一定要让我的丈夫成为我的陪衬，而非对手。他不能和我竞争‘王位’，我要享有不可分割的尊敬，他对我的忠诚，不能与他镜中看到的那个人分享。罗切斯特先生，现在唱歌吧，我为你伴奏。”
"I am all obedience," was the response.
“一切遵命。”罗切斯特先生回答。

“那就唱首海盗歌。要知道我最喜欢海盗歌了，所以要唱地精神饱满。”
"Commands from Miss Ingram's lips would put spirit into a mug of milk and water." 
“出自英格拉姆小姐的命令可以让一杯牛奶或一杯水都精神抖擞。”

“那要小心了，如果你唱得不如我意，我就要展示应该怎么唱，让你丢尽颜面。”

“这是对无能的一种奖赏，现在我要努力失败了。”

"Now is my time to slip away," thought I: but the tones that then severed the air arrested me. Mrs. Fairfax had said Mr. Rochester possessed a fine voice: he did—a mellow, powerful bass, into which he threw his own feeling, his own force; finding a way through the ear to the heart, and there waking sensation strangely. Thence a narrow passage led into the hall: in crossing it, I perceived my sandal was loose; I stopped to tie it, kneeling down for that purpose on the mat at the foot of the staircase.
