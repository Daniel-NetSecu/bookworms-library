CHAPTER VIII  

第八章  

And Pelet himself? How did I continue to like him? Oh, extremely well! Nothing could be more smooth, gentlemanlike, and even friendly than his demeanour to me. I had to endure from him neither cold neglect, irritating interference, nor pretentious assumption of superiority. I fear, however, two poor, hard-worked Belgian ushers in the establishment could not have said as much; to them the director's manner was invariably dry, stem, and cool. I believe he perceived once or twice that I was a little shocked at the difference he made between them and me, and accounted for it by saying, with a quiet, sarcastic smile,— 
那么珀莱先生本人呢？我后来还喜欢他吗？噢，极其喜欢！没有什么比他对我的态度更温和、更有风度、甚至更友好了。在他手下，我既不需要忍受冷酷的忽视和恼人的干涉，也没见过他有过自命不凡的优越感。但是，我恐怕学校那两位努力工作的贫穷的比利时招待对他不会有我这样的看法；对于他们，校长的态度总是那么冷淡、严峻、冷若冰霜。我相信有一两次他也发觉了，我对他对待我跟那两个人的不同态度有些吃惊，便带着平静而嘲讽的微笑解释说：
'Ce ne sont que des Flamands—allez!' 
“只是两个佛兰芒人——无所谓！”
And then he took his cigar gently from his lips and spat on the painted floor of the room in which we were sitting. Flamands certainly they were, and both had the true Flamand physiognomy, where intellectual inferiority is marked in lines none can mistake. Still they were men, and, in the main, honest men; and I could not see why their being aboriginals of the flat, dull soil should serve as a pretext for treating them with perpetual severity and contempt. This idea of injustice somewhat poisoned the pleasure I might otherwise have derived from Pelet's soft affable manner to myself. Certainly it was agreeable, when the day's work was over, to find one's employer an intelligent and cheerful companion; and if he was sometimes a little sarcastic and sometimes a little too insinuating, and if I did discover that his mildness was more a matter of appearance than of reality—if I did occasionally suspect the existence of flint or steel under an external covering of velvet— still we are none of us perfect; and weary as I was of the atmosphere of brutality and insolence in which I had constantly lived at X—, I had no inclination now, on casting anchor in calmer regions, to institute at once a prying search after defects that were scrupulously withdrawn and carefully veiled from my view. I was willing to take Pelet for what he seemed, to believe him benevolent and friendly until some untoward event should prove him otherwise. He was not married, and I soon perceived he had all a Frenchman's, all a Parisian's, notions about matrimony and women. I suspected a degree of laxity in his code of morals, there was something so cold and blasé in his tone whenever he alluded to what he called le beau sexe; but he was too gentlemanlike to intrude topics I did not invite, and as he was really intelligent and really fond of intellectual subjects of discourse, he and I always found enough to talk about, without seeking themes in the mire. I hated his fashion of mentioning love. I abhorred from my soul mere licentiousness. He felt the difference of our notions, and, by mutual consent, we kept off ground debatable.
然后他便轻轻地拿掉嘴上的香烟，向我们所坐房间的油漆地板上吐了口口水。他们确实是佛兰芒人，并且两个都带有真正佛兰芒人的特征，他们智力低下，并显示在外形中，没有人会看错。他们仍然是人，基本上都是老实人；我不懂，为什么别人就因为他们是那块贫瘠荒凉的土地上的原住民，就有借口用无止尽的刻薄和蔑视来对待他们。珀莱对我温文尔雅、和蔼可亲，这本会让我对他大有好感，可这种不公平的思想让我对他的好感多少有点减弱。一天的工作结束之后发现自己的雇主是个聪明而开朗的同伴，这当然是件愉快的事；虽然有时他显得有点尖酸刻薄，有时又有点阿谀奉承；虽然我发现他只是表面上温文尔雅，而实际上并非如此——虽然我偶尔察觉他温和的外表下实际上有着冷酷无情的一面——但我们都不是完美的；而且我早已厌倦了长期生活在X镇，那种暴虐横行、傲慢无礼的氛围了；所以在一个风平浪静的海港拔锚驻扎后，我现在可不想马上去窥探那些被小心翼翼地隐藏起来，仔细掩饰着不想让我察觉的缺点。我倒情愿认为珀莱是表里如一的，相信他是个仁慈而友好的人，除非有什么出乎意料的情况来表明他并非如此。他未婚，不久后我又发现他对于婚姻和女人完全是法国人的、巴黎人的观念。我怀疑他的道德观有些放纵，每当他提及自己所称的美丽女性时，语气中总带有一种冷淡和厌恶；但是他彬彬有礼，凡是我没有提及的话题他都不会涉及的。因为他十分聪明并且真正地喜欢谈有知识性的话题，我们总是有聊不完的话，绝不会陷入没话题的困境。我讨厌他提及爱情的方式。我从内心里憎恨逢场作戏、放浪形骸。他也感觉到我们两个的不同观念，于是我们都不约而同地避开有争议的话题。
Pelet's house was kept and his kitchen managed by his mother, a real old Frenchwoman. She had been handsome—at least she told me so, and I strove to believe her; she was now ugly, as only Continental old women can be. Perhaps, though, her style of dress made her look uglier than she really was. Indoors she would go about without cap, her gray hair strangely dishevelled; then, when at home, she seldom wore a gown—only a shabby cotton camisole. Shoes, too, were strangers to her feet, and in lieu of them she sported roomy slippers, trodden down at the heels. On the other hand, whenever it was her pleasure to appear abroad, as on Sundays and féte-days, she would put on some very brilliant-coloured dress, usually of thin teXture, a silk bonnet with a wreath of flowers, and a very fine shawl. She was not, in the main, an ill-natured old woman, but an incessant and most indiscreet talker. She kept chiefly in and about the kitchen, and seemed rather to avoid her son's august presence. Of him, indeed, she evidently stood in awe. When he reproved her, his reproofs were bitter and unsparing; but he seldom gave himself that trouble.
珀莱的房屋和厨房都由他的母亲打理，她是一位真正的法国老太太。她曾经是个美貌的女人——至少她对我这样说过，我也尽力相信她的话；现在她却十分丑陋，丑陋到大陆的老妇人才会有的那么丑陋。不过也许是她的穿着方式使她看起来比实际上更丑陋。在室内时她会不带帽子到处走动，灰白的头发凌乱不堪；在家里时她很少穿长外衣——只穿一件破旧的棉背心。她也很少穿鞋，而是穿着一双大拖鞋，那鞋后脚跟都给踩烂了。可是，当她高兴出去逛逛时，比如星期天或者节日时，她就会穿上色彩艳丽、质地通常都很薄的衣服，戴上一顶装饰着花环的丝绸软帽，还披了一条很漂亮的披肩。总的来说，她并不是一个坏脾气的老妇人，而是一个啰嗦又爱随随便便说话的人。她主要是在厨房里外忙，似乎很想避开她那令人敬畏的儿子。的确，她对他显然有些畏惧。当他责骂她时，他的言辞既尖刻又严厉；但是他很少自找那种麻烦。
Madame Pelet had her own society, her own circle of chosen visitors, whom, however, I seldom saw, as she generally entertained them in what she called her cabinet, a small den of a place adjoining the kitchen, and descending into it by one or two steps. On these steps, by-the-bye, I have not unfrequently seen Madame Pelet seated with a trencher on her knee, engaged in the three-fold employment of eating her dinner, gossiping with her favourite servant, the housemaid, and scolding her antagonist, the cook. She never dined, and seldom indeed took any meal with her son, and as to showing her face at the boys' table, that was quite out of the question. These details will sound very odd in English ears, but Belgium is not England, and its ways are not our ways.
珀莱夫人有她自己的社交圈，有她固定的来访者，不过她的那些客人我倒是很少见，因为她一般在她所谓的密室里接待他们，那是个连着厨房、下一两级台阶才能进去的小房间。顺便说一下，我常看到珀莱夫人坐在那些台阶上，膝盖上放个木盘，一边吃着饭，一边跟她喜欢的仆人、女佣聊天，还一边责骂她的冤家——厨师，三不耽误。她从来不跟儿子一起吃正餐，事实上她也很少跟儿子一起吃任何饭。至于她在学生们的餐桌上露面那更是不可能了。这些细节在英国人听来真是匪夷所思，但是比利时不是英国，他们的习惯跟我们的不同。
Madame Pelet's habits of life, then, being taken into consideration, I was a good deal surprised when, one Thursday evening (Thursday was always a half-holiday), as I was sitting all alone in my apartment, correcting a huge pile of English and Latin exercises, a servant tapped at the door, and, on its being opened, presented Madame Pelet's compliments, and she would be happy to see me to take my goûter (a meal which answers to our English 'tea') with her in the dining-room.
考虑到珀莱夫人这样的生活习惯，有一天发生的一件事让我感到很吃惊，那是个星期四的晚上（星期四通常放半天假），当我一个人在房间里改大叠的英文和拉丁文练习时，一个佣人敲了敲门，门打开后，那个佣人向我转达了珀莱夫人的问候，并说如果我能去餐厅跟夫人吃茶点（在我们英国称之为“茶点”）的话，夫人将十分开心。
'Plaît-il?' said I, for I thought I must have misunderstood, the message and invitation were so unusual. The same words were repeated. I accepted, of course; and as I descended the stairs, I wondered what whim had entered the old lady's brain. Her son was out, gone to pass the evening at the salle of the Grande Harmonie, or some other club of which he was a member. Just as I laid my hand on the handle of the dining-room door a queer idea glanced across my mind.
“你说什么？”我说，因为我想我肯定是误解了，因为这个口信和邀请都太不同寻常了。佣人又重复了一遍同样的话。当然我接受了邀请；当我下楼时，我在想这个老太太葫芦里到底在卖什么药。她儿子出门了，到阿默尼大音乐厅或者别的某个他参加的俱乐部打发夜间时光去了。就在我的手刚抓住餐厅的门把时，一个奇怪的想法在我脑海中一闪而过。
'Surely she's not going to make love to me,' said I. 'I've heard of old Frenchwomen doing odd things in that line; and the goûter! They generally begin such affairs with eating and drinking, I believe.' 
“她肯定不会是向我求爱吧。”我想。我曾听说过法国老太太喜欢干那种类型的无稽之事；而那个茶点又是怎么回事呢！我相信，她们通常是通过吃喝来开始那种事情的。
There was a fearful dismay in this suggestion of my excited imagination, and if I had allowed myself time to dwell upon it, I should no doubt have cut there and then, rushed back to my chamber, and bolted myself in; but whenever a danger or a horror is veiled with uncertainty, the primary wish of the mind is to ascertain first the naked truth, reserving the expedient of flight for the moment when its dread anticipation shall be realized. I turned the door-handle, and in an instant had crossed the fatal threshold, closed the door behind me and stood in the presence of Madame Pelet.
我这活跃的想象让我惊慌害怕起来，如果还有时间仔细考虑的话，我无疑会立即止步，奔回房间，将自己反锁在房间里；但是每当有一种危险或者恐惧被不确定性笼罩的话，大脑中的首要想法就是弄清楚真相，当可怕的预料成真时，便可以准备逃脱的应急手段了。我扭开门把手，马上跨过那致命的门槛，随手关上门，站在珀莱夫人的面前。
Gracious heavens! The first view of her seemed to confirm my worst apprehensions. There she sat, dressed out in a light green muslin gown, on her head a lace cap with flourishing red roses in the frill. Her table was carefully spread. There were fruit, cakes, and coffee, with a bottle of something—I did not know what. Already the cold sweat started on my brow already I glanced back over my shoulder at the closed door when, to my unspeakable relief, my eye, wandering wildly in the direction of the stove, rested upon a second figure, seated in a large fauteuil beside it. This was a woman, too, and, moreover, an old woman, and as fat and as rubicund as Madame Pelet was meagre and yellow. Her attire was likewise very fine, and spring flowers of different hues circled in a bright wreath the crown of her violet-coloured velvet bonnet.
我的上帝啊！第一眼看到她便似乎证实了我最担心的事情。她坐在那里，身穿浅绿色的棉布长袍，头戴一顶绣着花边的便帽，褶皱里还装饰着朵朵盛开的红色玫瑰。她的桌子上已经精心摆好食品。有水果、蛋糕、咖啡，还有一瓶我说不上名字的东西。我的额头已经开始冒冷汗，我已经回头瞟了一眼关好的门，当我眼睛四处打转看见炉边的大扶手椅上还坐了个人，我感到一种不可言状的安慰。那人也是女人，并且也是个老妇人，她体态丰满、面色红润，而珀莱夫人则是面黄肌瘦。她的打扮同样很华丽，春天里五彩缤纷的花朵把她的紫色天鹅绒帽子编织成一顶绚丽的花冠。
I had only time to make these general observations when Madame Pelet, coming forward with what she intended should be a graceful and elastic step, thus accosted me,— 
我只来得及大概观察一番，珀莱夫人就迈着她臆想的优雅而灵活的步伐走上前来跟我搭讪——
'Monsieur is indeed most obliging to quit his books, his studies, at the request of an insignificant person like me. Will monsieur complete his kindness by allowing me to present him to my dear friend Madame Reuter, who resides in the neighbouring house—the young ladies' school?' 
“先生能应我这样一个微不足道的人的邀请丢下书本和学习，真是太赏脸了。先生能否再赏个脸，允许我向您介绍我亲爱的朋友勒泰夫人吗？她就住在隔壁——女子学校里。”
'Ah!' thought I, 'I knew she was old.' And I bowed and took my seat. Madame Reuter placed herself at the table opposite to me.
“啊！”我想，“我就知道她很老。”我向她鞠了一躬后便就坐了。勒泰夫人坐在我的对面。
'How do you like Belgium, monsieur?' asked she, in an accent of the broadest Bruxellois. I could now well distinguish the difference between the fine and pure Parisian utterance of M. Pelet, for instance, and the guttural enunciation of the Flamands. I answered politely, and then wondered how so coarse and clumsy an old woman as the one before me should be at the head of a ladies'seminary, which I had always heard spoken of in terms of high commendation. in truth there was something to wonder at. Madame Reuter looked more like a joyous, free-living old Flemish fermière, or even a maîtresse d'auberge, than a staid, grave, rigid directrice de pensionnat. In general the Continental, or at least the Belgian, old women permit themselves a license of manners, speech, and aspect such as our venerable grand-dames would recoil from as absolutely disreputable and Madame Reuter's jolly face bore evidence that she was no exception to the rule of her country. There was a twinkle and leer in her left eye; her right she kept habitually half shut, which I thought very odd indeed. After several vain attempts to comprehend the motives of these two droll old creatures for inviting me to join them at their goéter, I at last fairly gave it up, and resigning myself to inevitable mystification, I sat and looked first at one, then at the other, taking care meantime to do justice to the confitures, cakes, and coffee, with which they amply supplied me. They, too, ate, and that with no delicate appetite; and having demolished a large portion of the solids, they proposed a petit verre. I declined. Not so Mesdames Pelet and Reuter; each mixed herself what I thought rather a stiff tumbler of punch, and placing it on a stand near the stove, they drew up their chairs to that convenience, and invited me to do the same. I obeyed; and being seated fairly between them, I was thus addressed first by Madame Pelet, then by Madame Reuter.
“您觉得比利时怎么样啊，先生？”她操着极重的布鲁塞尔口音问我。现在我已经能够很好地区分优美纯正的巴黎人发音，比如说珀莱先生的发音，和喉音很重的佛兰芒人的发音了。我礼貌地回答了她的问题，暗自纳闷我面前这样一位粗俗而笨拙的老太太是怎么成为那所声誉极高的女子学校校长的。说真的，这里面肯定有问题。勒泰夫人看起来更像一位开朗快乐、自由自在的佛兰芒老妇人，甚至像一位小客栈的老板娘，而不像一位沉着、严肃而死板的寄宿学校女校长。一般来说，大陆国家的老妇人，至少是比利时人，对自己的举止，言行以及神态都无所顾忌，而我们英国庄严的老太太则对此嗤之以鼻，认为有失体面。从勒泰夫人的那张欢快的脸可以看出她同自己国家别的老妇没有什么两样。让我觉得实在奇怪的是她的左眼总是一眨一眨地斜瞟着，右眼习惯性地半睁着。我几次尝试弄清楚这两个古怪的老家伙邀请我来用茶点的动机，但都未成功，最终只好放弃了，听任它作为一个不解之谜，我坐在那里，先看看这个，再看看那个，同时还要尽情享用她们提供的充足的蜜饯、蛋糕和咖啡。她们也吃东西，但是没有什么极好的食欲；吃完一大半的食物之后，她们建议喝一小杯酒。我婉言谢绝了。珀莱夫人和勒泰夫人就没那么拘束；她们每人给自己调了一杯在我看来烈度相当高的鸡尾酒，放在炉子旁的一个台子上，为了方便起见，她们把椅子往那边拉了拉，并邀请我也拉过去。我照做了；我正好坐在她们中间，这样两人轮流跟我聊起来，先是珀莱夫人，接着又是勒泰夫人。
'We will now speak of business,' said Madame Pelet, and she went on to make an elaborate speech, which, being interpreted, was to the effect that she had asked for the pleasure of my company that evening in order to give her friend Madame Reuter an opportunity of broaching an important proposal, which might rum out greatly to my advantage.
“我们现在谈正事吧，”珀莱夫人说，然后她便发表了她精心准备的演说，大概意思是她有幸邀请我来是为了给她朋友勒泰夫人一个机会，让她开口提出一项对我有很多益处的重要建议。
'Pourvu que vous soyez sage,' said Madame Reuter, 'et à vrai dire, vous en avez bien l'air. Take one drop of the punch (or ponche, as she pronounced it); it is an agreeable and wholesome beverage after a full meal.' 
“要是你懂得这事能给你带来多少好处，”勒泰夫人说，“要我说，你像是个挺机灵的人。喝一点鸡尾酒吧（或者是奇尾酒，她是那么说的）；吃饱饭后喝点倒是一种适宜的有益于健康的饮料。”
I bowed, but again declined it. She went on,— 'I feel,' said she, after a solemn sip—'I feel profoundly the importance of the commission with which my dear daughter has entrusted me; for you are aware, monsieur, that it is my daughter who directs the establishment in the next house?' 
我鞠躬致谢，但还是谢绝了。她继续说道：“我觉得，”她严肃地抿了一小口后说，“我深深地感觉到我的爱女委托给我办的事十分重要；因为您知道，先生，掌管隔壁那所学校的是我的女儿吧？”
'Ah! I thought it was yourself, madame.' Though, indeed at that moment I recollected that it was called Mademoiselle, not Madame, Reuter's pensionnat.
“啊！我还以为是您掌管呢，夫人。”尽管那么说，那时我确实回想起那所学校叫勒泰小姐寄宿学校，而不是勒泰夫人寄宿学校。
'I! Oh no! I manage the house and look after the servants, as my friend Madame Pelet does for monsieur her son—nothing more. Ah! you thought I gave lessons in class, did you?' 
“我！噢，不！我管理家务，看管仆人，就像我的朋友珀莱夫人为她的儿子所做的那样——别的都不干。啊！你还以为我在学校上课吧，是吗？”
And she laughed loud and long, as though the idea tickled her fancy amazingly.
她大笑不止，似乎我的想法不可思议地激起了她的幻想。
'Madame is in the wrong to laugh,' I observed. 'If she does not give lessons, I am sure it is not because she cannot.' And I whipped out a white pocket-handkerchief, and wafted it, with a French grace, past my nose, bowing at the same time.
“夫人您不该笑的，”我说道。“如果您没有授课，我确定并不是因为您教不了。”说着我从口袋里抽出一方白手帕，抖开它，以法国式的优雅擦了擦鼻子，同时鞠了个躬。
'Quel charmant jeune homme!' murmured Madame Pelet in a low voice, Madame Reuter, being less sentimental, as she was Flamand and not French, only laughed again.
“多么讨人喜欢的年轻人呐！”珀莱夫人低声夸道，勒泰夫人并不那么富有情感，只是又笑了笑，因为她是佛兰芒人而不是法国人。
'You are a dangerous person, I fear,' said she. 'If you can forge compliments at that rate, Zoraïde will positively be afraid of you; but if you are good, I will keep your secret, and not tell her how well you can flatter. Now, listen what sort of a proposal she makes to you. She has heard that you are an excellent professor, and as she wishes to get the very best masters for her school (car Zoraïde fait tout comme une reine, c'est une veritable maitresse-femme), she has commissioned me to step over this afternoon, and sound Madame Pelet as to the possibility of engaging you. Zoraéde is a wary general; she never advances without first examining well her ground. I don't think she would be pleased if she knew I had already disclosed her intentions to you. She did not order me to go so far, but I thought there would be no harm in letting you into the secret, and Madame Pelet was of the same opinion. Take care, however, you don't betray either of us to Zoraïde—to my daughter, I mean. She is so discreet and circumspect herself, she cannot understand that one should find a pleasure in gossiping a little.'

Having regained my own room, I set myself to turn over in my mind the incident of the evening. It seemed a queer affair altogether, and queerly managed. The two old women had made quite a little intricate mess of it; still, I found that the uppermost feeling in my mind on the subject was one of satisfaction. In the first place, it would be a change to give lessons in another seminary; and then to teach young ladies would be an occupation so interesting; to be admitted at all into a ladies' boarding-school would be an incident so new in my life. Besides, thought I, as I glanced at the boarded window, 'I shall now at last see the mysterious garden. I shall gaze both on the angels and their Eden.' 
回到自己的房间，我便自己在脑海里将晚上的事情顺了一遍。总而言之这件事很蹊跷，而且处理得很奇怪。两位老太太把整件事情搅合得有点错综复杂；但是，我心中对于这件事最主要的感觉倒是一种满足感。首先，在另一所学校授课是一种改变；其次教年轻的女学生们会是一件有趣的事情；被邀请去给寄宿学校的女学生们上课将是我人生中一种全新的体验。此外，当我瞥见那个被木板钉起来的窗户时，我心想，“现在起码我可以见到那个神秘的花园了。我将看到天使们和她们的伊甸园了。”
