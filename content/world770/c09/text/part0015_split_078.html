CIVIC VALOR.  (Monthly Story.)  

市民英雄  （每月故事）  

At one o'clock we went with our schoolmaster to the front of the town-hall, to see the medal for civic valor bestowed on the lad who saved one of his comrades from the Po.
下午一点钟，老师领我们来到市政厅前，观看给那个从波河救起同伴的少年颁发市民英雄奖的仪式。
On the front terrace waved a huge tricolored flag.
宅邸正面的露台上飘着一面巨大的三色旗。
We entered the courtyard of the palace.
我们走进宅邸的庭院。
It was already full of people. At the further end of it there was visible a table with a red cover, and papers on it, and behind it a row of gilded chairs for the mayor and the council; the ushers of the municipality were there, with their under-waistcoats of sky-blue and their white stockings. To the right of the courtyard a detachment of policemen, who had a great many medals, was drawn up in line; and beside them a detachment of custom-house officers; on the other side were the firemen in festive array; and numerous soldiers not in line, who had come to look on,—cavalrymen, sharpshooters, artillery-men. Then all around were gentlemen, country people, and some officers and women and boys who had assembled. We crowded into a corner where many scholars from other buildings were already collected with their teachers; and near us was a group of boys belonging to the common people, between ten and eighteen years of age, who were talking and laughing loudly; and we made out that they were all from Borgo Po, comrades or acquaintances of the boy who was to receive the medal. Above, all the windows were thronged with the employees of the city government; the balcony of the library was also filled with people, who pressed against the balustrade; and in the one on the opposite side, which is over the entrance gate, stood a crowd of girls from the public schools, and many Daughters of military men, with their pretty blue veils. It looked like a theatre. All were talking merrily, glancing every now and then at the red table, to see whether any one had made his appearance. A band of music was playing softly at the extremity of the portico. The sun beat down on the lofty walls. It was beautiful.
那里已是人山人海。在庭院的另一头摆着张铺着红色桌布的桌子，桌上放着文件，桌后摆着市长和议员们的镀金座椅，旁边站着身着天蓝色背心和白袜子的市政厅引座员。在庭院右边，一队胸前挂满勋章的警察笔直地排成一列，他们的一侧站着一队海关官员，另一侧则站着身着盛装的消防员；还有许多分散站着的士兵——骑兵、狙击手和炮兵，他们是前来旁观的。庭院四周聚集着绅士、平民、一些官员、妇女和孩子。我们挤到一个角落里，那里已集合了许多来自其他学校的师生。我们附近有一群十到十八岁的平民少年，他们大声地谈笑着。据说他们都来自波镇，是授勋少年的同学或者朋友。楼上的窗口处挤满了市政厅的雇员，图书室的阳台上也有许多人靠着栏杆观看，对面大门楼上的阳台还站着一群公立学校的女学生和许多戴着美丽蓝色面纱的军人女子协会的会员。整个庭院就像是一个剧场。大家都愉快地交谈着，时不时地向红桌子的方向望一眼，看看是否有人出来。一个乐队在廊下一角演奏着轻柔的音乐。明亮的太阳光照耀着高大的围墙。场景十分美丽。
All at once every one began to clap their hands, from the courtyard, from the balconies, from the windows.
突然，庭院中、阳台上、窗口处，到处都响起了掌声。
I raised myself on tiptoe to look.
我踮起脚尖张望。
The crowd which stood behind the red table had parted, and a man and woman had come forward. The man was leading a boy by the hand.
站在红桌子后面的人群已经分成左右两排，一男一女走上前来。男子牵着一位少年的手。
This was the lad who had saved his comrade.
这就是救出同伴的少年。
The man was his father, a mason, dressed in his best. The woman, his mother, small and blond, had on a black gown. The boy, also small and blond, had on a gray jacket.
男子是他的父亲，一个石匠，身着盛装。女子就是他的母亲，身材娇小，长着一头金发，穿着一件黑色礼服。少年也是身材矮小，一头金发，穿着灰色的夹克。
At the sight of all those people, and at the sound of that thunder of applause, all three stood still, not daring to look nor to move. A municipal usher pushed them along to the side of the table on the right. All remained quiet for a moment, and then once more the applause broke out on all sides. The boy glanced up at the windows, and then at the balcony with the Daughters of military men; he held his cap in his hand, and did not seem to understand very thoroughly where he was. It struck me that he looked a little like Coretti, in the face; but he was redder. His father and mother kept their eyes fixed on the table.
看见众人，听见那如雷般的掌声，三人全都僵僵地站在那里，既不敢张望也不敢走动。一位市政厅的引座员把他们领到了桌子的右边。大家安静了一会儿，而后又掌声四起。少年先是仰望着窗口，又看了看站着军人女子协会会员的阳台。他手中拿着帽子，似乎不太明白自己在哪里。我突然觉得少年长得有些像可莱谛，只不过他的面色更红润一些。他的父母双眼紧盯着桌子。
In the meantime, all the boys from Borgo Po who were near us were making motions to their comrade, to attract his attention, and hailing him in a low tone: Pin! Pin! Pinot! By dint of calling they made themselves heard. The boy glanced at them, and hid his smile behind his cap.
与此同时，我们旁边那群来自波镇的男孩们都向少年招手示意，想吸引他的注意力，并且小声地喊着：皮！皮！皮诺！他们反复地喊着，少年终于听见了。他看着他们，用帽子掩饰着自己的笑容。
At a certain moment the guards put themselves in the attitude of attention.
突然，士兵们立正以待。
The mayor entered, accompanied by numerous gentlemen.
市长和许多乡绅走了进来。
The mayor, all white, with a big tricolored scarf, placed himself beside the table, standing; all the others took their places behind and beside him.
市长身着一身白衣，围着一大块三色围巾。他站到桌前，其余的人在他两旁或者后面就坐。
The band ceased playing; the mayor made a sign, and every one kept quiet.
乐队停止演奏。市长做了一个手势，全场都肃静下来。
He began to speak. I did not understand the first words perfectly; but I gathered that he was telling the story of the boy's feat. Then he raised his voice, and it rang out so clear and sonorous through the whole court, that I did not lose another word: "When he saw, from the shore, his comrade struggling in the river, already overcome with the fear of death, he tore the clothes from his back, and hastened to his assistance, without hesitating an instant. They shouted to him, 'You will be drowned!'—he made no reply; they caught hold of him—he freed himself; they called him by name—he was already in the water. The river was swollen; the risk terrible, even for a man. But he flung himself to meet death with all the strength of his little body and of his great heart; he reached the unfortunate fellow and seized him just in time, when he was already under water, and dragged him to the surface; he fought furiously with the waves, which strove to overwhelm him, with his companion who tried to cling to him; and several times he disappeared beneath the water, and rose again with a desperate effort; obstinate, invincible in his purpose, not like a boy who was trying to save another boy, but like a man, like a father who is struggling to save his son, who is his hope and his life. In short, God did not permit so generous a prowess to be displayed in vain. The child swimmer tore the victim from the gigantic river, and brought him to land, and with the assistance of others, rendered him his first succor; after which he returned home quietly and alone, and ingenuously narrated his deed.
他便开始演说。我没有听清他开头的几句话，但估计那是在叙说少年的功绩。后来他的声音渐高，清楚地响彻全场，我便没再漏掉一句：“当少年在河岸上看见他的同伴在水中挣扎、被死亡的恐惧攫住时，他毫不犹豫地脱去衣服，跳入水中救人。旁边的孩子向他喊道：‘你会被淹死的！’——他却不予理会；他们又抓住他——他挣脱了阻拦；他们叫着他的名字——他已经跳入水中了。河水正满，即使大人下去也很危险。但他凭着弱小的身躯和一颗伟大的心灵，拼尽全力同死神抗争；及时地抓住了已经沉入水里的同伴，将其拽回水面；又带着依傍着自己的同伴，同迎面扑来的波浪英勇搏斗；他几次沉入水底，又拼死重返水面；顽强不屈地坚定着自己的目标，不像是一位试图救助伙伴的少年，而更像是一个大人，一个挣扎着挽救自己儿子的父亲——儿子就是他的希望和生命。简言之，上帝也不允许这样的英勇行径付之东流。少年从巨大的河流中救起了落水者，将他带回岸上，并在其他人的帮助下救活了他。事后少年若无其事地独自回到家里，平淡地讲述了这件事。
"Gentlemen, beautiful, and worthy of veneration is heroism in a man! But in a child, in whom there can be no prompting of ambition or of profit whatever; in a child, who must have all the more ardor in proportion as he has less strength; in a child, from whom we require nothing, who is bound to nothing, who already appears to us so noble and lovable, not when he acts, but when he merely understands, and is grateful for the sacrifices of others;—in a child, heroism is divine! I will say nothing more, gentlemen. I do not care to deck, with superfluous praises, such simple grandeur. Here before you stands the noble and valorous rescuer. Soldier, greet him as a brother; mothers, bless him like a son; children, remember his name, engrave on your minds his visage, that it may nevermore be erased from your memories and from your hearts. Approach, my boy. In the name of the king of Italy, I give you the medal for civic valor." 
“先生们，对于一个成年人来说，勇敢已是一种美好并令人尊敬的美德！但他只是一个孩子，一个没有名利之念的孩子，一个身体弱小却热心奉献的孩子，一个没有任何义务与责任的孩子——即使他不做什么，只要懂得并对他人的牺牲感恩，就够高尚与可爱了——对于一个孩子来说，他的勇敢是神圣的！我无需多说，先生们。对于这样简单的义举，我无需再加无谓的赞扬。现在站在大家面前的就是那位高尚而勇敢的救人者。军人们，请像欢迎兄弟一样欢迎他；母亲们，请像祝福儿子一样祝福他；孩子们，请记住他的名字，把他的容貌印在心里，让他在你们的记忆里和心中永存。请过来，我的孩子。以意大利国王的名义，我授予你公民英雄勋章。”
An extremely loud hurrah, uttered at the same moment by many voices, made the palace ring.
一时间，众人雷鸣般的喝彩声把整个宅邸震得直响。
The mayor took the medal from the table, and fastened it on the boy's breast. Then he embraced and kissed him. The mother placed one hand over her eyes; the father held his chin on his breast.
市长从桌上拿过勋章，挂在了少年的胸前。接着他又拥抱并亲吻了少年。那母亲一只手捂住双眼，父亲也垂下了头。
The mayor shook hands with both; and taking the decree of decoration, which was bound with a ribbon, he handed it to the woman.
市长和少年的父母握手，将束着丝带的奖状递给母亲。
Then he turned to the boy again, and said: "May the memory of this day, which is such a glorious one for you, such a happy one for your father and mother, keep you all your life in the path of virtue and honor! Farewell!" 
之后他又转向少年，说道：“今天是你非常光荣的一天，是你父母非常幸福的一天，愿你永生不忘今天，在美德和荣誉的道路上前行。再见！”
The mayor withdrew, the band struck up, and everything seemed to be at an end, when the detachment of firemen opened, and a lad of eight or nine years, pushed forwards by a woman who instantly concealed herself, rushed towards the boy with the decoration, and flung himself in his arms.
市长退了下去，乐队演奏起来，仪式似乎到此结束了。这时一位妇女从消防员队列中推出一个八九岁的孩子，之后就立刻藏了起来。孩子跑向拿着奖状的少年，扑入他的怀抱。
Another outburst of hurrahs and applause made the courtyard echo; every one had instantly understood that this was the boy who had been saved from the Po, and who had come to thank his rescuer. After kissing him, he clung to one arm, in order to accompany him out. These two, with the father and mother following behind, took their way towards the door, making a path with difficulty among the people who formed in line to let them pass,—policemen, boys, soldiers, women, all mingled together in confusion. All pressed forwards and raised on tiptoe to see the boy. Those who stood near him as he passed, touched his hand. When he passed before the schoolboys, they all waved their caps in the air. Those from Borgo Po made a great uproar, pulling him by the arms and by his jacket and shouting. "Pin! hurrah for Pin! bravo, Pinot!" I saw him pass very close to me. His face was all aflame and happy; his medal had a red, white, and green ribbon. His mother was crying and smiling; his father was twirling his mustache with one hand, which trembled violently, as though he had a fever. And from the windows and the balconies the people continued to lean out and applaud. All at once, when they were on the point of entering the portico, there descended from the balcony of the Daughters of military men a veritable shower of pansies, of bunches of violets and daisies, which fell upon the head of the boy, and of his father and mother, and scattered over the ground. Many people stooped to pick them up and hand them to the mother. And the band at the further end of the courtyard played, very, very softly, a most entrancing air, which seemed like a song by a great many silver voices fading slowly into the distance on the banks of a river.
喝彩声再次响起，掌声在庭院中回荡。人们立刻明白他就是被少年从波河救起的孩子，前来感谢自己的救命恩人。他亲吻少年之后，挽着少年的手臂一起往外走。人们排成队列，让出了过道，两个孩子和他们身后的那对父母艰难地从过道挤向大门——警察、小孩、军人和妇女全都乱成一团。人们都向前拥挤，踮起脚尖去看少年。他经过时，离他近的人们就抚摸他的手。他走过学生队伍时，他们都在空中挥舞着帽子。波镇来的孩子们一阵骚动，纷纷握住他的手臂或是拉住他的上衣，狂呼着。“皮！皮万岁！万岁，皮诺！”我看见少年在离我很近的地方经过。他红光满面，非常高兴。他的勋章上系着一条红白绿三色的丝带。他的母亲又哭又笑，父亲也用颤抖的手捻着胡须。他的手抖得非常厉害，就好像发烧了一样。窗口和阳台上的人们也都探着身子鼓掌。他们一走进门廊，军人女子协会的会员们就纷纷从阳台上抛下三色堇花瓣、成串的紫罗兰和雏菊。花落在少年和他父母的头上，并且散落了一地。许多人俯身拾起它们，并递给那个母亲。庭院另一边的乐队演奏着一段非常非常轻柔、最为引人痴迷的曲调，像是许多银铃般的声音在歌唱，慢慢地消失在远处的河岸上。
