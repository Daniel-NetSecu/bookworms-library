Chapter 18  

第十八章  

To tell you of our new life in any detail would be no easy matter. It was made up of a series of frivolous diversions which, though delightful to us, would be quite meaningless to anyone who heard me recount them. You know what it is to love a woman. You know how short the days seem and how loving the ease with which you let yourself drift towards the morrow. You are acquainted with that general neglect of things which is bred of violent, trusting, requited love. Any mortal being who is not the woman you love seems superfluous to creation. You regret having tossed pieces of your heart to other women, and you cannot imagine the prospect of ever holding a hand which is not the hand that you now hold clasped in yours. Your brain will entertain neither work nor memories, nor anything which might divert it from the one thought with which it is endlessly regaled. Each day you discover some new attraction in your mistress, some unknown sensual delight.
要把我们的新生活详详细细地告诉您可不是件容易的事。它由一系列琐碎的消遣组成，尽管对我们来说充满了快乐，但对听故事的人来说却是没有意义的。您知道爱一个女人是怎样一回事。您知道那些白天显得多么短暂，第二天清晨那么轻易地到来又是多么令人欢喜。您也知道那种相互分享、相互信赖的热烈爱情，可以让人忘掉一切。除了所爱的女人，其他任何人似乎都与您毫不相干。您后悔曾经喜欢过其他女人，甚至无法想象除了现在紧握着的这只手，您还会握住其他人的手。您的头脑不再考虑工作，也不再回忆过去，心里只有一个念头，那个给大脑带来无尽欢乐的念头，其他的任何想法都没法动摇它。每天您都能在情人身上发现一点儿新的魅力和一种未知的快感。
Life is no more than the repeated fulfilling of a permanent desire. The soul is merely the vestal handmaid whose task is to keep the sacred flame of love burning.
人生不过是在不断满足一种永恒的欲望。灵魂不过是维持爱情圣火不灭的贞洁圣女。
Often, after dark, we would go and sit in the little wood which overlooked the house. There we listened to the happy song of evening as we both thought of the approaching moment which would leave us in each other's arms till morning. At other times, we would stay in bed all day and not let even the sun into our bedroom. The curtains would be tightly drawn, and for us the world outside momentarily stopped turning. Nanine alone was authorized to open our door, but only to bring us our meals—and even so we ate them without getting up, and interrupted them constantly with laughter and all kinds of foolishness. And then would follow a few moments of sleep, for, retreating completely into our love, we were like two persistent divers who return to the surface only to take breath.
到了晚上，我们经常坐在那片可以俯瞰到我们房子的小树林里。我们倾听着夜晚快乐的歌曲，想着在那个即将到来的时刻我们又可以相互拥抱着直到清晨。有些时候，我们会整日地躺在床上，甚至不让阳光照进我们的卧室来。窗帘紧闭着，对我们而言，外面的一切都暂时停止了运转。只有纳尼娜可以打开我们的房门，但也只是送饭给我们，即使这样，我们也不起来而是在床上吃，还不时被嬉笑和各种蠢事打断。接着我们就再小睡一会儿，因为我们深深地陷入这样的爱情中，就像两个执着的潜水员，只有在换气时才会浮出水面。
However, I would catch Marguerite looking sad, and sometimes there were tears in her eyes. I would ask what was the reason for her sudden dejection and she would answer: This love of ours, my dearest Armand, is no ordinary love. You love me as though I'd never belonged to anyone else, and I tremble for fear that with time, regretting that you ever loved me and turning my past into a crime to hold against me, you might force me to resume the life from which you took me. Remember this: now that I've tasted a new kind of life, I should die if I had to take up the old one. So tell me you'll never leave me.'
但有好几次，我都看到玛格丽特很难过的样子，有时甚至眼里含着眼泪。我问她为什么忽然这么伤心，她回答说：“我们的爱情不是普通的爱情，我最亲爱的阿尔芒。你那么爱我，就像我从未失身于别人一样，但我很害怕总有一天，您会后悔您爱过我，把我的过去当作一种罪恶来指责我，逼迫我不得不回到您帮我脱离掉的之前的那种生活。请记住这点：现在我已经尝到新生活的滋味，如果再回到过去，我肯定会死的。所以告诉我，你永远不会离开我。”
'I swear it!'
“我向你发誓！”
At this, she would stare at me, as though she could read in my eyes whether my oath was sincere. Then she would throw herself into my arms and, burying her head in my chest, say: It's just that you have no idea how much I love you!'
听到这句话，她会盯着我看，似乎想从我的眼里看出这誓言到底是不是真的。然后她会扑到我怀里，把头埋在我的胸口，对我说：“只因你不知道我有多么爱你！”
One evening, we were leaning over the balcony outside our window. We gazed at the moon struggling to rise from its bed of clouds. We listened to the noise of the wind as it shook the trees. We held hands, and had not spoken for a good quarter of an hour when Marguerite said: Winter's coming. Would you like us to go away?'
一天晚上，我们倚在窗外的阳台上。我们凝望月亮，它正从云做的床上挣扎着起来。我们倾听着风摇动树木时发出的沙沙声。我们手握着手，大概有一刻钟没有说话，玛格丽特突然说：“冬天就要来了。我们离开这儿吧，你说好吗？”
'Where would we go?'
“我们去哪里呢？”
'Italy.'
“意大利。”
'Are you bored here?'
“你觉得在这儿呆腻了？”
'I'm afraid of winter. And I'm even more afraid of our going back to Paris.'
“我害怕冬天。我更害怕回到巴黎去。”
'Why?'
“为什么呢？”
'Lots of reasons.'
“原因很多。”
And she went on quickly, without explaining the reasons for her fears: Do you want to leave this place? I'll sell everything I have. We'll go and live far away. There'll be nothing left of the person I used to be. No one will know who I am. Would you like that?'
她没有告诉我她害怕的原因，只是立刻接着说：“你想要离开这儿吗？我会把我的财产全都卖掉。我们会离开这儿，住得远远的。在那儿不会留下任何我过去的痕迹。没有人知道我是谁。你觉得好吗？”
'We'll go, if that's what you want, Marguerite. Let's travel,' I said, 'but why the need to sell things you'll be glad to have when we get back? I haven't got enough money to accept a sacrifice like that, but I do have enough for us to travel in style for five or six months, if you fancy the idea at all.'
“如果你想去的话，我们就去，玛格丽特。我们去那旅行，”我说，“但你为什么要把东西都卖掉呢？你回来时看到这些东西，还是会很开心的啊。我没有足够的钱来接受你这样的牺牲，但我的钱足以支撑我们作一次五六个月的旅行，只要你喜欢的话。”
'If that's the way of it, no,' she continued, leaving the window and moving to the sofa in the dark shadow of the bedroom. What's the point of going all that way to spend money? I cost you enough here as it is.'
“如果是这样的话，我们还是不要去了，”她边说边离开窗户，坐到了房间暗处的沙发里。“到那里去花钱有什么意义呢？我在这儿已经花了你不少钱了。”
'That sounds like a reproach, Marguerite. You're being ungracious.'
“这听起来像是埋怨啊，玛格丽特。这样可不好。”
'Forgive me, my dear,' she said, holding out her hand to me, 'this stormy weather makes me irritable. I'm not saying what I mean.'
“原谅我，亲爱的，”她伸出手对我说，“这种暴风雨天气使我变得急躁。我并不是那个意思。”
And, after kissing me, she sat for a long time, lost in thought.
她吻了吻我，又在那里坐了好久，陷入了沉思。
Scenes like this occurred on several occasions and, though I remained ignorant as to their cause, I nevertheless sensed in Marguerite a feeling of anxiety for the future. It was not that she could have any doubts about my love for her, for it grew deeper with each passing day. And yet I ofter saw that she was sad, though she never explained why she was sad other than by alleging some physical reason.
这样的情况出现了好几次，尽管我仍不知道其中的原因，但我却感觉到了玛格丽特对未来的忧虑。她并不是怀疑我对她的爱，因为在过去的日子里，我对她的爱与日俱增。但是我却常常看到她一脸忧愁，尽管她从不向我解释她忧愁的原因，只是声称身体不舒服。
Fearing that she would weary of too monotonous a life, I suggested that we might return to Paris, but she invariably rejected the suggestion, and assured me that she could not be as happy anywhere as she was in the country.
我担心她对这种单调的生活厌烦了，就建议我们可以回巴黎去，但她还是一样拒绝了，还说再也没有什么地方会比这个乡下更令她快乐。
Prudence made only rare visits now. On the other hand, she wrote a number of letters which I never asked to see, although each one left Marguerite deeply preoccupied. I did not know what to make of it.
普鲁登斯现在也不怎么来了。但她经常写信过来，我从未要求看那些信，尽管玛格丽特总会被它们弄得心事重重。我不知道为什么。
One day, Marguerite remained in her room. I entered. She was writing.
有一天，玛格丽特呆在自己的房间里。我进去了。她正在写信。
'Who are you writing to?' I asked her.
“在给谁写信？”我问她。
'Prudence. Do you want me to read out what I've written?'
“普鲁登斯。要不要我把信读给你听？”
I had a profound distaste for anything that could seem like suspiciousness. So I answered Marguerite saying that there was no need for me to know what she was writing. And yet, I was sure of it, that letter would have acquainted me with the real reason for her fits of sadness.
对任何猜忌的行为我都讨厌至极。因此，我回答玛格丽特说我不需要知道她写些什么。但是，我可以断定这封信可以告诉我玛格丽特阵阵忧愁的真正原因。
The next day, the weather was superb. Marguerite suggested that we might take a boat out on the river and visit the le de Croissy. She seemed in the best of spirits. It was five o'clock by the time we got back.
第二天，天气特别好。玛格丽特提议我们乘船去克鲁瓦西岛玩。她看起来心情特别好。我们回到家时已经五点钟了。
'Madame Duvernoy came,' said Nanine as soon as she saw us come in.
“迪韦努瓦太太来过。”纳尼娜一看到我们进门就说。
'Did she go away again?' asked Marguerite.
“她走了吗？”玛格丽特问。
'Yes, in Madame's carriage. She said it was all right to take it.'
“是的，坐夫人的马车走的。她说这没关系。”
'Very good,' said Marguerite quickly. Let dinner be served at once.'
“很好。”玛格丽特紧接着说。“快点开饭吧。”
Two days later, there was a letter from Prudence, and for the next fortnight Marguerite seemed to have done with her mysterious sad moods, for which she never stopped asking me to forgive her now that they had ceased.
两天之后，普鲁登斯来了一封信，之后的两个星期，玛格丽特似乎也告别了她那神秘的忧伤情绪，而且还不断地要求我为这件事原谅她。
However, the carriage did not come back.
然而，马车却再也没有回来。
'How is it that Prudence hasn't returned your brougham?' I asked one day.
“普鲁登斯为什么还不把马车还给你呢？”有天我问她。
One of the horses is sick, and the carriage needs some repairs. It's better for all that to be done while we are still here where we don't need a carriage, than to wait until we get back to Paris.'
“有匹马病了，而且马车也需要修理了。我们在这里也用不到马车，现在就把它修理好，总比等到我们回巴黎之后再修好。”
Prudence came down to see us a few days after this and confirmed what Marguerite had told me.
过了几天，普鲁登斯来看我们，向我证实了之前玛格丽特的话。
The two women went for a stroll by themselves in the garden, and when I joined them they changed the subject they had been discussing.
两个女人在花园中散步，当我走近她们时，她们马上转变了刚才的话题。
That evening, as she was going, Prudence complained of the cold and asked Marguerite to lend her an Indian shawl.
那天晚上，普鲁登斯临走前抱怨天气太冷，把玛格丽特的开司米披肩借走了。
And so a month went by during which Marguerite was gayer and more loving than she had ever been.
之后一个月，玛格丽特比过去都要高兴，也更加爱我了。
However, the carriage had not come back, and the Indian shawl had not been returned. All this puzzled me in spite of myself and, since I knew in which drawer Marguerite kept Prudence's letters, I took advantage of a moment when she was at the bottom of the garden, hurried to the drawer and tried to open it. But it was no use: it was double-locked.
然而，马车没有回来，开司米披肩也再没送回来。我不由得开始对这一切产生怀疑，我知道玛格丽特把普鲁登斯的信都放在哪个抽屉里，于是，我利用玛格丽特在花园的一小会儿时间，匆忙跑到这个抽屉跟前，想打开看看。但一点用都没有，它被上了两层锁。
I then searched through the drawers where her trinkets and diamonds were normally kept. They opened without difficulty, but the jewel-cases had disappeared—along with their contents, naturally.
我又翻了她之前存放首饰和钻石的抽屉。我不费力气就打开了它们，但那些首饰盒却都消失了——自然里面的首饰也不见了。
A pang of fear shot through my heart.
一阵恐慌涌上我的心头。
I was about to go and ask Marguerite to tell me exactly why these items were missing. But I knew for certain that she would not admit the truth.
我很想去问问玛格丽特这些东西到底是怎么不见的。但我知道她是肯定不会告诉我实情的。
So I said: 'My dear Marguerite, I want to ask if it's all right for me to go up to town. No one where I live knows where I am, and there must have been letters from my father. I expect he's worried. I must write to him.'
因此我对她说：“亲爱的玛格丽特，我想问我是否可以回巴黎一趟。我住的地方没有人知道我现在在哪儿，那儿肯定有很多我父亲的来信。我想他肯定很担心。我必须给他写封信回去。”
'Go, my dear,' she said. But be back soon.'
“去吧，亲爱的。”她说。“但要快点儿回来啊。”
I left.
我走了。
I hurried round to Prudence's at once.
我立即赶到了普鲁登斯那里。
'Look here,' I said, without preamble of any sort, 'answer me frankly: where are Marguerite's horses?'
“听着，”我开门见山地说，“诚实地回答我，玛格丽特的马都去哪了？”
'Sold.'
“卖掉了。”
'Her shawl?'
“她的披肩呢？”
'Sold.'
“卖掉了。”
'The diamonds?'
“那些钻石呢？”
'Pawned.'
“当掉了。”
'And who did the selling and the pawning?'
“是谁把它们卖掉或者当掉的？”
'I did.'
“是我。”
'Why didn't you tell me about all this?'
“您为什么不把这些告诉我？”
'Because Marguerite ordered me not to.'
“因为玛格丽特不让我告诉您。”
'And why didn't you ask me for money?'
“为什么您不找我要钱呢？”
'Because she wouldn't let me.'
“因为她不让我这样做的。”
'And what's the money been spent on?'
“这些钱是拿来做什么的？”
'Paying debts.'
“还债。”
'So she owes a great deal?'
“这么说她欠了很多债？”
'There's thirty thousand francs or so outstanding. I told you, dear, didn't I? You just wouldn't believe me. Well then, are you convinced now? The upholsterer, who had the Duke as her guarantor, was shown the door when he went to see the Duke who wrote him a letter the next day saying that he wouldn't lift a finger for Mademoiselle Gautier. The man wanted money. He was given something on account—the few thousand francs I asked you for. Then some kind souls let him know that his non-paying customer had been dropped by the Duke and was living with some young man who had no money. The other creditors were likewise told. They demanded money, and repossessed some of their goods. Marguerite wanted to sell everything, but it was too late and besides, I should have been against it. She had to pay of course, and to avoid asking you for money, she sold her horses and her Indian shawls and pawned her jewels. Do you want the buyers' receipts and the pawn tickets?'
“欠了三万法郎左右。我告诉过您，亲爱的，不是吗？您只是不相信我。现在您相信了吧？那个地毯商，之前有公爵替她做担保人，去找公爵时吃了闭门羹，第二天公爵给他写信说他再也不会为玛格丽特·戈蒂埃付一个铜板了。那个人来要钱。我们只好分期付给他，我之前从您这里要走的那几千法郎就是给他的。后来，他从一些好心人那里听说了公爵已经不再理会他的债务人了，她现在跟一个身无分文的年轻人住在一起。其他债主也同样知道了这件事。他们也来要债，还查封了她的一些财产。玛格丽特想把东西都卖了，但已经太晚了，而且，我也不赞成这个做法。她不得不还债，而她又不想找您要钱，因此她卖掉了她的马和开司米披肩，当掉了她的珠宝。您想看那些收据和当票吗？”
And, pulling out a drawer, Prudence showed me the papers.
她拉开一个抽屉，把里面的票据递给我看。
'Do you imagine,' she continued, as persistent as any woman who is entitled to say: 'I was right!', 'do you imagine that it's enough to love each other and go off to the country and live some dreamy, rustic life? Oh no, my dear. Alongside the ideal life, there's the necessities to think of, and the purest designs are earthbound, secured by threads which, ludicrous though they may be, are made of steel and cannot be easily snapped. If Marguerite hasn't deceived you twenty times and more it's because she has an exceptional nature. It's not her fault if I advised her to do so, because it grieved me to see the poor girl strip herself of everything. And she wouldn't have anything to do with it! She told me she loved you and wouldn't deceive you for anything. All that's very nice, very poetic, but it's not coin you can pay off creditors with. And now she's reached the stage where she won't get away with it unless she comes up with, let me say it again, thirty thousand francs.'
“您真的以为，”她接着说，脸上的表情跟那些洋洋得意地宣布着“看吧，我说对了”的女人一模一样，“您真的以为，你们只要相爱，然后躲到乡下去就能过那种梦一般的田园生活了吗？噢，不是的，我亲爱的。除了这种理想生活，我们还要考虑现实，那些最纯洁的欲望有时不得不被一些看来可笑，但却被钢铁般难以折断的绳索束缚。如果玛格丽特从来不骗您，那是因为她有着与众不同的性格。我劝她这么做，她并没有做错，因为我真不忍心看这个可怜的姑娘失去一切。但她却不听我的话！她告诉我说她爱您，她绝不会欺骗您。这些听起来那么美好，那么诗情画意，但这不能替她还债。现在到了无法逃脱的地步，除非凑齐，我再告诉您一次，三万法郎。”
'It's all right. I'll find the money.'
“没关系。我会弄到钱的。”
'You'll borrow it?'
“您打算去借钱？”
'But of course.'
“当然了。”
'Now that would be really clever. You'll fall out with your father, tie up your allowance and, anyway, you can't just come up with thirty thousand francs from one day to the next. Take it from me, my dear Armand, I know women better than you do. Don't do it: it would be sheer folly and you'd regret it some day. Be reasonable. I don't say you should leave Marguerite; just live with her on the same footing as at the start of the summer. Let her find ways out of this mess. The Duke will come round gradually. Count de N, if she takes him on, he was telling me just yesterday, will pay all her debts and give her four or five thousand francs a month. He's got two hundred thousand livres a year. She'll be set up, whereas you're going to have to leave her in any case: don't wait until you're ruined, especially since this Count de N is a fool and there'll be nothing to stop you being Marguerite's lover. She'll cry a little to start with, but she'll get used to it in the end, and she'll thank you one day for what you did. Tell yourself that Marguerite's married, and then deceive her husband. That's all there's to it.
“这主意可真不错啊。您会跟您父亲闹翻，经济来源也会被冻结，无论如何，您也没办法在一两天内弄到三万法郎。听我的话吧，我亲爱的阿尔芒，我比您更了解女人。别这么做，这种做法太愚蠢了，您总有一天会后悔的。理智点儿吧。我并不是要您离开玛格丽特，您只要像初夏时那样跟她生活在一起就行了。让她自己来想办法解决问题。公爵会慢慢改变主意的。N伯爵昨天还告诉我，如果她愿意接待他，他可以帮她把债都还上，每个月再给她四五千法郎。他每年能赚二十万利弗尔。她可以依靠他，而您总有一天是要离开她的，您不要等到破产之后才这么做，况且N伯爵就是一个笨蛋，他绝不会影响您继续做玛格丽特的情人。她最开始肯定要伤心一阵子，但她总会习惯，最后没准儿她还会感谢您所做的一切。告诉您自己，玛格丽特已经结婚了，您欺骗了她的丈夫。这不就行了。
'I've already told you all this once. But then I was just giving you advice. Today, you've got very little option.'
“我之前曾给您说过这些。那时我只是给您一些忠告。但现在您已经别无选择了。”
Prudence was right, cruelly right.
普鲁登斯是对的，对得残酷。
'That's how it is,' she continued, shutting away the papers she had just shown me. Kept women always expect that there'll be men around who'll love them, but they never imagine that they themselves will fall in love. Otherwise, they'd put a bit to one side and, by the time they're thirty, they'd be able to afford the luxury of taking a lover who pays nothing. If only I'd known once what I know now! But that's by the by. Don't say anything to Marguerite; just bring her back to Paris. You've had four or five months alone with her, which isn't bad. Turn a blind eye, that's all you're asked to do. Within a fortnight, she'll take on Count de N, she'll put some money by this winter, and then next summer you can pick up where you left off. That's how it's done, my dear!'
“就是这么回事，”她收起之前给我看过的那些票据，接着说道。“妓女总期盼着周围有男人来爱她，但她们却从没想到，自己有一天也会爱上别人。不然，她们就会开始攒钱，等到三十岁的时候，就能负担得起和一个一分钱不给的情人的生活了。要是我能早点儿明白这点就好了！只不过是顺带提一句。您跟玛格丽特什么也别说，只把她带回巴黎就好了。您已经跟她单独呆了四五个月了，这已经很不错了。您所需要做的就是睁一只眼闭一只眼。两周之内，她就会再接待N伯爵，她能在这个冬天攒点儿钱，这样明年夏天您就可以继续跟她过这种生活。事情就这么办，我亲爱的！”
Prudence seemed delighted with her advice, which I rejected indignantly.
普鲁登斯似乎对自己的提议很满意，但我却愤怒地拒绝了。
Not only did love and self-respect make it impossible for me to act along these lines, but I was further convinced that, having got to the stage she had now reached, Marguerite would rather die than accept such an arrangement.
我的爱和自尊不允许我这么做，而且我深信既然走到了这一步，玛格丽特就是死也不愿接受这样的安排。
'Enough of this nonsense,' I told Prudence. How much exactly does Marguerite need?'
“停止您的胡言乱语吧。”我对普鲁登斯说。“玛格丽特到底需要多少钱？”
'I told you. Around thirty thousand francs.'
“我告诉过您了。三万法郎左右。”
'And when must she have it?'
“这笔钱什么时候要？”
'Within two months.'
“两个月内。”
'She'll have it.'
“她会有这笔钱的。”
Prudence shrugged her shoulders.
普鲁登斯耸耸肩。
'I'll get it to you,' I continued. But you must swear you'll never tell Marguerite that I gave it to you.'
“我会把这笔钱给您的。”我接着说，“但您必须保证不告诉玛格丽特是我把钱给您的。”
'Don't worry, I won't.'
“别担心，我不会告诉她的。”
'And if she sends you anything else to sell or pawn, let me know.'
“还有，如果她再给您什么东西让您卖掉或当掉的话，请一定告诉我。”
'There's no danger of that. She's got nothing left.'
“不用担心这个了。她已经没有什么东西了。”
From there, I went to my apartment to see if there were any letters from my father.
从那儿出来，我回到自己家去查看有没有我父亲寄给我的信。
There were four.
有四封。
