CHAPTER XIII  

第十三章  

While these events were happening in Saumur, Charles was making his fortune in the Indies. His commercial outfit had sold well. He began by realizing a sum of six thousand dollars. Crossing the line had brushed a good many cobwebs out of his brain; he perceived that the best means of attaining fortune in tropical regions, as well as in Europe, was to buy and sell men. He went to the coast of Africa and bought Negroes, combining his traffic in human flesh with that of other merchandise equally advantageous to his interests. He carried into this business an activity which left him not a moment of leisure. He was governed by the desire of reappearing in Paris with all the prestige of a large fortune, and by the hope of regaining a position even more brilliant than the one from which he had fallen.
在索米尔发生这些事件的时候，夏尔在西印度群岛发了财。他的那批货卖得很好。他一开始就挣到了六千美元。一过赤道，他便丢掉了脑子里许多陈腐的观念；他发现，和欧洲一样，热带地区最好的发财办法是买卖人口。他去非洲海岸买了一些黑人，在贩卖人口的同时，他还贩运其他一些同样有利可图的货物。他把全部精力投入到生意当中，没有片刻闲暇。他一心想发了大财，好风风光光地重返巴黎，并希望重新获得一个比他落魄前还要显耀的地位。
By dint of jostling with men, travelling through many lands, and studying a variety of conflicting customs, his ideas had been modified and had become sceptical. He ceased to have fixed principles of right and wrong, for he saw what was called a crime in one country lauded as a virtue in another. In the perpetual struggle of selfish interests his heart grew cold, then contracted, and then dried up. The blood of the Grandets did not fail of its destiny; Charles became hard, and eager for prey. He sold Chinamen, Negroes, birds' nests, children, artists; he practised usury on a large scale; the habit of defrauding custom-houses soon made him less scrupulous about the rights of his fellow men. He went to the Island of St. Thomas and bought, for a mere song, merchandise that had been captured by pirates, and took it to ports where he could sell it at a good price. If the pure and noble face of Eugenie went with him on his first voyage, like that image of the Virgin which Spanish mariners fastened to their masts, if he attributed his first success to the magic influence of the prayers and intercessions of his gentle love, later on women of other kinds,—blacks, mulattoes, whites, and Indian dancing-girls,—orgies and adventures in many lands, completely effaced all recollection of his cousin, of Saumur, of the house, the bench, the kiss snatched in the dark passage.
由于在人堆里混，在多个地方跑，还见识了各种迥异的风俗，他的观念改变了，变得爱猜疑了。他不再有一成不变的是非原则，因为他看到在一个国家被称为犯罪的事情，在另一个国家却被赞为美德。在为谋私利不断进行的搏斗中，他的心变冷酷了，然后又变得狭小，再后来就干枯了。葛朗台家族的血统没有失传；夏尔变得冷酷无情、贪得无厌。他贩卖中国人、黑人、燕窝、儿童和艺人；他大范围地放高利贷；他惯于欺诈海关，这很快就让他不怎么把人权当回事了。他到圣托马斯岛贱价收购海盗掠取的货物，然后再运到能卖个好价钱的港口。如果说在他初次出海时，他还想着欧仁妮纯洁而高贵的面容，就像西班牙水手把圣母像挂在桅杆上一样，如果说他把开始的成功还归功于他温柔的情人所做的祈祷和祝福的魔力上，那后来，他到处跟其他各种女人——黑人、穆拉托人、白人和印度舞女——花天酒地、放浪形骸，便把他对堂姐、索米尔、那座宅子、长凳和黑暗过道里的匆匆一吻这所有的回忆都抹得一干二净了。
He remembered only the little garden shut in with crumbling walls, for it was there he learned the fate that had overtaken him; but he rejected all connection with his family. His uncle was an old dog who had filched his jewels; Eugenie had no place in his heart nor in his thoughts, though she did have a place in his accounts as a creditor for the sum of six thousand francs.
他只记得围在破旧墙垣里的小花园，因为正是在那里，他得知了突如其来的命运；但是他拒绝一切跟他的家族有关联的东西。他的伯父是只老狗，偷走了他的首饰；欧仁妮不管是在他的心里，还是在他的思想上都没有地位，尽管在他的账目里，她确实占着个六千法郎的债主的位置。
Such conduct and such ideas explain Charles Grandet's silence. In the Indies, at St. Thomas, on the coast of Africa, at Lisbon, and in the United States the adventurer had taken the pseudonym of Shepherd, that he might not compromise his own name. Charles Shepherd could safely be indefatigable, bold, grasping, and greedy of gain, like a man who resolves to snatch his fortune quibus cumque viis, and makes haste to have done with villainy, that he may spend the rest of his life as an honest man.
这种行为和这些想法便是夏尔·葛朗台杳无音信的原因。在西印度群岛、圣托马斯、非洲海岸、里斯本和美国，这个投机家取了个假名字——谢泼德，这样就不会连累他的本姓了。夏尔·谢泼德可以毫无风险、不知疲倦地大胆捞钱，贪图利益了，就像一个人决心不择手段地狠捞一把，以便早日金盆洗手，好在余生里做得像个正人君子。
With such methods, prosperity was rapid and brilliant; and in 1827 Charles Grandet returned to Bordeaux on the "Marie Caroline," a fine brig belonging to a royalist house of business. He brought with him nineteen hundred thousand francs worth of gold-dust, from which he expected to derive seven or eight per cent more at the Paris mint. On the brig he met a gentleman-in-ordinary to His Majesty Charles X., Monsieur d'Aubrion, a worthy old man who had committed the folly of marrying a woman of fashion with a fortune derived from the West India Islands. To meet the costs of Madame d'Aubrion's extravagance, he had gone out to the Indies to sell the property, and was now returning with his family to France.
靠这种做法，他发迹又快又显赫；一八二七年，夏尔·葛朗台乘坐一艘隶属于保王党商号的豪华双桅横帆船——“玛丽·卡罗琳”号，返回了波尔多。他随身带有价值一百九十万法郎的金砂，希望换成法币时能从中获取百分之七或百分之八的利。在船上，他遇到了一位国王查理十世陛下的御用侍从——德奥布里翁先生，这位值得尊敬的老人一时糊涂娶了个上流社会的女人，而他的财产都是在西印度群岛赚来的。为了补上德奥布里翁太太挥霍的亏空，他去西印度群岛变卖了产业，此时正和家人返回法国。
Monsieur and Madame d'Aubrion, of the house of d'Aubrion de Buch, a family of southern France, whose last captal, or chief, died before 1789, were now reduced to an income of about twenty thousand francs, and they possessed an ugly daughter whom the mother was resolved to marry without a dot,—the family fortune being scarcely sufficient for the demands of her own life in Paris. This was an enterprise whose success might have seemed problematical to most men of the world, in spite of the cleverness with which such men credit a fashionable woman; in fact, Madame d'Aubrion herself, when she looked at her daughter, almost despaired of getting rid of her to any one, even to a man craving connection with nobility. Mademoiselle d'Aubrion was a long, spare, spindling demoiselle, like her namesake the insect; her mouth was disdainful; over it hung a nose that was too long, thick at the end, sallow in its normal condition, but very red after a meal,—a sort of vegetable phenomenon which is particularly disagreeable when it appears in the middle of a pale, dull, and uninteresting face. In one sense she was all that a worldly mother, thirty-eight years of age and still a beauty with claims to admiration, could have wished. However, to counterbalance her personal defects, the marquise gave her daughter a distinguished air, subjected her to hygienic treatment which provisionally kept her nose at a reasonable flesh-tint, taught her the art of dressing well, endowed her with charming manners, showed her the trick of melancholy glances which interest a man and make him believe that he has found a long-sought angel, taught her the manoeuvre of the foot,—letting it peep beneath the petticoat, to show its tiny size, at the moment when the nose became aggressively red; in short, Madame d'Aubrion had cleverly made the very best of her offspring.
德奥布里翁先生和太太是法国南部的一个家族——德奥布里翁·德比什家族的后裔，其家族最后一位领主，或者说首领，一七八九年以前就死了，他们现在家境衰落，收入大约仅为两万法郎。他们有个长相丑陋的女儿，做母亲的打算不给嫁妆就把她嫁出去——因为家里的财产只够勉强维持她在巴黎的生活需求。尽管交际场中的多数人都认为，一个交际花总是机灵过人，但他们也觉得这似乎是一番难于成功的事业；实际上，德奥布里翁太太本人每当看着自己的女儿，也差不多对她嫁人不抱任何希望了，哪怕是嫁给一个一心想跟贵族攀亲的人。德奥布里翁小姐很像跟她同名的昆虫，仿佛一个又高又瘦、又细又长的蜻蜓；她的嘴一副鄙夷的样子，上面长着个过于长的鼻子，鼻头很厚，平时是灰黄色的，可是吃过饭后，就变得非常红——当这种植物现象出现在一张苍白、沮丧而且乏味的脸上时，就会令人极为厌恶。从某种意义上说，她正是一个三十八岁，却风韵犹存、老于世故的母亲所希望的那样。不过，为了弥补女儿的个人缺陷，侯爵夫人教给了女儿一种雍容华贵的姿态，让她养成了良好的卫生习惯，使她的鼻子暂时保持着正常的肤色；她教给女儿穿着得体的技巧，使她拥有迷人的举止；向她展示做出忧郁眼神的诀窍，以打动男人，让那男人相信他已经找到了寻觅已久的天使；她还教给女儿如何抬足迈步——以便在鼻子肆无忌惮发红的时候，使纤足在裙裾下若隐若现，露出其娇小的尺寸。总之，德奥布里翁太太已经巧妙地将自己的后代精心打造了一番。
By means of full sleeves, deceptive pads, puffed dresses amply trimmed, and high-pressure corsets, she had obtained such curious feminine developments that she ought, for the instruction of mothers, to have exhibited them in a museum.
凭借宽大的袖子、掩人耳目的衬垫、裁剪宽松的蓬蓬长裙，还有紧绷绷的紧身胸衣，她居然拥有了一些精致的女性特点，简直应该把它们都放到博物馆中去展览，以供母亲们借鉴。
Charles became very intimate with Madame d'Aubrion precisely because she was desirous of becoming intimate with him. Persons who were on board the brig declared that the handsome Madame d'Aubrion neglected no means of capturing so rich a son-in-law. On landing at Bordeaux in June, 1827, Monsieur, Madame, Mademoiselle d'Aubrion, and Charles lodged at the same hotel and started together for Paris. The hotel d'Aubrion was hampered with mortgages; Charles was destined to free it. The mother told him how delighted she would be to give up the ground-floor to a son-in-law. Not sharing Monsieur d'Aubrion's prejudices on the score of nobility, she promised Charles Grandet to obtain a royal ordinance from Charles X. which would authorize him, Grandet, to take the name and arms of d'Aubrion and to succeed, by purchasing the entailed estate for thirty-six thousand francs a year, to the titles of Captal de Buch and Marquis d'Aubrion. By thus uniting their fortunes, living on good terms, and profiting by sinecures, the two families might occupy the hotel d'Aubrion with an income of over a hundred thousand francs.
夏尔之所以跟德奥布里翁太太套近乎，正是因为这位太太很想跟他接近。船上的人声称，美丽的德奥布里翁太太用尽了所有手段来俘虏这个有钱的女婿。一八二七年六月，在波尔多下船时，德奥布里翁先生、太太、小姐，和夏尔住进了同一家旅店，并一起动身去往巴黎。德奥布里翁府邸早已被抵押出去，指定夏尔去赎回来。那位母亲告诉他，把底楼让给当女婿的住，她会有多么高兴。她不像德奥布里翁先生那样对贵族门第怀有偏见，她答应夏尔·葛朗台，要向查理十世请道御旨，恩准他——葛朗台改姓德奥布里翁，并使用德奥布里翁的爵徽，而且只要一年出三万六千法郎，就能购买下限嗣继承的不动产，承袭德比什领主和德奥布里翁侯爵的头衔。这样，通过将他们的财产合并起来，相互处得又融洽，再加上白拿的俸禄，两家人就可以住在德奥布里翁府邸，拥有超过十几万法郎的收入。
"And when a man has a hundred thousand francs a year, a name, a family, and a position at court,—for I will get you appointed as gentleman-of-the-bedchamber,—he can do what he likes," she said to Charles. "You can then become anything you choose,—master of the rolls in the council of State, prefect, secretary to an embassy, the ambassador himself, if you like. Charles X. is fond of d'Aubrion; they have known each other from childhood.” 
“等一个人一年有了十万法郎，有了姓氏，有了门第，有了宫廷职位——因为我会让你当个王室侍从的——他就可以做他喜欢做的事了，”她对夏尔说道，“这样，你就可以想当什么就当什么——政务院的审查官、省长、大使馆秘书、大使本人，由你挑。查理十世很喜欢德奥布里翁，他们从小就认识。”
Intoxicated with ambition, Charles toyed with the hopes thus cleverly presented to him in the guise of confidences poured from heart to heart. Believing his father's affairs to have been settled by his uncle, he imagined himself suddenly anchored in the Faubourg Saint-Germain,—that social object of all desire, where, under shelter of Mademoiselle Mathilde's purple nose, he was to reappear as the Comte d'Aubrion, very much as the Dreux reappeared in Breze. Dazzled by the prosperity of the Restoration, which was tottering when he left France, fascinated by the splendor of aristocratic ideas, his intoxication, which began on the brig, increased after he reached Paris, and he finally determined to take the course and reach the high position which the selfish hopes of his would-be mother-in-law pointed out to him. His cousin counted for no more than a speck in this brilliant perspective; but he went to see Annette. True woman of the world, Annette advised her old friend to make the marriage, and promised him her support in all his ambitious projects. In her heart she was enchanted to fasten an ugly and uninteresting girl on Charles, whose life in the West Indies had rendered him very attractive. His complexion had bronzed, his manners had grown decided and bold, like those of a man accustomed to make sharp decisions, to rule, and to succeed. Charles breathed more at his ease in Paris, conscious that he now had a part to play.
夏儿陶醉于野心当中，他对这番巧妙地扮成个人隐私话的痴心妄想竟沉迷起来。他满以为他父亲的事已经由伯父料理完毕，便觉得可以一步踏进圣－日耳曼区——那个人人向往的上流社会，在那里，在玛蒂尔德小姐紫色鼻子的庇护下，他要以德奥布里翁伯爵的身份再度现身，好似德勒一家摇身一变，成了布雷兹一样。在他离开法国时，王政复辟还摇摇欲坠，现在却一派繁荣，令他眼花缭乱，贵族思想的光辉让他着迷，从船上就开始有的醉意，等他到了巴黎后更是有增无减。最后，他决心采取行动，把他那未来的岳母出于自私的愿望而指给他的高官厚禄弄到手。他的堂姐在这个光辉灿烂的远景中只不过是个小点子而已。不过，他去见了安妮特。作为真正的交际花，安妮特建议她的老朋友结这个婚，还向他许诺，她会支持他所有那些雄心勃勃的计划。在她的内心，她非常乐意把一个又丑又无趣的姑娘和夏尔夏尔栓到一起，因为西印度群岛的生活使夏尔变得非常有魅力。他的肤色变成了古铜色，他的举手投足变得坚定而果敢，好似那些惯于当机立断、支配一切，并获得成功的人一样。夏尔意识到自己现在可以有所作为了，他在巴黎活得更自在了。
Des Grassins, hearing of his return, of his approaching marriage and his large fortune, came to see him, and inquired about the three hundred thousand francs still required to settle his father's debts. He found Grandet in conference with a goldsmith, from whom he had ordered jewels for Mademoiselle d'Aubrion's corbeille, and who was then submitting the designs. Charles had brought back magnificent diamonds, and the value of their setting, together with the plate and jewelry of the new establishment, amounted to more than two hundred thousand francs. He received des Grassins, whom he did not recognize, with the impertinence of a young man of fashion conscious of having killed four men in as many duels in the Indies. Monsieur des Grassins had already called several times. Charles listened to him coldly, and then replied, without fully understanding what had been said to him,— 
德格拉桑听说他回来了，而且即将结婚，还发了大财，就跑来见他，想打听了结他父亲债务还需要的那三十万法郎。他看到葛朗台正在和一个金器商商议事情，从这个金器商那里，夏尔定制了首饰给德奥布里翁小姐做聘礼，当时金器商正在向他提交图样。夏尔带回了精美的钻石，那镶嵌式样的价值，连同新居所需的金银餐具和首饰，合计达二十多万法郎。他没有认出德格拉桑来，自以为曾在西印度群岛的多次决斗中杀死过四个人，他接待德格拉桑时，带着一副时髦青年的傲慢劲。德格拉桑先生已经来过几次了。夏尔冷冷地听着他说话，还没完全听懂他的意思，就回答道——
"My father's affairs are not mine. I am much obliged, monsieur, for the trouble you have been good enough to take,—by which, however, I really cannot profit. I have not earned two millions by the sweat of my brow to fling them at the head of my father's creditors.” 
“我父亲的事不是我的事。先生，我很感谢您这样费心——不过，对此，我确实无法领情。我汗流满面地挣来了两百万，不是为了把它们扔到我父亲的债主头上的。”
"But suppose that your father's estate were within a few days to be declared bankrupt?” 
“可要是令尊的财产几天之内就要宣告破产呢？”
"Monsieur, in a few days I shall be called the Comte d'Aubrion; you will understand, therefore, that what you threaten is of no consequence to me. Besides, you know as well as I do that when a man has an income of a hundred thousand francs his father has never failed.” So saying, he politely edged Monsieur des Grassins to the door.
“先生，再过几天，我就要被称作德奥布里翁伯爵了；因此，你会明白你的威胁对我毫无影响。而且你和我一样清楚，当一个人有十万法郎的收入时，他的父亲是决不会破产的。”这样说着，他客气地把德格拉桑先生推到了门口。
At the beginning of August in the same year, Eugenie was sitting on the little wooden bench where her cousin had sworn to love her eternally, and where she usually breakfasted if the weather were fine. The poor girl was happy, for the moment, in the fresh and joyous summer air, letting her memory recall the great and the little events of her love and the catastrophes which had followed it. The sun had just reached the angle of the ruined wall, so full of chinks, which no one, through a caprice of the mistress, was allowed to touch, though Cornoiller often remarked to his wife that "it would fall and crush somebody one of these days." At this moment the postman knocked, and gave a letter to Madame Cornoiller, who ran into the garden, crying out: 
当年八月初，欧仁妮正坐在堂弟曾发誓永远爱她的那个小木凳上，如果天气好的话，她通常就在这里用早餐。此时，在这清新、舒适的夏日空气里，这可怜的姑娘心情愉快，她回忆起爱情中的大小往事和随之而来的灾难。太阳刚刚晒到了破败的墙角，墙上布满了裂缝，尽管科努瓦耶经常跟他妻子说：“早晚有一天，它会倒下来砸着人的。”可是由于女主人的怪癖，谁也不许碰。这时，邮差敲响门，给了科努瓦耶太太一封信，她一边往花园跑，一边大声喊着：
"Mademoiselle, a letter!" She gave it to her mistress, adding, "Is it the one you expected?" 
“小姐，信！”她把信给了女主人，又说道，“是你等的那封吧？”
The words rang as loudly in the heart of Eugenie as they echoed in sound from wall to wall of the court and garden.
这几个字在欧仁妮的心头大声回响，强度不亚于回荡在院子和花园的墙壁之间的回声。
"Paris—from him—he has returned!” 
“巴黎——他写的——他回来了！”
Eugenie turned pale and held the letter for a moment. She trembled so violently that she could not break the seal. La Grande Nanon stood before her, both hands on her hips, her joy puffing as it were like smoke through the cracks of her brown face.
欧仁妮脸色变得苍白，举着信呆了一会儿。她颤抖得厉害，简直都拆不了信。大个子娜农站在她前面，两手叉着腰，她的快乐好像烟一样，从她棕色脸庞的皱纹中冒了出来。
"Read it, mademoiselle!" 
“看信吧，小姐！”
"Ah, Nanon, why did he return to Paris? He went from Saumur." 
“啊，娜农，他为什么回了巴黎？他是从索米尔走的。”
"Read it, and you'll find out.” 
“看了信，你就知道了。”
Eugenie opened the letter with trembling fingers. A cheque on the house of "Madame des Grassins and Coret, of Saumur," fluttered down. Nanon picked it up.
欧仁妮用颤抖的手指打开了信。一张“索米尔德格拉桑太太和科雷”银行的汇票飘然而落。娜农把它捡了起来。
My dear Cousin,— 
我亲爱的堂姐——

“不再是‘欧仁妮’了。”她想着，心存恐惧。

Nothing can resist time, as I am well aware. Yes, my dear cousin, the day of illusions is, unfortunately, gone for me. How could it be otherwise? Travelling through many lands, I have reflected upon life. I was a child when I went away,—I have come back a man.
什么都无法抗拒时间，对此我深有体会。是的，我亲爱的堂姐，不幸的是，对我来说，幻想的时代已经过去。不然又有什么办法呢？走过很多地方以后，我已经对人生进行了思考。走时，我还是个孩子——现在回来，我已是个大人了。
Today, I think of many I did not dream of then. You are free, my dear cousin, and I am free still. Nothing apparently hinders the realization of our early hopes; but my nature is too loyal to hide from you the situation in which I find myself. An alliance has been proposed to me which satisfies all my ideas of matrimony. Love in marriage is a delusion. I will not here speak of your customs and inclinations, your education, nor yet of your habits, none of which are in keeping with Parisian life, or with the future which I have marked out for myself. My intention is to keep my household on a stately footing, to receive much company,—in short, to live in the world; and I think I remember that you love a quiet and tranquil life. Thus in course of time my son, when he becomes Marquis d'Aubrion, having, as he then will have, an entailed estate with a rental of forty thousand francs a year, can obtain any position in the State which he may think proper to select. You see, my cousin, with what good faith I lay the state of my heart, my hopes, and my fortune before you. I remember all, even words that were lightly uttered,—words by which a man less conscientious than I, with a heart less youthful and less upright, would scarcely feel himself bound. In telling you that the marriage I propose to make is solely one of convenience, that I still remember our childish love, am I not putting myself entirely in your hands and making you the mistress of my fate? Am I not telling you that if I must renounce my social ambitions, I shall willingly content myself with the pure and simple happiness of which you have shown me so sweet an image?
您看，我的堂姐，我是多么真诚地向您表白了我心里的想法、我的希望及我的财产。也许在七年的离别之后，您已经忘记了我们年轻幼稚的爱情；可是，我从来没有忘记您的善良，也没忘记我自己的诺言。我记得所有一切，甚至包括那些不经意说出的话语——那些话语如果是由一个不像我这样认真、内心也不那么天真和正直的人说出的，他不会觉着自己有负责任的必要。我之所以告诉您我要缔结的婚约只是便利之需，还有我仍旧记得我们幼稚的爱情，我不就是在把我自己完全交到您的手中，并让您来主宰我的命运吗？难道我不是在告诉您，如果我必须放弃我对社会的野心，我会心甘情愿地满足于那纯洁而简单的幸福吗？那甜美的情景您早已让我领略到了。

Terrible and utter disaster! The ship went down, leaving not a spar, not a plank, on a vast ocean of hope! Some women when they see themselves abandoned will try to tear their lover from the arms of a rival, they will kill her, and rush to the ends of the earth,—to the scaffold, to their tomb. That, no doubt, is fine; the motive of the crime is a great passion, which awes even human justice. Other women bow their heads and suffer in silence; they go their way dying, resigned, weeping, forgiving, praying, and recollecting, till they draw their last breath. This is love,—true love, the love of angels, the proud love which lives upon its anguish and dies of it. Such was Eugenie's love after she had read that dreadful letter. She raised her eyes to heaven, thinking of the last words uttered by her dying mother, who, with the prescience of death, had looked into the future with clear and penetrating eyes: Eugenie, remembering that prophetic death, that prophetic life, measured with one glance her own destiny. Nothing was left for her; she could only unfold her wings, stretch upward to the skies, and live in prayer until the day of her deliverance.
可怕的灭顶之灾！在希望的茫茫大海上，船沉了，没有留下一节圆杆，也没有留下一块木板！有些女人在看到自己被抛弃时，会竭力把她们的爱人从情敌怀里夺回来，她们会杀掉情敌，逃到天涯海角——上断头台，进坟墓。毫无疑问，那是很高贵的；其犯罪动机是一种伟大的激情，甚至会令人类的公正敬畏。另一些女人低下了头，默默承受痛苦；她们半死不活，听天由命，痛哭流涕，宽恕，祈祷，回忆，直到咽下最后一口气。这就是爱——真正的爱、天使的爱和在痛苦中生、在痛苦中死的高傲的爱。这就是看完那封可怕的信之后欧仁妮的爱。她抬头望天，想起她母亲临终时留下的最后遗言，母亲凭着临死时所具有的预见本能，把未来看得清楚而透彻：欧仁妮想起了母亲那先知般的死亡，那先知般的一生，转瞬间悟出了自己的命运。什么也没给她留下，她只能展开她的翅膀，振翼飞向天空，在祈祷中活着，直到她解脱的那一天。
