CHAPTER XV  

第十五章  

Some time elapsed before I again gave a lesson in the first class. The holiday of Whitsuntide occupied three days, and on the fourth it was the turn of the second division to receive my instructions. As I made the transit of the carré, I observed, as usual, the band of sewers surrounding Mdlle. Henri. There were only about a dozen of them, but they made as much noise as might have sufficed for fifty; they seemed very little under her control. Three or four at once assailed her with importunate requirements; she looked harassed, she demanded silence, but in vain. She saw me, and I read in her eye pain that a stranger should witness the insubordination of her pupils. She seemed to entreat order. Her prayers were useless. Then I remarked that she compressed her lips and contracted her brow, and her countenance, if I read it correctly, said, 'I have done my best. I seem to merit blame notwithstanding. blame me, then, who will.' I passed on. As I closed the schoolroom door I heard her say, suddenly and sharply, addressing one of the eldest and most turbulent of the lot— 
再次给一班上课时，已过了好几天。圣灵降临节的假期占去了三天，第四天又轮到给二班上课。经过苗圃时，像往常一样，我看到一群学习针线活的女孩子围着亨利小姐。她们只有十几个人，但声音却可能足有五十人那样大；她们似乎不大听从亨利小姐的管束。三四个人一起胡搅蛮缠地要求着什么；她看上去有些厌烦，要大家安静，但却白搭。她看到了我，我从她眼神里看出了苦恼，那种被陌生人撞见自己管不住学生的苦恼。她好像在恳求大家遵守规矩。可她的祈求却无济于事。接着我看到她闭紧了嘴唇，眉头皱起，她的面容，如果我理解正确的话，像是在说：“我已经尽力而为了。尽管如此，我受责备看来也是应该的。谁愿意责备我就来吧。”我继续向前走。正当我关上教室门时，听到她突然尖声对其中一个年龄最长也是最吵闹的学生说：
'Amelie Mullenberg, ask me no question, and request of me no assistance, for a week to come; during that space of time I will neither speak to you nor help you.' 
“阿梅莉·米朗贝，接下来的一周内别问我任何问题，别要我帮助你；这一周，我既不会跟你讲话也不会帮你。”
The words were uttered with emphasis—nay, with vehemence and a comparative silence followed. Whether the calm was permanent, I know not; two doors now closed between me and the carré 
说这话的时候加重了语气——不仅如此，言辞也很激烈，接下来，姑娘们相对安静了。这安静能否持久，我不清楚；现在在我和苗圃之间的两扇门都关上了。
Next day was appropriated to the first class. On my arrival I found the directress seated, as usual, in a chair between the two estrades, and before her was standing Mdlle. Henri, in an attitude (as it seemed to me) of somewhat reluctant attention.
隔天是要给一班上课的。我一到教室，便看到女校长照旧坐在两个讲台之间的一张椅子上，面前站着亨利小姐。她的姿态在我看来，似乎有些不大情愿专心听。
The directness was knitting and talking at the same time. Amidst the hum of a large schoolroom it was easy so to speak in the ear of one person as to be heard by that person alone, and it was thus Mdlle. Reuter parleyed with her teacher. The face of the latter was a little flushed, not a little troubled; there was vexation in it—whence resulting I know not, for the directress looked very placid indeed. She could not be scolding in such gentle whispers and with so equable a mien. No; it was presently proved that her discourse had been of the most friendly tendency, for I heard the closing words, — 
女校长边织东西边说话。在嘈杂的大教室里，对一个人耳语并且只让她一人听到是很简单的事。勒泰小姐和她手下的老师就这样讨论着什么。这老师的脸有些泛红，不是因为有些焦虑，而是出于烦恼；这烦恼根源何处，我不知道，因为实际上女校长看起来非常心平气和。她不可能用这么柔和的耳语，这么平静的态度来训斥她。当然不会了。不一会儿，我就证实了她的谈话是非常友好的，因为我听到了最后几句：
'C'est assez, ma bonne amie; a present je ne veux pas vous retenir davantage.' 
“我的好朋友，就说这么多吧。此刻，我不想再耽误你的时间了。”
Without reply, Mdlle. Henri turned away.
亨利小姐没有回答，就转身走开了。
Dissatisfaction was plainly evinced in her face, and a smile, slight and brief, but bitter, distrustful, and, I thought, scornful, curled her lip as she took her place in the class. It was a secret, involuntary smile, which lasted but a second; an air of depression succeeded, chased away presently by one of attention and interest, when I gave the word for all the pupils to take their reading-books. In general I hated the reading-lesson it was such a torture to the ear to listen to their uncouth mouthing of my native tongue, and no effort of example or precept on my part ever seemed to effect the slightest improvement in their accent. Today each, in her appropriate key, lisped, stuttered, mumbled, and jabbered as usual. About fifteen had racked me in turn, and my auricular nerve was expecting with resignation the discords of the sixteenth, when a full though low voice read out, in clear, correct English, — 
她脸上的表情明显地显示着自己的不满；她在教室的位子上坐下时，嘴唇一撇，露出一丝微笑，轻微而短暂，但包含着苦涩和质疑，我觉得还带着一丝鄙夷。她无意的微笑仅仅持续了一秒，这是个秘密，接着是沮丧的神情，但不一会儿，当我告诉大家拿出阅读课本时，她的沮丧就被专注和兴趣盎然的表情驱散了。通常我很厌恶上阅读课，听她们用粗俗的发音读着我的母语，简直是对耳朵的折磨；我费力地言传身教似乎对纠正她们的口音起不了一点点作用。今天同往常一样，每人操着自己的调子，含混不清、结结巴巴、叽里咕哝、吱吱喳喳地读了下来。大约十五人轮番折磨过我之后，我的听觉神经在无可奈何地等着第十六个噪音，此时一个低沉但却饱满的声音读出声来，读着清楚、准确的英语：
'On his way to Perth the king was met by a Highland woman calling herself a prophetess. She stood at the side of the ferry by which he was about to travel to the north, and cried with a loud voice, "My lord the king, if you pass this water you will never return again alive."' (Vide the 'History of Scotland.') 
“在去珀斯的路上，国王遇见一位高地妇人，她称自己是先知。她站在国王北去要经过的渡口边上高声叫道：‘国王陛下，如果你过了这条河，你将永远不会活着回来。’”（见《苏格兰历史》）
I looked up in amazement. The voice was a voice of Albion; the accent was pure and silvery; it only wanted firmness and assurance to be the counterpart of what any well-educated lady in Essex or Middlesex might have enounced; yet the speaker or reader was no other than Mdlle. Henri, in whose grave, joyless face I saw no mark of consciousness that she had performed any extraordinary feat. No one else evinced surprise either. Mdlle. Reuter knitted away assiduously. I was aware, however, that at the conclusion of the paragraph she had lifted her eyelid and honoured me with a glance sideways. She did not know the full excellency of the teacher's style of reading, but she perceived that her accent was not that of the others, and wanted to discover what I thought. I masked my visage with indifference, and ordered the next girl to proceed.
我诧异地抬头看去。这声音是英国口音，听上去纯正、悦耳，只是缺了与埃塞克斯或者米德尔塞克斯那些受过良好教育的女士的发音相类似的坚定和自信。然而说话的人或者说读书的人不是别人，正是亨利小姐。我见她一脸庄重、阴郁，没有丝毫迹象表明她意识到自己的壮举。也没有一个人对此表示惊讶。勒泰小姐还在认认真真地织着东西。然而我发觉，亨利小姐读完这一段话时，她眼皮抬起来，斜视了我一眼。她不了解这位女教师读得多么出色，不过她觉察到她的口音不像其他人，于是她想了解我的想法。我用漠然掩饰着表情，命令下一位女孩子继续读。
When the lesson was over, I took advantage of the confusion caused by breaking up to approach Mdlle. Henri. She was standing near the window, and retired as I advanced. She thought I wanted to look out, and did not imagine that I could have anything to say to her. I took her exercise-book out of her hand. As I turned over the leaves I addressed her.
课上完了，我趁人群散去时混乱之际走近亨利小姐。她站在窗前，看见我走上前去，就退了回来。她以为我要向外看，没有想到我会有话跟她说。我把练习本从她手里拿过来。我一边翻看，一边同她讲话。
'You have had lessons in English before?' I asked.
“你以前上过英语课吗？”我问。
'No, sir.' 
“没有，先生。”
'No! You read very well. You have been in England?' 
“没有？你读得很不错。你曾经去过英格兰吗？”
'Oh no!' with some animation.
“噢，没有！”她情绪有些高涨地回答。
'You have been in English families?' 
“那你曾经住在英国人家庭里吗？”
Still the answer was 'No.' Here my eye, resting on the flyleaf of the book, saw written, 'Frances Evans Henri.' 
答案依旧是“没有”。这会儿我的目光停留在本子的衬页上，看到上面写着‘弗朗西斯·埃文斯·亨利’。
'Your name?' I asked.
“这是你的名字吗？”我问。
'Yes, sir.' 
“是的，先生。”
My interrogations were cut short. I heard a little rustling behind me, and close at my back was the directress, professing to be examining the interior of a desk.
我的询问被打断了。我听到背后一阵轻微的沙沙声——背后的正是女校长——她宣称要检查一张课桌的里面。
'Mademoiselle,' said she, looking up and addressing the teacher, 'will you have the goodness to go and stand in the corridor, while the young ladies are putting on their things, and try to keep some order?' 
“小姐”，她抬起头来对这位女教师说道，“当那些年轻的姑娘们放东西时，你可否好心站到走廊里，设法维持一下秩序？”
Mdlle. Henri obeyed.
亨利小姐听从了她的命令。
'What splendid weather!' observed the directress cheerfully, glancing at the same time from the window.
“天气真棒！”女校长一边快活地说着，一边向窗外看去。
I assented, and was withdrawing. 'What of your new pupil, monsieur?' continued she, following my retreating steps. 'Is she likely to make progress in English?' 
我表示赞同，并打算离去。“先生，您的新学生怎么样？”她继续说道，跟着我离去的脚步。“她的英语有希望进步吗？”
'Indeed I can hardly judge. She possesses pretty good accent. Of her real knowledge of the language I have as yet had no opportunity of forming an opinion.' 
“这确实很难判断。她拥有漂亮的口音。说到她真正的英语知识水平，我还没有机会去了解，也没什么观点。”
'And her natural capacity, monsieur? I have had my fears about that. Can you relieve me by an assurance at least of its average power?' 
“那么先生，她的天分如何？我对这个有点担心。您能否确信她的能力至少在一般水平上，好让我放心？”
'I see no reason to doubt its average power, mademoiselle, but really I scarcely know her, and have not had time to study the calibre of her capacity. I wish you a very good afternoon.' 
“小姐，我没有任何理由怀疑她天分一般，但事实上，我确实不怎么了解她，也还没有时间研究她的能力如何。我祝您下午好！”
She still pursued me. 'You will observe, monsieur, and tell me what you think; I could so much better rely on your opinion than on my own. Women cannot judge of these things as men can; and, excuse my pertinacity, monsieur, but it is natural I should feel interested about this poor little girl. She has scarcely any relations; her own efforts are all she has to look to; her acquirements must be her sole fortune. Her present position has once been mine, or nearly so; it is then but natural I should sympathize with her; and sometimes, when I see the difficulty she has in managing pupils, I feel quite chagrined. I doubt not she does her best; her intentions are excellent; but, monsieur, she wants tact and firmness. I have talked to her on the subject, but I am not fluent, and probably did not express myself with clearness; she never appears to comprehend me. Now, would you occasionally, when you see an opportunity, slip in a word of advice to her on the subject? Men have so much more influence than women have—they argue so much more logically than we do; and you, monsieur, in particular, have so paramount a power of making yourself obeyed. A word of advice from you could not but do her good. Even if she were sullen and headstrong (which I hope she is not) she would scarcely refuse to listen to you. For my own part, I can truly say that I never attend one of your lessons without deriving benefit from witnessing your management of the pupils. The other masters are a constant source of anxiety to me; they cannot impress the young ladies with sentiments of respect, nor restrain the levity natural to youth. In you, monsieur, I feel the most absolute confidence. Try, then, to put this poor child into the way of controlling our giddy, high-spirited Brabantoises. But, monsieur, I would add one word more: don't alarm her amour propre, beware of inflicting a wound there. I reluctantly admit that in that particular she is blamably—some would say ridiculously—susceptible. I fear I have touched this sore point inadvertently, and she cannot get over it.' 
她仍旧追着我不放。“您会注意到的，先生，而且您要告诉我您的想法；相比我自己的看法，我更加相信您的看法。女人无法像男人一样对这些事情作出合理判断；先生，请原谅我的固执，然而我对这个可怜的小姑娘有兴趣是很自然的。她没什么亲戚，只能全靠自己的努力。她的学识是她唯一的财富。她现在的职务我曾经也做过，或者说我做过几乎一样的工作，那么我自然而然就对她产生了怜悯之情。有时看到她费那么大力气管理学生，我会觉得懊悔。我没有怀疑她已经尽了力，她的意图是好的，可是先生，她缺乏机智和坚定。我也就此跟她谈过，但我口齿不够伶俐，很可能没表达清楚。她好像一点也没理解我的意思。偶尔看到有合适的机会的话，您能就此向她提点建议吗？男人的影响力比女人大得多——男人论证问题比女人更有逻辑性，尤其是先生您，您有种强大的力量，能让您的学生们服从命令。您的建议只会给她带来好处。即便她会闷闷不乐、固执任性（我希望她不会这样），她也决不会拒绝听您的话。至于我自己，我可以很肯定地说，每一回听您的课，亲眼看见您如何管理学生，我无不觉得从中受益。我总为其他教师担心。他们没法赢得这些年轻女学生的尊重，也遏制不了年轻人本能的轻浮行为。在先生您身上，我感受到了绝对的信心。因而，就试着帮帮这个可怜的孩子吧，让她能管住这些举止轻浮、精力过剩的布拉班特女孩子吧。然而先生，我还要再说一句：不要触动她的自尊，当心别伤害了她的自尊。虽然不情愿，但我不得不承认这一点——有人会觉得可笑——她的敏感真该受责备。恐怕无意间我已经触到了她的痛处，而且她还没法恢复过来。”
During the greater part of this harangue my hand was on the lock of the outer door. I now turned it.
在她发表这篇高谈阔论的大部分时间里，我的手都放在外门的锁上。这会儿我把门打开了。
'An revoir, mademoiselle,' said I, and I escaped. I saw the directress's stock of words was yet far from exhausted. She looked after me; she would fain have detained me longer. Her manner towards me had been altered ever since I had begun to treat her with hardness and indifference. She almost cringed to me on every occasion; she consulted my countenance incessantly, and beset me with innumerable little officious attentions. Servility creates despotism. This slavish homage, instead of softening my heart, only pampered whatever was stern and exacting in its mood. The very circumstance of her hovering round me like a fascinated bird seemed to transform me into a rigid pillar of stone; her Batteries irritated my scorn, her blandishments confirmed my reserve. At times I wondered what she meant by giving herself such trouble to win me, when the more profitable Pelet was already in her nets, and when, too, she was aware that I possessed her secret—for I had not scrupled to tell her as much; but the fact is that as it was her nature to doubt the reality and undervalue the worth of modesty, affection, disinterestedness—to regard these qualities as foibles of character—so it was equally her tendency to consider pride, hardness, selfishness, as proofs of strength. She would trample on the neck of humility, she would kneel at the feet of disdain; she would meet tenderness with secret contempt, indifference she would woo with ceaseless assiduities. Benevolence, devotedness, enthusiasm were her antipathies; for dissimulation and self-interest she had a preference—they were real wisdom in her eyes. Moral and physical degradation, mental and bodily inferiority she regarded with indulgence: they were foils capable of being turned to good account as set-offs for her own endowments. To violence, injustice, tyranny, she succumbed: they were her natural masters. She had no propensity to hate, no impulse to resist them; the indignation their behests awake in some hearts was unknown in hers. From all this it resulted that the false and selfish called her wise, the vulgar and debased termed her charitable, the insolent and unjust dubbed her amiable, the conscientious and benevolent generally at first accepted as valid her claim to be considered one of themselves; but ere long the plating of pretension wore off, the real material appeared below, and they laid her aside as a deception.
“再见了，小姐。”我说着就逃了出去。我知道离女校长的阔论结束还早。她注视着我的背影；她倒很乐意挽留我。从我对她的态度开始变得强硬、冷漠之后，她对我的态度也变了。几乎每次她都要对我谄媚奉承，她不断揣测我的表情，对我表现出无微不至的关心。奴颜媚骨能酿造暴政。这种奴隶般的顺从，不会让我心软，只会让我的心更加严厉、苛刻。她现在这个样子，像只着了迷的小鸟般围着我打转，却似乎只能把我变成刚硬的石柱。她像连珠炮一样说个不停，只会激起我的鄙夷，她的奉承话让我更坚定了缄默的态度。有时我很想知道，既然对她更有好处的珀莱先生已经在她掌控之中，她为何要如此自寻烦恼来赢得我？况且，她也觉察到我知道她的秘密——因为我毫无顾忌地告诉了她；事实是这样的，她本性怀疑现实，轻视谦虚、温情和无私的价值——她把这些品格看成是性格上的缺陷——因而也是因为这样，她认为骄傲、冷酷和自私才能证明力量。她会践踏谦虚的颈项，却会屈膝于鄙视的脚下；她会暗带蔑视地幽会温柔，却百般殷切地追求冷漠。仁慈、忠实和热忱都是她反感的，虚伪和私心才是她的偏好——在她眼里，这些才是真正的智慧。她嗜好道德的沦丧与身体的退化，心智与身体的卑劣，这些像是金箔，正好能够派上用场，衬托她自己的禀赋。对于残暴、不义和专横，她听之任之：这些就是她天生的主宰者。她没有憎恶倾向，也没有抵制主宰者们的冲动；这些在别人心里唤醒义愤的东西在她的心里却没有。这一切所产生的后果是：虚假和自私称她为明智，鄙俗和低劣称她为宽厚，目中无人、不公不正称她为和蔼可亲；通常，而善良、仁慈起初认为她靠得住，因为她宣称自己也善良、仁慈；而不久，掩饰的镀层被磨掉，真正的材料从下面露出，善良、仁慈就会抛弃她这个骗子。
