CHAPTER XI  A Lover and His Lass  

第十一章  情人和少女  

Kilmeny was in the orchard when Eric reached it, and he lingered for a moment in the shadow of the spruce wood to dream over her beauty.
埃里克到果树园的时候，琦梅妮已在那儿了。他在云杉林的树阴下停留了一会儿，想象她的美貌。
The orchard had lately overflowed in waves of old-fashioned caraway, and she was standing in the midst of its sea of bloom, with the lace-like blossoms swaying around her in the wind. She wore the simple dress of pale blue print in which he had first seen her; silk attire could not better have become her loveliness. She had woven herself a chaplet of half open white rosebuds and placed it on her dark hair, where the delicate blossoms seemed less wonderful than her face.
果树园最近变成了一片过时的葛缕子的花海。她站在花海中央，周围的花朵像蕾丝一般，在风中摇曳生姿。她穿着他们初次相遇时的那条简单的淡蓝色印花裙子，丝质的衣服最能衬托出她的美。她用半开的白色玫瑰花蕾为自己编了一顶花冠，戴在乌黑的头发上，但是那么精美的花和她的相貌比起来似乎都要逊色几分。
When Eric stepped through the gap she ran to meet him with outstretched hands, smiling. He took her hands and looked into her eyes with an expression before which hers for the first time faltered. She looked down, and a warm blush strained the ivory curves of her cheek and throat. His heart bounded, for in that blush he recognized the banner of love's vanguard.
埃里克穿过空地时，她跑着过来欢迎他，伸出双手，面带微笑。他拉着她的手，用一种眼神看进她的眼睛。迎着这种目光，她的眼睛第一次躲闪颤抖了。她低下头，乳白色的面颊和脖子上泛起一阵热情的红晕。他的心怦怦直跳，因为在红晕里，他意识到爱情先锋队旗帜的存在。
"Are you glad to see me, Kilmeny?" he asked, in a low significant tone.
“琦梅妮，看到我你高兴吗？”他问道。语气低沉而意味深长。
She nodded, and wrote in a somewhat embarrassed fashion, "Yes. Why do you ask? You know I am always glad to see you. I was afraid you would not come. You did not come last night and I was so sorry. Nothing in the orchard seemed nice any longer. I couldn't even play. I tried to, and my violin only cried. I waited until it was dark and then I went home." 
她点点头，有点儿不好意思地写道：“高兴。你为什么这么问呢？你知道，看到你我总是很开心的。我之前怕你不会来了。你昨天晚上没有来，我很难过。果树园里的一切看起来都不再美好了。我甚至都不能拉小提琴了。我试过，可是拉出来的只有刺耳的尖叫。我一直等到天黑，然后就回家了。”
"I am sorry you were disappointed, Kilmeny. I couldn't come last night. Some day I shall tell you why. I stayed home to learn a new lesson. I am sorry you missed me—no, I am glad. Can you understand how a person may be glad and sorry for the same thing?" 
“很抱歉让你失望了，琦梅妮。昨晚我不能来。有一天我一定会告诉你为什么的。我待在家里，学到了新东西。抱歉让你惦记了——不，我很高兴。你能明白一个人怎么能为同一件事既高兴又难过呢？”
She nodded again, with a return of her usual sweet composure.
她再次点了点头，恢复了她一贯的沉静甜美。
"Yes, I could not have understood once, but I can now. Did you learn your new lesson?" 
“能，曾经我不能明白，但是现在，我可以明白了。你学新东西了？”
"Yes, very thoroughly. It was a delightful lesson when I once understood it. I must try to teach it to you some day. Come over to the old bench, Kilmeny. There is something I want to say to you. But first, will you give me a rose?" 
“是的，学得很透彻。一旦理解了，我就觉得这是令人愉快的一课。某天，我一定要试着把它教给你。到老长椅这儿来吧，琦梅妮。我有话想跟你说。但是，你能先送给我一支玫瑰花吗？”
She ran to the bush, and, after careful deliberation, selected a perfect half-open bud and brought it to him—a white bud with a faint, sunrise flush about its golden heart.
她跑到灌木丛中，一翻深思熟虑后，挑选了一支完美的花骨朵，白色的花蕾半开着，金色的花心外有一圈日出般浅淡的绯红——她把这朵花拿给他。
"Thank you. It is as beautiful as—as a woman I know," Eric said.
“谢谢。它和——和我认识的一个女人一样美，”埃里克说。
A wistful look came into her face at his words, and she walked with a drooping head across the orchard to the bench.
听到他的话，她的脸上流露出渴望的神情。她低着头，穿过果树园，走到长凳那儿。
"Kilmeny," he said, seriously, "I am going to ask you to do something for me. I want you to take me home with you and introduce me to your uncle and aunt." 
“琦梅妮，”他认真地说，“我要你为我做些事。我想要你带我去你家，并把我介绍给你的舅舅和舅妈。”
She lifted her head and stared at him incredulously, as if he had asked her to do something wildly impossible. Understanding from his grave face that he meant what he said, a look of dismay dawned in her eyes. She shook her head almost violently and seemed to be making a passionate, instinctive effort to speak. Then she caught up her pencil and wrote with feverish haste: "I cannot do that. Do not ask me to. You do not understand. They would be very angry. They do not want to see any one coming to the house. And they would never let me come here again. Oh, you do not mean it?" 
她抬起头，怀疑地盯着他，好像他叫她去做一件根本不可能的事。从他严肃的脸上，她看出了他不是在开玩笑。忧虑的神色出现在她眼里。她几乎是狠狠地摇了摇头，似乎正迫切而本能地努力说出话来。然后，她抓起笔，焦躁不安地匆忙写道：“我不能做到。不要叫我那样做。你不明白。他们会非常生气的。他们不想见到任何人来家里。而且他们将不会允许我再来这儿的。哎，你在开玩笑吧？”
He pitied her for the pain and bewilderment in her eyes; but he took her slender hands in his and said firmly, "Yes, Kilmeny, I do mean it. It is not quite right for us to be meeting each other here as we have been doing, without the knowledge and consent of your friends. You cannot now understand this, but—believe me—it is so." 
她眼里的痛苦和为难让他难过，但是他把她纤细的手放在他的手心，坚定地说：“不，琦梅妮，我是认真的。我们像这样一直在这儿见面，却没有告知你的朋友，得到他们的同意，这完全是不对的。现在，你不能理解这点，但是——相信我——这是事实。”
She looked questioningly, pityingly into his eyes. What she read there seemed to convince her, for she turned very pale and an expression of hopelessness came into her face. Releasing her hands, she wrote slowly, "If you say it is wrong I must believe it. I did not know anything so pleasant could be wrong. But if it is wrong we must not meet here any more. Mother told me I must never do anything that was wrong. But I did not know this was wrong." 
她迷惑而又同情地看着他的眼睛。从他眼里读到的东西似乎使她相信了他的话，因为她脸色突然变得惨白，无助的表情浮现在脸上。她抽出手，慢慢写道：“如果你说这是错的，我一定会相信的。我之前不觉得如此令人愉快的事会是错的。但如果是错的，那我们以后一定别在这里见面了。母亲曾教导我一定不要做错事。但是，我先前不知道这是错的。”
"It was not wrong for you, Kilmeny. But it was a little wrong for me, because I knew better—or rather, should have known better. I didn't stop to think, as the children say. Some day you will understand fully. Now, you will take me to your uncle and aunt, and after I have said to them what I want to say it will be all right for us to meet here or anywhere." 
“这不是你的错，琦梅妮。但我却犯了点儿错，因为我知道得更清楚——或者说，本应该知道得更清楚。我没有停下来想想，就像孩子们说的那样。总有一天你会完全明白的。现在你带我去见你的舅舅和舅妈，我跟他们说完我想说的话之后，我们在这儿或者其他地方见面都将完全可以了。”
She shook her head.
她摇摇头。
"No," she wrote, "Uncle Thomas and Aunt Janet will tell you to go away and never come back. And they will never let me come here any more. Since it is not right to meet you I will not come, but it is no use to think of going to them. I did not tell them about you because I knew that they would forbid me to see you, but I am sorry, since it is so wrong." 
“不，”她写到，“托马斯舅舅和珍妮特舅妈会叫你离开，叫你永远都别再来。而且他们也不允许我再到这儿来了。既然与你见面是不对的，那么我不会再来了，但你想去见他们是没用的。我没有告诉他们关于你的事，因为我知道他们会不准我去见你。但我很难过，因为这样做很不对。
"You must take me to them," said Eric firmly. "I am quite sure that things will not be as you fear when they hear what I have to say." 
“你一定得带我去见他们，”埃里克坚定地说。“我非常确定，他们听了我要讲的话后，事情不会像你所担心的那样。”
Uncomforted, she wrote forlornly, "I must do it, since you insist, but I am sure it will be no use. I cannot take you to-night because they are away. They went to the store at Radnor. But I will take you to-morrow night; and after that I shall not see you any more." 
她丝毫没得到宽慰，凄楚地写道：“因为你的坚持，我一定会照做的，但我确信这样做，是没有用的。今晚我不能带你去，因为他们不在。他们到拉德纳的店铺去了。但明晚我会带你去，从那以后，我可能就再也见不到你了。”
Two great tears brimmed over in her big blue eyes and splashed down on her slate. Her lips quivered like a hurt child's. Eric put his arm impulsively about her and drew her head down upon his shoulder. As she cried there, softly, miserably, he pressed his lips to the silky black hair with its coronal of rosebuds. He did not see two burning eyes which were looking at him over the old fence behind him with hatred and mad passion blazing in their depths. Neil Gordon was crouched there, with clenched hands and heaving breast, watching them.
两颗豆大的泪珠从她大大的蓝眼睛里涌出来，溅落在纸板上。她像个受了伤的孩子似的，嘴唇颤抖着。埃里克情不自禁地抱着她，让她的头靠在他肩上。她在他肩上悲伤地轻声哭泣，埃里克吻着她丝绸般的黑头发，她头上还带着玫瑰花苞编成的花冠。他没有看到，身后的老篱笆外，一双愤怒的眼睛正在看着他。在那双眼睛的深处，憎恨和狂怒之火正熊熊燃烧。尼尔·戈登蹲在那儿看着他们，双手紧握，胸口一起一伏。
"Kilmeny, dear, don't cry," said Eric tenderly. "You shall see me again. I promise you that, whatever happens. I do not think your uncle and aunt will be as unreasonable as you fear, but even if they are they shall not prevent me from meeting you somehow." 
“琦梅妮，亲爱的，不要哭，”埃里克温柔地说，“你会再见到我的。我向你保证，无论发生什么，你都会再见到我的。我想你舅舅和舅妈不会像你担心的那样不讲理，但即使他们不讲理，他们也不能阻止我用某种方法来见你。”
Kilmeny lifted her head, and wiped the tears from her eyes. "You do not know what they are like," she wrote. "They will lock me into my room. That is the way they always punished me when I was a little girl. And once, not so very long ago, when I was a big girl, they did it." 
琦梅妮抬起头，擦去眼里的泪水。“你不知道他们是什么样的人，”她写道，“他们会把我锁在房间里。当我还是个小女孩的时候，他们就总是用那种方式来惩罚我。而且，就在不久前，我都是个大姑娘了，他们还这样惩罚我。”
"If they do I'll get you out somehow," said Eric, laughing a little.
“如果他们那样做，我无论如何都会把你救出来的。”埃里克微微笑着说。
She allowed herself to smile, but it was a rather forlorn little effort. She did not cry any more, but her spirits did not come back to her. Eric talked gaily, but she only listened in a pensive, absent way, as if she scarcely heard him. When he asked her to play, she shook her head. "I cannot think any music to-night," she wrote, "I must go home, for my head aches and I feel very stupid." 
她让自己笑了笑，但是笑得很凄楚。她不再哭了，可是她的情绪还是没有恢复过来。埃里克兴高采烈地说着，但她只是忧虑重重、心不在焉地听着，好像她几乎没有听见他说话似的。他要她拉琴，她摇了摇头。“今晚，我想不起任何旋律，”她写道，“我必须回去了，因为我头疼，感觉昏昏沉沉的。”
"Very well, Kilmeny. Now, don't worry, little girl. It will all come out all right." 
“好吧，琦梅妮。现在，不要担心，小姑娘。一切都会好起来的。”
Evidently she did not share his confidence, for her head drooped again as they walked together across the orchard. At the entrance of the wild cherry lane she paused and looked at him half reproachfully, her eyes filling again. She seemed to be bidding him a mute farewell. With an impulse of tenderness which he could not control, Eric put his arm about her and kissed her red, trembling mouth. She started back with a little cry. A burning colour swept over her face, and the next moment she fled swiftly up the darkening lane.
很明显，她并没有受到他的信心的鼓舞，因为他们一起穿过果树园的时候，她又垂下了头。在野樱桃小道的入口处，她停了下来，略带责怪地看着他，眼里又噙满了泪水。她似乎在跟他无声地告别。在一阵难以抑制的柔情下，埃里克情不自禁地抱住了她，并吻了她红润颤抖的嘴唇。她轻轻地叫了一声便往后退。她的面庞如火烧一般红彤彤的，一秒后，她就快速地逃进了黑暗的小道。
The sweetness of that involuntary kiss clung to Eric's lips as he went homeward, half-intoxicating him. He knew that it had opened the gates of womanhood to Kilmeny. Never again, he felt, would her eyes meet his with their old unclouded frankness. When next he looked into them he knew that he should see there the consciousness of his kiss. Behind her in the orchard that night Kilmeny had left her childhood.
那个情不自禁的吻的甜蜜还萦绕在埃里克的唇边，他在回家的路上都半沉醉于其中。他知道这个吻为琦梅妮开启了成熟女子的大门。他感到，她再也不会用以前那种坦诚明朗的眼神，看着他了。下次再看她眼睛的时候，他知道他会在那里看到，她意识到他的吻了。那天晚上，琦梅妮把童年留在了身后的果树园里。
