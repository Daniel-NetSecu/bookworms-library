CHAPTER XXI  

第二十一章  

Directly as I closed the door I saw laid on the table two letters. My thought was that they were notes of invitation from the friends of some of my pupils. I had received such marks of attention occasionally, and with me, who had no friends, correspondence of more interest was out of the question. The postman's arrival had never yet been an event of interest to me since I came to Brussels. I laid my hand carelessly on the documents, and coldly and slowly glancing at them, I prepared to break the seals. My eye was arrested and my hand too; I saw what excited me, as if I had found a vivid picture where I expected only to discover a blank page. On one cover was an English postmark; on the other a lady's clear, fine autograph. The last I opened first.
我刚关上门就看见桌子上摆了两封信。我先想到的是我某个学生的朋友寄过来的邀请信。我过去偶尔收到过这类邀请信，对于我这种没有朋友的人，根本不会有更为重要的信件。自我来到布鲁塞尔，邮差的到来从来就不关我的事。我毫不在意地将手放在信上，冷淡而缓慢地看着它们，准备撕信封。忽然我的眼睛和手都停住了，我明白是什么让我激动了，那就像是我本以为只是白纸一张，结果却看到了一副鲜艳的图画。一个信封上是英国邮戳，另一张上是一位女子清晰娟秀的笔迹。我先打开了后一封。
'Monsieur,—I found out what you had done the very morning after your visit to me. You might be sure I should dust the china every day; and as no one but you had been in my room for a week, and as fairy-money is not current in Brussels, I could not doubt who left the twenty francs on the chimney-piece. I thought I heard you stir the vase when I was stooping to look for your glove under the table, and I wondered you should imagine it had got into such a little cup. Now, monsieur, the money is not mine, and I shall not keep it. I will not send it in this note, because it might be lost—besides, it is heavy—but I will restore it to you the first time I see you. And you must make no difficulties about taking it: because, in the first place, I am sure, monsieur, you can understand that one likes to pay one's debts—that it is satisfactory to owe no man anything; and, in the second place, I can now very well afford to be honest, as I am provided with a situation. This last circumstance is, indeed, the reason of my writing to you, for it is pleasant to communicate good news and in these days I have only my master to whom I can tell anything.
“先生，在您拜访我的第二天早晨我便发现您所做的事情了。您也许确定我每天都会给瓷器除尘，因为除了您之外，这周没有人来过我的房间，而且这种带有仙女图像的硬币在布鲁塞尔并不流通，我毫无疑问就断定是谁将这二十法郎放在壁炉台上的。我想起当我弯腰去桌子下帮您找手套的时候曾经听见您弄那个花瓶，我还想您怎么会把手套掉到那么小的杯子里呢。先生，既然这钱不是我的，我不能留着它。我没有将它们装到这个信封里寄给您，因为可能会丢——而且那也太重了——我会暂时收着，等到下次见到您再还给您。您一定要毫不客气地拿走它们，首先因为我确定先生您能够理解任何人都想还清他的债务——正所谓“无债一身轻”；其次我之所以能够真心诚意地将钱还给您是因为我已经找到一份工作。事实上，这也是我写信给您的真正原因，因为我很乐意给您报喜，这些日子里我唯能跟我的老师说一些事情。
'A week ago, monsieur, I was sent for by a Mrs. Wharton, an English lady. Her eldest daughter was going to be married, and some rich relation having made her a present of a veil and dress in costly old lace, as precious, they said, almost as jewels, but a little damaged by time, I was commissioned to put them in repair. I had to do it at the house. They gave me, besides some embroidery to complete, and nearly a week elapsed before I had finished everything. While I worked, Miss Wharton often came into the room and sat with me, and so did Mrs. Wharton. They made me talk English; asked how I had learned to speak it so well. Then they inquired what I knew besides—what books I had read; soon they seemed to make a sort of wonder of me, considering me no doubt as a learned grisette. One afternoon Mrs. Wharton brought in a Parisian lady to test the accuracy of my knowledge of French. The result of it was that, owing probably in a great degree to the mother's and daughter's good- humour about the marriage, which inclined them to do beneficent deeds, and partly, I think, because they are naturally benevolent people, they decided that the wish I had expressed to do something more than mend lace was a very legitimate one, and the same day they took me in their carriage to Mrs. D—'s, who is the directress of the first English school at Brussels. It seems she happened to be in want of a French lady to give lessons in geography, history, grammar, and composition, in the French language. Mrs. Wharton recommended me very warmly; and as two of her younger daughters are pupils in the house, her patronage availed to get me the place. It was settled that I am to attend six hours daily (for, happily, it was not required that I should live in the house; I should have been sorry to leave my lodgings), and for this Mrs. D— will give me twelve hundred francs per annum.
“先生，一周前一个叫沃顿太太的英国夫人派人叫我过去。她的大女儿要结婚，有一位富亲戚送给她一方面纱和一件镶着昂贵古花边的衣服，她们说那件衣服几乎像珍宝一样珍贵，但是因为年代久远而有一些损坏，她们请我去修一下。我只好在她家里修。此外她们还给了我一些刺绣活，我花了快一个星期才把一切弄好。在我工作的时候，沃顿小姐经常来房间里和我坐在一起，沃顿夫人也是一样。她们让我说英语，问我怎么学的，能讲那么好。然后她们问我还知道什么，读过什么书，不久她们似乎对我有了好奇心，认为我一定是个有学识的女工。一天下午沃顿太太带来一个巴黎夫人，想测试我的法语水平。可能很大程度上由于这对好脾气的母女人逢喜事，乐于行善，我想还部分地因为她们天性向善，所以她们认为我表达过想做一些比缝补花边更重要的事的愿望很理所当然。她们当天便带我坐马车到德太太家，德太太是布鲁塞尔第一所英语学校的女校长。好像她正好在找一个法国女士用法语教授地理、历史、语法和作文。沃顿太太很热情地推荐了我；由于她的两个小女儿在这所学校读书，她又是该校赞助人，这两点帮助我谋得了那个职位。最后确定我每天上六个小时的课（所幸的是没有要求我住在学校；要我离开家我会很难过的），沃顿太太每年付我一千两百法郎。
'You see, therefore, monsieur, that I am now rich—richer almost than I ever hoped to be. I feel thankful for it, especially as my sight was beginning to be injured by constant working at fine lace; and I was getting, too, very weary of sitting up late at nights, and yet not being able to find time for reading or study. I began to fear that I should fall ill, and be unable to pay my way. This fear is now in a great measure removed; and in truth, monsieur, I am very grateful to God for the relief, and I feel it necessary, almost, to speak of my happiness to some one who is kind-hearted enough to derive joy from seeing others joyful. I could not, therefore, resist the temptation of writing to you. I argued with myself it is very pleasant for me to write, and it will not be exactly painful, though it may be tiresome, to monsieur to read. Do not be too angry with my circumlocution and inelegancies of expression, and believe me your attached pupil, 
“所以先生您看，我现在富起来了——几乎比我以前所希望的还富有。对此我很感激，尤其是我的视力开始由于长期做精细女工而受到损害，而且夜里经常熬夜到很晚，也弄得我非常厌倦，都没有时间读书学习。我开始担心这样迟早会生病，甚至无力支付日常开销。这种担心如今已经在很大程度上不存在了；说实在的，先生，我很感激上帝为我减轻了负担，我觉得很有必要向乐他人所乐的好心人诉说我心中的喜悦之情。于是我忍不住提笔给您写信。我和自己争辩，写这封信令我很开心，先生您读起来尽管可能会觉得很无聊，但也不至于痛苦。我拙词劣句地赘述了这么多，还请先生见谅，并请相信您忠实的学生。
F. E. Henri.' 
弗·埃·亨利。”
Having read this letter, I mused on its contents for a few moments—whether with sentiments pleasurable or otherwise I will hereafter note—and then took up the other. It was directed in a hand to me unknown—small, and rather neat, neither masculine nor exactly feminine. The seal bore a coat of arms, concerning which I could only decipher that it was not that of the Seacombe family; consequently the epistle could be from none of my almost forgotten, and certainly quite forgetting, patrician relations. From whom, then, was it? I removed the envelope; the note folded within ran as follows:— 
读完这封信，我对里面的内容沉思了一会儿——心里究竟是伤感还是喜悦留到以后再说——跟着又拿起了第二封。那个笔迹我并不熟识——字体很小又很工整，既不像男人的字也不像女人的字。封条上是个盾形纹徽，我只能确定这不是西科姆家族的标志，这样我就知道这封信不会来自我几乎已经遗忘，当然也几乎遗忘我的贵族亲戚。那它到底是谁寄的呢？我打开信封，拆开叠着的信纸，信的内容如下：
'I have no doubt in the world that you are doing well in that greasy Flanders; living probably on the fat of the unctuous land; sitting like a black-haired, tawny-skinned, long-nosed Israelite by the flesh-pots of Egypt; or like a rascally son of Levi near the brass cauldrons of the sanctuary, and every now and then plunging in a consecrated hook, and drawing out of the sea of broth the fattest heave-shoulders and the fleshiest of wave-breasts. I know this, because you never write to any one in England. Thankless dog that you are! I, by the sovereign efficacy of my recommendation, got you the place where you are now living in clover, and yet not a word of gratitude, or even acknowledgment, have you ever offered in return. But I am coming to see you, and small conception can you, with your addled aristocratic brains, form of the sort of moral kicking I have ready packed in my carpet bag, destined to be presented to you immediately on my arrival.
“我肯定你在那块法兰德斯的沃土上混得不错，你极有可能在富得流油的土地上过着奢侈的生活，就像一个坐在埃及人肉锅边上的黑头发、黄褐色皮肤、长鼻子的以色列人，或者就像利未那卑劣的儿子坐在神殿的大铜锅旁，时不时放进一个圣钩，从肉汤中捞出最肥的膀子和肉最多的前肋。因为你从没有给英国的任何人写过信，所以我知道这些。你真是条忘恩负义的狗啊！全赖我的鼎力推荐，你方能得到今天的位置，过着此般安逸的日子，而你却没有一句感激之言，甚至没听你说过一句谢谢来回赠我。但是我还是要去看你的，你那混乱的贵族头脑很难想象我是多么气愤，我已经将这种怨恨收到我的旅行箱里，准备带到布鲁塞尔去，一见你面就拿出来让你瞧瞧。
'Meantime I know all about your affairs, and have just got information, by Brown's last letter, that you are said to be on the point of forming an advantageous match with a pursy little Belgian schoolmistress—a Mdlle. Zoraïbe, or some such name. Won't I have a look at her when I come over! And this you may rely on—if she pleases my taste, or if I think it worth while in a pecuniary point of view, I'll pounce on your prize and bear her away triumphant in spite of your teeth. Yet I don't like dumpies either, and Brown says she is little and stout—the better fitted for a wiry, starved-looking chap like you.
“同时我还知道你所有的事情，并且刚从布朗先生的上封信里得到消息，据说你即将和一位矮小肥胖的比利时女校长——叫佐诺比小姐还是什么的——喜结连理。我去你那儿的时候一定要见一见她！我可以向你保证——如果她合我的胃口，或者从经济的角度我觉得她很合适的话，我会向你猛扑过去，就算扳开你的牙齿也要成功地抢走她。但是我不喜欢矮胖型的，布朗说她就是又矮又胖——这正好适合你这种又瘦又长饿死鬼般的家伙。
'Be on the lookout, for you know neither the day nor hour when your (I don't wish to blaspheme, so I'll leave a blank) cometh. —Yours truly, 
“注意这点，你不会知道你的——（我不想亵渎神明，所以用了一个破折号来代表我要说的那个词）何日何时会到。——你忠实的，
Hunsden Yorke Hunsden.
亨斯登·约克·亨斯登。
'Humph!' said I; and ere I laid the letter down I again glanced at the small, neat handwriting, not a bit like that of a mercantile man, nor, indeed, of any man except Hunsden himself. They talk of affinities between the autograph and the character; what affinity was there here? I recalled the writer's peculiar face, and certain traits I suspected, rather than knew, to appertain to his nature, and I answered, 'A great deal.' 
我“哼”了一声，放下信之前我又看了眼那细小而工整的字迹，一点都不像是出于一个商人之手，也确实不像除了亨斯登之外的任何人之手。人们说字如其人，那这封信与它主人的性格有什么相同之处呢？我回忆起写信者那古怪的面孔和一些我所猜测而非我所了解的特征，便自己答道：“真是极为相像。”
Hunsden, then, was coming to Brussels, and coming I knew not when—coming charged with the expectation of finding me on the summit of prosperity, about to be married, to step into a warm nest, to lie comfortably down by the side of a snug, well-fed little mate.
那么亨斯登很快就要到布鲁塞尔了，我也不知道何时会到，他来是想看到我意气风发，即将娶得娇妻，步入温暖的巢窝，十分舒服地躺在一个温暖肥胖的小女人身边。
'I wish him joy of the fidelity of the picture he has painted,' thought I. 'What will he say when, instead of a pair of plump turtle-doves, billing and cooing in a bower of roses, he finds a single lean cormorant, standing mateless and shelterless on poverty's bleak cliff? Oh, confound him! Let him come, and let him laugh at the contrast between rumour and fact. Were he the devil himself, instead of being merely very like him I'd not condescend to get out of his way, or to forge a smile or a cheerful word wherewith to avert his sarcasm.' 
“我倒希望能满足他，让他看到他所想象的场景，”我暗想，“但当看到的不是在玫瑰花丛里卿卿我我的一对肥斑鸠，而是一只孤孤单单无依无靠，站在荒凉的贫困悬崖边上的瘦鸬鹚，他会作何感想？哦，去他的吧！让他来吧，让他看着谣言和现实之间的差距来嘲笑我吧。如果他自己就是魔鬼，而不是我所想象的那个他，我就不用屈尊远离他了，也不会对他的讽刺挖苦付诸一笑，故作乐观。
Then I recurred to the other letter. That struck a chord whose sound I could not deaden by thrusting my fingers into my ears, for it vibrated within; and though its swell might be exquisite music, its cadence was a groan.
然后我又想起之前那封信。它像一根奏响的琴弦，即使我用指头塞住耳朵也能听见那声音，因为它搅动着我的内心，虽然它的攀升很像优雅的音乐，可它的降落却像是在呻吟。
That Frances was relieved from the pressure of want, that the curse of excessive labour was taken off her, filled me with happiness; that her first thought in prosperity should be to augment her joy by sharing it with me met and satisfied the wish of my heart. Two results of her letter were then pleasant sweet as two draughts of nectar; but applying my lips for the third time to the cup, and they were excoriated as with vinegar and gall.
弗朗西斯摆脱了贫困的压力，告别了过分繁重的工作，这使我很高兴；她在得意时第一刻想到与我分享她的快乐，这满足了我内心的愿望。前两次读她的来信就好像两杯琼浆一样甘甜，但是当我将嘴唇靠近杯子喝第三次时，它却如醋和胆汁般又酸又苦。
Two persons whose desires are moderate may live well enough in Brussels on an income which would scarcely afford a respectable maintenance for one in London, and that not because the necessaries of life are so much dearer in the latter capital, or taxes so much higher than in the former, but because the English surpass in folly all the nations on God's earth, and are more abject slaves to custom, to opinion, to the desire to keep up a certain appearance, than the Italians are to priestcraft, the French to vainglory, the Russians to their czar or the Germans to black beer. I have seen a degree of sense in the modest arrangement of one homely Belgian household that might put to shame the elegance, the superfluities, the luxuries, the strained refinements of a hundred genteel English mansions. In Belgium, provided you can make money, you may save it. This is scarcely possible in England; ostentation there lavishes in a month what industry has earned in a year. More shame to all classes in that most bountiful and beggarly country for their servile following of fashion. I could write a chapter or two on this subject, but must forbear, at least for the present. Had I retained my é60 per annum, I could, now that Frances was in possession of é50, have gone straight to her this very evening and spoken out the words which, repressed, kept fretting my heart with fever. Our united income would, as we should have managed it, have sufficed well for our mutual support, since we lived in a country where economy was not confounded with meanness, where frugality in dress, food, and furniture was not synonymous with vulgarity in these various points. But the placeless usher, bare of resource, and unsupported by connections, must not think of this; such a sentiment as love, such a word as marriage, were misplaced in his heart and on his lips. Now for the first time did I truly feel what it was to be poor; now did the sacrifice I had made in casting from me the means of living put on a new aspect. Instead of a correct, just, honourable act, it seemed a deed at once light and fanatical. I took several turns in my room, under the goading influence of most poignant remorse. I walked a quarter of an hour from the wall to the window; and at the window self-reproach seemed to face, me, at the wall self-disdain. All at once out spoke Conscience.
同样的收入在伦敦几乎不足以维持一个人的体面生活，但是在布鲁塞尔却足以让两个生活节制的人过得很好。这并不是因为伦敦的生活必需品价格或者税收比布鲁塞尔高很多，而是因为英国人比世界上其他民族的人愚蠢得多，他们屈从于习惯风俗，顾及外界的风言风语，渴望保持体面的形象，这比意大利人对牧师的尊敬、俄国人对沙皇崇拜、法国人对虚荣的追求以及德国人对黑啤的依赖都更强烈。我见过一个比利时家庭，那朴素大方的布置足以使一百个上流社会的英国宅邸中优雅冗余而奢华的装饰相形见绌。在比利时，只要你能赚钱你就能存钱。这在英国几乎不可能；英国人为了虚荣可以在一个月之内挥霍掉一年所赚的钱。更为可耻的是，在那个既富裕又贫穷的国家，所有人不分阶级都卑躬屈膝地追赶潮流。关于这个话题我能写一两章，但我必须克制，至少现在要克制。如果我保持以前每年六十英镑的收入，而如今弗朗西斯每年大概也有五十英镑，今晚我就可以直奔她家，向她说出我一直压抑着的话——那些话一直在我心里燃烧，使我烦躁不安。由于在我们生活的国家，节约并不被看做吝啬，衣食住行等方面的节俭并不等同于粗俗，所以只要我们合理安排，我们的收入加在一起足以维持我们共同的生活。但是一个无家可归又失去经济来源，而且毫无依靠的穷教师，是断不该有此想法的；他心里不该有爱情，嘴里也不该说出“结婚”二字。现在我第一次真切地明白贫穷的含义；我所作出的切断经济来源的牺牲，此刻看起来竟是另一种结果。这并不是一个正确、合适并体面的决定，反而似乎是一种轻率荒唐的行为。我在房间里走来走去，悔恨的痛苦不断扎着我。我从墙边走到窗边，足足走了有一刻钟，当我面对窗户时，有一种自责感，面对墙壁时又有一种自卑感。突然良心喊道：
'Down, stupid tormentors!' cried she. 'The man has done his duty. You shall not bait him thus by thoughts of what might have been. He relinquished a temporary and contingent good to avoid a permanent and certain evil; he did well. Let him reflect now, and when your blinding dust and deafening hum subside he will discover a path.' 
“趴下，你这个让人痛苦的蠢货！”她喊道，“这个人已经尽了他的职责。你不应该再用各种幻想引诱他。他放弃了一些暂时的有可能的好处，避免了一场长久而必然的不幸，他干得很好。现在让他好好想想，当你蒙蔽双眼的尘土和震耳欲聋的噪声退去之后，他就会找到一条出路的。”
I sat down; I propped my forehead on both my hands; I thought and thought an hour—two hours—vainly. I seemed like one sealed in a subterranean vault, who gazes at utter blackness, at blackness ensured by yard-thick stone walls around, and by piles of building above expecting light to penetrate through granite, and through cement firm as granite. But there are chinks, or there may be chinks, in the best-adjusted masonry. There was a chink in my cavernous cell, for eventually I saw, or seemed to see, a ray—pallid, indeed, and cold, and doubtful, but still a ray, for it showed that narrow path which conscience had promised. After two, three hours' torturing research in brain and memory, I disinterred certain remains of circumstances, and conceived a hope that by putting them together an expedient might be framed and a resource discovered. The circumstances were briefly these:— 
我坐下来，双手托着头，苦苦思考了一个小时——两个小时——还是徒劳无功。我似乎是在一个隐蔽的地窖里，凝视着四下的黑暗，因为周围是一码厚的石墙，头顶是层层的建筑，我盼望着光线能够穿透花岗岩，穿透如花岗岩般结实的水泥。但即使是最结实的建筑也会有或可能有裂缝。我这个如同洞穴般的小房间里就有，因为我终于看到，或者说似乎看到有一束光——那束光虽然暗淡、冰凉、可疑，但它毕竟是一束光，因为它照出了一条我的良心所认定的狭窄小径。在两三个小时的苦思冥想之后，我发掘了一些残余的情况，把这些情况拼凑起来也许有希望构成一个权宜之计，找到一些线索。情况大致如下：
Some three months ago M. Pelet had, on the occasion of his fete, given the boys a treat, which treat consisted in a party of pleasure to a certain place of public resort in the outskirts of Brussels, of which I do not at this moment remember the name, but near it were several of those lakelets called etangs; and there was one etang, larger than the rest, where on holidays people were accustomed to amuse themselves by rowing round it in little boats. The boys having eaten an unlimited quantity of gaufres, and drunk several bottles of Louvain beer amid the shades of a garden made and provided for such crams, petitioned the director for leave to take a row on the etang. Half a dozen of the eldest succeeded in obtaining leave, and I was commissioned to accompany them as surveillant. Among the half- dozen happened to be a certain Jean Baptiste Vandenhuten, a most ponderous young Flamand, not tall, but even now, at the early age of sixteen, possessing a breadth and depth of personal development truly national. It chanced that Jean was the first lad to step into the boat. He stumbled, rolled to one side; the boat revolted at his weight, and capsized. Vandenhuten sank like lead, rose, sank again. My coat and waistcoat were off in an instant. I had not been brought up at Eton and boated and bathed and swum there ten long years for nothing; it was a natural and easy act for me to leap to the rescue. The lads and the boatmen yelled; they thought there would be two deaths by drowning instead of one. But as Jean rose the third time I clutched him by one leg and the collar, and in three minutes more both he and I were safe landed. To speak heaven's truth, my merit in the action was small indeed, for I had run no risk, and subsequently did not even catch cold from the wetting; but when M. and Madame Vandenhuten, of whom Jean Baptiste was the sole hope, came to hear of the exploit, they seemed to think I had evinced a bravery and devotion which no thanks could sufficiently repay. Madame, in particular, was 'certain I must have dearly loved their sweet son, or I would not thus have hazarded my own life to save his.' Monsieur, an honest-looking though phlegmatic man, said very little; but he would not suffer me to leave the room till I had promised that in case I ever stood in need of help I would, by applying to him, give him a chance of discharging the obligation under which he affirmed I had laid him. These words, then, were my glimmer of light; it was here I found my sole outlet; and in truth, though the cold light roused, it did not cheer me, nor did the outlet seem such as I should like to pass through. Right I had none to M. Vandenhuten's good offices. It was not on the ground of merit I could apply to him; no, I must stand on that of necessity. I had no work; I wanted work. My best chance of obtaining it lay in securing his recommendation. This I knew could be had by asking for it; not to ask because the request revolted my pride and contradicted my habits would, I felt, be an indulgence of false and indolent fastidiousness. I might repent the omission all my life. I would not, then, be guilty of it.
大概三个月前珀莱先生在生日之际设宴款待了他的学生们，那次宴会是在布鲁塞尔郊外一个人们常去郊游的地方举行的，现在我不太记得它的名字，只记得那附近有很多叫做池塘的小湖泊；有一个池塘比其他的都要大，节假日时人们总是喜欢在里面乘小船游玩。学生们已经吃了一大份薄脆松饼，又喝了几瓶卢汶啤酒，坐在一个专为他们这种酒足饭饱的人修建的花园里乘凉，恳请校长让他们去池塘里划船。大概六个年龄较大的学生成功地得到了准假，我被派去监护他们。在这六个学生中恰巧有一个叫吉恩·巴普蒂斯特·范登赫顿的最笨重的佛兰芒青年，他个子不高，虽然当时只有十六岁，却已是虎背熊腰，典型的一副法国人身材。又碰巧吉恩是第一个上船的。他摇摇晃晃，滚向一侧；船经不住他的体重，一下子就翻了。范登赫顿像铅块一样沉了又浮，浮了又沉。我立马脱下外套和背心。我从小在伊顿长大，在那漫长的十年中我划船、洗澡、游泳，水性极佳，对我来说跳入水中救人是件自然而简单的事情。小伙子和船夫们大叫起来，他们认为现在不是溺死一个而是要淹死一双了。当吉恩第三次浮起来时我抓住他的一只胳膊和衣领，三分多钟后我们两个就都安全上岸了。说实话，这件事实在是微不足道，因为我没有冒任何风险，虽然衣服湿了，但是后来甚至都没有感冒。可是吉恩·巴普蒂斯特是范登赫顿夫妻的独生子，他们听说了我的英勇事迹后，似乎就认为我展示出的英勇与奉献精神是无论怎么感谢都无以回报的。尤其是那位太太，她确信我肯定非常爱他们的宝贝儿子，否则我断然不会如此舍命相救；那位先生看起来十分老实，表情平淡，话说得很少，但也是直到我答应日后一旦需要帮助就回来麻烦他，给他一个报答我的机会，他才肯放我离开他家。当时我说那些话只是一道微弱的光线，而此时这却是我的唯一出路；事实上，这冰冷的光线虽然已燃起，却并没有使我高兴，似乎那条路也不是我愿意走的。我没有权利要求范登赫顿先生帮助我。我并没有做出什么能让他回报的丰功伟绩，没有，我只是迫不得已。我没有工作，我想找到工作。我能找到工作的最好机会就是寻求他的推荐。我知道只要我提出来他就会推荐我；但是我不想提出来，因为这样做会伤及我的自尊，有悖我的习惯，我觉得那样做是一种错误的放纵和懒惰的挑剔。不那样做，我也许会后悔一辈子。但我不会为此感到愧疚。
That evening I went to M. Vandenhuten's, but I had bent the bow and adjusted the shaft in vain; the string broke. I rang the bell at the great door (it was a large, handsome house in an expensive part of the town); a manservant opened; I asked for M. Vandenhuten. M. Vandenhuten and family were all out of town—gone to Ostend—did not know when they would be back. I left my card, and retraced my steps.
那天晚上我来到范登赫顿家，但是我又是拉弓又是调箭却是一场空；因为弓弦断了。我按了大门的门铃（这是城市繁华地带上的一座高大富丽的房子），一位男仆人开了门，我求见范登赫顿先生，但是范登赫顿先生全家都不在城里——他们去奥斯坦德了——也不知道什么时候回来。我留下名片，便往回走了。
