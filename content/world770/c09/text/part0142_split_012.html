CHAPTER XIII  

第十三章  

Next morning I rose with the dawn, and having dressed myself and stood half an hour, my elbow leaning on the chest of drawers, considering what means I should adopt to restore my spirits, fagged with sleeplessness, to their ordinary tone—for I had no intention of getting up a scene with M. Pelet, reproaching him with perfidy, sending him a challenge, or performing other gambadoes of the sort—I hit at last on the expedient of walking out in the cool of the morning to a neighbouring establishment of baths, and treating myself to a bracing plunge. The remedy produced the desired effect. I came back at seven o'clock steadied and invigorated, and was able to greet M. Pelet when he entered to breakfast with an unchanged and tranquil countenance. Even a cordial offering of the hand and the flattering appellation of 'mon fils,' pronounced in that caressing tone with which monsieur had, of late days especially, been accustomed to address me, did not elicit any external sign of the feeling which, though subdued, still glowed at my heart. Not that I nursed vengeance—no; but the sense of insult and treachery lived in me like a kindling, though as yet smothered, coal. God knows I am not by nature vindictive; I would not hurt a man because I can no longer trust or like him; but neither my reason nor feelings are of the vacillating order—they are not of that sand-like sort where impressions, if soon made, are as soon effaced. Once convinced that my friend's disposition is incompatible with my own, once assured that he is indelibly stained with certain defects obnoxious to my principles, and I dissolve the connection. I did so with Edward. As to Pelet, the discovery was yet new. Should I act thus with him? Should I act thus with him? It was the question I placed before my mind as I stirred my cup of coffee with a half pistolet (we never had spoons), Pelet meantime being seated opposite, his pallid face looking as knowing and more haggard than usual, his blue eye turned, now sternly on his boys and ushers, and now graciously on me.
次日清晨，破晓时分我就起来了。我穿好衣服，手肘靠在衣橱上，站了半个小时，思索着该用什么方法才能打起精神，像往日一样，失眠已经让我精疲力竭——我没打算与珀莱先生吵架，责备他背信弃义，向他提出挑战或者搞出其他类似的恶作剧——后来我忽然想到一个好办法，早晨清爽宜人，我该到附近的浴场好好洗个澡，提提神。这个补救办法果真取得了理想的效果。我七点钟返回时，情绪已经稳定下来，也精神多了。珀莱先生进来吃早餐时，我也能带着一贯平静的表情招呼他了。我甚至热情地伸出手来，还用亲切的语气同他说话；他习惯于用这种语气跟我说话，尤其是最近，他讨好地称呼我为“我的孩子”时，就是这腔调。我外表丝毫没有表现出什么，但是内心仍旧有些气愤，尽管这种感觉已消减了许多。并不是我怀着报复之心——不是；然而那种遭遇侮辱和背叛的感觉依然在我心里，就像可引燃的煤火一样，虽然现在它被闷灭了。上帝知道我不是生来就具有报复性，不会因为不能再相信或者喜欢一个人而去伤害他；然而我的理智和情感都不会摇摆不定——它们不似沙子那样，迅速留下痕迹，还能同样迅速地抹去。一旦确信朋友的性情与我的性情难以相容，一旦知道他沾染了某些恶习，违背了我的原则，并难以改掉，我会结束这种朋友关系。对于爱德华，我就是这样做的。对于珀莱先生，我才刚刚发现这点。我应该也这么对他吗？我用半个奶油面包搅拌杯子里的咖啡（我们从不用勺子）时，脑子里思索的正是这个问题。与此同时，珀莱先生正坐在对面，苍白的脸看起来与平时一样睿智，但也比平时更加憔悴。他转动着一双蓝眼睛，时而严厉地看着他的男仆和使者，时而温和地看着我。
'Circumstances must guide me,' said I; and meeting Pelet's false glance and insinuating smile, I thanked Heaven that I had last night opened my window and read by the light of a full moon the true meaning of that guileful countenance. I felt half his master, because the reality of his nature was now known to me. Smile and flatter as he would, I saw his soul lurk behind his smile, and heard in every one of his smooth phrases a voice interpreting their treacherous import.
“命运必定在指引我。”我暗自说道；碰上珀莱先生虚假的瞥视和谄媚的微笑时，我感谢上天让我昨晚打开了窗户，借着满月的光，明白了那狡诈表情真正的意思。我觉得自己是他的半个主人，因为现在我知道他的本性。尽管他对人微笑、恭维，但我看到了他微笑背后隐藏的灵魂，从他的每一句花言巧语中听出一种声音，这声音让我领会到他不怀好意。
But Zoraéde Reuter? Of course her defection had cut me to the quick? That sting must have gone too deep for any consolations of philosophy to be available in curing its smart? Not at all. The night fever over, I looked about for balm to that wound also, and found some nearer home than at Gilead. Reason was my physician. She began by proving that the prize I had missed was of little value. She admitted that, physically, Zoraéde might have suited me, but affirmed that our souls were not in harmony, and that discord must have resulted from the union of her mind with mine. She then insisted on the suppression of all repining, and commanded me rather to rejoice that I had escaped a snare. Her medicament did me good. I felt its strengthening effect when I met the directress the next day. Its stringent operation on the nerves suffered no trembling, no faltering; it enabled me to face her with firmness, to pass her with case. She had held out her hand to me that I did not choose to see. She had greeted me with a charming smile; it fell on my heart like light on stone. I passed on to the estrade; she followed me. Her eye, fastened on my face, demanded of every feature the meaning of my changed and careless manner. 'I will give her an answer,' thought I; and meeting her gaze full, arresting, fixing her glance, I shot into her eyes from my own a look where there was no respect, no love, no tenderness, no gallantry, where the strictest analysis could detect nothing but scorn, hardihood, irony. I made her bear it and feel it. Her steady countenance did not change, but her colour rose, and she approached me as if fascinated. She stepped on to the estrade, and stood close by my side; she had nothing to say. I would not relieve her embarrassment, and negligently turned over the leaves of a book.
可是佐蕾德·勒泰小姐呢？当然，她的背叛深深地伤害了我的感情？这伤痛钻得太深，任何富于哲理的安慰都不能消除这伤痛？根本不是这样。夜里的高烧退去，我四处寻求治疗创伤的膏药，却找到了比吉利厄德更近的家园。理智就是我的医生。她开始向我证明我所失去的奖品毫无价值。她承认，肉体上佐蕾德可能会适合我。然而她断言我们的心灵无法和谐共处，那种不合必定源自两人思想的结合。然后她坚持要我停止一切抱怨和牢骚，命令我反而应该高兴起来，因为我躲过了一个陷阱。她的药对我很有益处。次日再遇到女校长，我感受到了这药的巩固疗效。它严格地地作用于我的神经，我没有颤抖，没有踌躇；它让我坚定地面对她，让我从容走过她身边。她向我伸出手，而我选择视而不见。她用妩媚的微笑向我打招呼，而我的心丝毫不为所动。我继续走，一直来到了讲台上，她跟在后面。她的眼睛盯住我的脸。她要从我面貌的每个细节中弄明白我态度变冷漠的真正含义。“我会给她一个答复。”我这样想着，眼睛与她注视的目光撞个正着，我捕捉并盯住她的目光。从我眼里投向她的目光没有尊重，没有爱意，没有温柔，没有殷勤；再缜密的分析也只能发现鄙夷、刚毅和嘲讽。我要让她感受并忍受这种目光。她沉稳的表情没有改变，但脸色变红了，她像着了迷似的走近我。她走上讲台，站在我身旁；她没什么可说的。我不会为她缓解这种尴尬局面，便若无其事地翻起一本书来。
'I hope you feel quite recovered today,' at last she said, in a low tone.
“希望你今天恢复得好多了。”最终她用低沉的语调说道。
'And I, mademoiselle, hope that you took no cold last night in consequence of your late walk in the garden.' 
“我呢，倒是希望小姐你昨晚没有因为大半夜的在花园里散步而着凉。”
Quick enough of comprehension, she understood me directly. Her face became a little blanched—a very little—but no muscle in her rather marked features moved; and, calm and self-possessed, she retired from the estrade, taking her seat quietly at a little distance, and occupying herself with netting a purse. I proceeded to give my lesson. It was a 'composition'—that is, I dictated certain general questions, of which the pupils were to compose the answers from memory access to books being forbidden.
她理解得很快，即刻明白了我的意思。她的脸色变得有些苍白——稍稍白了一点而已——然而她分明的五官上的肌肉没有为之动一下；她很镇静从容地从讲台上退下来，静静地在一个离讲台不远的座位上坐下，忙着织起钱包来。我接着讲课。这是一节“作文”课——也就是，我口述一些一般问句，学生根据记忆将答案组成一篇文章，禁止看书。
While Mdlles. Eulalie, Hortense, Caroline, etc., were pondering over the string of rather abstruse grammatical interrogatories I had propounded, I was at liberty to employ the vacant half-hour in further observing the directress herself. The green silk purse was progressing fast in her hands. Her eyes were bent upon it; her attitude, as she sat netting within two yards of me, was still yet guarded. In her whole person were expressed at once, and with equal clearness, vigilance, and repose a rare union. Looking at her, I was forced, as I had often been before, to offer her good sense, her wondrous self-control, the tribute of involuntary admiration. She had felt that I had withdrawn from her my esteem; she had seen contempt and coldness in my eye; and to her, who coveted the approbation of all around her, who thirsted after universal good opinion, such discovery must have been an acute wound. I had witnessed its effect in the momentary pallor of her cheek—cheek unused to vary; yet how quickly, by dint of self-control, had she recovered her composure! With what quiet dignity she now sat, almost at my side, sustained by her sound and vigorous sense—no trembling in her somewhat lengthened though shrewd upper lip, no coward shame on her austere forehead!
当厄拉利、奥尔唐斯、卡罗琳等人正思考着我所提出的一系列艰深的语法问题时，我可以随意地利用这半小时的闲暇时间，更进一步地观察女校长。她快速地织着手中的绿色丝织钱包。她的眼睛注视着钱包，态度平静而警觉，因为她坐着织钱包的地方离我只有两码距离。她整个人清楚地同时表现出一种安详和警觉，这两者的结合很罕见。就像从前一样，我这样看着她，就会不由自主地对她良好的判断力和令人惊叹的自制力感到钦佩。她觉察到我已经收回了对她的尊重，她从我的眼睛里看到了轻蔑和冷漠，对她这样一个贪求身边所有人的赞扬，渴望博得一致好评的人来讲，这发现一定是种严重的伤害。她脸颊上片刻的苍白让我目睹了这一发现的效果——她的面色是不易改变的，但她的自制力又多么迅速地让她恢复了沉着！她几乎就坐在我旁边，健全而又有力的心智支持着她，她此刻显得平静而又威严——稍长而机敏的上唇没有一丝颤抖，皱起的眉头上也看不出一点怯懦和愧疚。
'There is metal there,' I said as I gazed. 'Would that there were fire also, living ardour to make the steel glow! Then I could love her.' 
“她头脑里有块金属。”我盯着她，自言自语道，“要是那里面还有火，那高温会把钢铁也烧红！那么我可能会爱她。”
Presently I discovered that she knew I was watching her, for she stirred not, she lifted not her crafty eyelid.
不一会儿我发觉她知道我在看她，因为她身体动也不动，狡黠的眼皮抬也不抬。
She had glanced down from her netting to her small foot, peeping from the soft folds of her purple merino gown; thence her eye reverted to her hand, ivory white, with a bright garnet ring on the forefinger, and a light frill of lace round the wrist. With a scarcely perceptible movement she turned her head, causing her nut-brown curls to wave gracefully. In these slight signs I read that the wish of her heart, the design of her brain, was to lure back the game she had scared. A little incident gave her the opportunity of addressing me again.
她的眼睛顺着正在织的钱包上看下去，透过紫色精纺长袍柔和的褶皱，瞧了瞧她小巧的脚；之后她的目光又移回手上，她的手像象牙一般白皙，食指上戴着一枚闪亮的石榴红戒指，手腕上系着一条打着小褶的蕾丝花边饰带。她转过头来，动作简直让人难以察觉，栗色的发卷随之优雅地摆动。从这些细微的迹象中，我看懂了她内心的愿望和脑中的打算，她想把曾经吓走的猎物引诱回来。一件小事让她有了再次和我说话的机会。
While all was silence in the class—silence but for the rustling of copy-books and the travelling of pens over their pages—a leaf of the large folding-door, opening from the hall, unclosed, admitting a pupil who, after making a hasty obeisance, ensconced herself, with some appearance of trepidation, probably occasioned by her entering so late, in a vacant seat at the desk nearest the door. Being seated, she proceeded, still with an air of hurry and embarrassment, to open her cabas, to take out her books; and while I was waiting for her to look up, in order to make out her identity—for, short-sighted as I was, I had not recognized her at her entrance—Mdlle. Reuter, leaving her chair, approached the estrade.
当教室里正鸦雀无声时——安静得只有学生们翻练习本和钢笔书写在纸张上的沙沙声——通向门厅的大折叠门的一扇打开了，进来一个学生，匆匆行了个礼，坐在了离门最近的一个空位上。她神情有些不安，或许是因为来得太迟了。坐下来后，她依旧用一副慌张、尴尬的神情打开手提包，拿出课本。我在等她抬起脸来，好认出她是谁——因为我是近视，她进来时我没认出是谁。勒泰小姐离开座位，向讲台走来。
'Monsieur Creemsvort,' said she, in a whisper, for when the schoolrooms were silent the directness always moved with velvet tread, and spoke in the most subdued key, enforcing order and stillness fully as much by example as precept—‘Monsieur Creemsvort, that young person who has just entered wishes to have the advantage of taking lessons with you in English.
“克里姆斯沃先生，”她小声说道，教室里一片寂静，女校长总是把脚步放得很轻，说话的声音也很小，以身作则地极力维护着课堂秩序和教室安静，“克里姆斯沃先生，刚刚进来的那个年轻人希望可以在您的课上跟您学英语。
She is not a pupil of the house; she is, indeed, in one sense, a teacher, for she gives instruction in lace-mending and in little varieties of ornamental needlework. She very properly proposes to qualify herself for a higher department of education, and has asked permission to attend your lessons, in order to perfect her knowledge of English, in which language she has, I believe, already made some progress. Of course it is my wish to aid her in an effort so praiseworthy. You will permit her, then, to benefit by your instruction—n'est-ce pas, monsieur?' And Mdlle Reuter's eyes were raised to mine with a look at once naive, benign, and beseeching.
她不是这所学校的学生。实际上，从某种意义上来说，她是个老师，她指导学生们缝补花边，做一些装饰用的针线活之类的。她提出合理要求，想要提升自己以胜任更高层的教学工作，她还请求我们允许她上您的课，以提高自己的英文水平。我觉得她的英语已经有进步了。当然帮助她只是我的个人愿望。她付出的努力值得称道。您会允许的，而且您会帮助她，给她指导——是吧，先生？”勒泰小姐抬眼望着我，眼神一下子变得天真、温和、恳切。
I replied, 'Of course,' very laconically, almost abruptly.
“当然。”我用几乎生硬的语气简单答道。
'Another word,' she said, with softness. 'Mdlle. Henri has not received a regular education. Perhaps her natural talents are not of the highest order, but I can assure you of the excellence of her intentions, and even of the amiability of her disposition. Monsieur will then, I am sure, have the goodness to be considerate with her at first, and not expose her backwardness, her inevitable deficiencies, before the young ladies, who, in a sense, are her pupils. Will Monsieur Creemsvort favour me by attending to this hint?' 
“另外，”她轻柔地说道，“亨利小姐没有接受过正规教育。也许她的天分不是最高的，但我可以向你保证，她的动机良好，性格也随和。我确信先生刚开始会为她考虑周到，不会在这些年轻的女孩子面前暴露她的落后和一些难免的不足之处，因为从某种意义上说，这些女孩子都是她的学生。克里姆斯沃先生，您愿意帮我个忙，听取这个建议吗？”
I nodded. She continued with subdued earnestness,— 
我点了点头。她继续镇静而恳切地说道:
'Pardon me, monsieur, if I venture to add that what I have just said is of importance to the poor girl. She already experiences great difficulty in impressing these giddy young things with a due degree of deference for her authority, and should that difficulty he increased by new discoveries of her incapacity, she might find her position in my establishment too painful to be retained—a circumstance I should much regret for her sake, as she can ill afford to lose the profits of her occupation here.' 
“先生，请恕我冒昧地再加一句，我刚才所说的对于这位可怜的女孩子很重要。因为要赢得这些轻佻的年轻女孩子应有的尊重，她已经经历了很多困难了，要是她们再发现她能力不足，困难会再增加，她可能会觉得太痛苦而不愿在我们学校任职了——出现这种情况的话，我会为她感到十分遗憾，因为她几乎承受不起失去这里的工作。
Mdlle. Reuter possessed marvellous tact; but tact the most exquisite, unsupported by sincerity, will sometimes fail of its effect. Thus, on this occasion, the longer she preached about the necessity of being indulgent to the governess-pupil, the more impatient I felt as I listened. I discerned so clearly that while her professed motive was a wish to aid the dull though well-meaning Mdlle. Henri, her real one was no other than a design to impress me with an idea of her own exalted goodness and tender considerateness; so having again hastily nodded assent to her remarks, I obviated their renewal by suddenly demanding the compositions, in a sharp accent, and stepping from the estrade I proceeded to collect them.
勒泰小姐拥有过人的机智。但是再机智灵敏，没有真诚来帮衬，有时也达不到效果。因此，这一次，她越是长篇大论地讲宽厚对待这位教师学生的必要性，就越让我听得心烦。我分辨得很清楚。她对外声称自己的动机是想帮这位迟钝但是善良的亨利小姐，而真正的动机只不过是要给我留下一个崇高善良、温柔体贴的好印象，为了避免再次听到她喋喋不休，我又一次匆忙点头同意她的话，还突然间尖声要求同学们交上作文，然后我走下讲台，开始收作文。
As I passed the governess-pupil, I said to her,— 
我经过那位教师学生时，对她说：
'You have come in too late to receive a lesson today; try to be more punctual next time.' 
“你今天上课来得太晚了。下次尽量准时来上课。”
I was behind her, and could not read in her face the effect of my not very civil speech. Probably I should not have troubled myself to do so had I been full in front; but I observed that she immediately began to slip her books into her cabas again; and presently, after I had returned to the estrade, while I was arranging the mass of compositions, I heard the folding-door again open and close, and on looking up I perceived her place vacant. I thought to myself, "She will consider her first attempt at taking a lesson in English something of a failure"; and I wondered whether she had departed in the sulks, or whether stupidity had induced her to take my words too literally, or, finally, whether my irritable tone had wounded her feelings. The last notion I dismissed almost as soon as I had conceived it; for not having seen any appearance of sensitiveness in any human face since my arrival in Belgium, I had begun to regard it almost as a fabulous quality. Whether her physiognomy announced it I could not tell, for her speedy exit had allowed me no time to ascertain the circumstance. I had, indeed, on two or three previous occasions, caught a passing view of her (as I believe has been mentioned before), but I had never stopped to scrutinize either her face or person, and had but the most vague idea of her general appearance. Just as I had finished rolling up the compositions, the four o'clock bell rang. With my accustomed alertness in obeying that signal, I grasped my hat and evacuated the premises.
我在她后面，没能从她的脸上看出我那句不太客气的言语产生的效果。大概即使我在她面前也不会费工夫去观察她吧。然而我注意到她立刻把书又收回到提包里，我回到讲台上整理一大堆作文时，不久就听到折叠门又一次打开然后关上了，我抬眼看过去，只见她的位置已经空了。我心里想着：“她会觉得第一次尝试听用英文讲的课是一种失败。”我想知道她是不是赌气离开的，又或许她的愚昧让她把我的话太当真了，再者，我生气的口气有没有伤了她的心呢？这最后一种念头，我几乎一想到就把它排除了，因为自从我到了比利时，还没有从人们的脸上看到过这种敏感的表情。我几乎已经把敏感看作一种美好的品质了。她的脸上有没有显露出这种表情，我没法说，因为她离开得很迅速，我没有时间确定当时的情况。其实，以前有那么两三次，我匆匆看到过她（我相信我之前提到过），但是我从未停下脚步仔细观察她的脸或是人，对她的外貌我只是有一个很模糊的印象。我刚刚卷好作文，四点的钟声就响了。带着习惯性的警觉，我听从了钟声的指示，抓起帽子，离开了教室。
