XXV  

第二十五章  

The next morning Litvinov had only just come home from seeing the banker, with whom he had had another conversation on the playful instability of our exchange, and the best means of sending money abroad, when the hotel porter handed him a letter. He recognized Irina's handwriting, and without breaking the seal—a presentiment of evil, Heaven knows why, was astir in him—he went into his room. This was what he read (the letter was in French): 
第二天上午，李特维诺夫去见了一位银行家，又和他谈论了俄国汇率很有趣的不稳定，以及到国外花钱的最好办法。他才刚回旅馆，门房就递给他一封信。还没撕掉封印，他就认出那是伊琳娜的笔迹——天晓得那是为什么，一种不祥的预感涌上他的心头——他走进房间。这是信的内容（信是用法语写的）：
"My dear one, I have been thinking all night of your plan… I am not going to shuffle with you. You have been open with me, and I will be open with you; I cannot run away with you, I have not the strengthto do it. I feel how I am wronging you, my second sin is greater than the first, I despise myself, my cowardice. I cover myself with reproaches, but I cannot change myself. In vain I tell myself that I have destroyed your happiness, that you have the right now to regard me as a frivolous flirt, that I myself drew you on, that I have given you solemn promises… I am full of horror of hatred for myself, but I can't do otherwise, I can't, I can't. I don't want to justify myself, I won't tell you I was carried away myself... all that's of no importance; but I want to tell you, and to say it again, and yet again, I am yours, yours for ever, do with me as you will, when you will, free from all obligation, from all responsibility! I am yours... But run away, throw up everything… no! no! no! I besought you to save me, I hoped to wipe out everything, to burn up the past as in a fire... but I see there is no salvation for me; I see the poison has gone too deeply into me; I see one cannot breathe this atmosphere for years with impunity. I have long hesitated whether to write you this letter, I dread to think what decision you may come to, I trust only to your love for me. But I felt it would be dishonest on my part to hide the truth from you—especially as perhaps you have already begun to take the first steps for carrying out our project. Ah! it was lovely but impracticable. O my dear one, think me a weak, worthless woman, despise, but don't abandon me, don't abandon your Irina!... To leave this life I have not the courage, but live it without you I cannot either. We soon go back to Petersburg, come there, live there, we will find occupation for you, your labors in the past shall not be thrown away, you shall find good use for them... only live near me, only love me; such as I am, with all my weaknesses and my vices, and believe me, no heart will ever be so tenderly devoted to you as the heart of your Irina. Come soon to me, I shall not have an instant's peace until I see you.—Yours, yours, yours, I." 
“亲爱的，我整个晚上都在想您的计划……我不想对您遮遮掩掩。您一直是敞开心扉对我，我也要对您坦言相告。我不能和您私奔，我没有勇气这样做。我知道我有多么委屈您，我第二次的罪孽要比第一次深得多，我鄙视自己，鄙视自己的懦弱。我无数次地责怪自己，但是我还是不能改变自己。我对自己说，我毁掉了您的幸福，您现在有权利认为我是水性杨花的女人，是我先引诱您，还向您许下郑重的承诺，可这一切都说服不了我……我对自己充满了恐惧和厌恶，但是，我别无他法，只能这样做，只能这样。我并不想为自己辩解什么，也不想告诉您我已经失去了理智……这些都毫无意义。但是我想告诉您，再一次告诉您，我是您的，永远都是您的。您想怎样对我就怎样对我吧，不需要承担任何责任和义务！我是您的……但是和您私奔，抛弃一切……不行！不行！不行！我曾求您拯救我，我希望抹掉一切，将过去的一切付诸一炬……但是，我知道我自己已经无可救药了，我已经中毒太深。我知道多年来一直呼吸着这种空气，没有人能不被沾染。我犹豫了很久，不知道该不该给您写这封信，想到您会作出什么样的决定我就好害怕，只是，我相信您对我的爱。但是如果向您隐瞒真相，那我就太虚伪了——尤其是您或许已经开始为实现我们的计划，采取最初的行动了。啊！这个计划很美好，但是却不切实际。啊！亲爱的，把我看成一个懦弱无用的女人吧！鄙视我吧！但是，请您不要抛弃我，不要抛弃您的伊琳娜……我没有勇气离开当下的生活，但是没有您的话，我也活不下去啊。我们很快就回到彼得斯堡，您去那里，住在那里吧，我们会为您找一份工作，您过去所作的努力都将不会白费，它们将会得到更好的利用……只求您在我身边、爱着我。虽然我是那样软弱，那样邪恶，但是请相信我，没有谁的心会比您的伊琳娜的心更加温柔地忠诚于您。快来我身边吧，见不到您，我不会有片刻的安宁——属于您的，您的，您的伊。”
The blood beat like a sledge-hammer in Litvinov's head, then slowly and painfully sank to his heart, and was chill as a stone in it. He read through Irina's letter, and just as on that day at Moscow he fell in exhaustion on the sofa, and stayed there motionless. A dark abyss seemed suddenly to have opened on all sides of him, and he stared into this darkness in senseless despair. And so again, again deceit, no, worse than deceit, lying and baseness… And life shattered, everything torn up by its roots utterly, and the sole thing which he could cling to—the last prop in fragments, too! "Come after us to Petersburg," he repeated with a bitter inward laugh, "we will find you occupation… Find me a place as a head clerk, eh? and who are we? Here there's a hint of her past. Here we have the secret, hideous something I know nothing of, but which she has been trying to wipe out, to burn as in a fire. Here we have that world of intrigues, of secret relations, of shameful stories of Byelskys and Dolskys… And what a future, what a lovely part awaiting me! To live close to her, visit her, share with her the morbid melancholy of the lady of fashion who is sick and weary of the world, but can't live outside its circle, be the friend of the house of course, of his Excellency... until... until the whim changes and the plebeian lover loses his piquancy, and is replaced by that fat general or Mr. Finikov—that's possible and pleasant, and I dare say useful.... She talks of a good use for my talents?... but the other project's impracticable, impracticable..." In Litvinov's soul rose, like sudden gusts of wind before a storm, momentary impulses of fury.... Every expression in Irina's letter roused his indignation, her very assertions of her unchanging feelings affronted him. "She can't let it go like that," he cried at last, "I won't allow her to play with my life so mercilessly." 
李特维诺夫体内的血流就像一把大锤子敲击着他的头，接着缓缓地、痛苦地沉到心底，如一块冰凉的石头压在心头。读完了伊琳娜的信，他筋疲力竭地倒在沙发上，一动也不动，就跟那天在莫斯科的情形一样。忽然间一道黑暗的深渊似乎将他包围住，他只能绝望、麻木地凝视着这黑暗。又是欺骗，再一次的欺骗！不！比欺骗更加恶劣，这是谎言！这是无耻……生活已经支离破碎，一切都已经连根拔起，连他唯一的依靠——最后的救命稻草也都破碎了。“随我们到彼得斯堡来。”他重复着这句话，暗自苦笑着，“我们会为您找个工作……让我当一个银行职员领班吗，嗯？还有，“我们”是谁？从这里可以看出她的过去。这就是那不为我们所知的、罪恶的、她一直试图抹去或付之一炬的过去。这就是那种勾心斗角、偷鸡摸狗、男盗女娼的世界……等待我的是多么美好的未来，多么美好的角色啊！住在她身边，去看她，分担她这个虽然厌世却离不开这个社交圈的上流社会女人的病态的忧郁。而且，我还要成为她们一家的朋友，当然也要成为她丈夫的朋友……直到……直到兴致冷却，直到这个贫民情人失去了魅力，被那个胖将军或是菲尼科夫先生所取代——我敢说那是有可能的、令人愉快的、有用的。她不是说要充分利用我的才能吗？但是那个计划一点都不实际，不实际……”李特维诺夫内心顿时怒火中烧，就如同暴雨来临之前突如其来的狂风……伊琳娜信中的每句话都激起了他心中的愤怒，每个关于她感情不变的宣言都是在侮辱着他。“她不能这样做。”他最终呼喊道，“我不会让她这样无情地玩弄我的人生。”
Litvinov jumped up, snatched his hat. But what was he to do? Run to her? Answer her letter? He stopped short, and his hands fell.
李特维诺夫跳了起来，抓起自己的帽子。但是他又能做什么呢？跑去找她？给她回信？他停住了，垂下了双手。
"Yes, what was to be done?" 
“是啊，能做什么？”
Had he not himself put this fatal choice to her? It had not turned out as he had wished... there was that risk about every choice. She had changed her mind, it was true; she herself had declared at first that she would throw up everything and follow him; that was true, too; but she did not deny her guilt, she called herself a weak woman; she did not want to deceive him, she had been deceived in herself... What answer could be made to that? At any rate she was not hypocritical, she was not deceiving him... she was open, remorselessly open. There was nothing forced her to speak out, nothing to prevent her from soothing him with promises, putting things off, and keeping it all in uncertainty till her departure... till her departure with her husband for Italy? But she had ruined his life, ruined two lives… What of that?
不正是他自己要她作出这一重大选择的吗？但是结果却不如他愿……每一个选择都是存在风险的。她已变心，这是事实。一开始是她自己信誓旦旦地说要抛弃所有的一切，跟随着他。这也是事实。但是她没有否认自己的罪孽，她说自己是个软弱的女人；她不想欺骗他，而她却一直在自欺欺人……对于这些，又能如何回答呢？不管怎样，她不虚伪，她并没有欺骗他……她很坦诚，坦诚得无情。没有什么东西要逼她实话，也没有什么阻止她用诺言、拖延、躲躲闪闪来安慰他，一直到她离开……直至她和她的丈夫动身去意大利。但是她毁掉了他的一生，毁掉了两个人的人生……那又怎样呢？
But as regards Tatyana, she was not guilty; the guilt was his, his, Litvinov's alone, and he had no right to shake off the responsibility his own sin had laid with iron yoke upon him.... All this was so; but what was left him to do now?
说到塔吉娅娜，她是无辜的，有罪的是李特维诺夫他一个人，他无权逃避自己的罪行用铁链捆绑在他身上的责任……一切已成定局，现在他还能做什么？
Again he flung himself on the sofa and again in gloom, darkly, dimly, without trace, with devouring swiftness, the minutes raced past....
他又一次瘫坐在沙发上，时间再一次在灰暗阴郁中无声无息地飞逝……
"And why not obey her?" flashed through his brain. "She loves me, she is mine, and in our very yearning towards each other, in this passion, which after so many years has burst upon us, and forced its way out with such violence, is there not something inevitable, irresistible, like a law of nature? Live in Petersburg... and shall I be the first to be put in such a position? And how could we be in safety together?..." 
“为什么不听她的话呢？”这种念头闪过他的脑海，“她爱我，她是我的，我们是这么地渴望拥有对方，这么激情澎湃。这么多年之后，这种激情终于爆发了，并且呈迅猛之势散出。这当中是不是有什么无法避免、令人难以抵抗的类似自然规律的东西呢？如果我去彼得斯堡生活……那我会先面临这种处境吗？我们怎么可能安全地在一起呢吗？”
And he fell to musing, and Irina's shape, in the guise in which it was imprinted for ever in his late memories, softly rose before him… But not for long… He mastered himself, and with a fresh outburst of indignation drove away from him both those memories and that seductive image.
他陷入沉思。伊琳娜的身影，那个近期以来深深印在他脑海里的影子，现在又轻轻柔柔地浮现在他眼前……但是没过多久……他控制住了自己。一阵狂怒将那些记忆和那个诱人的身影驱散了。
"You give me to drink from that golden cup," he cried, "but there is poison in the draught, and your white wings are besmirched with mire… Away! Remain here with you after the way I... I drove away my betrothed... a deed of infamy, of infamy!” He wrung his hands with anguish, and another face with the stamp of suffering on its still features, with dumb reproach in its farewell eyes, rose from the depths… 
“您要我喝金樽里的美酒”，他呼喊道，“却在酒里下了毒，您洁白的翅膀沾满了污泥……没门！这么对待我还想让我和您一起留在这里……我赶走了我的未婚妻……这是多么无耻、多么无耻的行为啊！”他悲痛地搓着自己的双手，另外一张脸在内心深处慢慢浮现，那张脸依然带着痛苦的印记，别离的目光中带着无言的指责……
And for a long time Litvinov was in this agony still; for a long time, his tortured thought, like a man fever-stricken, tossed from side to side.... He grew calm at last; at last he came to a decision. From the very first instant he had a presentiment of this decision;... it had appeared to him at first like a distant, hardly perceptible point in the midst of the darkness and turmoil of his inward conflict; then it had begun to move nearer and nearer, till it ended by cutting with icy edge into his heart.
李特维诺夫久久地沉浸在痛苦之中。好长一段时间，他的思绪纠结万分，如同一个发着高烧的人在辗转反侧……他终于平静了下来。终于，他作出一个决定。她立刻对这一决定产生了一种预感……一开始，这个决定，如同他内心万分痛苦的黑色洪流之中的遥远的、隐约可见的一点，接着这一点越来越近，最后变成一把冰凉的利剑插在他的心里。
Litvinov once more dragged his box out of the corner, once more he packed all his things, without haste, even with a kind of stupid carefulness, rang for the waiter, paid his bill, and despatched to Irina a note in Russian to the following purport: 
李特维诺夫再次把他的行李箱拉出墙角，再一次整理好自己的所有行李，不慌不忙，甚至还仔细地有点蠢。他唤来侍者，结了账，并且让人给伊琳娜送了一封俄文信。下面是信的内容：
"I don't know whether you are doing me a greater wrong now than then; but I know this present blow is infinitely heavier… It is the end. You tell me, 'I cannot'; and I repeat to you, 'I cannot...' do what you want. I cannot and I don't want to. Don't answer me. You are not capable of giving me the only answer I would accept. I am going away to-morrow, early, by the first train. Good-by, may you be happy! We shall in all probability not see each other again." 
“我不知道您是不是打算时不时地给予我更重大的打击，但我知道此次打击无比沉重……一切都结束了。您对我说‘您做不到’，现在我再对您说一遍‘我做不到……’您想要我做的。我做不到，我也不愿意这样做。不要给我回信。您不能给我我唯一能够接受的答案。我明天一大早就搭第一班火车离开这里。再见！愿您幸福！我们很有可能永远不会再相见。”
Till night-time Litvinov did not leave his room; God knows whether he was expecting anything. About seven o'clock in the evening a lady in a black mantle with a veil on her face twice approached the steps of his hotel. Moving a little aside and gazing far away into the distance, she suddenly made a resolute gesture with her hand, and for the third time went towards the steps....
直到晚上，李特维诺夫才离开他的房间。天知道他是不是在期待着什么？晚上七点左右，一位身披黑色斗篷，脸上戴着黑色面纱的女人两次走近旅馆外的台阶。她往边上移了一点，朝着远方张望了一下，终于果断地打了个手势，第三次走向台阶……
"Where are you going, Irina Pavlovna?" she heard a voice utter with effort behind her.
“您要去哪里，伊琳娜·巴甫洛夫娜？”她的身后传来一个用力喊出的声音。
She turned with nervous swiftness… Potugin ran up to her.
她紧张地迅速回头一看……波图金正向她跑过来。
She stopped short, thought a moment, and fairly flung herself towards him, took his arm, and drew him away.
她停住了脚步，想了一下，简直是飞奔向他，然后拉住他的手，将他拉走了。
"Take me away, take me away," she repeated breathlessly.
“带我走吧！带我走吧！”她气喘吁吁地反复说道。
"What is it, Irina Pavlovna?" he muttered in bewilderment.
“怎么回事，伊琳娜·巴甫洛夫娜？”他疑惑不解地低语。
"Take me away," she reiterated with redoubled force, "if you don't want me to remain for ever... there." 
“带我走吧！”她重申了一遍，声音比之前更响亮了“如果您不想让我永远……留在这里的话。”
Potugin bent his head submissively, and hurriedly they went away together. The following morning early Litvinov was perfectly ready for his journey—into his room walked… Potugin.
波图金顺从地低下了头，接着他们就一起匆忙离开了。第二天一大早，李特维诺夫已经完全准备好上路——但是波图金走进了他的房间。
He went up to him in silence, and in silence shook his hand. Litvinov, too, said nothing. Both of them wore long faces, and both vainly tried to smile.
他默默地走向李特维诺夫，默默地与他握了握手。李特维诺夫也一样，一句话也没说。他们俩都拉长了脸，都想微笑，但都没笑出来。
"I came to wish you a good journey," Potugin brought out at last.
“我是过来给您送行的。”波图金终于说道。
"And how did you know I was going to-day?" asked Litvinov. Potugin looked on the floor around him... "I became aware of it... as you see. Our last conversation took in the end such a strange turn.... I did not want to part from you without expressing my sincere good feeling for you." 
“您怎么知道我今天要走？”李特维诺夫问道。波图金看了看周围的地板……“我意识到了……如您所见。我们最后的谈话居然有着这样怪异的结局……在和您分别之前，我想对您表达我对您真挚的好感。”
"You have good feeling for me now... when I am going away?" 
“看到我要走了……现在您倒是对我有好感了？”
Potugin looked mournfully at Litvinov. "Ah, Grigory Mihalitch, Grigory Mihalitch," he began with a short sigh, "it's no time for that with us now, no time for delicacy or fencing. You don't, so far as I have been able to perceive, take much interest in our national literature, and so, perhaps, you have no clear conception of Vaska Buslaev?" 
波图金悲伤地看着李特维诺夫。“啊，格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇，格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇。”他短暂地叹了一口气说道，“我们现在已经没有时间说这些了，没时间计较细节，也没有时间较量了。据我所了解，您对我国的文学并不十分感兴趣，因此您对瓦西卡·布斯拉耶夫或许也不是很了解吧？”
"Of whom?" 
“谁？”
"Of Vaska Buslaev, the hero of Novgorod... in Kirsch-Danilov's collection." 
“瓦西卡·布斯拉耶夫，基尔沙·达尼洛夫的文集里的诺夫格特英雄。”
"What Buslaev?" said Litvinov, somewhat puzzled by the unexpected turn of the conversation. "I don't know." 
“什么布斯拉耶夫？”李特维诺夫有点被这个突如其来的话题给弄糊涂了。“我不知道。”
"Well, never mind. I only wanted to draw your attention to something. Vaska Buslaev, after he had taken away his Novgorodians on a pilgrimage to Jerusalem, and there, to their horror, bathed all naked in the holy river Jordan, for he believed not 'in omen nor in dream, nor in the flight of birds,' this logical Vaska Buslaev climbed up Mount Tabor, and on the top of this mountain there lies a great stone, over which men of every kind have tried in vain to jump.
“嗯，没关系。我只是想请您注意一件事。瓦西卡·布斯拉耶夫在带领一批诺夫格拉特朝圣者到达耶路撒冷之后，令人瞠目结舌的是，他居然一丝不挂在圣河——约旦河里洗澡。因为他不相信‘寓言、梦境或者飞鸟能预测凶吉。’这位理智的瓦西卡·布斯拉耶夫又爬上了伏法山，山顶上有一块巨大的石头。各种各样的人都试图跳过这块巨石，但是谁都没有成功。
Vaska too ventured to try his luck. And he chanced upon a dead head, a human skull in his road; he kicked it away with his foot. So the skull said to him; "Why do you kick me? I knew how to live, and I know how to roll in the dust—and it will be the same with you." And in fact, Vaska jumps over the stone, and he did quite clear it, but he caught his heel and broke his skull. And in this place, I must by the way observe that it wouldn't be amiss for our friends, the Slavophils, who are so fond of kicking dead heads and decaying nationalities underfoot to ponder over that legend." 
瓦西卡也跃跃欲试，想碰碰运气。他在路上恰巧看到了一个死人的头，是人的头盖骨。他一脚把它踢开了。于是这个骷髅头对他说：‘您为什么要踢我？我曾经知道如何生活，现在也知道如何安睡在这尘土之中——您的命运将和我的一样。’事实上，瓦西卡跳过了那块巨石，也确实清除了它，但是他的脚后跟被绊住了，摔死了。讲到这里，我必须顺便说一下，我们的朋友，这些如此热衷于踢脚下的骷髅以及堕落的民族的斯拉夫人如果能仔细想想这个传说，是不会错的。”
"But what does all that mean?" Litvinov interposed impatiently at last. "Excuse me, it's time for me ...." 
“您说这些到底是什么意思呢？”李特维诺夫终于不耐烦地打断了他。“真抱歉，我是时候……”
"Why, this," answered Potugin, and his eyes beamed with such affectionate warmth as Litvinov had not even expected of him, "this, that you do not spurn a dead human head, and for your goodness, perhaps you may succeed in leaping over the fatal stone. I won't keep you any longer, only let me embrace you at parting." 
“啊，我说这些……”波图金回答，眼睛里闪烁着的光芒如此亲切、温暖，李特维诺夫怎么也没料想到。“我说这些，是希望您不要去踢死人的头颅，是为了您好，或许能成功跳过那块要命的石头。我不想再耽误您了，只是离别在即，让我抱抱您吧。”
"I'm not going to try to leap over it, even," Litvinov declared, kissing Potugin three times, and the bitter sensations filling his soul were replaced for an instant by pity for the poor, lonely creature.
“我甚至都不准备跳过那块石头。”李特维诺夫说道，亲吻了波图金三下。一时间，他满腔的悲痛化成了对面前这个孤独、可怜虫的同情。
"But I must go, I must go..." he moved about the room. "Can I carry anything for you?" Potugin proffered his services.
“我得走了，我得走了……”他在房间里走了起来。“我能帮您提点什么东西吗？”波图金主动提供帮助。
"No, thank you, don't trouble, I can manage..." 
“不用了，谢谢。不必麻烦了，我能行……”
He put on his cap, took up his bag. "So you say," he queried, stopping in the doorway, "you have seen her?" 
他戴上帽子，拎起袋子。“您是说，”他在门口停了下来，问道，“您已经见过她了？”
"Yes, I've seen her." 
“是的，我见过她了。”
"Well... tell me about her." 
“那……告诉我有关她的情况吧。”
Potugin was silent a moment. "She expected you yesterday... and to-day she will expect you." 
波图金沉默了一会儿。“她昨天在等您……今天还会等您。”
"Ah! Well, tell her… No, there's no need, no need of anything. Good-by... Good-by!” 
“啊！告诉她……不要这样，没有必要，没有任何必要。再见……再见！”
"Good-by, Grigory Mihalitch… Let me say one word more to you. You still have time to listen to me; there's more than half an hour before the train starts. You are returning to Russia… There you will... in time... get to work… Allow an old chatterbox—for, alas, I am a chatterbox, and nothing more—to give you advice for your journey. Every time it is your lot to undertake any piece of work, ask yourself: Are you serving the cause of civilization, in the true and strict sense of the word; are you promoting one of the ideals of civilization; have your labors that educating, Europeanizing character which alone is beneficial and profitable in our day among us? If it is so, go boldly forward, you are on the right path, and your work is a blessing! Thank God for it! You are not alone now. You will not be a 'sower in the desert'; there are plenty of workers... pioneers... even among us now… But you have no ears for this now. Good-by, don't forget me!” Litvinov descended the staircase at a run, flung himself into a carriage, and drove to the station, not once looking round at the town where so much of his personal life was left behind. He abandoned himself, as it were, to the tide; it snatched him up and bore him along, and he firmly resolved not to struggle against it... all other exercise of independent will he renounced.
“再见！格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇……让我再对您说一句话。您还有时间听我把话说完，火车还要过半个小时才开呢。您将回到俄国……您会准时到那里……开始您的工作……让我这个喜欢多嘴的老家伙——哎，我就是喜欢多嘴而已——给您一点临别的忠言吧。每次当您开始事业时，都问问您自己：您是否在为真正、严格意义上的文明服务；您是否在推进某种文明理念；您的劳动是否能教育人，能塑造那种现今有益于我们的欧洲人的品格？如果真是这样，您就勇往直前吧！您的道路是正确的，您的事业是造福于人民的！感谢上帝！这时候您不会是独自一人。您不会是‘沙漠中的播种者’，就连我们当中……都还有很多同事……很多开拓者……。但是现在您没时间听我说这些了。再见！不要忘记我！”李特维诺夫跑下楼梯，钻进一辆马车，驶往火车站，一次也不曾回头看这个留下了他这么多个人生活的城镇。他仿佛已听任自己随波逐流。潮水抓住他将他带走，他决心不去反抗……放弃了所有的独立意志。
He was just taking his seat in the railway carriage.
他刚进车厢坐下来。
"Grigory Mihalitch... Grigory..." he heard a supplicating whisper behind him.
“格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇……格里高利……”他听到有人轻声恳求他。
He started... Could it be Irina? Yes; it was she. Wrapped in her maid's shawl, a traveling hat on her disheveled hair, she was standing on the platform, and gazing at him with worn and weary eyes.
他吓了一跳……会是伊琳娜吗？是的，就是她。她披着披肩，凌乱的头发上戴着一顶旅行帽。她正站在站台上，疲惫地凝视着李特维诺夫。
"Come back, come back, I have come for you," those eyes were saying. And what, what were they not promising? She did not move, she had not power to add a word; everything about her, even the disorder of her dress, everything seemed entreating forgiveness… 
“回来吧！回来吧！我来找您了！”那双眼睛似乎在哀求着。有什么是这双眼睛所不能许诺的呢？她一动不动，连说话的力气都没有了。有关她的一切，就连那凌乱的衣服，所有的一切似乎都在恳求他原谅……
Litvinov was almost beaten, scarcely could he keep from rushing to her... But the tide to which he had surrendered himself reasserted itself… He jumped into the carriage, and turning round, he motioned Irina to a place beside him. She understood him. There was still time. One step, one movement, and two lives made one for ever would have been hurried away into the uncertain distance.... While she wavered, a loud whistle sounded and the train moved off.
李特维诺夫简直就被打败了，差点就冲向她了……但是那已经将他降服了的潮水将他拉了回去……他跳进车厢，回头，对伊琳娜指了指自己身边的一个座位。她明白了他的意思。还有时间。只要跨出一步，一个动作，两个永远结合在一起的生命就能逃往那未知的远方了……正当她踌躇之时，响亮的汽笛声响起，火车开走了。
Litvinov sank back, while Irina moved staggering to a seat, and fell on it, to the immense astonishment of a supernumerary diplomatic official who chanced to be lounging about the railway station. He was slightly acquainted with Irina, and greatly admired her, and seeing that she lay as though overcome by faintness, he imagined that she had "une attaque de nerfs," and therefore deemed it his duty, the duty d'un galant chevalier, to go to her assistance. But his astonishment assumed far greater proportions when, at the first word addressed to her, she suddenly got up, repulsed his proffered arm, and hurrying out into the street, had in a few instants vanished in the milky vapor of fog, so characteristic of the climate of the Black Forest in the early days of autumn.
李特维诺夫倒在椅子上，伊琳娜则跌跌撞撞地走近椅子，坐了上去，令一位碰巧坐在那里候车的临时外交官大吃一惊。他跟伊琳娜并不是很熟，但是却非常地爱慕她。看到她躺在那里，像是昏倒了一样，他以为她“神经性昏厥”了，因此觉得有责任帮助他，这是勇敢骑士的责任。但是令他更为意外的是，他刚开口和她说话，她突然就站了起来，推开他的援助之手，慌忙地冲到了大街上，很快消失在初秋时节黑森林区特有的乳白色雾霭之中。
