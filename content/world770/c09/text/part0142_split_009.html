CHAPTER X  

第十章  

Next day the morning hours seemed to pass very slowly at M. Pelet's. I wanted the afternoon to come, that I might go again to the neighbouring pensionnat and give my first lesson within its pleasant precincts, for pleasant they appeared to me. At noon the hour of recreation arrived; at one o'clock we had lunch. This got on the time; and at last St. Gudule's deep bell, tolling slowly two, marked the moment for which I had been waiting.
第二天上午我在珀莱先生家度过，时间似乎过得很慢。我恨不得快点到下午，那样我就能再去隔壁的寄宿学校，在那环境宜人的地方给学生上第一堂课，在我眼里，那地方真是太可爱了。中午时，午休时间到了；我们一点钟用餐。很快就到了上课的时间。终于，圣居迪勒教堂深沉的大钟缓缓敲响了下午两点的钟声，预示着我满心期待的时刻来临了。
At the foot of the narrow back stairs that descended from my room I met M. Pelet.
就在那窄窄的后楼梯下面，我碰到了珀莱先生。
'Comme vous avez l'air rayonnant!' said he. 'Je ne vous ai jamais vu aussi gai. Que s'est-il donc passé?' 
“你今天看上去真是神采奕奕啊！”他说，“我还从没见你这么开心过呢。什么事那么开心呢？”
'Apparemment que j'aime les changements,' replied I.
“还用说嘛，我太喜欢这些改变了。”我回答说。
'Ah! je comprends—c'est cela—soyez sage seulement. Vous ètes bien jeune—trop jeune pour le role que vous allez jouer; il faut prendre garde—savez-vous?' 
“哦！明白了——原来是这样啊——不过我还是希望你明智一点儿。你年纪还小——还很年轻，相对你即将承担的责任，你得谨慎一点儿——明白吗？”
'Mais quel danger y a-t-il?' 
“可是有什么该小心的呢？”
'Je n'en sais rien—ne vous laissez pas aller à de vives impressions—violà tout.' 
“我也说不好——不要给人留下深刻印象——就是这样吧。”
I laughed. A sentiment of exquisite pleasure played over my nerves at the thought that "vives impressions" were likely to be created. It was the deadness, the sameness of life's daily ongoings that had hitherto been my bane. My blouse-clad élèves in the boys' seminary never stirred in me any "vives impressions" , except it might be occasionally some of anger. I broke from M. Pelet, and as I strode down the passage he followed me with one of his laughs—a very French, rakish, mocking sound.
我笑了。一想到我可能会创造深刻的印象，一阵细微的喜悦感就会涌上心头。而正是那死气沉沉、单调乏味的日常琐事一直折磨着我。男子学院里那些身穿宽大外套的孩子从未在我心里激起过什么深刻印象，除了偶尔在我发怒的时候。我从珀莱先生面前冲过去，沿着走廊阔步走去，他的笑声随之从我身后传来——一种纯正的法式的、放荡的、嘲讽的笑声。
Again I stood at the neighbouring door, and soon was readmitted into the cheerful passage with its clear dove-colour imitation marble walls. I followed the portress, and descending a step, and making a turn, I found myself in a sort of corridor. A side door opened; Mdlle. Reuter's little figure, as graceful as it was plump, appeared. I could now see her dress in full daylight. A neat, simple mousseline-laine gown fitted her compact round shape to perfection; delicate little collar and manchettes of lace, trim Parisian brodequins showed her neck, wrists, and feet to complete advantage; but how grave was her face as she came suddenly upon me! Solicitude and business were in her eye, on her forehead. She looked almost stern. Her 'Bonjour, monsieur' was quite polite, but so orderly, so commonplace, it spread directly a cool, damp towel over my vives impressions. The servant turned back when her mistress appeared, and I walked slowly along the corridor, side by side with Mdlle. Reuter.
我又一次站立在隔壁的门边，很快就又被请上那条让人心情愉快的通道，这通道四周是鸽灰色的仿大理石墙壁。我随着女仆走下台阶，转了个弯，发觉自己来到了一个类似走廊的地方。一个侧门开了，勒泰小姐那娇小丰满却不失优雅的身姿出现了。因为是大白天，所以我现在能看清她的衣着打扮。她穿着一件简单朴素的真丝长袍，长袍剪裁合适，将她那结实圆润的身形衬托得近乎完美；精致的小领子、绣着蕾丝的袖口和干净的巴黎风格短靴，将她的脖颈、手腕和双脚展现得美丽无比；可当她突然向我走近时，表情又是那么庄重！她的眼睛里和前额上都写着焦虑和忙碌。她看起来简直是严苛。“早安，先生。”她相当客气地问候我，却如此漠然、平淡，给我的深刻印象迎面甩来一条又冷又湿的毛巾。主人露面之后，女仆就转身离去了，我和勒泰小姐于走廊上并肩慢慢走着。
'Monsieur will give a lesson in the first class today,' said she; 'dictation or reading will perhaps be the best thing to begin with, for those are the easiest forms of communicating instruction in a foreign language; and at the first a master naturally feels a little unsettled.' 
“先生今天要给一班授课，”她说，“教新课之前最好先来个听写或阅读，毕竟在外语教学中那些是最简单的形式；当然，第一次登台讲课感到紧张是在所难免的。”
She was quite right, as I had found from experience. It only remained for me to acquiesce. We proceeded now in silence. The corridor terminated in a hall, large, lofty, and square. A glass door on one side showed within a long narrow refectory, with tables, an armoire, and two lamps. It was empty. Large glass doors in front opened on the playground and garden; a broad staircase ascended spirally on the opposite side; the remaining wall showed a pair of great folding doors, now closed, and admitting, doubtless, to the classes.
依我的经验来看，她说得很对。我只好沉默以示赞同。我们往前走着，都默默不语。走廊的尽头是一个又高又大的方形的大厅。从一边的玻璃门看进去，会发现里面有一个长长的、窄窄的餐厅，有几张桌子、一个橱柜和两盏灯。大厅什么也没有。正面那几扇大玻璃门向运动场和花园打开着；对面是一条宽大而弯曲盘旋的楼梯；大厅的其他两面墙上各有一扇巨大的折叠门，此时都紧闭着，毋庸置疑，通过那对折叠门就可以进到教室。
Mdlle. Reuter turned her eye laterally on me, to ascertain, probably, whether I was collected enough to be ushered into her sanctum sanctorum. I suppose she judged me to be in a tolerable state of self-government, for she opened the door, and I followed her through. A rustling sound of uprising greeted our entrance. Without looking to the right or left, I walked straight up the lane between two sets of benches and desks, and took possession of the empty chair and isolated desk raised on an estrade, of one step high, so as to command one division, the other division being under the surveillance of a maétresse similarly elevated. At the back of the estrade, and attached to a movable partition dividing this schoolroom from another beyond, was a large tableau of wood painted black and varnished; a thick crayon of white chalk lay on my desk, for the convenience of elucidating any grammatical or verbal obscurity which might occur in my lessons, by writing it upon the tableau; a wet sponge appeared beside the chalk, to enable me to efface the marks when they had served the purpose intended.
勒泰小姐用余光瞥了我一下，或许是想确认我是否足够冷静，好被领进她那神秘的殿堂。我想，她应该觉得我的神情还算镇静，因为她已经推开了那扇门，我跟在她后面走了进去。迎接我们的是一阵沙沙的起立声。我并未左顾右盼，径直沿着两排桌凳之间的过道走上讲台，讲台有一个台阶那么高，上面有张孤零零的讲桌，我便坐在桌旁的空椅子上，这样就可以监视教室的一部分了，而另外一部分由坐在差不多高度的讲台上的女教师监视。讲台后面有一块可移动的隔板，可将这个教室和那边的教室隔开，隔板上连着一块漆得乌黑发亮的木制大黑板；讲桌上放着一支白色粗粉笔，以便老师将课文中出现的语法或语言难点写在黑板上进行讲解。粉笔旁边有一块湿海绵，可以擦去黑板上已经讲解过的字迹。
I carefully and deliberately made these observations before allowing myself to take one glance at the benches before me. Having handled the crayon, looked back at the tableau, fingered the sponge, in order to ascertain that it was in a right state of moisture, I found myself cool enough to admit of looking calmly up and gazing deliberately round me.
我仔细并特意地做了这些观察才扫视了一遍坐在我眼前这些学生。我拿了支粉笔，回头看了下黑板，又摸了摸那块海绵，以确认湿度是否合适，此时，我发觉自己足可冷静地抬起头，从容地环顾四周。
And first I observd that Mdlle. Reuter had already glided away; she was nowhere visible. A maîtresse, or teacher—the one who occupied the corresponding estrade to my own—alone remained to keep guard over me. She was a little in the shade, and with my short sight I could only see that she was of a thin bony figure and rather tallowy complexion, and that her attitude, as she sat, partook equally of listlessness and affectation.
我先是发现勒泰小姐已然溜走了，到处都见不到她的身影。只有一位女管事，或者是教师——就是坐在和我同样高度台子上的那位——独自留在这里观察我的表现。她呆在一个光线稍微昏暗的地方，因为我眼睛近视，所以只能看见她那瘦骨嶙峋的身材和苍白的脸色。她坐着时，完全是一副无精打采、装腔作势的神态。
More obvious, more prominent, shone on by the full light of the large window, were the occupants of the benches just before me, of whom some were girls of fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, some young women from eighteen (as it appeared to me) up to twenty. The most modest attire, the simplest fashion of wearing the hair, were apparent in all; and good features, ruddy, blooming complexions, large and brilliant eyes, forms full, even to solidity, seemed to abound. I did not bear the first view like a stoic. I was dazzled, my eyes fell, and in a voice somewhat too low I murmured,— 
明亮的光线从宽大的玻璃窗射进来，我觉得更清晰、更显眼的还是坐在我眼前的学生们，她们中有些是十四、十五和十六岁的女孩子，有些则是十八（在我看来）到二十岁的少女。她们全都穿着最朴素的衣服，梳着最简单的发型；并且一个个都容貌姣好，脸色红润，笑逐颜开，大眼睛亮晶晶的，丰满的身材甚至显得有点结实。刚看过去我就忍受不了，就像禁欲主义者那样。我头昏目眩，眉眼低垂，用极低的声音嘟哝道——
'Prenez vos cahiers de dictée, mesdemoiselles.' 
“小姐们，请把听写本拿出来。”
Not so had I bid the boys at Pelet's take their reading-books. A rustle followed, and an opening of desks; behind the lifted lids which momentarily screened the heads bent down to search for exercise-books, I heard tittering and whispers.
我命令珀莱先生的学生们把阅读课本拿出来时，语气可不这样。一阵沙沙声和拉开课桌的声音紧随其后，被掀起的课桌盖暂时挡住了一个个低下去掏练习本的脑袋，这时，我听见了她们的嘻笑声和低语声。
'Eulalie, je suis prête à pâmer de rire,' observed one.
“厄拉利，我快笑抽了。”一个学生说。
'Comme il a rougi en parlant!' 
“他讲话的时候脸可真红啊！”
'Oui, c'est un veritable blanc-bec.' 
“是啊，这真是个缺乏经验的人。”
'Tais-toi, Hortense; il nous écoute.' 
“别说了，奥尔唐斯，他听见了。”
And now the lids sank and the heads reappeared. I had marked three, the whisperers, and I did not scruple to take a very steady look at them as they emerged from their temporary eclipse. It is astonishing what case and courage their little phrases of flippancy had given me. The idea by which I had been awed was that the youthful beings before me, with their dark nun-like robes and softly-braided hair, were a kind of half-angels. The light titter, the giddy whisper, had already in some measure relieved my mind of that fond and oppressive fancy.
这时，课桌盖沉了下去，脑袋们重现了出来。我注意到三个窃窃私语的学生。在她们的脸庞消失了一会儿又重新出现的时候，我毫不顾忌地盯着她们看。她们几句不痛不痒的尖酸私语竟让我如此气愤，甚至让我鼓起勇气决心要治服她们。这太令人惊奇了。我觉得坐在面前的这些穿着黑长袍、梳着蓬松辫子像修女一般的年轻女孩们竟有几分天使的模样，连我自己也被这样的想法吓到了。刚才那低低的嗤笑和轻浮的私语，使我的头脑从那让人喜欢又感到压迫的幻想中得到了几分释放。
The three I allude to were just in front, within half a yard of my estrade, and were among the most womanly-looking present. Their names I knew afterwards, and may as well mention now; they were Eulalie, Hortense, Caroline. Eulalie was tall, and very finely shaped. She was fair, and her features were those of a Low Country Madonna. Many a figure de Vierge have I seen in Dutch pictures exactly resembling hers. There were no angles in her shape or in her face—all was curve and roundness; neither thought, sentiment, nor passion disturbed by line or flush the equality of her pale, clear skin. Her noble bust heaved with her regular breathing, her eyes moved a little. By these evidences of life alone could I have distinguished her from some large handsome figure moulded in wax. Hortense was of middle size, and stout. Her form was ungraceful, her face striking, more alive and brilliant than Eulalie's; her hair was dark brown, her complexion richly coloured. there were frolic and mischief in her eye. Consistency and good sense she might possess, but none of her features betokened those qualities.
我说的那三个学生就坐在前排，离讲台不到半码远的位置，她们可算是班上最具女性美的几位了。我是后来才知道她们的名字的，现在不妨提一下；她们是厄拉利、奥尔唐斯和卡罗琳。厄拉利是一个身材高挑，身形曼妙的女生。肌白如雪，容貌堪比这个低地国家的圣母玛利亚。跟我见过的荷兰画家笔下的许多少女画像十分相似。她的身材或脸上没有一丝棱角——非常圆润光滑；不管思考也好，哀伤也罢，亦或是生气和害羞，她的肤色总是那样白皙，那样剔透。丰满的胸脯随着均匀的呼吸高低起伏着，眼睛微微转动。只凭着这些生命的迹象，我就能分辨出她们并不是什么漂亮的人物蜡像，而是真真实实的人。奥尔唐斯中等个子，身材肥胖。她体形并不优雅，但脸庞却很迷人，比厄拉利的还要富有朝气和活力；一头深褐色的头发，脸上的表情也复杂多样。她的眼眼里透着一钟古灵精怪的淘气样。她或许还很坚毅、理智，但这些从她的相貌上完全看不出来。
Caroline was little, though evidently full-grown. Raven-black hair, very dark eyes, absolutely regular features, with a colourless olive complexion, clear as to the face and sallow about the neck, formed in her that assemblage of points whose union many persons regard as the perfection of beauty. How, with the tintless pallor of her skin and the classic straightness of her lineaments, she managed to look sensual, I don't know. I think her lips and eyes contrived the affair between them, and the result left no uncertainty on the beholder's mind. She was sensual now, and in ten years' time she would be coarse, promise plain was written in her face of much future folly.
卡罗琳显然已经发育成熟，但是身材却有点娇小。她的头发乌黑亮丽，双眼深邃幽黑，神情端庄无比，脸色是苍白的橄榄色，肌肤晶莹剔透，颈部周围也洁白如洗。在很多人眼里，这一切组合成了一个完美无瑕的美人。真不知道，她那苍白的皮肤和端庄的神情，怎么能引起人们感官欲望。我觉得那种感官欲望定然来自她的嘴唇和眼睛，所有见过她的人都无法否认她的魅力。现在她就已经如此性感，十年以后，肯定变得粗俗不堪，将来也一定会做不少傻事，这一点很明显地写在她脸上。
If I looked at these girls with little scruple, they looked at me with still less. Eulalie raised her unmoved eye to mine, and seemed to expect, passively but securely, an impromptu tribute to her majestic charms. Hortense regarded me boldly, and giggled at the same time, while she said, with an air of impudent freedom,— 
如果说在看这些女孩们的时候我的眼神是毫无顾忌的，那么，她们的眼神可以说是近乎放肆了。厄拉利目不转睛地盯着我，看似被动，实是自信地等着我为她那高贵的魅力即兴赞叹一番。奥尔唐斯一边放肆地注视着我，一边咯咯地笑着。突然，她莽撞地对我说：
'Dictez-nous quelque chose de facile pour commencer, monsieur.' 
“先生，先给我们做点简单的听写吧。”
Caroline shook her loose ringlets of abundant but somewhat coarse hair over her rolling black eyes. parting her lips, as full as those of a hot-blooded Maroon, she showed her- well-set teeth sparkling between them, and treated me at the same time to a smile de sa faéon. Beautiful as Pauline Borghese, she looked at the moment scarcely purer than Lucréce de Borgia. Caroline was of noble family. I heard her lady-mother's character afterwards, and then I ceased to wonder at the precocious accomplishments of the daughter. These three, I at once saw, deemed themselves the queens of the school, and conceived that by their splendour they threw all the rest into the shade. In less than five minutes they had thus revealed to me their characters, and in less than five minutes I had buckled on a breast-plate of steely indifference, and let down a visor of impassable austerity.
卡罗琳甩了甩头，将遮在她那双黝黑滚圆的眼睛上的蓬松浓密又略显粗糙的卷发甩到一边。她的嘴巴张得像一个逃亡的黑奴的嘴巴那么大，两排整齐、洁白的牙齿显露无遗；接着，她向我投来了一个含情脉脉的微笑。就如同波利娜·伯吉斯那样美，却不如柳克丽丝·德波吉亚纯洁。卡罗琳出生贵族家庭。在我后来听闻她那贵族母亲的个性之后，我也就丝毫不讶异于她的早熟了。从我看到她们的第一眼开始，我就知道，这三位小姐肯定认为自己是学校的皇后，自己的魅力定能使其他同学黯然无光。还不到五分钟，她们就将自己的性格暴露在我眼前，在不到五分钟的时间里，我就横下心来决定用强硬的手腕让她们屈服于我。
'Take your pens and commence writing,' said I, in as dry and trite a voice as if I had been addressing only Jules Vanderkelkov and Co.
“拿起笔来开始听写，”我用一种冷漠而平淡的语气说道，就像跟朱尔·范德凯科夫那伙人讲话一样。
The dictée now commenced. My three belles interrupted me perpetually with little silly questions and uncalled-for remarks, to some of which I made no answer, and to others replied very quietly and briefly.
听写开始了。那三位美丽的女孩不停地打断我，尽是问些愚蠢的问题或说些没用的话；对有些问题，我默而不答，而另一些问题，也只是小声简洁地应付过去。
'Comment dit-on point et virgule en anglais, monsieur?' 
“先生，英文里面有个句号加一个逗号用英语怎么说来着？”
'Semi-colon, mademoiselle.' 
“是分号，小姐。”
'Semi-collong? Ah, comme c'est drôle!' (Giggle.) 
“分号?啊，真是太好笑了！”（咯咯的笑声。）
'J'ai une si mauvaise plume—impossible d'écrire!' 
“我的笔真是太难用——简直没办法写了！”
'Mais, monsieur—je ne sais pas suivre—vous allez si vite.' 
“可是，先生——我跟不上您的节奏——您念得太快了。”
'Je n'ai rien compris, moi!' 
“我怎么一点也没听懂！”
Here a general murmur arose, and the teacher, opening her lips for the first time, ejaculated,— 
这时班里响起一阵嗡嗡声，那位教师第一次开了口，喊道——
'Silence, mesdemoiselles!' 
“小姐们，请安静！”
No silence followed; on the contrary, the three ladies in front began to talk more loudly.
安静并没有到来。反而，前排的那三位小姐说得更大声了。
'C'est si difficile, l'anglais!' 
“学英语真是太难了！”
'Je déteste la dictée.' 
“我讨厌听写。”
'Quel ennui d'écrire quelque chose quc l'on ne comprend pas!' 
“净是听写这些不懂的东西，真讨厌！”
Some of those behind laughed. A degree of confusion began to pervade the class. It was necessary to take prompt measures.
坐在后排的几名学生笑了起来。混乱开始弥漫于教室。现在我必须采取些及时的措施了。
'Donnez-moi votre cahier,' said I to Eulalie in an abrupt tone; and bending over, I took it before she had time to give it.
“把练习本拿给我，”我对厄拉利说道，语气相当严厉，在她还没有来得及递给我前，我就俯下身抓起她的本子。
'Et vous, mademoiselle; donnez-moi le votre,' continued I, more mildly, addressing a little pale, plain-looking girl, who sat in the first row of the other division, and whom I had remarked as being at once the ugliest and the most attentive in the room. She rose up, walked over to me, and delivered her book with a grave, modest curtsy. I glanced over the two dictations. Eulalie's was slurred, blotted, and full of silly mistakes; Sylvie's (such was the name of the ugly little girl) was clearly written, it contained no error against sense, and but few faults of orthography. I coolly read aloud both exercises, marking the faults; then I looked at Eulalie.
“还有你，小姐，把你的也拿给我，”随后，我又对坐在第一排的另一边的一位脸色苍白、相貌平平的姑娘说，语气比刚才缓和多了，我立刻就发现，她的外表是班上最丑陋的，上课却是最专心的。她起身走近我，大方而恭敬地行了一个屈膝礼后把练习本递给了我。我迅速地扫视了一下两人的听写。厄拉利的字模糊难辨，本子上也墨迹斑斑，满是粗心而犯的错误；西尔维（就是那个丑女孩的名字）则写得工工整整，没有一点不合情理的错误，只有些拼写错误。我镇定地大声地宣读了这两份听写练习，指出了其中的错误，然后，看着厄拉利。
'C'est honteux!' said I, and I deliberately tore her dictation in four parts, and presented her with the fragments I returned Sylvie her book with a smile, saying,— 
“真是丢人！”我说完便故意把她的本子撕成四片并将碎片扔给她，然后微笑着把练习本还给西尔薇，对她说——
'C'est bien; je suis content de vous.' 
“很好，我真为你感到骄傲。”
Sylvie looked calmly pleased. Eulalie swelled like an incensed turkey. But the mutiny was quelled. The conceited coquetry and futile flirtation of the first bench were exchanged for a taciturn sullenness, much more convenient to me, and the rest of my lesson passed without interruption.
西尔维看似镇定而开心。而厄拉利却鼓鼓得像只被斗得发怒的火鸡。不过她没有发作。第一排学生刚才那种自负和轻佻的神气不见了，取而代之的是默不作声和闷闷不乐。这种情形于我非常有利，接下来的课就会不受任何干扰地顺利进行了。
A bell clanging out in the yard announced the moment for the cessation of school labours. I heard our own bell at the same time, and that of a certain public college immediately after. Order dissolved instantly. Up started every pupil. I hastened to seize my hat, bow to the maétresse, and quit the room before the tide of externats should pour from the inner class, where I knew near a hundred were prisoned, and whose rising tumult I already heard.
这时，院子里钟声叮当，宣布是下课的时间了。我也听到了我们自己的钟声，随即，某一所公立大学的钟声也跟着响起来。教室的秩序瞬间消失。每个学生都站了起来。我急忙抓起我的帽子，向那位女老师点头致意了一下，赶在里面一帮学生蜂拥而出的高潮前，离开了教室；据我所知，里面还有大约一百名学生，我已经听到了她们起立时的嘈杂声。
I had scarcely crossed the hall and gained the corridor when Mdlle. Reuter came again upon me.
我刚穿过大厅，来到走廊上，恰好勒泰小姐再次向我迎了上来。
'Step in here a moment,' said she, and she held open the door of the side room from whence she had issued on my arrival. It was a salle-éEmanger, as appeared from the beaufet, and the armoire vitrée, filled with glass and china, which formed part of its furniture. Ere she had closed the door on me and herself, the corridor was already filled with day-pupils, tearing down their cloaks, bonnets, and cabas from the wooden pegs on which they were suspended. The shrill voice of a maîtresse was heard at intervals, vainly endeavouring to enforce some sort of order—vainly, I say. Discipline there was none in these rough ranks, and yet this was considered one of the best conducted schools in Brussels.
“到这儿来坐会儿吧。”她说着打开走廊一侧房间的房门，刚才她就是从那里出来的。从那个精致的玻璃柜可以看出这是一个餐厅，玻璃柜里满是杯子和瓷器，构成了餐厅家具的一部分。我们刚一走进房间关上门，走读生们就把整个走廊挤得水泄不通，扯取着挂在木制衣架上的的斗篷、帽子和手提包。隔一段时间，就能听见一位女管事大声呵斥，竭尽全力地想要维持秩序，却无济于事。我也觉得这没有用。这帮粗野的学生根本不知道什么是纪律，亏得这所学校还被认为是布鲁塞尔管理最好的学校之一。
'Well, you have given your first lesson,' began Mdlle. Reuter in the most calm, equable voice, as though quite unconscious of the chaos from which we were separated only by a single wall.
“好了，你的第一堂课结束了。”勒泰小姐用极其镇静平淡的预期开口说道。虽然我上课的教室和她的房间仅仅隔着一堵墙，但她却好像对刚才的混乱毫不知情一样。
'Were you satisfied with your pupils, or did any circumstance in their conduct give you cause for complaint? Conceal nothing from me; repose in me entire confidence.' 
“你满意学生的表现吗？或者说她们有没有任何行为使你恼怒？不必对我有丝毫隐瞒，请你完全相信我。”
Happily, I felt in myself complete power to manage my pupils without aid. The enchantment, the golden haze which had dazzled my perspicuity at first, had been a good deal dissipated. I cannot say I was chagrined or downcast by the contrast which the reality of a pensionnat de demoiselles presented to my vague ideal of the same community. I was only enlightened and amused; consequently, I felt in no disposition to complain to Mdlle. Reuter, and I received her considerate invitation to confidence with a smile.
很高兴的是，我觉得我完全有能力管理好我的学生，无需任何人的帮助。一开始，学校里那种魅力，那种金色的光晕，让我有坠入迷雾的感觉，现在这都已经大大地消散了。我不能说女子寄宿学校的现实与之前那种模糊的想像有差别会让我感到心烦或丧气。我只是觉得受到了启发，被人逗乐了，所以，我觉得我没有任何理由向勒泰小姐抱怨，对于她的关心和信任，我抱以微笑。
'A thousand thanks, mademoiselle; all has gone very smoothly.' 
“非常感谢，小姐；一切都进展得很顺利。”
She looked more than doubtful.
她看上去更多的是怀疑。
'Et les trois demoiselles du premier bane?' said she.
“那么第一排那三个女孩呢？”她说。
'Ah! tout va an mieux!' was my answer, and Mdlle. Reuter ceased to question me; but her eye—not large, not brilliant, not melting, or kindling, but astute, penetrating, practical—showed she was even with me. It let out a momentary gleam, which said plainly, 'Be as close as you like; I am not dependent on your candour. What you would conceal I already know.' 
“啊！一切都会好起来的！”我回答道。勒泰小姐不再问我问题了。但她的眼睛——不大、不亮、不动人、也不愠怒，却充满狡黠、敏锐和实在的眼睛——显出一丝怀疑的神色。她的眼睛瞬间闪现出一道光芒，好像在说：“随你怎么守口如瓶吧，我并不指望你坦白。你想要隐瞒的事，我早就知道了。”
By a transition so quiet as to be scarcely perceptible, the directress's manner changed. The anxious business air passed from her face, and she began chatting about the weather and the town, and asking in neighbourly wise after M. and Madame Pelet. I answered all her little questions. She prolonged her talk; I went on following its many little windings. She sat so long, said so much, varied so often the topics of discourse, that it was not difficult to perceive she had a particular aim in thus detaining me. Her mere words could have afforded no clue to this aim, but her countenance aided, while her lips uttered only affable commonplaces, her eyes reverted continually to my face. Her glances were not given in full, but out of the corners, so quietly, so stealthily, yet I think I lost not one. I watched her as keenly as she watched me. I perceived soon that she was feeling after my real character. She was searching for salient points, and weak points, and eccentric points. She was applying now this test, now that, hoping in the end to find some chink, some niche where she could put in her little firm foot and stand upon my neck, mistress of my nature. Do not mistake me, reader—it was no amorous influence she wished to gain; at that time it was only the power of the politician to which she aspired. I was now installed as a professor in her establishment, and she wanted to know where her mind was superior to mine, by what feeling or opinion she could lead me.
经过一番平静、不露痕迹的过渡之后，女校长的态度转变了。那种焦急关切的表情不见了，她开始聊起了天气和小镇，天南地北地侃起来，并客套式地问候起珀莱先生和珀莱夫人。我一一回答了她的所有问题。她一直拖延着谈话的时间，我也只好应和着。她坐了很久，谈了很多，并不时地转换话题；很显然，她这样纠缠我是有特别目的的。单凭她的谈话看不出来究竟，但从她的表情里我找到了一些线索，她的话不过都是些客套之语，眼睛却时不时地盯着我的脸看。她并不是正视着我，而是斜看过来，目光如此娴静和诡秘，但没有一次能过逃过我的眼睛。她急切地观察着我，我对她也毫不放松。很快我便发觉，她是在观察我的真实个性。她在探寻我性格中坚强、脆弱以及怪异之处。她一下用这种方法测试我，一下又用那种方法考验我，希望能够找到一条裂缝，一个漏洞，好趁机插足，将她有力的小脚踏在我的脖子上，成为主宰我天性的女主人。读者们，不要误解我的意思，她并非想在爱情上赢得优势，她那时渴望的只是政治家的权力。如今，我在她的学校里当了老师，她想知道她的智力在哪方面胜过我，她要用什么感情、什么方法才能领导我。
I enjoyed the game much, and did not hasten its conclusion. Sometimes I gave her hopes, beginning a sentence rather weakly, when her shrewd eye would light up; she thought she had me. Having led her a little way, I delighted to turn round and finish with sound, hard sense, whereat her countenance would fall. At last a servant entered to announce dinner. The conflict being thus necessarily terminated, we parted without having gained any advantage on either side. Mdlle. Reuter had not even given me an opportunity of attacking her with feeling, and I had managed to baffle her little schemes of craft. It was a regular drawn battle. I again held out my hand when I left the room; she gave me hers. It was a small and white hand, but how cool! I met her eye, too, in full, obliging her to give me a straightforward look. This last test went against me. It left her as it found her—moderate, temperate, tranquil; me it disappointed.
我很享受这样的游戏，并不急着结束。有时，我给她一点的希望，说话语气就故意松软一点；等她以为自己占了上风，那敏锐的目光就变得明亮起来。引诱她一会后，我便沾沾自喜地突然改变语气，用精明、有力的语气结束一番对话；这时，她的脸又拉了下来。最后，一个仆人进来通知用餐。我们不得不结束这场心理战；到分开时，也没得出个胜负。即使勒泰小姐没有给我任何机会对她进行情感攻势，我也成功地拆穿她的一个个小阴谋。这场战斗不分胜负。走出房间时，我又一次伸出手来；她也把手给了我。那是一双纤小、白嫩的手，却冰凉无比！我直视着她的眼睛，她也不得不直视我。这场最后的较量于我不利。我的目光一碰上她的就退却了——她的目光还是那样稳重、温和和平静；这种目光令我大失所望。
'I am growing wiser,' thought I, as I walked back to M. Pelet's. 'Look at this little woman. Is she like the women of novelists and romancers? To read of female character as depicted in poetry and fiction, one would think it was made up of sentiment, either for good or bad. Here is a specimen and a most sensible and respectable specimen too, whose staple ingredient is abstract reason. No Talleyrand was ever more passionless than Zoraïde Reuter.' So I thought then. I found afterwards that blunt susceptibilities are very consistent with strong propensities.
“我变得更明智了。”在返回珀莱先生家的路上我这样想着，“瞧这个小女人。她不正像小说家和浪漫作家笔下的那些妇女吗？要读懂诗歌和小说中描绘的女性性格，那就得思考，不管是善良还是邪恶的性格，那都是由情感塑造的。这就是一个标本，而且是一个最具理性、最可敬的标本，而且大部分由抽象的理性构成。连塔列朗那种毫无激情的人，都不可拿来跟佐蕾德·勒泰相提并论。”这就是我那时候的想法。后来我才发现，情感迟钝的人个性通常也比较鲜明。
