THE WORKSHOP.  

车间  

Saturday, 18th.

星期六，18日

Precossi came last night to remind me that I was to go and see his workshop, which is down the street, and this morning when I went out with my father, I got him to take me there for a moment. As we approached the shop, Garoffi issued from it on a run, with a package in his hand, and making his big cloak, with which he covers up his merchandise, flutter. Ah! now I know where he goes to pilfer iron filings, which he sells for old papers, that barterer of a Garoffi! When we arrived in front of the door, we saw Precossi seated on a little pile of bricks, engaged in studying his lesson, with his book resting on his knees. He rose quickly and invited us to enter. It was a large apartment, full of coal-dust, bristling with hammers, pincers, bars, and old iron of every description; and in one corner burned a fire in a small furnace, where puffed a pair of bellows worked by a boy. Precossi, the father, was standing near the anvil, and a young man was holding a bar of iron in the fire.
泼来可西昨晚过来提醒我去看看他在街边的车间。今早我和父亲出门的时候，就让他带我去了一会儿。我们快到车间的时候，卡洛斐从里面跑了出来，手里拿着一个包袱，而他用来遮货物的披风飘了起来。啊！现在我知道他卖掉去换旧纸的铁屑是从哪里偷来的了，好个倒爷卡洛斐！我们走到门前时，看到泼来可西坐在一小堆砖块上，书放在膝盖上，正在忙着做功课。他马上站起来，邀请我们进去。那是间大房间，屋里满是煤灰，竖着各式各样的锤子、钳子、棍子和废铁；一个小熔炉在角落里生着火，一个少年在那里把一对风箱拉得呼呼作响。泼来可西的父亲站在铁砧旁，一个年轻人正拿着一根铁棒在火中烧。
"Ah! here he is," said the smith, as soon as he caught sight of us, and he lifted his cap, "the nice boy who gives away railway trains! He has come to see me work a little, has he not? I shall be at your service in a moment." And as he said it, he smiled; and he no longer had the ferocious face, the malevolent eyes of former days. The young man handed him a long bar of iron heated red-hot on one end, and the smith placed it on the anvil. He was making one of those curved bars for the rail of terrace balustrades. He raised a large hammer and began to beat it, pushing the heated part now here, now there, between one point of the anvil and the middle, and turning it about in various ways; and it was a marvel to see how the iron curved beneath the rapid and accurate blows of the hammer, and twisted, and gradually assumed the graceful form of a leaf torn from a flower, like a pipe of dough which he had modelled with his hands. And meanwhile his son watched us with a certain air of pride, as much as to say, "See how my father works!" 
“啊！他来了，”铁匠一看到我们就说道，并且摘下帽子，“那个送火车的好孩子！他是来看我工作的，对吗？我马上为您效劳。”他说话时面带微笑，不再有先前那副凶神恶煞的表情和充满恶意的眼神了。年轻人递给他一根一头烧得通红的长铁棍，铁匠把它放在铁砧上。他正在做一根楼梯扶手上用的弧形栏杆。他抡起一把大锤开始敲打它，在铁砧的中部和一头之间来回地敲打烧热的部分，并把它以各种方式弯来弯去；能看到铁棍在快速而准确的锤子敲打下弯曲、扭转，最后变成像从花上摘下的一片优雅的花瓣形状，真是太神奇了，它就像是他用手捏出的一条面团。与此同时，他的儿子带着一种无比骄傲的神情看着我们，就像在说：“看我父亲多能干啊！”
"Do you see how it is done, little master?" the blacksmith asked me, when he had finished, holding out the bar, which looked like a bishop's crosier. Then he laid it aside, and thrust another into the fire.
“你看到那是怎么做的了吗，小主人？”完成后，铁匠拿出那根看起来像主教权杖一样的铁棍，问我。然后他把它放到一边，又往火里扔了一根。
"That was very well made, indeed," my father said to him. And he added, "So you are working—eh! You have returned to good habits?" 
“那做得很好，真的。”我父亲对他说。然后接着说，“那么，你又工作了——嗯！你已经重拾好习惯了？”
"Yes, I have returned," replied the workman, wiping away the perspiration, and reddening a little. "And do you know who has made me return to them?" My father pretended not to understand. "This brave boy," said the blacksmith, indicating his son with his finger; "that brave boy there, who studied and did honor to his father, while his father rioted, and treated him like a dog. When I saw that medal—Ah! thou little lad of mine, no bigger than a soldo of cheese, come hither, that I may take a good look at thy phiz!" 
“是的，我已经变回来了。”那工人擦着汗回答道，脸稍稍变红了，“还有，你知道是谁让我重拾它们的吗？”我父亲假装不知道。“是这个勇敢的孩子。”铁匠说着用手指了指他的儿子，“就是那儿那个勇敢的孩子，当他父亲闹事，像对狗那样对待他时，他却努力学习，为他的父亲争光。当我看到那个奖牌时——啊！你这个小家伙，还没有一铜币的奶酪大，过来，让我好好看看你的小脸！”
The boy ran to him instantly; the smith took him and set him directly on the anvil, holding him under the arms, and said to him:— 
那男孩立刻朝他跑过去；铁匠直接把他放在铁砧上，从腋下托住他，对他说：
"Polish off the frontispiece of this big beast of a daddy of yours a little!" 
“把你这个大野兽爸爸的脸擦干净点儿！”
And then Precossi covered his father's black face with kisses, until he was all black himself.
然后泼来可西一直亲吻着他父亲黑黑的脸，直到把自己也弄黑了。
"That's as it should be," said the smith, and he set him on the ground again.
“这样才对。”铁匠说着又把他放回地上。
"That really is as it should be, Precossi!" exclaimed my father, delighted. And bidding the smith and his son good day, he led me away. As I was going out, little Precossi said to me, "Excuse me," and thrust a little packet of nails into my pocket. I invited him to come and view the Carnival from my house.
“就是这样才对嘛，泼来可西！”我父亲高兴地叫着。告别了铁匠和他的儿子，他带我离开了。我正要走出去时，小泼来可西对我说：“不好意思。”然后在我口袋里塞了一小包钉子。我邀请他来我家参观狂欢节。
"You gave him your railway train," my father said to me in the street; "but if it had been made of gold and filled with pearls, it would still have been but a petty gift to that sainted son, who has reformed his father's heart." 
“你把你的火车送给了他。”父亲在街上对我说，“但就算那是金子做的，还镶满了珍珠，对那个令他父亲洗心革面的圣人般的男孩来说，也只是一份微不足道的礼物。”
