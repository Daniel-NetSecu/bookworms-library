第十九章

This gave them time for each other that they had never had before, and their intimacy ripened fast.
鲁思和她的家人又都回到家了，马丁也回到了奥克兰，能经常见到她。拿到学位以后，鲁思便不再钻研学业了；而他，已经耗尽了他全身心的活力，不再写作。这给了他们彼此更多的时间，在过去是没有的，他们的亲密感也迅速倍增。
At first, Martin had done nothing but rest. He had slept a great deal, and spent long hours musing and thinking and doing nothing. He was like one recovering from some terrible bout if hardship. The first signs of reawakening came when he discovered more than languid interest in the daily paper. Then he began to read again—light novels, and poetry; and after several days more he was head over heels in his long-neglected Fiske. His splendid body and health made new vitality, and he possessed all the resiliency and rebound of youth.
最初，马丁什么也不做，除了休息。他睡得很多，花很长时间在沉思默想上，什么也不做。他像个从一次可怕的苦难中恢复过来的人。他复苏的最初迹象是当他发觉自己对日报不只是厌倦，还有一点儿兴趣的时候。然后他又开始阅读了——轻松的小说和诗歌；几天后他便完全投入自己荒废已久的菲斯克了。他强壮的身体和健康创造了新的活力，他有着青春的所有韧性与弹力。
Ruth showed her disappointment plainly when he announced that he was going to sea for another voyage as soon as he was well rested.
当他宣布他一修养好就准备再次出海的时候，鲁思明显表现出的失望。
"Why do you want to do that?" she asked.
“你为什么要再去呢？”她问。
"Money," was the answer.
“为钱，”这是回答，
"I'll have to lay in a supply for my next attack on the editors. Money is the sinews of war, in my case—money and patience.”
“我要去赚一笔钱，让我能向编辑们再次进攻的钱。钱是战斗的原动力，就我而言是——钱和忍耐。”
"But if all you wanted was money, why didn't you stay in the laundry?"
“可是如果你要的只是钱，你为什么不留在洗衣房呢？”
"Because the laundry was making a beast of me. Too much work of that sort drives to drink."
“因为洗衣房把我变成了一个牲口。那种工作做得太多逼得我不得不喝酒。”
She stared at him with horror in her eyes.
她盯着他，眼中带着恐怖的神色。
"Do you mean—?" she quavered.
“你的意思是——”她颤声说。
It would have been easy for him to get out of it; but his natural impulse was for frankness, and he remembered his old resolve to be frank, no matter what happened.
他要避开这个问题原是很容易的；但是他的自然冲动却要求诚实，他也记得往日不管发生什么事情也要诚实的决心。
"Yes," he answered.
“对，”他答道，
"Just that. Several times."
“就是那样。喝了好几次。”
She shivered and drew away from him.
她颤抖着，避开了他。
"No man that I have ever known did that—ever did that.”
“在我所认识的人中没有人这样做——从没这样做过。”
"Then they never worked in the laundry at Shelly Hot Springs," he laughed bitterly. "Toil is a good thing. It is necessary for human health, so all the preachers say, and Heaven knows I've never been afraid of it. But there is such a thing as too much of a good thing, and the laundry up there is one of them. And that's why I'm going to sea one more voyage. It will be my last, I think, for when I come back, I shall break into the magazines. I am certain of it."
“那么他们也从没在谢利温泉馆的洗衣房里干过活。”他苦笑着。“苦活是一件好事。它对于人类的健康是必要的，正如所有牧师所说，老天也知道我从没害怕过劳动。可是世上却有好过头的事，那儿的洗衣房就是其中一种。这就是我为什么要再出海一趟。我想这会是我最后的一次了，因为当我回来的时候，我就要打进文坛了。这一点我是有把握的。”
She was silent, unsympathetic, and he watched her moodily, realizing how impossible it was for her to understand what he had been through.
她沉默了，不表同情，他闷闷不乐地望着她，知道要她理解他所经历过的生活是多么不可能。
"Some day I shall write it up—The Degradation of Toil or the Psychology of Drink in the Working-class, or something like that for a title.”
“早晚我要把它写出来——《苦干使人堕落》或是《工人阶层酗酒的心理研究》，或是诸如此类的题目。”
Never, since the first meeting, had they seemed so far apart as that day. His confession, told in frankness, with the spirit of revolt behind, had repelled her. But she was more shocked by the repulsion itself than by the cause of it. It pointed out to her how near she had drawn to him, and once accepted, it paved the way for greater intimacy. Pity, too, was aroused, and innocent, idealistic thoughts of reform. She would save this raw young man who had come so far. She would save him from the curse of his early environment, and she would save him from himself in spite of himself. And all this affected her as a very noble state of consciousness; nor did she dream that behind it and underlying it were the jealousy and desire of love.
自从他们第一次见面以来，他们之间的距离从没有像那天那样远。他直率的坦白带着反抗精神，使她反感。但是比起令她反感的原因，她更为这反感的情绪所震惊。这表明她离他有多近，一旦接受，它便为他们进一步的亲密铺平了道路。怜悯之情也被唤起，还有天真的理想化的改造思想。她要挽救这位离他很远的原生态的年轻人。她要把他从早期环境的诅咒中拯救出来，不管他愿不愿意她也要拯救他。影响她的这一切像是一种极高贵的意识；她做梦也没想过藏在这背后和下面的是对爱情的嫉妒和渴求。
They rode on their wheels much in the delightful fall weather, and out in the hills they read poetry aloud, now one and now the other, noble, uplifting poetry that turned one's thoughts to higher things. Renunciation, sacrifice, patience, industry, and high endeavor were the principles shepri情 thus indirectly preached—such abstractions being objectified in her mind by her father, and Mr. Butler, and by Andrew Carnegie, who, from a poor immigrant boy had arisen to be the book-giver of the world. All of which was appreciated and enjoyed by Martin. He followed her mental processes more clearly now, and her soul was no longer the sealed wonder it had been. He was on terms of intellectual equality with her. But the points of disagreement did not affect his love. His love was more ardent than ever, for he loved her for what she was, and even her physical frailty was an added charm in his eyes. He read of sickly Elizabeth Barrett, who for years had not placed her feet upon the ground, until that day of flame when she eloped with Browning and stood upright, upon the earth, under the open sky; and what Browning had done for her, Martin decided he could do for Ruth. But first, she must love him. The rest would be easy. He would give her strength and health. And he caught glimpses of their life, in the years to come, wherein, against a background of work and comfort and general well-being, he saw himself and Ruth reading and discussing poetry, she propped amid a multitude of cushions on the ground while she read aloud to him. This was the key to the life they would live. And always he saw that particular picture. Sometimes it was she who leaned against him while he read, one arm about her, her head upon his shoulder. Sometimes they pored together over the printed pages of beauty. Then, too, she loved nature, and with generous imagination he changed the scene of their reading—sometimes they read in closed-in valleys with precipitous walls, or in high mountain meadows,and, again, down by the gray sand-dunes with a wreath of billows at their feet, or afar on some volcanic tropic isle where waterfalls descended and became mist, reaching the sea in vapor veils that swayed and shivered to every vagrant wisp of wind.
他们经常在那秋色宜人的日子里骑着脚踏车外出，他们在山上高声朗诵诗歌，时而他读，时而她读，高雅而令人振奋的诗歌把人的思想引向更高处。克己、牺牲、忍耐、勤恳和刻苦上进是她间接向他宣扬的原则——这些抽象的品格在她心里都体现在她父亲和巴特勒先生身上，还有安德鲁·卡内基，他从一个贫穷的移民男孩发展成为一位世界闻名的著作者。这一切都为马丁所欣赏和受用。现在，他越加清楚地理解她的心理过程了，她的灵魂不再像过去那样是一个封闭的奇迹了。他跟她在知识上是平等的。但是意见不同并不能影响他对她的爱。他的爱比从前更炽烈，因为他爱的就是她这个人，甚至她身体的娇弱在他眼里也是一种魅力。他读过病弱的伊丽莎白·巴雷特的故事，许多年来，她的双脚都不曾沾过地面，直到那光辉的一天，当她和布朗宁私奔的时候，她笔直地站在地上，站在空旷的天空下；布朗宁为她所做的，马丁确定自己也能为鲁思做。但是首先，她必须爱他。剩下的就容易了。他会给她力量和健康。他瞥见了他们的生活，在未来的日子里，以工作、舒适和普遍的优裕生活为背景，他看见自己和鲁思诵读并讨论着诗歌，她靠在地上一大推垫子中间，给他大声朗读者。这便是他们未来生活的基调。他常常看见那一幅特别的图景。有时候是她依偎着他，而他在朗读，一只胳膊搂着她，她的头靠在他肩上。有时候，他们又一起沉浸于那些印在书页上美丽篇章。跟着，她也热爱自然，凭着丰富的想象力他改变了他们读书的场景——有时候他们在峭壁环绕、与世隔绝的山谷中读书，或是在高山上的草地上，或是又来到灰白的沙丘附近，他们的脚下是一圈滚滚的波涛，或是在遥远的热带火山岛上，那里瀑布奔流而下，成了一片迷蒙的雾，像一幅幅随风飘动的烟云般的雾纱，直垂跟她到海里。
But always, in the foreground, lords of beauty and eternally reading and sharing, lay he and Ruth, and always in the background that was beyond the background of nature, dim and hazy, were work and success and money earned that made them free of the world and all its treasures.
但在前景中的常常是美的主人，他和鲁思，永远在诵读着，分享着，而在大自然这个背景之外的总是一个朦胧模糊的背景，其中有工作、成功和赚得的钱，这使他们摆脱全世界和世上的宝藏获得自由。
"I should recommend my little girl to be careful," her mother warned her one day.
“我应该提醒我的小女儿当心一点啦。”有一天，她的母亲警告她。
"I know what you mean. But it is impossible. He is; not—”
“我知道你指的是什么。但那是不能的。他不是——”
Ruth was blushing, but it was the blush of maidenhood called upon for the first time to discuss the sacred things of life with a mother held equally sacred.
鲁思脸红了，但这是少女的羞红，当她第一次跟一个母亲谈论人生中神圣的事情时，她的母亲也同样持着神圣的态度。
"Your kind." Her mother finished the sentence for her.
“与你般配的人。”她的母亲替她说完了那句话。
Ruth nodded.
鲁思点点头。
"I did not want to say it, but he is not. He is rough, brutal, strong—too strong. He has not—”
“我是不想说的，但是他确实不是。他粗鲁、野蛮、强壮——太强壮了。他没有——”
She hesitated and could not go on. It was a new experience, talking over such matters with her mother. And again her mother completed her thought for her.
她迟疑着，说不下去了。跟母亲谈这样的事情，是一种新体验。她的母亲再次替她补充了她的想法。
"He has not lived a clean life, is what you wanted to say."
“你想说的是，他过着不干净的生活。”
Again Ruth nodded, and again a blush mantled her face.
鲁思又点点头，她又满脸绯红。
"It is just that," she said. "It has not been his fault, but he has played much with—”
“是这样的，”她说，“这不是他的错，但他太过——”
"With pitch?"
“高调？”
"Yes, with pitch. And he frightens me. Sometimes I am positively in terror of him, when he talks in that free and easy way of the things he has done—as if they did not matter. They do matter, don't they?"
“是的，很高调。他吓着我了。有时候我确实很怕他，当他以那种轻松自由的方式谈论他做过的事情——好像没关系似的。其实是有关系的，不是吗？”
They sat with their arms twined around each other, and in the pause her mother patted her hand and waited for her to go on.
她们手挽着手坐着，在谈话间歇的时候，母亲拍拍她的手，等着她说下去。
"But I am interested in him dreadfully," she continued. "In a way he is my protégé. Then, too, he is my first boy friend—but not exactly friend; rather protégé and friend combined. Sometimes, too, when he frightens me, it seems that he is a bulldog I have taken for a plaything, like some of the 'frat' girls, and he is tugging hard, and showing his teeth, and threatening to break loose."
“可我对他非常感兴趣，”她接着说，“可以说，他是我的弟子。然而，他也是我的第一个男朋友——但严格来说又不是朋友；是弟子和朋友的结合体吧。有时候，当他吓到我的时候，他又似乎是我——像一些‘兄弟会’里的女孩——养着玩的一只牛头狗，猛拉着链子，龇着牙，威胁着要挣脱链子。”
Again her mother waited.
她母亲又等着她说下去。
"He interests me, I suppose, like the bulldog. And there is much good in him, too; but there is much in him that I would not like in—in the other way. You see, I have been thinking. He swears, he smokes, he drinks, he has fought with his fists (he has told me so, and he likes it; he says so). He is all that a man should not be—a man I would want for my—” her voice sank very low—“husband. Then he is too strong. My prince must be tall, and slender, and dark—a graceful, bewitching prince. No, there is no danger of my failing in love with Martin Eden. It would be the worst fate that could befall me.”
“他吸引着我，我想，就像那只牛头狗。他也有很多长处；可是他身上有不少我不喜欢的东西——从另一方面看。你瞧，我一直在思索。他说粗话、吸烟、喝酒、用拳头打架（他这么告诉过我，他喜欢这个；他是这么说的）。他的所为全是一个男人不当为的——一个我愿意他做我的——”她的声音放得很低——“丈夫的男人。再说，他太强壮了。我的王子应该是高挑的、颀长的、微黑的——一位优雅迷人的王子。不，我不会冒险爱上马丁·伊登。那会是我最坏的命运。”
"But it is not that that I spoke about," her mother equivocated. "Have you thought about him? He is so ineligible in every way, you know, and suppose he should come to love you?"
“可那并不是我谈到的事情，”她的母亲含糊地说，“你有没有考虑过他呢？他在各方面都是这么不合适，你知道，但假如他爱上你呢？”
"But he does—already," she cried.
“但是他确实——已经爱上了。”她叫道。
"It was to be expected," Mrs. Morse said gently. "How could it be otherwise with any one who knew you?"
“这是在意料之中的，”莫尔斯太太轻轻地说，“如果是一个了解你的人呢？”
"Olney hates me!" she exclaimed passionately.
“奥尔尼讨厌我！”她激动地叫道。
"And I hate Olney. I feel always like a cat when he is around. I feel that I must be nasty to him, and even when I don't happen to feel that way, why, he's nasty to me, anyway. But I am happy with Martin Eden. No one ever loved me before—no man, I mean, in that way. And it is sweet to be loved—that way. You know what I mean, mother dear. It is sweet to feel that you are really and truly a woman.” She buried her face in her mother's lap, sobbing. "You think I am dreadful, I know, but I am honest, and I tell you just how I feel."
“我也讨厌奥尔尼。但他在身边的时候我就感觉那像是一只猫。我感觉他一定讨厌我，即使我没这么感觉，他在我看来，无论如何也是可恶的。但是和马丁·伊登在一起我很快乐。以前没有人曾爱过我——我的意思是，没有一个男人那样爱我。被人那样爱着是很甜蜜的。你知道我是什么意思吧，亲爱的妈妈。发觉你已是一个真真正正的女人，我很愉快。”她把脸伏到母亲的膝上，抽咽着。“你觉得我很可怕，我知道，但是我是诚实的，我怎么想的便怎么告诉你。”
Mrs. Morse was strangely sad and happy. Her child-daughter, who was a bachelor of arts, was gone; but in her place was a woman-daughter. The experiment had succeeded. The strange void in Ruth's nature had been filled, and filled without danger or penalty. This rough sailor-fellow had been the instrument, and, though Ruth did not love him, he had made her conscious of her womanhood.
不可思议的是，莫尔斯太太悲喜交加。她的女儿，那个文学学士的女孩儿不见了；代替她的是一个成年的女儿。实验已经成功了。鲁思本性中那个出奇的空洞已经被填满，没有带来危险或惩罚。这位粗鲁的海员成了一件工具，尽管鲁思不爱他，但他使她意识到自己的女人的情怀。
"His hand trembles," Ruth was confessing, her face, for shame's sake, still buried. "It is most amusing and ridiculous, but I feel sorry for him, too. And when his hands are too trembly, and his eyes too shiny, why, I lecture him about his life and the wrong way he is going about it to mend it. But he worships me, I know. His eyes and his hands do not lie. And it makes me feel grown-up, the thought of it, the very thought of it; and I feel that I am possessed of something that is by rights my own—that makes me like the other girls—and—and young women. And, then, too, I knew that I was not like them before, and I knew that it worried you. You thought you did not let me know that dear worry of yours, but I did, and I wanted to—'to make good,' as Martin Eden says."
“他的手在颤抖，”鲁思承认，她的脸因为害羞仍然伏在母亲膝上。“这是最有趣最滑稽的，但是我也为他感到难过。而当他的手抖得太厉害，眼睛闪着太强的光时，哎，我便开导他，关于他的人生，关于他想要改进的错误方法。然而他是崇拜我的，我知道。他的眼睛和双手是不会撒谎的。这叫我觉得自己已经成长起来了，一想到这个，只要一想到这个；而且我觉得我拥有了理应属于我的东西——那使我像其他女孩儿们——以及——以及其他年轻女人们一样。我也知道以前我和她们不一样，我知道这让你担心了。你以为你没有让我知道你的烦恼，但是我却明白，我还要——‘做好’，像马丁·伊登所说的那样。”
It was a holy hour for mother and daughter, and their eyes were wet as they talked on in the twilight, Ruth all white innocence and frankness, her mother sympathetic, receptive, yet calmly explaining and guiding.
这对母亲和女儿来说是神圣的时刻。她们在暮色中交谈的时候，眼睛都湿润了，鲁思天真无邪，坦率真诚，她的母亲心怀同情，善于接纳，却也冷静地解释并引导着。
"He is four years younger than you," she said. "He has no place in the world. He has neither position nor salary. He is impractical. Loving you, he should, in the name of common sense, be doing something that would give him the right to marry, instead of paltering around with those stories of his and with childish dreams. Martin Eden, I am afraid, will never grow up. He does notp. 谎的。 take to responsibility and a man's work in the world like your father did, or like all our friends, Mr. Butler for one. Martin Eden, I am afraid, will never be a money-earner. And this world is so ordered that money is necessary to happiness—oh, no, not these swollen fortunes, but enough of money to permit of common comfort and decency. He—he has never spoken?”
“他比你年轻四岁呢，”她说，“他在社会上没有地位。他既没职业也没薪金。他是不切实际的。爱上了你，凭常识来说，他应该做点什么事以让他有结婚的权利，而不是到处卖弄他那些小说，做着幼稚的梦。马丁·伊登，我恐怕他永远不会成熟。他并不负起责任来，在这世上去做一个男人应做的工作，像你父亲，或是我们所有的朋友那样，巴特勒先生便是其中一个。马丁·伊登，我担心他永远不会成为一个会赚钱的人。然而这个世界如此安排，金钱对于幸福是必要的——噢，不，并不是指大量的财产，但是要有足够的金钱，才可以过得舒适体面。他——他从没和你说过吗？”
"He has not breathed a word. He has not attempted to; but if he did, I would not let him, because, you see, I do not love him."
“他半个字也没提过。他没有打算过；即使他有打算，我也不会让他这么做的，因为，你知道的，我并不爱他。”
"I am glad of that. I should not care to see my daughter, my one daughter, who is so clean and pure, love a man like him. There are noble men in the world who are clean and true and manly. Wait for them. You will find one some day, and you will love him and be loved by him, and you will be happy with him as your father and I have been happy with each other. And there is one thing you must always carry in mind—”
“这叫我高兴。我当然不愿看见我的女儿，我唯一的女儿，如此干净纯洁的女儿，去爱一个像他那样的人。世上有的是高贵的男人，干净、真诚、阳刚的男人。等待他们吧。总有一天你会遇到这样一个人的，而且你会爱上他，也会被他爱着，你和他在一起会很幸福，就像你父亲和我彼此都幸福一样。有一点你必须时常记在心里——”
"Yes, mother."
“好的，妈妈。”
Mrs. Morse's voice was low and sweet as she said, "And that is the children."
莫尔斯太太的声音低沉而悦耳地说道：“那就是小孩子。”
"I—have thought about them," Ruth confessed, remembering the wanton thoughts that had vexed her in the past, her face again red with maiden shame that she should be telling such things.
“我——已经考虑过孩子的问题，”鲁思承认道，一面回忆起过去烦扰她的那些放肆的想法，因为得谈论这些事情，她的脸又泛起了少女的羞红。
"And it is that, the children, that makes Mr. Eden impossible," Mrs. Morse went on incisively. "Their heritage must be clean, and he is, I am afraid, not clean. Your father has told me of sailors' lives, and—and you understand.”
“就是因为孩子，马丁·伊登就不可能，”莫尔斯太太一针见血地接下去。“他们的血统应当洁净，而他，依我看来，并不洁净。你父亲曾经告诉过我海员们的生活，而——而你是懂得的。”
Ruth pressed her mother's hand in assent, feeling that she really did understand, though her conception was of something vague, remote va height=", and terrible that was beyond the scope of imagination.
鲁思捏着母亲的手表示同意，她觉得自己的确了解，尽管她的观念中只是一些模糊遥远的、可怕的、超乎她想象的东西。
"You know I do nothing without telling you," she began. “—Only, sometimes you must ask me, like this time. I wanted to tell you, but I did not know how. It is false modesty, I know it is that, but you can make it easy for me. Sometimes, like this time, you must ask me, you must give me a chance.”
“你知道我做什么事都会告诉你的，”她开始说，“——只是，有时候你得问我，就像这次一样。我曾想过要告诉你，可我不知道怎么说。这是一种错误的谦逊，我知道的，可你问我的话，我就容易开口。有时候，像这次一样，你得问我，你得给我一个机会。”
"Why, mother, you are a woman, too!" she cried exultantly, as they stood up, catching her mother's hands and standing erect, facing her in the twilight, conscious of a strangely sweet equality between them.
“啊，妈妈，你也是女人！”她们站起来的时候，她欣喜地叫道，拉着母亲的手，站得笔直，在暮色中面对着她，意识到她们之间是一种妙不可言的平等关系。
"I should never have thought of you in that way if we had not had this talk. I had to learn that I was a woman to know that you were one, too."
“要不是我们的这番谈话，我是不会这样看你的。我得知道我是个女人，才知道你也是个女人。”
"We are women together," her mother said, drawing her to her and kissing her. "We are women together," she repeated, as they went out of the room, their arms around each other's waists, their hearts swelling with a new sense of companionship.
“我们都是女人，”她母亲说，把她拉到怀里亲吻她。“我们都是女人。”她们走出屋子时，她又说了一遍，她们用胳膊互相搂着腰，她们的心里洋溢着一种新的伙伴之情。
"Our little girl has become a woman," Mrs. Morse said proudly to her husband an hour later.
“我们的小女孩已经长成一个女人了。”一个钟头后，莫尔斯太太骄傲地跟她丈夫说。
"That means," he said, after a long look at his wife, "that means she is in love."
“这是说，”注视了妻子好一会儿，他说，“这是说，她在谈恋爱了。”
"No, but that she is loved," was the smiling rejoinder. "The experiment has succeeded. She is awakened at last."
“不，不过她是被爱着了，”这是一个含笑的回答，“实验已经成功了。她终于觉醒了。”
"Then we'll have to get rid of him." Mr. Morse spoke briskly, in matter-of-fact, businesslike tones.
“那么我们就得摆脱他。”莫尔斯先生以一种实事求是、公事公办的口吻斩钉截铁地说。
But his wife shook her head. "It will not be necessary. Ruth says he is going to sea in a few days. When he comes back, she will not be here. We will send her to Aunt Clara's. And, besides, a year in the East, with the change in climate, people, ideas, and everything, is just the thing she needs."
但他的太太摇摇头。“不必这样。鲁思说他几天后就要去出海了。他回来的时候，她已经不在这儿了。我们要将她送到克拉拉姑母家去。再说，在东部住上一年，气候、人、想法和一切东西都会变，这正是她所需要的。”
