THIRTY-TWO DEGREES.  

三十二度  

Friday, 16th.

星期五，16日

During the five days which have passed since the National Festival, the heat has increased by three degrees. We are in full summer now, and begin to feel weary; all have lost their fine rosy color of springtime; necks and legs are growing thin, heads droop and eyes close. Poor Nelli, who suffers much from the heat, has turned the color of wax in the face; he sometimes falls into a heavy sleep, with his head on his copy-book; but Garrone is always watchful, and places an open book upright in front of him, so that the master may not see him. Crossi rests his red head against the bench in a certain way, so that it looks as though it had been detached from his body and placed there separately. Nobis complains that there are too many of us, and that we corrupt the air. Ah, what an effort it costs now to study! I gaze through the windows at those beautiful trees which cast so deep a shade, where I should be so glad to run, and sadness and wrath overwhelm me at being obliged to go and shut myself up among the benches. But then I take courage at the sight of my kind mother, who is always watching me, scrutinizing me, when I return from school, to see whether I am not pale; and at every page of my work she says to me:—"Do you still feel well?" 
国庆节之后的五天里，气温升高了三度。现在我们已经进入仲夏，开始感到疲倦。所有人在春天里那美丽的红润肤色都不见了，脖子和腿也都日渐消瘦，脑袋低垂，眼睛也睁不开了。可怜的内利受不住酷热，脸色已经变成了蜡黄色，有时还会将头伏在字帖上沉沉地睡过去。但卡隆总是很警惕，把一本打开的书竖立在内利的前方，好替他挡住老师的视线。克洛西将他那长满红发的头靠在椅背上，那姿势看起来就像头脱离了身体，单独放在那里一样。诺琵斯抱怨着我们人太多，空气变污浊了。啊，现在上课真辛苦！我盯着窗外美丽的树木投下的深深的树阴，真想跑过去，一想到自己必须要坐在凳子上，就感到一股悲伤与愤怒。但一见到我那善良的母亲，我就又重新振作了起来。我从学校回家之后，母亲总是看着我，仔细地观察着我，看我脸色是否苍白。我每做一页功课，她都要问：“你还好吗？”
and every morning at six, when she wakes me for my lesson, "Courage! there are only so many days more: then you will be free, and will get rested,—you will go to the shade of country lanes." 
每天早上六点钟，她叫我起床上学的时候也都会说：“加油！只剩几天了，之后你就自由了，你可以休息——可以去乡间小巷的阴凉处了。”
Yes, she is perfectly right to remind me of the boys who are working in the fields in the full heat of the sun, or among the white sands of the river, which blind and scorch them, and of those in the glass-factories, who stand all day long motionless, with head bent over a flame of gas; and all of them rise earlier than we do, and have no vacations. Courage, then! And even in this respect, Derossi is at the head of all, for he suffers neither from heat nor drowsiness; he is always wide awake, and cheery, with his golden curls, as he was in the winter, and he studies without effort, and keeps all about him alert, as though he freshened the air with his voice.
是的，她让我想起了那些顶着炎炎烈日、在田野里或者白色的河滩上工作的孩子们，阳光暴晒着他们，眼睛也花了；还有那些在玻璃厂工作的孩子们，他们每天一动不动地站着，冲着玻璃火焰低垂着脑袋。他们早晨起得比我们早，而且还没有假期。她这么做非常正确。那么，加油！说到奋发，代洛西当属第一，他既不喊热也不犯困。一头金色卷发的他总是精神抖擞、兴高采烈，就和冬天一样，学习起来丝毫不觉得费劲，这使得他周围的人也都警醒起来，仿佛他的声音使空气也变得新鲜了。
And there are two others, also, who are always awake and attentive: stubborn Stardi, who pricks his face, to prevent himself from going to sleep; and the more weary and heated he is, the more he sets his teeth, and he opens his eyes so wide that it seems as though he wanted to eat the teacher; and that barterer of a Garoffi, who is wholly absorbed in manufacturing fans out of red paper, decorated with little figures from match-boxes, which he sells at two centesimi apiece.
还有另外两个人也总是清醒而且专注：一个是固执的斯带地，为了不让自己睡着，他就戳自己的脸，越是觉得困倦和炎热，他就越是紧咬牙齿，大张着眼睛，仿佛要生吞老师一样；另一个是小贩卡洛斐，他全神贯注地把红纸折成扇子，再在扇面上贴上火柴盒上的花纸作装饰，一把扇子卖两分钱。
But the bravest of all is Coretti; poor Coretti, who gets up at five o'clock, to help his father carry wood! At eleven, in school, he can no longer keep his eyes open, and his head droops on his breast. And nevertheless, he shakes himself, punches himself on the back of the neck, asks permission to go out and wash his face, and makes his neighbors shake and pinch him. But this morning he could not resist, and he fell into a leaden sleep. The master called him loudly; "Coretti!" He did not hear. The master, irritated, repeated, "Coretti!" Then the son of the charcoal-man, who lives next to him at home, rose and said:— 
但最勇敢的要数可莱谛。可怜的可莱谛，早晨五点钟就要起床帮助父亲运柴！到了学校，每到十一点钟，他便眼皮打架，头低垂到胸前。但是，他抖抖身体，捶打后颈，向老师要求出去洗把脸，还让隔壁桌的同学们摇晃或者拧醒他。可是今天早晨，他终于坚持不住，沉沉地睡去了。老师大声地喊他：“可莱谛！”他没听见。老师恼怒了，再次喊道：“可莱谛！”住在可莱谛家隔壁的卖炭人的儿子站起来说道：
"He worked from five until seven carrying faggots." The teacher allowed him to sleep on, and continued with the lesson for half an hour. Then he went to Coretti's seat, and wakened him very, very gently, by blowing in his face. On beholding the master in front of him, he started back in alarm. But the master took his head in his hands, and said, as he kissed him on the hair:— 
“他今天早上从五点钟一直搬柴搬到七点钟。”于是老师让可莱谛继续睡着，又接着讲了半小时的课。然后，他走到可莱谛的座位边上，非常非常轻柔地对着可莱谛的脸吹气，把他弄醒。看见老师站在自己的前面，可莱谛吓得直往后缩。但是老师双手捧着他的头，一边亲吻着他的头发，一边说：
"I am not reproving you, my son. Your sleep is not at all that of laziness; it is the sleep of fatigue." 
“我不责怪你，我的孩子。你睡觉并不是因为懒惰，而是因为你太疲劳了。”
