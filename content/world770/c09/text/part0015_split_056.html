THE STREET.  

街道  

Saturday, 25th.

星期六，25日

I was watching you from the window this afternoon, when you were on your way home from the master's; you came in collision with a woman. Take more heed to your manner of walking in the street. There are duties to be fulfilled even there. If you keep your steps and gestures within bounds in a private house, why should you not do the same in the street, which is everybody's house. Remember this, Enrico. Every time that you meet a feeble old man, a poor person, a woman with a child in her arms, a cripple with his crutches, a man bending beneath a burden, a family dressed in mourning, make way for them respectfully. We must respect age, misery, maternal love, infirmity, labor, death. Whenever you see a person on the point of being run down by a vehicle, drag him away, if it is a child; warn him, if he is a man; always ask what ails the child who is crying all alone; pick up the aged man's cane, when he lets it fall. If two boys are fighting, separate them; if it is two men, go away: do not look on a scene of brutal violence, which offends and hardens the heart. And when a man passes, bound, and walking between a couple of policemen, do not add your curiosity to the cruel curiosity of the crowd; he may be innocent. Cease to talk with your companion, and to smile, when you meet a hospital litter, which is, perhaps, bearing a dying person, or a funeral procession; for one may issue from your own home on the morrow. Look with reverence upon all boys from the asylums, who walk two and two,—the blind, the dumb, those afflicted with the rickets, orphans, abandoned children; reflect that it is misfortune and human charity which is passing by. Always pretend not to notice any one who has a repulsive or laughter-provoking deformity. Always extinguish every match that you find in your path; for it may cost some one his life. Always answer a passer-by who asks you the way, with politeness. Do not look at any one and laugh; do not run without necessity; do not shout. Respect the street. The education of a people is judged first of all by their behavior on the street. Where you find offences in the streets, there you will find offences in the houses. And study the streets; study the city in which you live. If you were to be hurled far away from it Tomorrow, you would be glad to have it clearly present in your memory, to be able to traverse it all again in memory. Your own city, and your little country—that which has been for so many years your world; where you took your first steps at your mother's side; where you experienced your first emotions, opened your mind to its first ideas; found your first friends. It has been a mother to you: it has taught you, loved you, protected you. Study it in its streets and in its people, and love it; and when you hear it insulted, defend it.
今天下午当你从老师那里回家时，我正在窗口看着你，你撞上了一个妇人。多注意一下你走在街上时的举止。即使在那里也有需要履行的义务。如果你能在私人场所约束好自己的脚步和姿势，为什么不能在街上也同样做到呢，那里是大家的居所。记住这一点，安利柯。每当遇到虚弱的老人、可怜的人、抱小孩的妇人、拄拐杖的残疾人、负重而被压弯腰的人，还有穿着丧服的一家人时，要恭敬地给他们让路。我们必须尊重年纪、痛苦、母爱、疾病、劳动，还有死亡。不管什么时候你看到有人快要被车撞倒了，要是个孩子，就把他拉开；要是个大人，就提醒他注意。每当你看到有小孩一个人在哭，就去问问他在烦恼什么。要是有老人弄掉了手杖，就去帮他捡起来。如果有两个男孩子在打架，就去分开他们；如果是两个大人，就走开：不要旁观那些会令人心里不快并变得冷漠的野蛮暴力场面。当一个被绑着、走在两名警察中间的人经过时，不要再在人群那残酷的好奇心中加入你的好奇心了，因为他可能是清白的。当你看到可能抬着垂死之人的医院的担架，或是一支送丧队伍时，停止跟你的同伴交谈，并停止微笑，因为明天可能你自己的家人就会如此。要带着敬意看待所有那些收容所里的两两走着的男孩子们——盲的、哑的、被佝偻病所折磨的、孤儿，还有被遗弃的孩子们。想想看，从你身边走过去的是不幸和人们的仁慈。每次都要假装没有注意任何一个有讨厌的或令人发笑的缺陷的人。每次看到路上有火柴就要把它熄灭，因为它可能会要了谁的命。每当有行人向你问路时，要礼貌地回答。不要看着任何人大笑；不要跑，除非有需要；不要大喊大叫。要尊重街道。一个人的教养首先会从他在街上的举止体现出来。在街上行为不端，在家里也不会好。要研究街道，研究你所居住的城市。如果明天你将要远离它，你会很乐意把它清楚地记在脑海里，并在记忆里再一次穿梭于它的各处。你自己的城市，和你的这一小片领域——这么多年来一直是你的世界；在这里，你站在母亲身边迈出第一步；在这里，你体验到了最初的情感，敞开心扉接受最初的思想，并找到了第一批朋友。它就像你的母亲：它教你、爱你、保护你。研究它的街道和人们，并要爱它；当你听到它受了侮辱，要为它辩护。
Thy Father.
你的父亲
