CHAPTER XV  

第十五章  

Mr. Rochester did, on a future occasion, explain it. It was one afternoon, when he chanced to meet me and Adele in the grounds: and while she played with Pilot and her shuttlecock, he asked me to walk up and down a long beech avenue within sight of her.
后来有一次，罗切斯特先生的确对此事做了解释。那是个午后，他在庭院里恰巧碰到我和阿德拉。趁着阿德拉正逗弄着派洛特，玩着板羽球的时候，他请我和他一起去一条长长的，布满山毛榉的林荫道上散步，从那里还可以看得见阿德拉。
He then said that she was the daughter of a French opera dancer, Celine Varens, towards whom he had once cherished what he called a "grande passion．” This passion Celine had professed to return with even superior ardour. He thought himself her idol, ugly as he was: he believed, as he said, that she preferred his "taille d'athlete" to the elegance of the Apollo Belvidere.
他告诉我阿德拉是法国歌剧演员塞莉纳·瓦伦的女儿，用他自己的话说，他曾一度对塞莉纳怀有“非常强烈的情感”。塞莉纳曾声称要以更加火热的情感回报他的爱情。他认为自己尽管外貌丑陋，却是她的偶像。他相信，正如他所说，比起贝尔维德尔·阿波罗的优美，她更喜欢自己“运动员的身材”。
"And, Miss Eyre, so much was I flattered by this preference of the Gallic sylph for her British gnome, that I installed her in an hotel; gave her a complete establishment of servants, a carriage, cashmeres, diamonds, dentelles, &c. In short, I began the process of ruining myself in the received style, like any other spoony. I had not, it seems, the originality to chalk out a new road to shame and destruction, but trode the old track with stupid exactness not to deviate an inch from the beaten centre. I had—as I deserved to have—the fate of all other spoonies. Happening to call one evening when Celine did not expect me, I found her out; but it was a warm night, and I was tired with strolling through Paris, so I sat down in her boudoir; happy to breathe the air consecrated so lately by her presence. No,—I exaggerate; I never thought there was any consecrating virtue about her: it was rather a sort of pastille perfume she had left; a scent of musk and amber, than an odour of sanctity. I was just beginning to stifle with the fumes of conservatory flowers and sprinkled essences, when I bethought myself to open the window and step out on to the balcony. It was moonlight and gaslight besides, and very still and serene. The balcony was furnished with a chair or two; I sat down, and took out a cigar,—I will take one now, if you will excuse me.” 
“爱小姐，这位法国美女竟然钟情于一位英国侏儒，我简直受宠若惊。于是我给她在酒店里安排了住处，给她配齐了仆役和马车，送给她山羊绒，钻石，花边，等等。总之，我开始像任何一个痴情汉一样，用惯常的方式毁灭自己。我似乎缺乏独创性，没法开创出一条通向羞辱和毁灭的新路，而是傻傻地严格遵循着旧道，一步都不肯偏离别人踏平的中心。我拥有了——我活该如此——与所有其他痴情汉一样的命运。一晚，我拜访塞莉纳，她不知道我要去，我发现她不在家。那晚天气很暖和，我因步行穿过巴黎，已有些疲倦，就在她的房间里坐了下来，愉快地呼吸着她刚走时留下的圣洁的空气。不——我有点夸大了。我从来不认为她身上有神圣的美德。那是她走时留下的一种香锭的香气，是麝香和琥珀的味道，才不是什么圣洁的香气。暖房里的鲜花和洒在空气中的香水味使我有些窒息，于是就想打开窗户走到阳台上去。这时月色朗照，汽灯闪亮，十分静谧。阳台上摆着一两把椅子，我坐了下来，掏出一支雪茄——你不介意吧，我现在就要抽一支。”
Here ensued a pause, filled up by the producing and lighting of a cigar; having placed it to his lips and breathed a trail of Havannah incense on the freezing and sunless air, he went on— 
说到这里他停顿了一下，拿出一支雪茄点燃。放到唇间，把一缕哈瓦那云雾喷进寒冷阴沉的空气中，继续说道——
"I liked bonbons too in those days, Miss Eyre, and I was croquant—(overlook the barbarism)—croquant chocolate comfits, and smoking alternately, watching meantime the equipages that rolled along the fashionable streets towards the neighbouring opera house, when in an elegant close carriage drawn by a beautiful pair of English horses, and distinctly seen in the brilliant city night, I recognised the 'voiture' I had given Celine. She was returning: of course my heart thumped with impatience against the iron rails I leant upon. The carriage stopped, as I had expected, at the hotel door; my flame (that is the very word for an opera inamorata) alighted: though muffed in a cloak—an unnecessary encumbrance, by-the-bye, on so warm a June evening—I knew her instantly by her little foot, seen peeping from the skirt of her dress, as she skipped from the carriage step. Bending over the balcony, I was about to murmur 'Mon ange'—in a tone, of course, which should be audible to the ear of love alone—when a figure jumped from the carriage after her; cloaked also; but that was a spurred heel which had rung on the pavement, and that was a hatted head which now passed under the arched porte cochere of the hotel.
“我那时还迷恋夹心糖，爱小姐。当时我一边大嚼（也顾不得野蛮了）巧克力糖果，一会儿抽两口烟，同时看着经过两旁时髦街道向旁边那个歌剧院驶去的马车。这时来了一辆精致的轿式马车，由一对漂亮的英国马拉着。在灯火辉煌的城市夜景中，我立刻认出那是我送给塞莉纳的马车。是她回来了。当然，我倚在铁栏杆上的心在急不可耐地跳动着。果然，马车在酒店门口停了下来。我的情人（对于唱歌剧的情人，这个词再恰当不过）从车上下来。尽管她披了斗篷——顺便说一句，在那么暖和的六月的夜晚，这完全是多此一举——我还是从跳下马车时露在裙子外面的小脚立刻认出了她。我从阳台上探出身体，刚要说一句‘我的天使’——当然，是用只有情人间才能听见的语调——这时，一个身影也跟在她身后跳下了马车。这人也披着斗篷，但踏在人行道上的却是带马刺的靴子后跟，一个戴礼帽的脑袋从酒店拱形的车行门下经过。
"You never felt jealousy, did you, Miss Eyre? Of course not: I need not ask you; because you never felt love. You have both sentiments yet to experience: your soul sleeps; the shock is yet to be given which shall waken it. You think all existence lapses in as quiet a flow as that in which your youth has hitherto slid away. Floating on with closed eyes and muffled ears, you neither see the rocks bristling not far off in the bed of the flood, nor hear the breakers boil at their base. But I tell you—and you may mark my words—you will come some day to a craggy pass in the channel, where the whole of life's stream will be broken up into whirl and tumult, foam and noise: either you will be dashed to atoms on crag points, or lifted up and borne on by some master wave into a calmer current—as I am now.
“你从来没有尝过嫉妒的滋味吧，爱小姐？肯定没有，我不应该问你，因为你从没恋爱过。这两种感情还有待你去体验。你的灵魂在沉睡，有待震惊使让它苏醒。你认为一切生活就如同你悄然逝去的青春一般，是静静地流走的。闭着眼睛塞住耳朵随波逐流，你既看不到不远处河床中礁石林立，也听不到浪涛在礁石底部翻腾。但是我告诉你——你记住我的话——总有一天你会来到河道中峭壁耸立的关卡，在那里整个生命的洪流会被震得粉碎，成为漩涡和骚动，泡沫与喧嚣。你要么在岩石尖上被撞得粉身碎骨，要么就被某个大浪掀起，抛入更平静的洪流——就如同我现在这样。
I like this day; I like that sky of steel; I like the sternness and stillness of the world under this frost. I like Thornfield, its antiquity, its retirement, its old crow trees and thorn trees, its grey facade, and lines of dark windows reflecting that metal welkin: and yet how long have I abhorred the very thought of it, shunned it like a great plague house? How I do still abhor—” 
我喜欢这样的日子。我喜欢灰色的天空，喜欢严寒中清冷安静的世界。我喜欢桑菲尔德，喜欢它的古朴，喜欢它的安逸，喜欢它乌鸦栖息的老树和荆棘，喜欢它灰色的正面，和它那映照苍穹的一排排黛色窗户。然而有多久了呢，我一想起它就觉得厌恶，像躲避疫所一般避之不迭？我现在还是那么地讨厌——”
He ground his teeth and was silent: he arrested his step and struck his boot against the hard ground. Some hated thought seemed to have him in its grip, and to hold him so tightly that he could not advance.
他咬了咬牙，沉默下来。他收住了脚步，用靴子跺跺坚硬的地面。某种厌恶感好像抓住了他，将他紧紧抓住，使他无法前进。
We were ascending the avenue when he thus paused; the hall was before us. Lifting his eye to its battlements, he cast over them a glare such as I never saw before or since. Pain, shame, ire, impatience, disgust, detestation, seemed momentarily to hold a quivering conflict in the large pupil dilating under his ebon eyebrow. Wild was the wrestle which should be paramount; but another feeling rose and triumphed: something hard and cynical: self-willed and resolute: it settled his passion and petrified his countenance: he went on— 
他停下来时，我们正沿着小路往上走。大宅就在我们面前。他抬眼望向城垛，那眼神中的愤怒是我以前从来没有见过的，自那以后也没有。痛苦、耻辱、愤怒、焦躁、厌恶、憎恨——这些情绪似乎同时在他乌黑的眉毛下面那张大的瞳孔中进行着令人震颤的交锋。交战如此激烈，需要得以控制。但另外一种情绪在他心中升起，占据上风。那是一种冷酷和玩世不恭，任性而坚定不移，平息了他的愤怒，僵化了他的表情。他继续道——
"During the moment I was silent, Miss Eyre, I was arranging a point with my destiny. She stood there, by that beech trunk—a hag like one of those who appeared to Macbeth on the heath of Forres. 'You like Thornfield?' she said, lifting her finger; and then she wrote in the air a memento, which ran in lurid hieroglyphics all along the house front, between the upper and lower row of windows, 'Like it if you can! Like it if you dare!' 
“我刚才沉默时，爱小姐，我正在和命运交涉。她就站在那里，山毛榉树旁边——她是一个巫婆，就像那些福里斯荒原上出现在麦克贝思面前的巫婆一样。‘你喜欢桑菲尔德吗？’她问道，说着竖起手指，在空中写了一条警语。那可怕的象形文字就在大宅的正面，上下两排窗户之间，‘如果你能，你就喜欢它吧！如果你敢，你就喜欢它吧!'
'I will like it,' said I; 'I dare like it;' and" (he subjoined moodily) "I will keep my word; I will break obstacles to happiness, to goodness—yes, goodness. I wish to be a better man than I has been, than I am; as Job's leviathan broke the spear, the dart, and the habergeon, hindrances which others count as iron and brass, I will esteem but straw and rotten wood." 
我当时说‘我会喜欢它，我敢。’而且（他略带愠色地补充说）我会说话算话，清除通往幸福和善良——是的，善良——路上的障碍。我希望自己能成为一个比过去和现在都好的人。就像乔布的海怪打碎了别人认为是铁和铜的长矛、标枪、铠甲和其他障碍物，我将视之为稻草和朽木而已。”
Adele here ran before him with her shuttlecock. "Away!" he cried harshly; "keep at a distance, child; or go in to Sophie!" Continuing then to pursue his walk in silence, I ventured to recall him to the point whence he had abruptly diverged— 
阿德拉拿着她的板羽球跑到他面前。“走开！”他严厉地喊道，“离我远点，孩子。要不就去找索菲！”我在沉默中继续跟着他的脚步，大胆提示他刚才突然岔开的话题——
"Did you leave the balcony, sir," I asked, "when Mdlle Varens entered?" 
我问道：“你离开阳台了吗，先生，瓦伦小姐进来的时候？”
I almost expected a rebuff for this hardly well-timed question, but, on the contrary, waking out of his scowling abstraction, he turned his eyes towards me, and the shade seemed to clear off his brow.
我几乎觉得他会拒绝回答这个不合时宜的问题；但是，相反，他停下沉思，目光转向我，眉头间的阴云好像散开来了。
"Oh, I had forgotten Celine! Well, to resume. When I saw my charmer thus come in accompanied by a cavalier, I seemed to hear a hiss, and the green snake of jealousy, rising on undulating coils from the moonlit balcony, glided within my waistcoat, and ate its way in two minutes to my heart's core. Strange!" he exclaimed, suddenly starting again from the point. "Strange that I should choose you for the confidant of all this, young lady; passing strange that you should listen to me quietly, as if it were the most usual thing in the world for a man like me to tell stories of his opera mistresses to a quaint, inexperienced girl like you! But the last singularity explains the first, as I intimated once before: you, with your gravity, considerateness, and caution were made to be the recipient of secrets. Besides, I know what sort of a mind I have placed in communication with my own: I know it is one not liable to take infection: it is a peculiar mind: it is a unique one. Happily I do not mean to harm it: but, if I did, it would not take harm from me. The more you and I converse, the better; for while I cannot blight you, you may refresh me.” After this digression he proceeded— 
“啊，我把塞莉纳忘了！好吧，接着讲。看到自己的梦中情人在她的白马王子的陪伴下走进来，我好像听到了嘶的一声，一条绿色的嫉妒之蛇正从沐浴在月光中的阳台上爬出，盘曲的身体慢慢展开。它滑过我的背心，两分钟就蚕食到了我的心脏。真是奇怪！”他大叫一声，突然又接着说下去,“真是奇怪，我竟然选择你来分享这些，年轻的女士；更奇怪的是你竟能这样安静地聆听，好像我这样一个男人给你这样一位奇怪又幼稚的女孩讲述我的歌剧演员情妇，是世上最平常的事似的！但是后一种奇怪正好解释了前一种奇怪。就像我曾经说过的一样，你的庄重、周到和谨慎，生来就是为了聆听别人的秘密。另外，我知道我正与之交流的心灵是什么样的，这种心灵不易受外界污染，特立独行，独一无二。令人欣慰的是，我并不希望伤害这样的心灵，而且即使我故意伤害它，也不会成功的。我们谈得越多越好；因为我不会伤害你，你却让我精神焕发。”他讲完这些离题的话后，他又继续——
"I remained in the balcony. 'They will come to her boudoir, no doubt,' thought I: 'let me prepare an ambush.' So putting my hand in through the open window, I drew the curtain over it, leaving only an opening through which I could take observations; then I closed the casement, all but a chink just wide enough to furnish an outlet to lovers' whispered vows: then I stole back to my chair; and as I resumed it the pair came in. My eye was quickly at the aperture. Celine's chambermaid entered, lit a lamp, left it on the table, and withdrew. The couple were thus revealed to me clearly: both removed their cloaks, and there was 'the Varens,' shining in satin and jewels,—my gifts of course,—and there was her companion in an officer's uniform; and I knew him for a young roue of a vicomte—a brainless and vicious youth whom I had sometimes met in society, and had never thought of hating because I despised him so absolutely. On recognising him, the fang of the snake Jealousy was instantly broken; because at the same moment my love for Celine sank under an extinguisher. A woman who could betray me for such a rival was not worth contending for; she deserved only scorn; less, however, than I, who had been her dupe.
“我站在阳台边没动。‘他们会来她的房间，肯定，’我想，‘我给他们来个伏击好了。’于是,我把手伸进开着的窗户，拉上窗帘，只留了一条缝隙以备我观察外面的情况；接着我关上了窗户，只留了一点缝隙，足够让我听到外面这对情侣低声耳语的甜言蜜语。然后，我又溜回到椅子上，刚坐下，他们就进来了。我的目光赶紧看向那道缝隙。塞莉纳的仆人进来了，把灯放在桌子上，点上后就离开了。这时，我能清楚地看到这对情侣了，他们双双脱去披风，瓦伦在那儿，全身闪耀着绫罗绸缎，珠光宝气——当然，那都是我送给她的礼物——她的伴侣身穿军官制服；我知道他是一个典型的只知享乐的子爵——毫无头脑，品行恶劣。我在社交场合曾见过他，但从来没有想过去恨他，因为我对他根本不屑一顾。认出他后，我那嫉妒之蛇的毒牙突然破碎了，因为与此同时，我对塞莉纳的爱被浇灭了。为这种情敌背叛我的女人不值得我去争，她只配受到嘲笑。而我，这个被她玩弄于股掌之中的人，才更应该受到嘲笑。
They began to talk; their conversation eased me completely: frivolous, mercenary, heartless, and senseless, it was rather calculated to weary than enrage a listener. A card of mine lay on the table; this being perceived, brought my name under discussion. Neither of them possessed energy or wit to belabour me soundly, but they insulted me as coarsely as they could in their little way: especially Celine, who even waxed rather brilliant on my personal defects—deformities she termed them. Now it had been her custom to launch out into fervent admiration of what she called my 'beaute male:' wherein she differed diametrically from you, who told me pointblank, at the second interview, that you did not think me handsome. The contrast struck me at the time and—” 
他们开始交谈；他们的谈话使我完全放松了，那些话轻佻无聊，唯利是图，无情冷酷，毫无意义，让人感到厌倦而非愤怒。桌子上放着一张我的名片；他们看到后，就开始谈论我。他们俩都没有精力或才智有理有据地责骂我，但还是用能想到的最鄙俗的方式侮辱我。尤其是塞莉纳，她刻意夸大我的个人缺陷——用她的话说是‘畸形’。然而，她曾经常常狂热地赞美她所谓的‘阳刚美’，恰好与你相反。你在第二次见面时就直白地告诉我你觉得我不美。当时这种强烈的反差让我深深震动了，而且——”
Adele here came running up again.
阿德拉又跑到我们面前来了。
"Monsieur, John has just been to say that your agent has called and wishes to see you." 
“先生，刚才约翰说您的代理人前来拜访，想要见您。”
"Ah! in that case I must abridge. Opening the window, I walked in upon them; liberated Celine from my protection; gave her notice to vacate her hotel; offered her a purse for immediate exigencies; disregarded screams, hysterics, prayers, protestations, convulsions; made an appointment with the vicomte for a meeting at the Bois de Boulogne. Next morning I had the pleasure of encountering him; left a bullet in one of his poor etiolated arms, feeble as the wing of a chicken in the pip, and then thought I had done with the whole crew. But unluckily the Varens, six months before, had given me this filette Adele, who, she affirmed, was my daughter; and perhaps she may be, though I see no proofs of such grim paternity written in her countenance: Pilot is more like me than she. Some years after I had broken with the mother, she abandoned her child, and ran away to Italy with a musician or singer. I acknowledged no natural claim on Adele's part to be supported by me, nor do I now acknowledge any, for I am not her father; but hearing that she was quite destitute, I e'en took the poor thing out of the slime and mud of Paris, and transplanted it here, to grow up clean in the wholesome soil of an English country garden. Mrs. Fairfax found you to train it; but now you know that it is the illegitimate offspring of a French operagirl, you will perhaps think differently of your post and protegee: you will be coming to me some day with notice that you have found another place—that you beg me to look out for a new governess, &c.—Eh?” 
“啊！这样的话，我必须要长话短说了。我打开了窗子，走到他们面前。我不管塞莉纳的歇斯底里的大喊大叫，祈祷请求，反抗和抽搐，决定不再保护她，要她离开旅店，并给了她一些短期内应急的钱。我还与那个子爵约好了在布洛涅树林里会面。第二天，我高兴地见了他，并在他那瘦弱不堪的胳膊上射了一刻子弹，他的胳膊虚弱地像得了喉舌病的小鸡的翅膀一样。我觉得把这一切都解决了。但不幸的是，瓦伦在六个月前给了我这个小女孩阿德拉，并称她是我的女儿；也许她真的是我的女儿，但我在她的面容上找不到一点父权的严厉，派洛特都比她长得像我。我与塞莉纳断绝关系几年后，她遗弃了女儿，与一位音乐家或是歌手移居到了意大利。我当时不承认自己负有抚养阿德拉的责任，现在也不承认，因为我不是她的父亲；但我听说她穷困潦倒，就把这个可怜的东西从巴黎的泥坑中解救出来，移植到这里，希望她能在英国乡村花园里的沃土中健康成长。费尔法克斯太太聘请你来给她当老师，但是现在你知道她是一个法国女歌剧演员的私生女，或许你对你的职务和学生有新的看法了吧。将来的某一天，你会来找我，说你已经找到了新工作——请我另找一位新的家庭教师等等——是吗？”
"No: Adele is not answerable for either her mother's faults or yours: I have a regard for her; and now that I know she is, in a sense, parentless—forsaken by her mother and disowned by you, sir—I shall cling closer to her than before. How could I possibly prefer the spoilt pet of a wealthy family, who would hate her governess as a nuisance, to a lonely little orphan, who leans towards her as a friend?” 
“不，阿德拉不应为她母亲或是您的过错负责。我很关心她，现在知道了她在某种意义上是个孤儿——遭到母亲的抛弃，而先生，您又不愿认她——我会比以往更关心她。我怎么可能会更喜欢一个厌恶家庭老师，被宠坏的富家孩子，而不喜欢一个把我当作朋友依靠的孤独的孤儿呢？”
"Oh, that is the light in which you view it! Well, I must go in now; and you too: it darkens.” 
“哦，这是你看问题的角度！好吧，我要进去了，你也进去吧，天黑了。”
But I stayed out a few minutes longer with Adele and Pilot—ran a race with her, and played a game of battledore and shuttlecock. When we went in, and I had removed her bonnet and coat, I took her on my knee; kept her there an hour, allowing her to prattle as she liked: not rebuking even some little freedoms and trivialities into which she was apt to stray when much noticed, and which betrayed in her a superficiality of character, inherited probably from her mother, hardly congenial to an English mind. Still she had her merits; and I was disposed to appreciate all that was good in her to the utmost. I sought in her countenance and features a likeness to Mr. Rochester, but found none: no trait, no turn of expression announced relationship. It was a pity: if she could but have been proved to resemble him, he would have thought more of her.
但是我又和阿德拉和派洛特呆了几分钟——玩了赛跑和板羽球的游戏。我们进屋后，我帮她摘下帽子，脱下外套，让她坐在我腿上。我们这样过了一个小时，任凭她絮絮叨叨地讲着小孩间的事。注意听她讲话时，会发现她常常不经意地说一些极其琐碎的事，而且讲话毫无遮掩，她的这种性格上的肤浅也许从她母亲那里遗传来的，不太符合英国人的思维，但我并没有为此责备她。不过，她还是有自己的优点的，我愿意尽量欣赏她所有的优点。我在她的表情和面貌上寻找与罗切斯特先生的相似之处，却一无所获。没有特征，没有表情举止可以证明他们之间的父女关系。真是遗憾，如果她能证明与他有相似之处，他会更关心她。
It was not till after I had withdrawn to my own chamber for the night, that I steadily reviewed the tale Mr. Rochester had told me. As he had said, there was probably nothing at all extraordinary in the substance of the narrative itself: a wealthy Englishman's passion for a French dancer, and her treachery to him, were everyday matters enough, no doubt, in society; but there was something decidedly strange in the paroxysm of emotion which had suddenly seized him when he was in the act of expressing the present contentment of his mood, and his newly revived pleasure in the old hall and its environs. I meditated wonderingly on this incident; but gradually quitting it, as I found it for the present inexplicable, I turned to the consideration of my master's manner to myself. The confidence he had thought fit to repose in me seemed a tribute to my discretion: I regarded and accepted it as such. His deportment had now for some weeks been more uniform towards me than at the first. I never seemed in his way; he did not take fits of chilling hauteur: when he met me unexpectedly, the encounter seemed welcome; he had always a word and sometimes a smile for me: when summoned by formal invitation to his presence, I was honoured by a cordiality of reception that made me feel I really possessed the power to amuse him, and that these evening conferences were sought as much for his pleasure as for my benefit.
直到晚上回到自己的卧室后，我才冷静地回味罗切斯特先生给我讲的故事。就像他自己说过的一样，或许这个故事的内容本身没有什么特别的。一位英国富豪对一位法国舞女的激情和她对他的背叛这种事在上流社会无疑不足为奇；但是，他表达目前的心满意足和新近对这幢旧宅及其环境生起的快乐时，突然萌生的情感中有一些非常奇怪的东西。我好奇地思索着这件事，但渐渐地就不去想了。因为我发现目前难以解释，就转而考虑主人对我的态度。他相信我值得信赖，似乎是对我行事慎重的一种赞美。我是这样认为的，也接受他的信任。最近几周，他对我的行为态度也不似当初那么变化莫测了。我似乎从不碍事，他也不再总摆出一副冷淡傲慢的样子。有时他偶然碰见我，显出欢迎的样子，总是会打个招呼或是对我笑一下。他邀请我去见他时，我总能有幸得到他诚恳的接待，使我感到自己真的有能力让他快乐，这些晚上的谈话不仅让他感到愉悦，而且我也从中获益。
I, indeed, talked comparatively little, but I heard him talk with relish. It was his nature to be communicative; he liked to open to a mind unacquainted with the world glimpses of its scenes and ways (I do not mean its corrupt scenes and wicked ways, but such as derived their interest from the great scale on which they were acted, the strange novelty by which they were characterised); and I had a keen delight in receiving the new ideas he offered, in imagining the new pictures he portrayed, and following him in thought through the new regions he disclosed, never startled or troubled by one noxious allusion.
我的话相对较少，但我很乐意听他说。他生性健谈，喜欢向不谙世事的心灵敞开心扉，让我领略各色场面和习气（我并不是指腐败的场面和邪恶的风气，而是那些已经普遍流行而又奇特新颖的事物）；我非常乐于接受他灌输的新观点，想象他描绘的新图景，跟随他穿过他新提到的地方，从不会因某个有害的暗示而惊讶或不安。
The ease of his manner freed me from painful restraint: the friendly frankness, as correct as cordial, with which he treated me, drew me to him. I felt at times as if he were my relation rather than my master: yet he was imperious sometimes still; but I did not mind that; I saw it was his way. So happy, so gratified did I become with this new interest added to life, that I ceased to pine after kindred: my thin crescent destiny seemed to enlarge; the blanks of existence were filled up; my bodily health improved; I gathered flesh and strength.
他随意的态度驱散了令我痛苦的束缚，与他接触时，他友好而真诚，正派而热情，吸引着我。有时，我觉得他更像是我的亲人，而非主人。虽然有时他依然颐指气使，但我不在意，因为我知道那是他的风格。我对生活中新出现的这种乐趣感到非常开心和满意，不再渴求亲戚。我那新月般的薄命开始扩张；我生活中的空白被填充；我的健康状况改善了；我体重增加了，也有力气了。
And was Mr. Rochester now ugly in my eyes? No, reader: gratitude, and many associations, all pleasurable and genial, made his face the object I best liked to see; his presence in a room was more cheering than the brightest fire. Yet I had not forgotten his faults; indeed, I could not, for he brought them frequently before me. He was proud, sardonic, harsh to inferiority of every description: in my secret soul I knew that his great kindness to me was balanced by unjust severity to many others. He was moody, too; unaccountably so; I more than once, when sent for to read to him, found him sitting in his library alone, with his head bent on his folded arms; and, when he looked up, a morose, almost a malignant, scowl blackened his features. But I believed that his moodiness, his harshness, and his former faults of morality (I say FORMER, for now he seemed corrected of them) had their source in some cruel cross of fate. I believed he was naturally a man of better tendencies, higher principles, and purer tastes than such as circumstances had developed, education instilled, or destiny encouraged. I thought there were excellent materials in him; though for the present they hung together somewhat spoiled and tangled. I cannot deny that I grieved for his grief, whatever that was, and would have given much to assuage it.
罗切斯特先生在我眼中还丑陋吗？不，读者。感激和许多真诚愉悦的联想使他的脸成为我的最爱；他在屋里的出现比最亮的火光更让人振奋。但是我没有忘记他的缺点。的确，我不能忘记，因为他不断地在我面前犯错。他傲慢、严厉地对待各种不如自己的人，嘲讽他们。我私下里觉得他对我宽大的仁慈之心正好平衡了对别人不公正的严厉。他还无缘无故地易怒。不止一次，我被叫去为他读书时，看到他独自坐在书房里，头埋在交叉的胳膊里；他抬起头时，忧郁和几乎恶意的怒容使他的脸色显得暗沉下来。但是，我相信他不稳定的情绪，严厉和以前道德上的种种不当，（我说以前，因为现在他似乎都一一纠正了。）都源自他命运的某种磨难。我认为他比起那些由环境造就，接受的教育所灌输或命运造化而形成上述特征的人相比，天生更善良，有着更高尚的品德和更纯洁的品位。我觉得他有优秀的潜质，只是现在它们现在有点被糟蹋了，纠缠在一起。我不能否认，我会为他的痛苦而痛苦——不管是何种痛苦，我都愿意做任何事来减轻它。
Though I had now extinguished my candle and was laid down in bed, I could not sleep for thinking of his look when he paused in the avenue, and told how his destiny had risen up before him, and dared him to be happy at Thornfield.
虽然我现在已经熄灭蜡烛，躺在床上，但是我睡不着，回忆着他在林荫道上停下脚步时的神情，告诉我他的命运如何出现在他面前，称他不会在桑菲尔德庄园过得开心。
"Why not?" I asked myself. "What alienates him from the house? Will he leave it again soon? Mrs. Fairfax said he seldom stayed here longer than a fortnight at a time; and he has now been resident eight weeks. If he does go, the change will be doleful. Suppose he should be absent spring, summer, and autumn: how joyless sunshine and fine days will seem!” 
“为什么会不开心呢？”我问自己。“是什么使他疏远这所房子呢？他不久又要离开吗？费尔法克斯太太说他每次在此停留的时间都不超过两个星期。而现在他已经在这儿住了八个星期了。如果他真的要离开，带来的变化一定会令人悲哀的。试想他整个春天、夏天和秋天都不在，那么阳光和晴朗的日子该显得多么无趣！”
I hardly know whether I had slept or not after this musing; at any rate, I started wide awake on hearing a vague murmur, peculiar and lugubrious, which sounded, I thought, just above me. I wished I had kept my candle burning: the night was drearily dark; my spirits were depressed. I rose and sat up in bed, listening. The sound was hushed.
我不清楚这样左思右想后，我是否入睡了。不过，一阵模糊的低语突然把我完全惊醒了。这个声响奇怪而悲惨，听起来就在我的上方。我真希望没有熄灭蜡烛，夜晚黑得可怕，我感到非常压抑。我起身坐在床上听着。那声音消失了。
I tried again to sleep; but my heart beat anxiously: my inward tranquillity was broken. The clock, far down in the hall, struck two. Just then it seemed my chamber door was touched; as if fingers had swept the panels in groping a way along the dark gallery outside. I said, "Who is there?" Nothing answered. I was chilled with fear.
我又试着入睡，但是我的心忧虑地跳动着，内心的平静被打破了。远在楼下大厅里的钟响了两下。就在那时，好像有人碰了一下我的卧室门，就像人在黑暗中摸索着在走廊走时，手指划过镶板。我问道：“谁在外边？”没有人回答。一阵恐惧袭来，我打了个寒颤。
All at once I remembered that it might be Pilot, who, when the kitchen door chanced to be left open, not unfrequently found his way up to the threshold of Mr. Rochester's chamber: I had seen him lying there myself in the mornings. The idea calmed me somewhat: I lay down. Silence composes the nerves; and as an unbroken hush now reigned again through the whole house, I began to feel the return of slumber. But it was not fated that I should sleep that night. A dream had scarcely approached my ear, when it fled affrighted, scared by a marrow freezing incident enough.
突然我想到可能是派洛特。厨房门偶尔开着时，他常常会跑到罗切斯特先生的门口。我有好几个早晨都看到它卧在那儿睡。这个想法使我平静了一些，我躺下了。寂静安抚了我紧张的神经；现在整座房子又恢复了安静，没有一点声响，我又感到了睡意。但是，我那天晚上好像注定不能入睡。我的梦还没开始，就被一件恐惧得足以渗入骨髓的事情吓跑了。
This was a demoniac laugh—low, suppressed, and deep—uttered, as it seemed, at the very keyhole of my chamber door. The head of my bed was near the door, and I thought at first the goblin-laugher stood at my bedside—or rather, crouched by my pillow: but I rose, looked round, and could see nothing; while, as I still gazed, the unnatural sound was reiterated: and I knew it came from behind the panels. My first impulse was to rise and fasten the bolt; my next, again to cry out, "Who is there?" 
这是一声如恶魔般的笑——低沉而压抑，好像是从我卧室门的钥匙孔里传出来的一样。我的床头就挨着门，起初我还以为这发出笑声的恶鬼就站在我的床边，或者甚至就蜷缩在我的枕头边。但是我起身，向四周望了一下，却什么也没看到。而就在我凝视四周时，那个奇异的声音又响起来了。这时我弄明白了，声音是从镶板背后传出来的。我的第一反应是起来把门闩牢，接下来大喊一声：“是谁在那儿呀？”
Something gurgled and moaned. Ere long, steps retreated up the gallery towards the third storey staircase: a door had lately been made to shut in that staircase; I heard it open and close, and all was still.
我听到了一阵咯咯声和呻吟的声音。瞬间，我听到一阵脚步声穿过走廊朝三楼的楼梯走去了。不久前，那儿装了一扇门，总是紧闭着。我听到那扇门打开又关上了，周围寂静无声。
"Was that Grace Poole? and is she possessed with a devil?" thought I. Impossible now to remain longer by myself: I must go to Mrs. Fairfax. I hurried on my frock and a shawl; I withdrew the bolt and opened the door with a trembling hand. There was a candle burning just outside, and on the matting in the gallery. I was surprised at this circumstance: but still more was I amazed to perceive the air quite dim, as if filled with smoke; and, while looking to the right hand and left, to find whence these blue wreaths issued, I became further aware of a strong smell of burning.
“是格雷斯·普尔吗？她撞上鬼了吗？”我想着。我再也不能独自呆在这屋里了，我必须去找费尔法克斯太太。我匆忙穿上大衣，戴上围巾，用颤抖的手拉开门闩，打开了门。门外一支正在燃烧的蜡烛，搁在走廊的席子上。看到这个情景，我吃了一惊。但更让我觉得不可思议的是空气很暗淡，好像充斥着烟雾一样。我左右寻找是什么引起了这些蓝色烟雾时，才意识到有一股强烈的烧焦味。
Something creaked: it was a door ajar; and that door was Mr. Rochester's, and the smoke rushed in a cloud from thence. I thought no more of Mrs. Fairfax; I thought no more of Grace Poole, or the laugh: in an instant, I was within the chamber. Tongues of flame darted round the bed: the curtains were on fire. In the midst of blaze and vapour, Mr. Rochester lay stretched motionless, in deep sleep.
什么东西响了一下，我看到一扇门半开着，是罗切斯特先生房间的门，大团烟雾正从那里逸出。我来不及再想费尔法克斯太太，或是格雷斯·普尔，或是刚才听到的笑声，我立即就冲进了那个房间。火舌在床周围乱窜，帐幔已经着火了。在这熊熊火焰和烟雾中，罗切斯特先生一动不动地躺在床上，还在沉睡中。
"Wake! wake!" I cried. I shook him, but he only murmured and turned: the smoke had stupefied him. Not a moment could be lost: the very sheets were kindling, I rushed to his basin and ewer; fortunately, one was wide and the other deep, and both were filled with water. I heaved them up, deluged the bed and its occupant, flew back to my own room, brought my own water jug, baptized the couch afresh, and, by God's aid, succeeded in extinguishing the flames which were devouring it.
“醒醒！醒醒！”我大叫道。我摇着他的身子，但他只低声说了什么，翻了个身，烟雾已经使他神志不清了。一分钟也不能耽误了。床单也着火了，我冲过去拿他的盆和水罐；幸亏那个盆很大，水罐很深，两个都装满了水。我端起盆和水罐，把水浇到床上和睡在其上的罗切斯特先生的身上，又飞快地跑到自己的房间，端来我自己的水罐，再次浇向床铺。感谢上帝，我终于扑灭了正吞噬整个床铺的大火。
The hiss of the quenched element, the breakage of a pitcher which I flung from my hand when I had emptied it, and, above all, the splash of the shower bath I had liberally bestowed, roused Mr. Rochester at last. Though it was now dark, I knew he was awake; because I heard him fulminating strange anathemas at finding himself lying in a pool of water.
灭火时发出的嘶嘶声，我把水泼出去后慌忙扔下的盆盆罐罐落地时发出的声响，重要的是，我毫无顾忌地泼洒的淋浴般的水，最终惊醒了罗切斯特先生。虽然屋里很暗，我还是知道他醒了，因为我听到了他发现自己睡在水泊之中时盛怒之下发出的古怪的咒骂。
"Is there a flood?" he cried.
他大叫道：“发水了吗？”
"No, sir," I answered; "but there has been a fire: get up, do; you are quenched now; I will fetch you a candle.” 
我答道：“没有，先生。但是刚才发生了火灾，请起来吧，您全身都湿透了。我马上给您取一支蜡烛去。”
"In the name of all the elves in Christendom, is that Jane Eyre?" he demanded. "What have you done with me, witch, sorceress? Who is in the room besides you? Have you plotted to drown me?" 
他诘问道：“看在基督徒中所有精灵的份上，这是简·爱吗？”“你这个女巫婆，女法师，到底对我做了什么？还有谁在房间里？你是故意谋划要淹死我吗？”
"I will fetch you a candle, sir; and, in Heaven's name, get up. Somebody has plotted something: you cannot too soon find out who and what it is.” 
“我会给您拿一支蜡烛；先生，看在老天的份上，起来吧。的确有人图谋不轨，但您也不能马上发现是谁，这个阴谋是什么。”
"There! I am up now; but at your peril you fetch a candle yet: wait two minutes till I get into some dry garments, if any dry there be—yes, here is my dressing gown. Now run!” 
“原来是这样！我起来了，但你还要冒着危险去取蜡烛。等我两分钟，我要找一些干衣服，要是能找到的话——嗯，我找到晨袍了。现在快跑！”
I did run; I brought the candle which still remained in the gallery. He took it from my hand, held it up, and surveyed the bed, all blackened and scorched, the sheets drenched, the carpet round swimming in water.
我跑过去，拿回了还留在走廊里的那支蜡烛。他从我手里拿过蜡烛，举起来照着检查床铺。一片焦黑，床单湿漉漉的，四周的地毯也泡在水里。
"What is it? and who did it?" he asked. I briefly related to him what had transpired: the strange laugh I had heard in the gallery: the step ascending to the third storey; the smoke,—the smell of fire which had conducted me to his room; in what state I had found matters there, and how I had deluged him with all the water I could lay hands on.
“这是怎么回事？谁干的？”他问我。我简要地告诉了他发生了什么。我听到的从走廊里传来的奇怪笑声，往三楼走的脚步声，烟雾——引导我来到他房间的烧焦味，以及我到他房间时看到的状况，和我是如何把我能找到的水都浇到了他身上。
He listened very gravely; his face, as I went on, expressed more concern than astonishment; he did not immediately speak when I had concluded.
他很严肃地听着。我讲述时发现他的表情更多是忧虑而非惊讶。我讲完后他没有立即讲话。
"Shall I call Mrs. Fairfax?" I asked.
“我要去叫费尔法克斯太太吗？”我问他。
"Mrs. Fairfax? No; what the deuce would you call her for? What can she do? Let her sleep unmolested." 
“费尔法克斯太太？不用了，你叫她又能怎么样呢？她能干什么？让她安稳地睡吧。”
"Then I will fetch Leah, and wake John and his wife." 
“那我去叫利厄和约翰夫妇吧。”
"Not at all: just be still. You have a shawl on. If you are not warm enough, you may take my cloak yonder; wrap it about you, and sit down in the armchair: there,—I will put it on. Now place your feet on the stool, to keep them out of the wet. I am going to leave you a few minutes. I shall take the candle. Remain where you are till I return; be as still as a mouse. I must pay a visit to the second storey. Don't move, remember, or call any one." 
“不用了，什么也不用做。披上你的披肩。如果还是冷，可以穿上我那边那件斗篷，裹在身上然后坐在那个扶手椅里——来，我帮你穿上。把脚放在凳子上，别浸在水里。我要离开几分钟。我要把蜡烛也拿走。我回来之前，你不要走动，要像老鼠一样安静。我必须要到二楼去一下。记住，不要动，不要叫别人。”
He went: I watched the light withdraw. He passed up the gallery very softly, unclosed the staircase door with as little noise as possible, shut it after him, and the last ray vanished. I was left in total darkness. I listened for some noise, but heard nothing. A very long time elapsed. I grew weary: it was cold, in spite of the cloak; and then I did not see the use of staying, as I was not to rouse the house. I was on the point of risking Mr. Rochester's displeasure by disobeying his orders, when the light once more gleamed dimly on the gallery wall, and I heard his unshod feet tread the matting. "I hope it is he," thought I, "and not something worse." 
他走了，我看着烛光渐弱。他轻轻地穿过走廊，尽量不出声地打开楼梯处的门，又从背后关上，最后一丝光线也消失了。我被留在一片漆黑之中。我想听到一些声音，却什么也听不到。过了很长一段时间了。我开始累了。披着斗篷还是觉得很冷。后来，我觉得既然他不让我叫醒其他人，那我留在这儿也无济于事。我正要冒着惹怒罗切斯特先生的危险离开——违背他的命令，这时，光线又微弱地照在走廊的墙上，我听到他光脚走在席子上的声音。“我希望是他，而不是别的更可怕的东西。”我想着。
He reentered, pale and very gloomy. "I have found it all out," said he, setting his candle down on the washstand; "it is as I thought." 
他走回到房间，脸色苍白而忧郁。他把蜡烛放在脸盆架上，说：“真相大白了，和我想的一样。”
"How, sir?" 
“怎么回事，先生？”
He made no reply, but stood with his arms folded, looking on the ground. At the end of a few minutes he inquired in rather a peculiar tone— 
他没有回答，双臂交叉在胸前站着，看着地面。过了几分钟，他用一种很奇怪的语调问我——
"I forget whether you said you saw anything when you opened your chamber door." 
“我忘了你有没有说你打开你的卧室门时看到了什么。”
"No, sir, only the candlestick on the ground." 
“没有，先生，我除了地上放着的烛台外什么也没看到。”
"But you heard an odd laugh? You have heard that laugh before, I should think, or something like it?" 
“但你听到了奇怪的笑声？我想你之前也听到过那种笑声，或是类似的？”
"Yes, sir: there is a woman who sews here, called Grace Poole,—she laughs in that way. She is a singular person.” 
“我听到过，先生。一位名叫格雷斯·普尔的做针线活的女仆——她就像那样笑。她很古怪。”
"Just so. Grace Poole—you have guessed it. She is, as you say, singular—very. Well, I shall reflect on the subject. Meantime, I am glad that you are the only person, besides myself, acquainted with the precise details of tonight's incident. You are no talking fool: say nothing about it. I will account for this state of affairs" (pointing to the bed): "and now return to your own room. I shall do very well on the sofa in the library for the rest of the night. It is near four:—in two hours the servants will be up.” 
“就是这样。格雷斯·普尔——你猜到了。她就是，像你说的那样，很古怪——非常古怪。嗯，关于这件事，我会再多加考虑的。同时，我很高兴你是除我之外唯一一个目睹今晚这件事具体细节的人。你不是到处乱讲的傻子，不要再提这件事。我会对这件事的状况作出解释。”（他指了指床），“现在，回你房间去。我在书房的沙发上睡到天亮也不错。现在快四点了，仆人们再过两小时就要起床了。”
"Goodnight, then, sir," said I, departing.
我起身离开：“那么晚安，先生。”
He seemed surprised—very inconsistently so, as he had just told me to go.
他像是吃了一惊——他刚刚让我离开，真是矛盾。
"What!" he exclaimed, "are you quitting me already, and in that way?" 
“什么！”他叫道，“你这就要离开我，就这样吗？”
"You said I might go, sir." 
“先生，您说我可以走了。”
"But not without taking leave; not without a word or two of acknowledgment and goodwill: not, in short, in that brief, dry fashion. Why, you have saved my life!—snatched me from a horrible and excruciating death! and you walk past me as if we were mutual strangers! At least shake hands.” 
“可你总不能不说些告辞的话，说一两句答谢和祝愿的话就走。总之，不能以那么粗略枯燥的方式告别。怎么，你可是救了我的命！你把我从痛苦骇人的鬼门关拉了回来！可你现在要向我们互不认识一样从我身旁走过！至少我们要握手。”
He held out his hand; I gave him mine: he took it first in one, them in both his own.
他伸出手，我把手伸向他。他先用一只手握着，然后用双手握住。
"You have saved my life: I have a pleasure in owing you so immense a debt. I cannot say more. Nothing else that has being would have been tolerable to me in the character of creditor for such an obligation: but you: it is different;—I feel your benefits no burden, Jane.” 
“你救了我的命。我很荣幸欠你这么多情。我不知道再说什么了。如果我欠了别人这么多情，我一定无法忍受任何事情。但是你不一样，你对我的恩情让我感觉不到负担，简。”
He paused; gazed at me: words almost visible trembled on his lips,—but his voice was checked.
他停顿了一下，注视着我。我几乎都看到话语在他的嘴唇上颤动——但他的声音被堵住了。
"Goodnight again, sir. There is no debt, benefit, burden, obligation, in the case." "To put Adele to bed: it is past her bedtime.” "I knew," he continued, "you would do me good in some way, at some time;—I saw it in your eyes when I first beheld you: their expression and smile did not”—(again he stopped)—"did not" (he proceeded hastily) "strike delight to my very inmost heart so for nothing. People talk of natural sympathies; I have heard of good genii: there are grains of truth in the wildest fable. My cherished preserver, goodnight!” 
“再次祝您晚安，先生。这件事上不存在债务，恩情，负担或是责任。”“我知道，”他继续说，“我第一次见你的时候，就从你的眼睛中看到你会在某个时刻以某种方式帮我。你眼中的表情和笑容没有——”（他又顿了一下）——“没有”（他急促地说）“这么无缘无故地在我的内心深处激起快乐的感觉。人们常常讲天性悯人，我也听说过善良的鬼怪，最荒诞的寓言也有些真理。我珍爱的保护神，晚安！”
Strange energy was in his voice, strange fire in his look.
他的声音中有一种奇怪的力量，他的眼睛闪着奇怪的火芒。
"I am glad I happened to be awake," I said: and then I was going.
“我很高兴自己碰巧醒了。”我说，然后正要走出去。
"What! you WILL go?" 
“什么！你就要走了？”
"I am cold, sir." 
“先生，我很冷。”
"Cold? Yes,—and standing in a pool! Go, then, Jane; go!” But he still retained my hand, and I could not free it. I bethought myself of an expedient.
“冷？是呀——你还在水泊中站着！走吧，简，那你走吧！”但是他仍然握着我的手，我无法挣脱。于是我为自己想了一个权宜之计。
"I think I hear Mrs. Fairfax move, sir," said I.
我说：“我想我听到费尔法克斯太太的声音了。先生。”
"Well, leave me．” he relaxed his fingers, and I was gone.
“好吧，你走吧。”他松开了手指，我走了。
I regained my couch, but never thought of sleep. Till morning dawned I was tossed on a buoyant but unquiet sea, where billows of trouble rolled under surges of joy. I thought sometimes I saw beyond its wild waters a shore, sweet as the hills of Beulah; and now and then a freshening gale, wakened by hope, bore my spirit triumphantly towards the bourne: but I could not reach it, even in fancy—a counteracting breeze blew off land, and continually drove me back. Sense would resist delirium: judgment would warn passion. Too feverish to rest, I rose as soon as day dawned.
我回到床上，却毫无睡意。直到黎明时分，我还在欢愉却不平静的海上辗转，烦恼的波涛在欢喜的海浪下翻滚。我想有时我看到在这波涛汹涌的大海那边有一处海岸，像比尤拉的群山一样美丽。时不时地，一阵让人神清气爽的大风在希望中醒来，载着我的灵魂一路高歌地向着彼岸进发。但是我怎么也不能到达那里，甚至在幻想中也不能——一阵迎面吹来的清风从陆地刮来，不断地把我吹回到原地。理智会抵制狂热，判断力会警告激情。我兴奋地难以成眠，天一亮就起来了。
