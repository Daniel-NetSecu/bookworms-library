VI  

六  

One afternoon, as I came down from my quarters to go out, I found Miss Tita in the sala: it was our first encounter on that ground since I had come to the house.
一天下午，我从自己的房间走下来要出门的时候，发现蒂塔小姐在正厅里：自从我搬进来之后，这是我们两人第一次在这里碰面。
She put on no air of being there by accident; there was an ignorance of such arts in her angular, diffident directness. That I might be quite sure she was waiting for me she informed me of the fact and told me that Miss Bordereau wished to see me: she would take me into the room at that moment if I had time. If I had been late for a love tryst I would have stayed for this, and I quickly signified that I should be delighted to wait upon the old lady.
她完全没有表现出巧遇的样子。在她这种笨拙而缺乏自信的直率里，是没有这样的技巧的。我几乎可以肯定，她在等我。她立即告诉我一个情况，说博尔德罗小姐想见我：只要我有时间，她马上带我去那间屋子。就算是和恋人约会要迟到了，我也要为此留下。于是我马上说，我很高兴博尔德罗小姐能接见我。
"She wants to talk with you—to know you," Miss Tita said, smiling as if she herself appreciated that idea; and she led me to the door of her aunt's apartment. I stopped her a moment before she had opened it, looking at her with some curiosity.
“她想和你谈谈——了解你。”蒂塔小姐说，微笑着，好像她对这个主意很欣赏。然后她带我去了她姑妈的房间。她开门之前，我拦住了她一会儿，好奇地看着她。
I told her that this was a great satisfaction to me and a great honor; but all the same I should like to ask what had made Miss Bordereau change so suddenly. It was only the other day that she wouldn't suffer me near her.
我告诉她，这让我非常高兴，也深感荣幸。但同样，我也想问问博尔德罗小姐为什么突然改变了。仅仅在几天之前，她还不能忍受我靠近她。
Miss Tita was not embarrassed by my question; she had as many little unexpected serenities as if she told fibs, but the odd part of them was that they had on the contrary their source in her truthfulness. "Oh, my aunt changes," she answered; "it's so terribly dull—I suppose she's tired.” 
蒂塔小姐没有因为我的问题而感到尴尬。她有许多出人意料的小镇定，就像在说些不痛不痒的小谎话那样。但奇怪的是，这些谎话又都是出自她的诚实。“啊，姑妈变了，”她回答，“日子太乏味了——我想，她厌倦了。”
"But you told me that she wanted more and more to be alone." 
“但你告诉过我，她越来越喜欢独自一人。”
Poor Miss Tita colored, as if she found me over-insistent. "Well, if you don't believe she wants to see you—I haven't invented it! I think people often are capricious when they are very old.” 
可怜的蒂塔小姐脸红了，她似乎觉得我太过执着。“好，如果你不信她想见你——我没有胡说！我想，人年龄太大的时候，总是反复无常。”
"That's perfectly true. I only wanted to be clear as to whether you have repeated to her what I told you the other night.” 
“那倒是千真万确。我只想搞清楚，我到底应不应该对她重复那天晚上我对你说过的话。”
"What you told me?" 
“你告诉我什么了？”
"About Jeffrey Aspern—that I am looking for materials.” 
“关于杰弗里·阿斯本——我在找他的资料。”
"If I had told her do you think she would have sent for you?" 
“如果我告诉了她，你觉得她还会找你来吗？”
"That's exactly what I want to know. If she wants to keep him to herself she might have sent for me to tell me so.” 
“我想知道的正是这点。如果她把他当成自己的秘密，她有可能会找我，告诉我这一点。“
"She won't speak of him," said Miss Tita. Then as she opened the door she added in a lower tone, "I have told her nothing." 
“她不会提到他的。”蒂塔小姐说。然后她打开门，压低声音补充道：“我什么也没有告诉她。”
The old woman was sitting in the same place in which I had seen her last, in the same position, with the same mystifying bandage over her eyes. her welcome was to turn her almost invisible face to me and show me that while she sat silent she saw me clearly.
这个老太太坐在上次和我见面时的那个地方，同样的姿势，同样神秘秘地用绷带遮着眼睛。她的欢迎仪式就是把她那张看不见的脸转向我，表明虽然她不说话，也能把我看得清清楚楚的。
I made no motion to shake hands with her; I felt too well on this occasion that that was out of place forever.
我没示意和她握手。我太清楚了，在这种情况下，握手永远是不合适的。
It had been sufficiently enjoined upon me that she was too sacred for that sort of reciprocity—too venerable to touch.
我已经充分地领教过了。她太神圣了，所以这种礼数往来不适合——她太庄严了，不容触碰。
There was something so grim in her aspect (it was partly the accident of her green shade), as I stood there to be measured, that I ceased on the spot to feel any doubt as to her knowing my secret, though I did not in the least suspect that Miss Tita had not just spoken the truth.
她的样子看起来有点儿令人生畏（部分是由于绿罩子的缘故），而我站在那里等着她打量。她那严厉的样子让我当场毫不怀疑地认为，她已经知道了我的秘密。尽管我一点儿也不怀疑蒂塔小姐刚才讲的是真话。
She had not betrayed me, but the old woman's brooding instinct had served her; she had turned me over and over in the long, still hours, and she had guessed.
她没有背叛我，但这老太太思索的功力实在了得。在漫长静止的时光里，她反复地把我揣摩了很多遍，已经猜到了我的来意。
The worst of it was that she looked terribly like an old woman who at a pinch would burn her papers.
最糟糕的是，她看起来非常像是一位稍稍一受刺激就会把手稿烧掉的老妇人。
Miss Tita pushed a chair forward, saying to me, "This will be a good place for you to sit." 
蒂塔小姐把一把椅子往前推了推，对我说：“你在这里就好。”
As I took possession of it I asked after Miss Bordereau's health; expressed the hope that in spite of the very hot weather it was satisfactory. She replied that it was good enough—good enough; that it was a great thing to be alive.
我坐下来就问候博尔德罗小姐身体健康。我告诉她，尽管天气炎热，我还是希望她身体好。她回答说，她身体够好的——够好的，说活着是件很好的事情。
"Oh, as to that, it depends upon what you compare it with!" I exclaimed, laughing.
“哦，这一点啊，要看你和什么比较！”我笑着感叹道。
"I don't compare—I don't compare. If I did that I should have given everything up long ago.” 
“我不和什么比较——不比较。要是我比较的话，我早就放弃一切了。”
I liked to think that this was a subtle allusion to the rapture she had known in the society of Jeffrey Aspern—though it was true that such an allusion would have accorded ill with the wish I imputed to her to keep him buried in her soul.
我愿意把这看作是一种暗示，微妙地影射了她在杰弗里·阿斯本那个社会里所知的欢乐——虽然这种暗示与我的希望格格不入，我希望她把他深埋心里。
What it accorded with was my constant conviction that no human being had ever had a more delightful social gift than his, and what it seemed to convey was that nothing in the world was worth speaking of if one pretended to speak of that.
但与我一直以来所深信的东西一致的是，没有人能有比他更有令人愉悦的社交天赋了。她的话要传达的是，要是有人胆敢说到这一点，世界上没有什么是值得一提的。
But one did not! Miss Tita sat down beside her aunt, looking as if she had reason to believe some very remarkable conversation would come off between us.
但没有人会这样说!蒂塔小姐在她姑妈旁边坐下，看起来好像有理由相信，我们两人之间将会有非常精彩的对话。
"It's about the beautiful flowers," said the old lady; "you sent us so many—I ought to have thanked you for them before. But I don't write letters and I receive only at long intervals.” 
“关于那些美丽的鲜花，”老太太说道，“你送了那么多给我们——我早就该谢谢你了。但我没有写信的习惯，而且会客也是很少有的事情。”
She had not thanked me while the flowers continued to come, but she departed from her custom so far as to send for me as soon as she began to fear that they would not come any more.
当我不断送花的时候，她没有感谢过我。但当她一开始害怕我不再送花的时候，竟一反常态，要见我。
I noted this; I remembered what an acquisitive propensity she had shown when it was a question of extracting gold from me, and I privately rejoiced at the happy thought I had had in suspending my tribute. She had missed it and she was willing to make a concession to bring it back.
我注意到了这一点。我记得，我身上有利可图时，她表现出了如此惊人的索取欲望。而我暗自高兴，庆幸自己停止了送花。她想念那些花，而且也愿意做出让步来重新获得那些花。
At the first sign of this concession I could only go to meet her. "I am afraid you have not had many, of late, but they shall begin again immediately—tomorrow, tonight.” 
但这个让步的第一个信号，只是让我见到了她。“最近恐怕你没有收到多少花，但是我马上开始再送过来——明天，今晚就送来。”
"Oh, do send us some tonight!" Miss Tita cried, as if it were an immense circumstance.
“哦，今晚就请送来吧！”蒂塔小姐央求道，就好像这是件大事。
"What else should you do with them? It isn't a manly taste to make a bower of your room," the old woman remarked.
“你拿这些花还有什么用途？把自己的房间变成个凉亭可不是男性的品味。”这个老妇人提醒道。
"I don't make a bower of my room, but I am exceedingly fond of growing flowers, of watching their ways. There is nothing unmanly in that: it has been the amusement of philosophers, of statesmen in retirement; even I think of great captains.” 
“我可不是把自己的房间变成凉亭，但我极度喜欢种花，观察它们的风采。这没有什么不男子气概的：哲学家，退休的政治家，我想，甚至是大将军们，都从中享受乐趣呢。”
"I suppose you know you can sell them—those you don't use," Miss Bordereau went on. "I daresay they wouldn't give you much for them; still, you could make a bargain.” 
“我想你知道，你可以卖掉这些花——那些你用不着的花，”博尔德罗小姐接着说，“我敢说，别人不会给你出高价，但你还是可以做一笔买卖。”
"Oh, I have never made a bargain, as you ought to know. My gardener disposes of them and I ask no questions." 
“啊，正如你知道的，我从来不做生意。我的园丁处理掉它们，而我并没有意见。”
"I would ask a few, I can promise you!" said Miss Bordereau; and it was the first time I had heard her laugh. I could not get used to the idea that this vision of pecuniary profit was what drew out the divine Juliana most.
“我倒想要几束，我保证！”博尔德罗小姐说，这是我第一次听见她笑。我没法习惯这个想法：金钱利润这类的想法最能打动这神圣的朱莉安娜。
"Come into the garden yourself and pick them; come as often as you like; come every day. They are all for you," I pursued, addressing Miss Tita and carrying off this veracious statement by treating it as an innocent joke. "I can't imagine why she doesn't come down," I added, for Miss Bordereau's benefit.
“到花园里来，你亲自去摘吧。你随时都可以来，每天都行。它们全都是你们的。”我讨好地对着蒂塔小姐说，把这句实话当成一个天真的笑话应付过去。“我真不明白她为什么不到花园里来。”我又说了一句，是说给博尔德罗小姐听的。
"You must make her come; you must come up and fetch her," said the old woman, to my stupefaction. "That odd thing you have made in the corner would be a capital place for her to sit." 
“你得要叫她去。你必须上来接她。”这个老太太说。我惊呆了。“你在园子角落里做的那个古怪的东西，对她来说是个极好的坐处。”
The allusion to my arbor was irreverent; it confirmed the impression I had already received that there was a flicker of impertinence in Miss Bordereau's talk, a strange mocking lambency which must have been a part of her adventurous youth and which had outlived passions and faculties.
她这样暗示我的凉亭是不礼貌的。这更进一步说明我之前对她的印象，那就是博尔德罗小姐讲话有一些莽撞的意味。她讲话时有一种陌生和戏谑的巧妙意味，那必是她年轻时大胆率性的一部分，比激情与才情都更持久。
Nonetheless I asked, "Wouldn't it be possible for you to come down there yourself? Wouldn't it do you good to sit there in the shade, in the sweet air?” 
尽管如此，我还是问道：“你可以亲自下来吗？在这么甜美的空气里，坐在阴凉的地方，对你来说不是一种享受吗？”
"Oh, sir, when I move out of this it won't be to sit in the air, and I'm afraid that any that may be stirring around me won't be particularly sweet! It will be a very dark shade indeed. But that won't be just yet," Miss Bordereau continued cannily, as if to correct any hopes that this courageous allusion to the last receptacle of her mortality might lead me to entertain. "I have sat here many a day and I have had enough of arbors in my time. But I'm not afraid to wait till I'm called.” 
“哦，先生，我出门去，不是要坐在那香气里，而且恐怕在我周围流动的空气就不怎么甜美了！那只不过是个隐蔽漆黑的地方罢了。不过，目前还不至于如此。”博尔德罗小姐继续狡黠地说道，似乎是为了纠正我想入非非的希冀，因为她的话让人联想到她遗体的容身之所。“我已经坐在这里很多日子了。我年轻的时候享受够了凉亭了。但我不害怕等待主的召唤。”
Miss Tita had expected some interesting talk, but perhaps she found it less genial on her aunt's side (considering that I had been sent for with a civil intention) than she had hoped. As if to give the conversation a turn that would put our companion in a light more favorable she said to me, "Didn't I tell you the other night that she had sent me out? You see that I can do what I like!” 
蒂塔小姐希望有趣的谈话，但是她发现她姑妈这一方没有如她所希望的那样亲切（考虑到我是被礼貌地请来的）。似乎是为了转变话题，好让我们的交谈变得稍稍融洽一点儿，她对我说：“我有没有告诉你，那天晚上，是她叫我出去的？你看，我可以做我想做的事情！”
"Do you pity her—do you teach her to pity herself?" Miss Bordereau demanded before I had time to answer this appeal.
“你同情她吗——你会教她爱惜她自己吗？”博尔德罗小姐问道，我还没有来得及回答这个请求。
"She has a much easier life than I had when I was her age." 
“她的生活，比我像她这个年纪的时候容易多了。”
"You must remember that it has been quite open to me to think you rather inhuman." 
“你得记住，这很容易让我觉得你不近人情。”
"Inhuman? That's what the poets used to call the women a hundred years ago.
“不近人情？那是一百年以前，诗人们称呼女人的话。
Don't try that; you won't do as well as they!" Juliana declared. "There is no more poetry in the world—that I know of at least. But I won't bandy words with you," she pursued, and I well remember the old-fashioned, artificial sound she gave to the speech. "You have made me talk, talk! It isn't good for me at all.” 
不要用这个词，你不会用得跟他们一样好！”朱莉安娜说，“世上再也没有诗歌啦——至少我不知道还有。但我不会和你争吵。”她继续道。而我记得很清楚她说话的时候用到的那种老派做作的声音。“你让我说啊说啊！这对我一点儿也不好。”
I got up at this and told her I would take no more of her time; but she detained me to ask, "Do you remember, the day I saw you about the rooms, that you offered us the use of your gondola?" And when I assented, promptly, struck again with her disposition to make a "good thing" of being there and wondering what she now had in her eye, she broke out, "Why don't you take that girl out in it and show her the place?” 
我一听，便站起身，对她说我不再占用她的时间了。她却把我留下，问我：“你记不记得，我因为租房子的事情而同你见面的那天，你邀请我坐你的平底小船？”我立即称是，心想她是不是要利用我在场的这件“好事”，不知道她现在心里在想什么。这时，她开口说：“你为什么不带这个姑娘坐船，带她在这附近转转？”
"Oh, dear Aunt, what do you want to do with me?" cried the "girl" with a piteous quaver. "I know all about the place!" 
“哦，亲爱的姑妈，你想对我做什么？”这“姑娘”嚷道，带点儿可怜的颤音，“我了解这里的一切！”
"Well then, go with him as a cicerone!" said Miss Bordereau with an effort of something like cruelty in her implacable power of retort—an incongruous suggestion that she was a sarcastic, profane, cynical old woman.
“那好，那就像个导游那样和他一起出去吧！”博尔德罗小姐说。她不留余地的反驳带有一点儿冷酷的效果——这不协调地反映出，她是一个爱讥诮的、粗俗的，又爱冷嘲热讽的老女人。
"Haven't we heard that there have been all sorts of changes in all these years? You ought to see them and at your age (I don't mean because you're so young) you ought to take the chances that come. You're old enough, my dear, and this gentleman won't hurt you. He will show you the famous sunsets, if they still go on—DO they go on? The sun set for me so long ago. But that's not a reason.
“我们这些年难道没有听到附近发生的种种变化吗？你该去看看，以你的年纪（我不是说因为你太年轻），你该抓住到来的机会。亲爱的，你年龄已经够大了，而且这位先生不会伤害你的。他会带你看著名的日落景色，要是还有的话——还有日落吗？我的太阳早就沉下去了。但那不是原因。
Besides, I shall never miss you; you think you are too important. Take her to the Piazza; it used to be very pretty," Miss Bordereau continued, addressing herself to me. "What have they done with the funny old church? I hope it hasn't tumbled down. let her look at the shops; she may take some money, she may buy what she likes.” 
另外，我绝不会想念你的，你以为自己太重要。带她到大广场去吧，那里以前很美。”博尔德罗小姐对我继续说。“那个滑稽的老教堂怎么样了，他们对它做了什么？我希望它没有倒塌。带她去商店看看，她可以带点儿钱，买点儿喜欢的东西。”
Poor Miss Tita had got up, discountenanced and helpless, and as we stood there before her aunt it would certainly have seemed to a spectator of the scene that the old woman was amusing herself at our expense.
可怜的蒂塔小姐站起身，扭扭怩怩，显得很无助。我们在她姑妈面前站着的样子，看起来会让旁观者觉得，这个老妇人在拿我们两人开玩笑。
Miss Tita protested, in a confusion of exclamations and murmurs; but I lost no time in saying that if she would do me the honor to accept the hospitality of my boat I would engage that she should not be bored. Or if she did not want so much of my company the boat itself, with the gondolier, was at her service; he was a capital oar and she might have every confidence.
蒂塔小姐又是惊呼，又是嘟囔地表示抗议。但我立即抓住时机，说如果她肯赏光坐我的船，让我款待她，那我会保证不让她觉得无聊。或者，如果她不希望由我陪伴，那么小船和我的船夫都听候她的差遣。船夫划船的技术了得，她完全可以放心。
Miss Tita, without definitely answering this speech, looked away from me, out of the window, as if she were going to cry; and I remarked that once we had Miss Bordereau's approval we could easily come to an understanding. We would take an hour, whichever she liked, one of the very next days. As I made my obeisance to the old lady I asked her if she would kindly permit me to see her again.
蒂塔小姐没有正面回应我的话，把眼神从我身上移开，望向窗外，要哭的样子。我说，一旦我们获得了博尔德罗小姐的同意，我们就可以很容易达成谅解了。接下来的某一天，我们可以抽出一小时的时间，任何时间都随她便。在我向她行礼告退的时候，我问这个老太太，她是否可以赏脸再和我见面。
For a moment she said nothing; then she inquired, "Is it very necessary to your happiness?" 
她沉默一阵，然后问道：“这直接关系到你的快乐心情吗？”
"It diverts me more than I can say." 
“难以用语言表达的快乐。”
"You are wonderfully civil. Don't you know it almost kills ME?” 
“你实在很有礼貌。但你不知道这几乎要了我的命吗？”
"How can I believe that when I see you more animated, more brilliant than when I came in?" 
“比起我刚来的时候，你现在更有精神，更有神采，我怎么能相信你这话呢？”
"That is very true, Aunt," said Miss Tita. "I think it does you good." 
“姑妈，他说得很对，”蒂塔小姐说，“我觉得这对你有好处。”
"Isn't it touching, the solicitude we each have that the other shall enjoy herself?" sneered Miss Bordereau. "If you think me brilliant today you don't know what you are talking about; you have never seen an agreeable woman. Don't try to pay me a compliment; I have been spoiled," she went on. "My door is shut, but you may sometimes knock." 
“我们关心对方，希望对方过得幸福，这是不是很感人？”博尔德罗小姐嘲讽地说道，“如果你们觉得我今天精神好，你们是不知所云。我从来就不是个好说话的女人。别想恭维我，我是被宠坏的人。”她接着说。“我的门关着，但你有时可以敲门。”
With this she dismissed me, and I left the room. The latch closed behind me, but Miss Tita, contrary to my hope, had remained within. I passed slowly across the hall and before taking my way downstairs I waited a little. My hope was answered; after a minute Miss Tita followed me.
她一边说这话一边送客，于是我就离开了房间。身后的门拴上了，但是蒂塔小姐，没有如我所愿，留在了里面。我慢吞吞地穿过大厅，在上楼之前还等了一会儿。我的希望有了回应，一分钟后，蒂塔小姐跟上了我。
"That's a delightful idea about the Piazza," I said. "When will you go—tonight, tomorrow?” 
“去大广场是个令人愉快的想法，”我说，“你打算什么时候去——今晚？明天？”
She had been disconcerted, as I have mentioned, but I had already perceived and I was to observe again that when Miss Tita was embarrassed she did not (as most women would have done) turn away from you and try to escape, but came closer, as it were, with a deprecating, clinging appeal to be spared, to be protected.
她一直惊慌不安，正如我之前提到的一样。但是我已经感觉到了，并且我要再确认一次，那就是，当蒂塔小姐觉得尴尬的时候，她不会（像大多数女性那样）转身跑开，试图逃避，而是会和你靠近，就像是以一种恳请的，粘人的样子讨得原谅，获得保护一样。

她的态度永远都带着一种祈求帮助和解释的感觉，而且世界上没有哪个女人比她更像喜剧演员了。从你表现出对她友善的那一刻开始，她就完全依赖你了，她的不自在就完全消失了。她自然而然地表现出最大的亲密，那种天真的亲密是她唯一能想到的方式。

她告诉我，她不知道姑妈到底在想什么，姑妈变得太快了，一定是在盘算什么。我回答说，她一定要弄明白姑妈的心思，然后告诉我。我们可以一起去弗洛里安吃个冰激凌，然后我们一边听乐队演奏，一边由她讲给我听。

“哦，想弄明白的话，我要花很长的时间！”她说道,相当发愁。而她不能保证当晚或是次晚就给我满意的答复。但我现在很有耐心，因为我明白我只有等她。而事实上，在那个周末，晚饭后一个迷人的夜晚，她上了我的平底小船，我还另外加了一个船夫，以示隆重。

我们五分钟就划进了大运河。她当时就发出了沉醉的低语，就像初来乍到的游客一样感觉新鲜。

她已经忘记了，在晴朗清亮的炎热夏夜，这条水道看起来有多么绚丽。忘记了在大理石府邸和波光之间泛舟的感觉，这波光会让人尽情舒心地交谈。

我们漂浮了很远很久。虽然蒂塔小姐没有尖声表达自己的喜悦，但我可以感觉到她在尽情地享受。她实在太高兴，简直是欣喜若狂。这整件事是莫大的解脱。

小船慢慢地划着，让她有时间享受。她听着船桨拍水的声音，那桨声在我们划入狭窄的运河的时候越来越大，越来越有音乐感，仿佛在展现着威尼斯似的。

最后她带着孩子般的害羞与不耐烦问：“我们不是要去大广场吗？那里才是我想看的！”

我马上要求船夫径直向那里划去，然后，我们都不说话，期望尽快到达。然而，又过了些时候，她自己突然说道：“我知道我姑妈的问题了：她怕你离开！”

“她为什么会那么想？”

“她觉得你不开心。那就是她现在转变的原因。”

“你是说，她想让我开心些？”

“嗯，她希望你别走，她希望你留下。”
"I suppose you mean on account of the rent," I remarked candidly.
“我想，你指的是因为租金的关系。”我坦率地说。

蒂塔小姐的坦白和我不相上下。“是的，你知道，这样我就可以得到更多的钱。”

“那她希望你得到多少？”我笑着问，“她该设定个金额，这样我就可以呆到钱够数的时候。”

“噢，我不高兴这样，”蒂塔小姐说，“让你来操劳，这真是闻所未闻。”

“但是，假如我自己有呆在威尼斯的理由呢？”
"Then it would be better for you to stay in some other house." 
“但你住别的房子更好些。”
"And what would your aunt say to that?" 
“那你姑妈对此怎么说？”
"She wouldn't like it at all. But I should think you would do well to give up your reasons and go away altogether.” 
“她一点儿也不喜欢这个主意。但我认为你最好放弃你的理由，然后彻底离开这里。”

“亲爱的蒂塔小姐！”我说，“放弃它们不容易啊！”

她没有立刻回答我，但过了一会儿，她开口道：“我想我知道你的动机是什么！”

“你当然知道，因为那天晚上我已经几乎全告诉你了，希望你能帮我达成呢。”
"I can't do that without being false to my aunt.” 
“如果不做对不起她的事，我就没办法做到。”

“哎，你想要的东西，她永远不会同意给你。有人索取过，有人写信给她。她大为光火。”

“那就是说，她的确有些有价值的文稿咯？”我立刻问道。

“哦，她什么都有！”蒂塔小姐叹口气,显得很疲惫，突然陷入了忧伤。

这些话调动了我的激动情绪，因为我把这看作是非常珍贵的证据。好几分钟，我都太激动了，讲不了话。而且这时候，平底船也正好靠近大广场了。

我们下船以后，我问同伴愿不愿意在广场四处走走，或者就坐到咖啡馆里去。她回答说，我喜欢什么，她就做什么——我只要再一次记住，她的时间非常有限。

我向她保证，我们有足够的时间两件事都做，于是我们绕着长拱廊走了一圈。一看到明亮的商店橱窗，她的精神就又振作了。她流连驻足，欣赏或是指责里面的东西，问我对这些事物的意见，推算它们的价格。

我的注意力从她身上游走开了。“哦，她什么都有！”她之前讲的这一句话在我的意识里不断回响。

我们最后坐在弗洛里安咖啡馆那圈热闹的桌子上，在广场上搭着的桌子中，我们找到了一张空的。那是个美好的夜晚，全世界都在户外；蒂塔小姐认为这是个让她重回社会的，无可比拟的吉兆。

她沉默不语，就像在思考着什么，带着一点儿隐秘的伤感，伤感机遇永远地失去了，而那些机遇本该很容易获得的。这给我一个机会对她说：“你刚刚不是说，你的姑妈有个计划要留下我，可以允许我偶尔见见她？”

听我这样说，博尔德罗小姐的侄女的脸上出现了一种独特的表情——一种承认自己无助的表情，一种祈求我公平慷慨地对她的表情。

我已经把她带到了大广场，让她置身于各种让人陶醉的事物之中，给予令她感动的关注，但如今我却让她认为，我所做的一切不过是种贿赂——一种让她转而同她姑妈作对的贿赂。

说来也够奇怪的，就像我后来想到的那样，我对她姑妈的个性缺乏尊重，她却一点儿也没有表现出厌恶的神情。如果什么不太重要（我认为）的东西遇上危险的话，这种个性会表现出最恶劣的一面。我觉得她并没有认真考虑过这一点。“你是说她做了什么坏事？”她过了一阵子问我。

“我要怎么阻止？”

“你不能从她身边拿走么？”

事情因此变得非常残酷了，虽然我肯定她没有讥诮的意图。

“啊，我是说，你可以让我看看，读一读。这不是为了我自己，我的意图里并没有个人的欲望。那只不过是公众会极其感兴趣的物件，对于杰弗里·阿斯本的生平来说，这有着不可估量的重要贡献。”
She listened to me in her usual manner, as if my speech were full of reference to things she had never heard of, and I felt particularly like the reporter of a newspaper who forces his way into a house of mourning. This was especially the case when after a moment she said. "There was a gentleman who some time ago wrote to her in very much those words. He also wanted her papers." 
她像往常一样倾听我讲话，仿佛我的话里满是她闻所未闻的事物似的。而我感觉自己像极了一位新闻主播，强行闯入了一户居丧的人家去进行报导一样。过了一阵子，她说话的时候，这种感觉更明显了。“前一段时间，有一位先生写信给她，说了非常类似的一段话。他也想要那些文稿。”
"And did she answer him?" I asked, rather ashamed of myself for not having her rectitude.
“那她回信了？”我说。这样问对她不公平，使我觉得很惭愧。
"Only when he had written two or three times. He made her very angry." 
“他写了两三次之后，她才回信的。他让她很生气。”
"And what did she say?" 
“那她说了什么？”
"She said he was a devil," Miss Tita replied simply.
“她说他是个恶棍。”蒂塔小姐坦白地说。
"She used that expression in her letter?" 
“她回信时也用了这个词？”
"Oh, no; she said it to me. She made me write to him." 
“啊，没有。她对我说的。她让我给他写了信。”
"And what did you say?" 
“那你写了什么？”
"I told him there were no papers at all." 
“我告诉他，那些文稿根本就不存在。”
"Ah, poor gentleman!" I exclaimed.
“哎，这位先生真可怜。”我叹口气。
"I knew there were, but I wrote what she bade me." 
“我知道文稿是有的，但我按姑妈的吩咐写了信。”
"Of course you had to do that. But I hope I shall not pass for a devil." 
“你当然要那么做了。但我希望我不要被当成个恶棍。”
"It will depend upon what you ask me to do for you," said Miss Tita, smiling.
“那得看你要我为你做什么事情。”蒂塔小姐微笑着说。
"Oh, if there is a chance of YOUR thinking so my affair is in a bad way! I shan't ask you to steal for me, nor even to fib—for you can't fib, unless on paper. But the principal thing is this—to prevent her from destroying the papers.” 
“哦，要是连你也这样想的话，我的事情也一定是糟糕的！我不会让你为我去偷，甚至不会叫你撒谎——因为，除了写信的时候，你根本都不会撒谎。但重要的是——要阻止她毁掉文稿。”
"Why, I have no control of her," said Miss Tita. "It's she who controls me.” 
“天哪，我不能控制她，”蒂塔小姐说，“是她在控制我。”
"But she doesn't control her own arms and legs, does she? The way she would naturally destroy her letters would be to burn them. Now she can't burn them without fire, and she can't get fire unless you give it to her.” 
“但她控制不了自己的胳膊和腿，不是吗？最自然地毁掉信件的方法就是烧掉它们。没有火她就烧不了，而如果你不给她，她就拿不到火。”
"I have always done everything she has asked," my companion rejoined. "Besides, there's Olimpia.” 
“我总是对她言听计从，”我的伙伴争辩说，“另外，还有欧林匹亚啊。”
I was on the point of saying that Olimpia was probably corruptible, but I thought it best not to sound that note. So I simply inquired if that faithful domestic could not be managed.
我本来正要说，欧林匹亚可能是可以收买的，但我还是觉得最好不要提这件事。于是我只是问她，姑妈是不是控制不了那个忠实的女仆。
"Everyone can be managed by my aunt," said Miss Tita. And then she observed that her holiday was over; she must go home.
“所有人都可能被姑妈控制。”蒂塔小姐说。然后，她想起她的假期该结束了，她必须要回家了。
I laid my hand on her arm, across the table, to stay her a moment. "What I want of you is a general promise to help me." 
我把手越过桌子，放在她的手臂上，让她停一会儿。“我想要的，只是你笼统地答应帮助我而已。”
"Oh, how can I—how can I?" she asked, wondering and troubled. She was half-surprised, half-frightened at my wishing to make her play an active part.
“噢，我怎么能——我怎么能？”她问道，很疑惑，也很困扰。见我希望她扮演一个积极的角色，她半是惊讶，半是害怕。
"This is the main thing: to watch her carefully and warn me in time, before she commits that horrible sacrilege.” 
“主要要做的事情就是：小心留神着她，在她做出那可怕的罪过之前，及时通知我。”
"I can't watch her when she makes me go out.” 
“她要我出来的时候，我没办法盯着她。”
"That's very true.” 
“这倒是事实。”
"And when you do, too." 
“而当你邀我出来的时候，也是如此。”
"Mercy on us; do you think she will have done anything tonight?" 
“天哪！你认为她今晚就会做什么事情？”
"I don't know; she is very cunning.” 
“我不知道，她很狡猾的。”
"Are you trying to frighten me?" I asked.
“你在试图吓退我？”我问。
I felt this inquiry sufficiently answered when my companion murmured in a musing, almost envious way, "Oh, but she loves them—she loves them!” 
我的同伴小声嘀咕，陷入沉思，带点儿妒忌的味道：“哦，但是她爱它们——她爱它们！”这时候，我觉得我的询问获得了足够的回答。
This reflection, repeated with such emphasis, gave me great comfort; but to obtain more of that balm I said, "If she shouldn't intend to destroy the objects we speak of before her death she will probably have made some disposition by will.” 
这个看法，她着重地重复两遍，让我很安心。但是为了更安心，我问：“要是她不打算在死前毁掉那些我们今晚讨论的东西，那她很可能会立个遗嘱，做某种安排。”
"By will?" 
“遗嘱？”
"Hasn't she made a will for your benefit?” 
“她没有因为你而立一份遗嘱吗？”
"Why, she has so little to leave. That's why she likes money," said Miss Tita.
“为什么？她没什么可留下的。那就是她为什么喜欢钱的原因。”蒂塔小姐说。
"Might I ask, since we are really talking things over, what you and she live on?" 
“请问，既然我们都把话讲到这里了，你和她到底是靠什么维持生计的？”
"On some money that comes from America, from a lawyer. He sends it every quarter. It isn't much!” 
“美国寄来的一点点钱，是个律师寄来的。他每个季度寄一次。钱不多！”
"And won't she have disposed of that?” 
“而她难道不用那些钱吗？”
My companion hesitated—I saw she was blushing. "I believe it's mine," she said; and the look and tone which accompanied these words betrayed so the absence of the habit of thinking of herself that I almost thought her charming. The next instant she added, "But she had a lawyer once, ever so long ago. And some people came and signed something." 
我的同伴犹豫了——我看见她脸红了。“我相信那是我的。”她说。她说话的神情和语气表露出为自己着想的一面，这是一种她几乎欠缺的习惯。这使得我几乎认为她是个可爱动人的。她马上又补充道：“但是她也曾经请过一位律师，很久很久以前。然后有些人来这里，签了些什么东西。”
"They were probably witnesses. And you were not asked to sign? Well then," I argued rapidly and hopefully, "it is because you are the legatee; she has left all her documents to you!" 
“他们很有可能是证人。你没有被叫去签名？那么，”我快速地、满心希望地指出，“因为你是遗产受赠人，所以她把所有的文稿都留给了你！”
"If she has it's with very strict conditions," Miss Tita responded, rising quickly, while the movement gave the words a little character of decision. They seemed to imply that the bequest would be accompanied with a command that the articles bequeathed should remain concealed from every inquisitive eye and that I was very much mistaken if I thought she was the person to depart from an injunction so solemn.
“如果是的话，那条件也很严苛。”蒂塔小姐回答，很快站起身，使她的话带着点儿笃定的味道。她的话暗示了，这个遗产一定有附带的条件，要求这些被赠与的物品绝对不要泄露给任何觊觎它们的眼睛。她的话表明我是多么错误，竟然以为，她是合适的人选，会违背如此庄严的要求。
"Oh, of course you will have to abide by the terms," I said; and she uttered nothing to mitigate the severity of this conclusion.
“哦，当然你要遵守那些规定。”我说。而她什么也没说，尽可能淡化这个结论的严肃性。
Nonetheless, later, just before we disembarked at her own door, on our return, which had taken place almost in silence, she said to me abruptly, "I will do what I can to help you." I was grateful for this—it was very well so far as it went; but it did not keep me from remembering that night in a worried waking hour that I now had her word for it to reinforce my own impression that the old woman was very cunning.
尽管如此，后来，在我们回家后，在我刚要和她在她门前分手的时候，本来一路沉默的她突然对我说：“我会尽我所能帮助你的。”我很感激——事情进展得太顺利了。但这也并没有让我忘记，那天晚上，当我躺在床上担忧不已，尚未入睡的时候，我想起她的话，觉得那更证明了我对这个老太太的印象：她非常狡猾。
