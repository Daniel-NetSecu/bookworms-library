CHAPTER XXXVIII  

第三十八章  

"Come on, let's go down to the local."
“来，我们到地方分会去。”
So spoke Brissenden, faint from a hemorrhage of half an hour before—the second hemorrhage in three days. The perennial whiskey glass was in his hands, and he drained it with shaking fingers.
布里森登如是说。他半小时前才吐了血，现在还处在眩晕中——这已经是他三天里第二次吐血了。他手上依旧握着威士忌酒杯，手指颤抖着将杯里的酒一饮而尽。
"What do I want with socialism?" Martin demanded.
“我要社会主义有何用？”马丁问道。
"Outsiders are allowed five-minute speeches," the sick man urged. "Get up and spout. Tell them why you don't want socialism. Tell them what you think about them and their ghetto ethics. Slam Nietzsche into them and get walloped for your pains. Make a scrap of it. It will do them good. Discussion is what they want, and what you want, too. You see, I'd like to see you a socialist before I'm gone. It will give you a sanction for your existence. It is the one thing that will save you in the time of disappointment that is coming to you."
“非党员可以演讲五分钟，”病怏怏的布里森登怂恿着他，“上去发表一番吧。告诉他们为什么你不需要社会主义。告诉他们你对他们以及他们的贫民窟道德的看法。拿尼采抨击他们，让他们为此与你争辩。粉碎他们。这会对他们有好处。他们需要辩论，你也一样。你瞧，我倒希望能在死之前看见你成为社会主义者。那将是你活下去的理由。社会主义正是你未来失望之时能拯救你的东西。”
"I never can puzzle out why you, of all men, are a socialist," Martin pondered. "You detest the crowd so. Surely there is nothing in the canaille to recommend it to your aesthetic soul." He pointed an accusing finger at the whiskey glass which the other was refilling. "Socialism doesn't seem to save you."
“我怎么也想不通你竟然会是个社会主义者。”马丁深思着。“你厌恶草莽之众。当然，这群愚民那里没有什么能打动你那充满审美意识的灵魂。”布里森登正在斟另一杯酒，马丁伸出一根手指头指着酒杯指责道。“社会主义似乎没办法救你的命。”
"I'm very sick," was the answer. "With you it is different. You have health and much to live for, and you must be handcuffed to life somehow. As for me, you wonder why I am a socialist. I'll tell you. It is because Socialism is inevitable; because the present rotten and irrational system cannot endure; because the day is past for your man on horseback. The slaves won't stand for it. They are too many, and willy-nilly they'll drag down the would-be equestrian before ever he gets astride. You can't get away from them, and you'll have to swallow the whole slave-morality. It's not a nice mess, I'll allow. But it's been a-brewing and swallow it you must. You are antediluvian anyway, with your Nietzsche ideas. The past is past, and the man who says history repeats itself is a liar. Of course I don't like the crowd, but what's a poor chap to do? We can't have the man on horseback, and anything is preferable to the timid swine that now rule. But come on, anyway. I'm loaded to the guards now, and if I sit here any longer, I'll get drunk. And you know the doctor says—damn the doctor! I'll fool him yet."
“我已经病入膏肓，”他答道，“可对你来说这不一样。你还身体强壮，还有许多值得活下去的东西，所以无论如何你必须和生活铐在一起。至于我，你不明白我为什么是个社会主义者。让我来告诉你吧。因为社会主义是不可避免的；因为现行的腐朽的、不合理的制度是不能长久的；因为你那马背上的人也已经不合时宜了。奴隶们不会支持该理论的。他们人数众多，不管他们愿不愿意，早在你的骑士跨上马背之前就已经被他们拉下马来了。你摆脱不了他们，只能忍受一整套的奴隶道德。我承认，这种混乱算不上好事。但是一切都已在酝酿中，你只能把它强咽下去。无论如何，你的尼采思想早已过时了。过去的已经过去，谁还说历史会重演谁就是骗子。当然了，我不喜欢乌合之众，但是像我这样的可怜人又能如何？马背上的英雄已经不存在了，可无论谁来统治都比现在这些胆小的猪猡的统治强。好吧，不管怎样。我已经有点晕得撑不住了，要是再坐下去，怕是要醉倒了。你也知道的，医生说过——该死的医生！我还要逗逗他。”
It was Sunday night, and they found the small hall packed by the Oakland socialists, chiefly members of the working class. The speaker, a clever Jew, won Martin's admiration at the same time that he aroused his antagonism. The man's stooped and narrow shoulders and weazened chest proclaimed him the true child of the crowded ghetto, and strong on Martin was the age-long struggle of the feeble, wretched slaves against the lordly handful of men who had ruled over them and would rule over them to the end of time. To Martin this withered wisp of a creature was a symbol. He was the figure that stood forth representative of the whole miserable mass of weaklings and inefficients who perished according to biological law on the ragged confines of life. They were the unfit. In spite of their cunning philosophy and of their antlike proclivities for cooperation, Nature rejected them for the exceptional man. Out of the plentiful spawn of life she flung from her prolific hand she selected only the best. It was by the same method that men, aping her, bred race-horses and cucumbers. Doubtless, a creator of a Cosmos could have devised a better method; but creatures of this particular Cosmos must put up with this particular method. Of course, they could squirm as they perished, as the socialists squirmed, as the speaker on the platform and the perspiring crowd were squirming even now as they counselled together for some new device with which to minimize the penalties of living and outwit the Cosmos. the Cos
那是周日晚上，他们发现小厅里挤满了奥克兰的社会主义者，大都是劳动阶级的成员。讲话者是个聪明的犹太人，赢得了马丁的钦佩，同时也引起了他的敌意。那人塌陷下去的窄小的肩膀和萎缩的胸膛都表明了他的确是在拥挤的贫民窟中长大的。他让马丁有个强烈的印象：尽管羸弱困苦的奴隶们为反对那一小撮颐指气使的统治者进行了多年的斗争，却还是要受他们的统治，直到世界末日。马丁认为这个萎缩的小个子生灵是一种象征。他是这整个悲惨的、懦弱无能的群体的突出代表，按照生物学的规律，他们将在生命逼仄破碎的境地里自生自灭。他们不是“适者”。尽管他们有着狡猾的哲学和蚂蚁般善于合作的天性，但是大自然为了优选之民而放弃了他们。大自然在她丰盈的手培育出的万物众生中只选拔出最优秀的那些。人类也用和大自然一样的方式在培养跑马和黄瓜。毋庸置疑，宇宙的创造者本可设计出更好的方法，但是这个特定宇宙里的生物只能忍受这个特定的方式。当然，他们在被消灭的时候可以蠕动挣扎，正如社会主义者们在蠕动挣扎一样。台上的那个发言人和正汗流浃背的人群即使在现在也在挣扎，他们聚在一起商量着新的办法以减少生活的刑罚，战胜宇宙适者生存的法则。
So Martin thought, and so he spoke when Brissenden urged him to give them hell. He obeyed the mandate, walking up to the platform, as was the custom, and addressing the chairman. He began in a low voice, haltingly, forming into order the ideas which had surged in his brain while the Jew was speaking. In such meetings five minutes was the time allotted to each speaker; but when Martin's five minutes were up, he was in full stride, his attack upon their doctrines but half completed. He had caught their interest, and the audience urged the chairman by acclamation to extend Martin's time. They appreciated him as a foeman worthy of their intellect, and they listened intently, following every word. He spoke with fire and conviction, mincing no words in his attack upon the slaves and their morality and tactics and frankly alluding to his hearers as the slaves in question. He quoted Spencer and Malthus, and enunciated the biological law of development.
马丁就这样想着。当布里森登劝他去教训他们一顿的时候，他上台也如此讲了。他服从指令，按照习惯走上讲台，向主席致了意。开始时他的声音低沉而犹豫，把他在听犹太人讲话时在他脑子里萌生的想法整理了一下。这种会议只给每个发言人五分钟时间，但是当马丁的五分钟用完时，他讲的正起劲，对他们教义的攻击也才讲了到一半。但是他的讲话引起了大家的兴趣，听众们纷纷鼓掌要求主席延长马丁的讲话时间。他们欣赏他，认为他是个值得用智慧的头脑去对待的敌手，于是听得非常仔细，一字不落。马丁的演讲感情炽烈，信心满满，他毫无保留地攻击了奴隶们和他们的道德以及战略，直言不讳地向他的听众暗示他们就是那些奴隶。他援引了斯宾塞和马尔萨斯的话，阐述了生物发展的法则。
"And so," he concluded, in a swift résumé, "no state composed of the slave-types can endure. The old law of development still holds. In the struggle for existence, as I have shown, the strong and the progeny of the strong tend to survive, while the weak and the progeny of the weak are crushed and tend to perish. The result is that the strong and the progeny of the strong survive, and, so long as the struggle obtains, the strength of each generation increases. That is development. But you slaves—it is too bad to be slaves, I grant—but you slaves dream of a society where ociety wthe law of development will be annulled, where no weaklings and inefficients will perish, where every inefficient will have as much as he wants to eat as many times a day as he desires, and where all will marry and have progeny—the weak as well as the strong. What will be the result? No longer will the strength and life-value of each generation increase. On the contrary, it will diminish. There is the Nemesis of your slave philosophy. Your society of slaves—of, by, and for, slaves—must inevitably weaken and go to pieces as the life which composes it weakens and goes to pieces.
“因此，”他迅速总结道，“没有任何奴隶性的国家是能长久的。古老的发展观仍然奏效。正如我之前指出的，在生存竞争中，强者和他们的后代更适于生存，而弱者和他们的后代则要被粉碎，注定会消灭。其结果是，强者和他们的后代生存了下来，只要斗争仍在继续，他们的力量会一代比一代更强大。这就是发展。可是你们这些奴隶——我承认身为奴隶是很悲惨的——你们这些奴隶梦想着一个发展法则被消灭，弱者和无能者却不会被消灭的社会，那里每个无能的人每天想吃多少顿就能吃上多少顿；他们都能结婚，养育后代——强者弱者都是如此。结果将会怎样？人的力量和生命的价值不是一代一代地增加。相反，倒是一代一代地削弱了。这就是复仇女神带给你们的奴隶哲学的报应。你们那奴隶治、奴隶有、奴隶享的社会必然会随着构成它的生命的削弱和溃散而垮掉的。
"Remember, I am enunciating biology and not sentimental ethics. No state of slaves can stand—”
记住，我阐述的是生物科学，而不是伤感的伦理道德。没有一个奴隶的社会能够经得起——”
"How about the United States?" a man yelled from the audience.
“那么美国会怎样呢？”听众里有人叫了起来。
"And how about it?" Martin retorted. "The thirteen colonies threw off their rulers and formed the Republic so-called. The slaves were their own masters. There were no more masters of the sword. But you couldn't get along without masters of some sort, and there arose a new set of masters—not the great, virile, noble men, but the shrewd and spidery traders and money-lenders. And they enslaved you over again—but not frankly, as the true, noble men would do with weight of their own right arms, but secretly, by spidery machinations and by wheedling and cajolery and lies. They have purchased your slave judges, they have debauched your slave legislatures, and they have forced to worse horrors than chattel slavery your slave boys and girls. Two million of your children are toiling to-day in this trader-oligarchy of the United States. Ten millions of you slaves are not properly sheltered nor properly fed.”
“它会怎么样？”马丁反驳，“当年的十三个殖民地推翻了他们的统治者，建立了一个所谓的北美共和国。奴隶成了自己的主人。再也没有手握利剑的奴隶主了。但是没有某种意义上的主人你们过不下去，于是出现了一批新的主人——不是那种伟大的、精力充沛的、高贵的人，而是像蜘蛛一样精明的生意人和放债人。他们重新奴役了你们——可是并不是坦率地奴役，像那些真诚的、高贵的人那样用他们坚实的高尚力量，而是像蜘蛛一样用阴谋、谎言和甜言蜜语阴险地统治你们。他们收买你们的奴隶法官，使你们的奴隶法制腐化，用比最残忍的奴役还可怕的方式对待你们的奴隶子女。在美国的今天，有两百万的你们的子女在这种生意人的寡头垄断专制之下做苦力。有一千万的人缺各你人缺少住，过着贫困的生活。”
"But to return. I have shown that no society of slaves can endure, because, in its very nature, such society must annul the law of development. No sooner can a slave society be organized than deterioration sets in. It is easy for you to talk of annulling the law of development, but where is the new law of development that will maintain your strength? Formulate it. Is it already formulated? Then state it."
“不过话说回来。我已经说过奴隶社会是不能长久的。因为就其本质而言，这样的社会必须消灭发展规律。奴隶社会一旦开始组建，就会立刻蜕变。你们谈论起消灭发展规律倒是很容易，但是让你们保留自己力量的新发展规律又在哪里？提出来。已经提出来了么？那就说说看呀。”
Martin took his seat amidst an uproar of voices. A score of men were on their feet clamoring for recognition from the chair. And one by one, encouraged by vociferous applause, speaking with fire and enthusiasm and excited gestures, they replied to the attack. It was a wild night—but it was wild intellectually, a battle of ideas. Some strayed from the point, but most of the speakers replied directly to Martin. They shook him with lines of thought that were new to him; and gave him insights, not into new biological laws, but into new applications of the old laws. They were too earnest to be always polite, and more than once the chairman rapped and pounded for order.
马丁在一片吵吵嚷嚷中回到了座位。一二十个人纷纷站着起来，嚷着要求主席同意他们发言。他们都受到喧闹的鼓掌声的鼓励，演讲的时候无不带着火焰般的热情，手舞足蹈，回答了对他们的攻击。这是一个疯狂的夜晚——但是是智力的疯狂，是思想的交锋。有些人偏离了话题，但是大多数的发言者直接反击了马丁的观点。他们用一些他从未听过的思路震撼了他，启发了他。他们并没有提出什么新的生物学规律，但却提出了从新的角度运用旧的规律。他们谈得如此激烈以致常常不够礼貌，主席不止一次要敲击桌子以维持秩序。
It chanced that a cub reporter sat in the audience, detailed there on a day dull of news and impressed by the urgent need of journalism for sensation. He was not a bright cub reporter. He was merely facile and glib. He was too dense to follow the discussion. In fact, he had a comfortable feeling that he was vastly superior to these wordy maniacs of the working class. Also, he had a great respect for those who sat in the high places and dictated the policies of nations and newspapers. Further, he had an ideal, namely, of achieving that excellence of the perfect reporter who is able to make something—even a great deal—out of nothing.
碰巧那天晚上在听众中坐了一个不懂规矩的新手记者。他是在那到处是新闻的日子里被派来的，一心一意只想搞到具有轰动性的新闻。他是个不大能干的新手记者。他只知道捡便宜和信口开河。他没有思想，听不懂他们的讨论。事实上，他还有一种高人一大等的自得感，觉得自己比这些满嘴胡言的工人阶级疯子要高尚得多。而且，他对那些身居高位并掌控着国家政策和报纸喉舌的人非常钦佩。另外，他还有一个理想，即出人头地，当个完美的记者，哪怕无中生有也要弄出点名堂——甚至是大名堂。
He did not know what all the talk was about. It was not necessary. Words like revolution gave him his cue. Like a paleontologist, able to reconstruct an entire skeleton from one fossil bone, he was able to reconstruct a whole speech from the one word revolution. He did it that night, that niand he did it well; and since Martin had made the biggest stir, he put it all into his mouth and made him the arch-anarch of the show, transforming his reactionary individualism into the most lurid, red-shirt socialist utterance. The cub reporter was an artist, and it was a large brush with which he laid on the local color—wild-eyed long-haired men, neurasthenia and degenerate types of men, voices shaken with passion, clenched fists raised on high, and all projected against a background of oaths, yells, and the throaty rumbling of angry men.
他不懂得这场谈话的意义。他也没必要懂得。革命这类字眼已经给了他线索。就像一个古生物学家能凭借一块骨骼化石建造出一副完整的骨架一样，他也能够仅凭革命这一个词就构思出整个发言。那天晚上他就是那样做的，且干得很漂亮。由于马丁的发言最轰动，他便把所有的话都写进了马丁的嘴里，把他写成了那场大辩论中的无政府主义元凶，把他反动的个人主义改造成了最阴险的红色社会主义的宣泄。这个新人记者是个艺术家，他大笔一挥，添加了些地方色彩——目光疯狂满头长发的人，神经质的退化的人，激情四溢而发抖的声音，高举着紧攥的拳头，背景声音则是愤怒的人们口中发出的咒骂声、叫喊声和低沉的咆哮声。
