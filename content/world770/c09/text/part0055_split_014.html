CHAPTER XV  

第十五章  

Soden is a little town half an hour's distance from Frankfort. It lies in a beautiful country among the spurs of the Taunus Mountains, and is known among us in Russia for its waters, which are supposed to be beneficial to people with weak lungs. The Frankforters visit it more for purposes of recreation, as Soden possesses a fine park and various "wirthschaften", where one may drink beer and coffee in the shade of the tall limes and maples. The road from Frankfort to Soden runs along the right bank of the Maine, and is planted all along with fruit trees. While the carriage was rolling slowly along an excellent road, Sanin stealthily watched how Gemma behaved to her betrothed; it was the first time he had seen them together. She was quiet and simple in her manner, but rather more reserved and serious than usual; he had the air of a condescending schoolmaster, permitting himself and those under his authority a discreet and decorous pleasure. Sanin saw no signs in him of any marked attentiveness, of what the French call "empressement", in his demeanour to Gemma. It was clear that Herr Kléber considered that it was a matter settled once for all, and that therefore he saw no reason to trouble or excite himself. But his condescension never left him for an instant! Even during a long ramble before dinner about the wooded hills and valleys behind Soden, even when enjoying the beauties of nature, he treated nature itself with the same condescension, through which his habitual magisterial severity peeped out from time to time. So, for example, he observed in regard to one stream that it ran too straight through the glade, instead of making a few picturesque curves; he disapproved, too, of the conduct of a bird—a chaffinch—for singing so monotonously. Gemma was not bored, and even, apparently, was enjoying herself; but Sanin did not recognise her as the Gemma of the preceding days; it was not that she seemed under a cloud—her beauty had never been more dazzling—but her soul seemed to have withdrawn into herself. With her parasol open and her gloves still buttoned up, she walked sedately, deliberately, as well-bred young girls walk, and spoke little. Emil, too, felt stiff, and Sanin more so than all. He was somewhat embarrassed too by the fact that the conversation was all the time in German. Only Tartaglia was in high spirits! He darted, barking frantically, after blackbirds, leaped over ravines, stumps and roots, rushed headlong into the water, lapped at it in desperate haste, shook himself, whining, and was off like an arrow, his red tongue trailing after him almost to his shoulder. Herr Klüber, for his part, did everything he supposed conducive to the mirthfulness of the company; he begged them to sit down in the shade of a spreading oak-tree, and taking out of a side pocket a small booklet entitled, "Knallerbsen; oder du sollst und wirst lachen!" (Squibs; or you must and shall laugh!) began reading the funny anecdotes of which the little book was full. He read them twelve specimens; he aroused very little mirth, however; only Sanin smiled, from politeness, and he himself, Herr Kléber, after each anecdote, gave vent to a brief, business-like, but still condescending laugh. At twelve o'clock the whole party returned to Soden to the best tavern there.
索登是个小镇子，离法兰克福有半小时的路程。它坐落在陶努斯山脉美丽的支脉中，这里的泉水在俄国很有名，据说对治疗肺病颇有成效。法兰克福的人大多是来这里游玩的，因为索登有一个美丽的公园，还有各种饭店。在那里，人们在高大挺拔的菩提树和枫树树阴下品尝啤酒和咖啡。从法兰克福去索登的路蜿蜒在曼恩河右岸的果树之间。当马车在平坦大道上缓缓而行时，萨宁暗暗地观察吉玛和她的未婚夫是怎么相处的，这还是他第一次看到他们俩在一起的情景。她很安静，举止自然，但比平常要矜持和严肃些；克吕贝尔则有一种谦逊的校长神气，仿佛要让自己和下属得到谨慎而礼貌的乐趣。萨宁在他身上看不到对吉玛献殷勤的任何迹象，也就是法国人所谓的“热情”。很明显，克吕贝尔认为这桩婚事已成定局，因此他没必要让自己麻烦或者兴奋。但是他始终保持着傲慢的态度。即使在饭前他们漫步于索登后面那葱郁的小山和峡谷时，即使在他们欣赏大自然的美景时，他也用同样的傲慢态度对待自然。期间，他也不时流露出他那惯常的上司的威严。举个例子，他指出，在林间奔流的那条小溪太过直板，毫无曲折之美。他也批评一种叫做花鸡的鸟，说它们叫声单调。吉玛没有觉得无聊，她看起来还很高兴，但是萨宁觉得她和前几天截然不同。不是有阴影遮住了她——她的美丽从来都很耀眼——而是她把自己的心情藏了起来。她打着阳伞，手套的纽扣也扣着，走路也镇定从容，和那些有教养的年轻女孩一样，言语不多。埃米尔也觉得不自在，更不用说萨宁了。谈话都是用德语进行的，这让他感觉有些尴尬。只有塔尔塔利阿仍然是精神百倍！它狂奔着，向着看到的画眉鸟狂吠着，跳过一道道坑洼、树桩和树根，猛冲进水里，匆忙地舔着河水，又抖了抖身子，尖叫一声，像箭一般飞奔而去，鲜红的舌头几乎要甩到了肩上。克吕贝尔先生，则做了他认为必要的事情来逗游伴们开心。他请他们坐在一棵枝繁叶茂的橡树下面，在一侧的衣兜里拿出了一本小册子那么大的书，名叫《讽刺短文——或你准会大笑！》，他开始读里面有趣的轶事。他给大家读了十二则有代表性的，可是没有多少笑声，只有萨宁出于礼貌笑一笑。而克吕贝尔先生自己，每读完一则都会发出一声简短的、古板的、谦逊的笑声。中午十二点的时候，他们一行回到了索登最好的一个小饭馆里。
They had to make arrangements about dinner.
他们要安排午饭了。
Herr Klüber proposed that the dinner should be served in a summer-house closed in on all sides—"im Gartensalon"; but at this point Gemma rebelled and declared that she would have dinner in the open air, in the garden, at one of the little tables set before the tavern; that she was tired of being all the while with the same faces, and she wanted to see fresh ones. At some of the little tables, groups of visitors were already sitting.
克吕贝尔先生建议在一个封闭的凉亭——“花园沙龙”里进餐，但是吉玛不同意，她要在户外，在餐馆前花园里的一个小桌子上进餐，说老是同这几个人在一起，她觉得厌倦了，她想看一些新面孔。一些小桌子周围已经坐满了顾客。
While Herr Klüber, yielding condescendingly to "the caprice of his betrothed", went off to interview the head waiter, Gemma stood immovable, biting her lips and looking on the ground; she was conscious that Sanin was persistently and, as it were, inquiringly looking at her—it seemed to enrage her. At last Herr Kléber returned, announced that dinner would be ready in half an hour, and proposed their employing the interval in a game of skittles, adding that this was very good for the appetite, he, he, he! Skittles he played in masterly fashion; as he threw the ball, he put himself into amazingly heroic postures, with artistic play of the muscles, with artistic flourish and shake of the leg. In his own way he was an athlete—and was superbly built! His hands, too, were so white and handsome, and he wiped them on such a sumptuous, gold-striped, Indian bandana!
而克吕贝尔先生宽容地听从了他的未婚妻的“任性要求”，去和堂倌头商量；吉玛则一动不动地站着，咬着嘴唇看着地面，她感觉萨宁老是好奇地瞧着自己，这似乎让她很生气。克吕贝尔先生终于回来了，告诉大家还有半小时午餐就准备好了，他还建议这半小时大家可以玩玩撞柱游戏，并说，玩这个游戏有益于增加食欲，呵呵呵！他玩撞柱游戏手法老练：抛球时，摆出令人惊异的炫耀的姿势，抖动着唯美的肌肉，优雅地抬起腿摆了一下。看他打球的风格就像是一个运动员——体格非常健美！他的双手，也是又白又嫩，他用一个华丽的、印着金色花纹的印度印花大手帕擦擦手！
The moment of dinner arrived, and the whole party seated themselves at the table.
午饭时间到了，全体游伴都在餐桌旁落座。
