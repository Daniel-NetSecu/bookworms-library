THE DISTRIBUTION OF PRIZES TO THE WORKINGMEN.  

工人颁奖仪式  

Sunday, 25th.

星期日，25日

As we had agreed, we all went together to the Theatre Vittorio Emanuele, to view the distribution of prizes to the workingmen.
按照之前的约定，我们一起去了维托里奥·埃马努埃莱剧院去看工人的颁奖仪式。
The theatre was adorned as on the 14th of March, and thronged, but almost wholly with the families of workmen; and the pit was occupied with the male and female pupils of the school of choral singing.
剧院的装饰和三月十四日那天一样，而且也是挤满了人，但几乎都是工人的家属。乐池里坐着音乐学校合唱队的男女学生。
These sang a hymn to the soldiers who had died in the Crimea; which was so beautiful that, when it was finished, all rose and clapped and shouted, so that the song had to be repeated from the beginning.
学生们唱了一首歌颂牺牲在克里米亚的士兵的歌。他们的歌声非常优美，唱完时大家都起身鼓掌喝彩，以至于他们又重头唱了一遍。
And then the prize-winners began immediately to march past the mayor, the prefect, and many others, who presented them with books, savings-bank books, diplomas, and medals. In one corner of the pit I espied the little mason, sitting beside his mother; and in another place there was the head-master; and behind him, the red head of my master of the second grade.
随后，获奖者们立刻开始一一走过市长、地方长官和其他颁奖嘉宾的面前，领取书籍、存折、证书或是奖章。我看见小石匠和他母亲坐在乐池的一角，校长坐在乐池的另一处，我三年级时的满头红发的老师坐在他身后。
The first to defile were the pupils of the evening drawing classes—the goldsmiths, engravers, lithographers, and also the carpenters and masons; then those of the commercial school; then those of the Musical Lyceum, among them several girls, workingwomen, all dressed in festal attire, who were saluted with great applause, and who laughed.
最先出场的是美术夜校的学生——有铁匠、雕刻工、石印工、木匠和石匠。接着是商业夜校的学生。之后是音乐学校的学生，他们之中有几个女孩和劳动妇女，都穿着节日的盛装，看到大家向她们热烈鼓掌致意，都笑了起来。
Last came the pupils of the elementary evening schools, and then it began to be a beautiful sight. They were of all ages, of all trades, and dressed in all sorts of ways,—men with gray hair, factory boys, artisans with big black beards. The little ones were at their ease; the men, a little embarrassed. The people clapped the oldest and the youngest, but none of the spectators laughed, as they did at our festival: all faces were attentive and serious.
最后是来自初级夜校的学生，那场景真是美丽。他们年龄不同，职业不同，衣服也各式各样——有白发的老人，也有工场的学徒和蓄着黑色胡须的技工。孩子们都安然自得，大人们则有些局促不安。无论年少年长，大家都为他们送上热烈的掌声，但没有一位观众发笑，而是就像在我们的节日里一样，都露出专注而庄重的神情。
Many of the prize-winners had wives and children in the pit, and there were little children who, when they saw their father pass across the stage, called him by name at the tops of their voices, and signalled to him with their hands, laughing violently.
许多受奖者的妻子和孩子都坐在乐池里观礼。有些小孩子看见自己的父亲走过舞台，就扯着嗓子喊他的名字，大笑着向他招手。
Peasants passed, and porters; they were from the Buoncompagni School.
农夫们和挑工们相继走过舞台，他们都来自毕恩康帕尼学校。
From the Cittadella School there was a bootblack whom my father knew, and the prefect gave him a diploma.
父亲认识的一位来自奇塔代拉学校的鞋匠也来了，地方官员颁发给他一张证书。
After him I saw approaching a man as big as a giant, whom I fancied that I had seen several times before. It was the father of the little mason, who had won the second prize. I remembered when I had seen him in the garret, at the bedside of his sick son, and I immediately sought out his son in the pit.
鞋匠之后是一位身材像巨人一样高大的男人，我觉得好像以前见过他几次。这就是小石匠的父亲，他赢得了二等奖。我想起曾在走廊里和他儿子的病床旁见过他，于是立刻回头去看乐池里的小石匠。
Poor little mason! he was staring at his father with beaming eyes, and, in order to conceal his emotion, he made his hare's face.
可怜的小石匠！他正目光热切地注视着他的父亲，做兔脸去掩盖自己的喜悦之情。
At that moment I heard a burst of applause, and I glanced at the stage: a little chimney-sweep stood there, with a clean face, but in his working-clothes, and the mayor was holding him by the hand and talking to him.
就在那时，我听见掌声四起，便又向舞台看去：一个小小的烟囱清扫工站在那里，脸洗得干干净净，但依然穿着工作服，市长正握着他的手，同他讲话。
After the chimney-sweep came a cook; then came one of the city sweepers, from the Raineri School, to get a prize.
烟囱清扫工之后是一个厨子，再后面是一个来自雷纳学校的城市清扫工。
I felt I know not what in my heart,—something like a great affection and a great respect, at the thought of how much those prizes had cost all those workingmen, fathers of families, full of care; how much toil added to their labors, how many hours snatched from their sleep, of which they stand in such great need, and what efforts of intelligences not habituated to study, and of huge hands rendered clumsy with work!
我心中有种说不出的感受——一这些工人是家里的顶梁柱，他们需要付出多少努力才能得奖啊。为了学习，他们要加倍辛劳，放弃无数他们迫切需要的睡眠时间，用那不曾习惯学习的大脑学习，用那粗笨的手执笔！一想到这些，我心中就升起一种类似于极度感动与崇高敬意的感情。
A factory boy passed, and it was evident that his father had lent him his jacket for the occasion, for his sleeves hung down so that he was forced to turn them back on the stage, in order to receive his prize: and many laughed; but the laugh was speedily stifled by the applause.
接着走过一个工厂的学徒，显然他父亲为了这个场合把自己的夹克上衣借给了他。因为袖子太长，他必须在舞台上挽起袖子才能接受奖品。许多人笑了，但是笑声立刻就淹没在掌声之中。
Next came an old man with a bald head and a white beard.
学徒后面是一个秃头白须的老人。
Several artillery soldiers passed, from among those who attended evening school in our schoolhouse; then came custom-house guards and policemen, from among those who guard our schools.
接着走过几个炮兵，他们之中有的人曾念过我们学校的夜校班。此外还有海关的守卫和警察，我们学校的守卫也在他们之中。
At the conclusion, the pupils of the evening schools again sang the hymn to the dead in the Crimea, but this time with so much dash, with a strength of affection which came so directly from the heart, that the audience hardly applauded at all, and all retired in deep emotion, slowly and noiselessly.
最后，夜校的学生又唱起了克里米亚烈士赞歌，但这次唱得激情迸发，充满了一股发自内心的深情，几乎没有观众鼓掌，他们只是无比感动地、缓慢而安静地退场。
In a few moments the whole street was thronged. In front of the entrance to the theatre was the chimney-sweep, with his prize book bound in red, and all around were gentlemen talking to him.
刹那间，街上挤满了人。烟囱清扫工拿着系着红绳的奖状站在剧院门前，绅士们都聚在他周围和他说话。
Many exchanged salutations from the opposite side of the street,—workmen, boys, policemen, teachers. My master of the second grade came out in the midst of the crowd, between two artillery men. And there were workmen's wives with babies in their arms, who held in their tiny hands their father's diploma, and exhibited it to the crowd in their pride.
许多人在街道两边相互打招呼——工人、孩子、警察和老师。我三年级的老师和两个炮兵从人群中走了出来，老师走在他们中间。工人们的妻子抱着孩子，孩子们的小手里拿着父亲的证书，炫耀地给大家看。
