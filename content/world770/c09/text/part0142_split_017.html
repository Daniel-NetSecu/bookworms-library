CHAPTER XVIII  

第十八章  

The young Anglo-Swiss evidently derived both pleasure and profit from the study of her mother-tongue. In teaching her I did not, of course, confine myself to the ordinary school routine. I made instruction in English a channel for instruction in literature. I prescribed to her a course of reading. She had a little selection of English classics, a few of which had been left her by her mother, and the others she had purchased with her own penny-fee. I lent her some more modern works. All these she read with avidity, giving me, in writing, a clear summary of each work when she had perused it. Composition, too, she delighted in. Such occupation seemed the very breath of her nostrils, and soon her improved productions wrung from me the avowal that those qualities in her I had termed taste and fancy ought rather to have been denominated judgment and imagination. When I intimated so much, which I did as usual in dry and stinted phrase, I looked for the radiant and exulting smile my one word of eulogy had elicited before; but Frances coloured. If she did smile, it was very softly and shyly; and instead of looking up to me with a conquering glance, her eyes rested on my hand, which, stretched over her shoulder, was writing some directions with a pencil on the margin of her book.
显然这个年轻的英瑞混血儿从她的母语学习中既获得了快乐，又有了收益。当然在教她时我并没有局限于一般学校的常规方式。我把教英语作为教文学的渠道。我为她规定了一门阅读课程。她有一小套英国文学名著选读，其中不多几本是她母亲留给她的，其他的都是她用自己的零花钱买的。我又借给了她一些现代作品。她迫不及待地读着这些书，每当读完一本，就会写清楚的摘要给我。她也喜欢作文。这些事情似乎占据了她所有的时间；不久后，她那有所进步的作品迫使我坦率承认，原先她那些我称之为爱好与幻想的品质，现在应该称作鉴赏力与想象力。我如往常一样用干瘪而克制的语言暗示了对她的嘉许，并去找寻以往我的一句赞扬话就能引得她容光焕发、欢欣鼓舞的微笑时，弗朗西斯却红了脸。即使她真的笑了，也笑得十分恬淡羞涩，她没有如征服者般地抬头瞥视我，而是把目光停留在我手上，我的手正从她的肩膀上伸过去，拿着一支铅笔在作业本空白处写着一些评语。
'Well, are you pleased that I am satisfied with your progress?' I asked.
“恩，我对你的进步很满意，你开心吗？”我问道。
'Yes,' said she slowly, gently, the blush that had half subsided returning.
“是的。”她缓慢轻柔地回答道，那已褪了一半的红晕又涌回脸颊。
'But I do not say enough, I suppose" I continued. 'My praises are too cool.' 
“我想，是不是我说得不够多？”我继续说道，“我的表扬太过平淡了。”
She made no answer, and, I thought, looked a little sad. I divined her thoughts, and should much have liked to have responded to them, had it been expedient so to do. She was not now very ambitious of my admiration, not eagerly desirous of dazzling me; a little affection—ever so little—pleased her better than all the panegyrics in the world. Feeling this, I stood a good while behind her, writing on the margin of her book. I could hardly quit my station or relinquish my occupation. Something retained me bending there, my head very near hers, and my hand near hers too; but the margin of a copy-book is not an illimitable space—so, doubtless, the directress thought; and she took occasion to walk past, in order to ascertain by what art I prolonged so disproportionately the period necessary for filling it. I was obliged to go. Distasteful effort—to leave what we most prefer.
她没有回答，我想她看起来有点难过。我揣测了她的想法，如果迎合她的想法有什么好处的话，我还是很愿意做的。此时她并不很想赢得我的欣赏，也不急于要我对她赞叹不已，只要我透露出一点柔情——哪怕只有一点点——也比全世界所有的赞颂更令她高兴。想到这里，我在她的身后又站了好一会，一直在她作业本的空白处写着。我几乎不能挪动我的位置，也无法停止写字。有一种东西使我俯在那里，我的头离她的头很近，我的手离她的手也很近；但是作业本上的空白并不是无限的——那位女校长无疑也是那么想的，于是，她便趁机从我身边走过，想看看我在耍什么花招，就那么点空白我却写了那么久。我必须要走了。这真令人厌恶——不得不放下我们最喜欢干的事。
Frances did not become pale or feeble in consequence of her sedentary employment; perhaps the stimulus it communicated to her mind counterbalanced the inaction it imposed on her body. She changed, indeed—changed obviously and rapidly; but it was for the better. When I first saw her, her countenance was sunless, her complexion colourless; she looked like one who had no source of enjoyment, no store of bliss anywhere in the world. Now the cloud had passed from her mien, leaving space for the dawn of hope and interest, and those feelings rose like a clear morning, animating what had been depressed, tinting what had been pale. Her eyes, whose colour I had not at first known, so dim were they with repressed tears, so shadowed with ceaseless dejection, now, lit by a ray of the sunshine that cheered her heart, revealed irids of bright hazel—irids large and full, screened with long lashes, and pupils instinct with fire. That look of wan emaciation which anxiety or low spirits often communicates to a thoughtful, thin face, rather long than round, having vanished from hers, a clearness of skin almost bloom and a plumpness almost embonpoint softened the decided lines of her features. Her figure shared in this beneficial change. It became rounder; and as the harmony of her form was complete and her stature of the graceful middle height, one did not regret (or at least I did not regret) the absence of confirmed fullness in contours still slight, though compact, elegant, flexible. The exquisite turning of waist, wrist, hand, foot, and ankle satisfied completely my notions of symmetry, and allowed a lightness and freedom of movement which corresponded with my ideas of grace.
弗朗西斯没有因为久坐而显得苍白无力；也许是因为这种状态传递到她大脑的刺激抵消了久坐不动给身体带来的疲劳。她确实变了——明显而迅速地变了，但是变得更好。我第一次见到她时，她表情阴翳，面无血色，看起来像是一个不会快乐的人，彷佛这世上任何地方都没有能让她幸福的事情。如今，那层阴云已经从她的脸上散去，取而代之的是希望与兴趣的曙光，这些感情如同晴朗的早晨般涌出来，使原先受压抑的灵魂获得生气，使本来苍白的脸庞多了几分色彩。起初我并不知道她眼睛的颜色，它们因为含着的压抑的泪水而显得浑浊，因为无尽的沮丧而显得暗淡。而今，她的眼睛已被一束暖人心房的阳光照亮，显露出那明亮的淡褐色双眸——那眸子大而饱满，被长长的睫毛遮掩着，瞳孔里充满着热情。她那因焦虑和消沉而显得心事重重的瘦长脸庞上总是显现着病态的憔悴；现如今，这些已经离她而去，透亮的皮肤几乎如花般盛开，丰腴的面容亦软化了原本生硬的脸部线条。她的身材也随之得到了改善。她圆润了些，整个人因此看上去更协调，加之她身高适中，所以尽管仍然纤细，也并不使人（起码是我）为她的轮廓欠缺饱满而感到遗憾，因为她依旧紧实，优雅，灵巧。她的腰肢、手腕、纤手、玉足以及脚踝转动起来那样优美，完全达到了我心中的匀称美；她的动作轻盈飘逸，正契合了我对于优雅的理解。
Thus improved, thus wakened to life, Mdlle. Henri began to take a new footing in the school. Her mental power manifested gradually but steadily, ere long extorted recognition even from the envious; and when the young and healthy saw that she could smile brightly, converse gaily, move with vivacity and alertness, they acknowledged in her a sisterhood of youth and health, and tolerated her as of their kind accordingly.
就这样，亨利小姐进步了，苏醒了，她开始以一种新的姿态出现在学校里。她的精神力量缓慢而稳定地展现出来，不久就连那些妒火中烧的人也不得不对她另眼相看；当那些年轻健康的女子们看到她居然也能灿烂地微笑、欢乐地交谈、轻盈快活地四处走动时，也承认她是个“年轻健康的好姐妹”，于是勉强将她视为同类中的一员了。
To speak truth, I watched this change much as a gardener watches the growth of a precious plant, and I contributed to it too, even as the said gardener contributes to the development of his favourite. To me it was not difficult to discover how I could best foster my pupil, cherish her starved feelings, and induce the out-ward manifestation of that inward vigour which sunless drought and blighting blast had hitherto forbidden to expand. Constancy of attention, a kindness as mute as watchful, always standing by her, cloaked in the rough garb of austerity, and making its real nature known only by a rare glance of interest or a cordial and gentle word; real respect masked with seeming imperiousness, directing, urging her actions, yet helping her too, and that with devoted care,— these were the means I used, for these means best suited Frances' feelings, as susceptible as deep-vibrating, her nature at once proud and shy.
说真话，我就像园丁注视着一棵珍稀植物成长一样关注着她的变化，为此我也倾注了心血，正如园丁为自己心爱植物的成长付出了汗水。对我而言，要了解怎样才能最好地培养她，关心她那枯竭的感情，并引导她表现出那一直饱受暗无阳光、干旱笼罩和狂风摧残而不能展现的内在活力，这一切都并非难事。我一直默默无闻地、警惕地对她关心有加，总是伴其左右，在严厉粗糙的外表掩饰下，仅凭偶尔关心的一瞥或者一句诚恳温和的话语表示真心，在专横的面具下，用真诚的尊重引导并督促她的行为，同时全心全意且谨慎地帮助她——这些就是我所使用的方法，因为这最适合弗朗西斯的性情，她既骄傲又害羞，既敏感又深沉。
The benefits of my system became apparent also in her altered demeanour as a teacher. She now took her place amongst her pupils with an air of spirit and firmness which assured them at once that she meant to be obeyed, and obeyed she was. They felt they had lost their power over her. If any girl had rebelled, she would no longer have taken her rebellion to heart. She possessed a source of comfort they could not drain, a pillar of support they could not overthrow. Formerly, when insulted, she wept; now, she smiled.
她作为老师的行为风格也改变了，由此可见，我这一套教育方法有明显的益处。如今她在上课时神采奕奕，表情坚定地坐在学生们中间，使她们立即明白她这是要她们规矩行事。她们也确实听话了。她们发觉对她失去了控制力。再有哪个女生捣蛋，她也不再会放在心上。因为她拥有了一个她们无法耗尽的自我安慰的源泉，一根她们无法推倒的精神支柱。以前，如果她们欺负她，她只有哭泣；而今，她一笑而过。
The public reading of one of her devoirs achieved the revelation of her talents to all and sundry. I remember the subject—it was an emigrant's letter to his friends at home. It opened with simplicity. Some natural and graphic touches disclosed to the reader the scene of virgin forest and great New World river—barren of sail and flag—amidst which the epistle was supposed to be indited. The difficulties and dangers that attend a settler's life were hinted at; and in the few words said on that subject, Mdlle. Henri failed not to render audible the voice of resolve, patience, endeavour. The disasters which had driven him from his native country were alluded to; stainless honour, inflexible independence, indestructible self-respect there took the word. Past days were spoken of; the grief of parting, the regrets of absence, were touched upon; feeling, forcible and fine, breathed eloquent in every period. At the close, consolation was suggested; religious faith became there the speaker, and she spoke well.
她的一篇作品被当众朗读，大家一下子意识到了她的才华。我还记得那个主题——那是一个移民写给故乡朋友们的一封信。文章开头简洁。几行自然而形象的描写，向读者展示了一幅由原始森林和新大陆大河构成的画面——河上没有帆船和旗帜——这封信应该就是在这场景中写的。信中，尽管亨利小姐只用寥寥数笔就暗示了移民者生活中遇到的重重险阻，但字里行间移民者的决心、耐心与努力使人读之如闻其声。文中暗示了迫使他背井离乡的灾难，并描写了他毫无瑕疵的荣誉感、不屈不挠的独立精神以及坚不可摧的自尊心。还回忆了过去的时光，触及了分离的痛苦以及人在异乡的惆怅；通篇用动人的文笔流露着强烈而细腻的感情。结尾处写到一丝安慰：宗教信仰与移民者的动人叙述融为一体。
The devoir was powerfully written in language at once chaste and choice, in a style nerved with vigour and graced with harmony.
作品通篇气势磅礴，语言朴实高雅，生动活泼的风格中不失优雅协调。
Mdlle. Reuter was quite sufficiently acquainted with English to understand it when read or spoken in her presence, though she could neither speak nor write it herself.
当着勒泰小姐的面读或者说英语，她还是完全听得懂的，虽然她自己既不会讲也不会写。
During the perusal of this devoir she sat placidly busy, her eyes and fingers occupied with the formation of a riviére, or open-work hem round a cambric handkerchief. She said nothing, and her face and forehead, clothed with a mask of purely negative expression, were as blank of comment as her lips. As neither surprise, pleasure, approbation, nor interest was evinced in her countenance, so no more were disdain, envy, annoyance, weariness; if that inscrutable mien said anything, it was simply this,— 
在我读亨利小姐的这篇作品时，她一直安静地坐在那里忙着，眼睛和手指一会忙着织领饰，一会忙着在一方麻纱手帕四周编花边。她什么都没说，脸和前额只表达出一丝不满的神情，双唇也不吐露一句评论。她的表情既没有吃惊、高兴和赞许，也没有任何兴趣或者蔑视、嫉妒、恼怒和厌倦；如果这种捉摸不透的态度说明了什么，那也仅是在说：
'The matter is too trite to excite an emotion or call forth an opinion.' 
“这事太陈腐了，完全无法让人激动，也没什么意见好提的。”
As soon as I had done a hum rose, several of the pupils, pressing round Mdlle. Henri, began to beset her with compliments. The composed voice of the directress was now heard,— 
我一读完就响起一阵嗡嗡的议论声。几个学生围住了亨利小姐，七嘴八舌地恭维起来。这时传来了女校长漫不经心的声音：
'Young ladies, such of you as have cloaks and umbrellas will hasten to return home before the shower becomes heavier' (it was raining a little); 'the remainder will wait till their respective servants arrive to fetch them.' And the school dispersed, for it was four o'clock.
“年轻的小姐们，你们中有斗篷和雨伞的就在雨下大之前快点回家吧”（当时正在下着小雨）；“其余的就等你们各自的仆人来接你们吧。”然后学校放学了，因为已经四点了。
'Monsieur, a word,' said Mdlle. Reuter, stepping on to the estrade, and signifying, by a movement of the hand, that she wished me to relinquish, for an instant, the castor I had clutched.
“先生，有句话想跟你说，”勒泰小姐说。她站上讲台，同时做了个手势暗示她希望我等一下，我立即领会了她的意思。
'Mademoiselle, I am at your service.' 
“小姐，我听候您的吩咐。”
'Monsieur, it is, of course, an excellent plan to encourage effort in young people by making conspicuous the progress of any particularly industrious pupil; but do you not think that, in the present instance, Mdlle. Henri can hardly be considered as a concurrent with the other pupils? She is older than most of them, and has had advantages of an exclusive nature for acquiring a knowledge of English. On the other hand, her sphere of life is somewhat beneath theirs. Under these circumstances, a public distinction conferred upon Mdlle. Henri may be the means of suggesting comparisons and exciting feelings such as would be far from advantageous to the individual forming their object. The interest I take in Mdlle. Henri's real welfare makes me desirous of screening her from annoyances of this sort; besides, monsieur, as I have before hinted to you, the sentiment of amour propre has a somewhat marked preponderance in her character. Celebrity has a tendency to foster this sentiment, and in her it should be rather repressed—she rather needs keeping down than bringing forward; and then I think, monsieur—it appears to me that ambition, literary ambition especially, is not a feeling to be cherished in the mind of a woman. Would not Mdlle. Henri be much safer and happier if taught to believe that in the quiet discharge of social duties consists her real vocation, than if stimulated to aspire after applause and publicity? She may never marry; scanty as are her resources, obscure as are her connections, uncertain as is her health (for I think her consumptive; her mother died of that complaint), it is more than probable she never will. I do not see how she can rise to a position whence such a step would be possible; but even in celibacy it would be better for her to retain the character and habits of a respectable decorous female.' 
“先生，在年轻人面前大张旗鼓地表扬某些特别努力的学生所取得的明显进步以激励他们勤奋学习，这确实是一个很好的方法；但是以今天为例，你难道没有想过亨利小姐几乎不能跟其他学生同日而语的吗？她比她们中的大多数人都要年长，又具有学习英语的特殊天分这一优势。另一方面，她的社会地位要比她们稍微低一些。在这些情况下，于众人面前给亨利小姐这种殊荣，也许会引起比较之风，并且可能激起一些对她远为不利的情绪，使她成为众矢之的。我正是从亨利小姐本身的利益出发，才想要让她远离这种烦恼的；此外，先生，我之前已向您暗示过，她强烈的自尊感在她的性格里占主导地位。而名声很容易滋生这种感情；对她来说，这种感情还是该受到压抑——她确实应该克制而不是一味出头；而且我想，先生——我认为野心，尤其是在文学方面的野心，在一个女人的心灵中可不是什么该珍视的情感。教导亨利女士，让她相信她真正的才能在于默默地履行她的社会职责，而不是鼓励她追求掌声和公众的注意，这样她不是会更安全更幸福吗？她可能永不结婚；她经济来源匮乏，缺乏社会关系，身体也不好（因为我认为她是肺结核，她的母亲就是死于那种病），也许她根本就没想结婚。至少我是看不出她有结婚的可能；但是即使是独身，保持一位受人尊敬的端庄女性的性格和习惯对她来说也是较好的。”
'Indisputably, mademoiselle,' was my answer. 'Your opinion admits of no doubt;' and fearful of the harangue being renewed, I retreated under cover of that cordial sentence of assent.
“无可置疑，小姐，”我回答道，“您的观点当然是对的。”我怕她又要长篇大论起来，便假意诚恳地附和着，一边匆忙退了出去。
At the date of a fortnight after the little incident noted above, I find it recorded in my diary that a hiatus occurred in Mdlle. Henri's usually regular attendance in class.
就在上述这件小事发生两周后的一天，我从我的教学日记里发现亨利小姐有段时间没像往常一样来正常上课。
The first day or two I wondered at her absence, but did not like to ask an explanation of it. I thought, indeed, some chance word might be dropped which would afford me the information I wished to obtain, without my running the risk of exciting silly smiles and gossiping whispers by demanding it. But when a week passed and the seat at the desk near the door still remained vacant, and when no allusion was made to the circumstance by any individual of the class—when, on the contrary, I found that all observed a marked silence on the point—I determined, coûte qu'il coûte, to break the ice of this silly reserve. I selected Sylvie as my informant, because from her I knew that I should at least get a sensible answer, unaccompanied by wriggle, titter, or other flourish of folly.
开始一两天我为她的缺席感到纳闷，但是不想去问原因。事实上，我想从别人的闲聊中得到我想要的信息，省得去问而招来傻笑和闲言闲语。但是一个星期过去了，门旁边的桌子边上的位子仍然是空的，班上也没有人问及这件事——相反，我发现所有人都对此事有意避而不谈——因此，我决心放弃自己愚蠢的坚持，打破这沉默的坚冰。我选择了去问西尔维，因为我知道从她那里我至少可以得到一个明确的答案，而不是搪塞、窃笑之类的打马虎眼。
'Où donc est Mdlle. Henri?' I said one day as I returned an exercise-book I had been examining.
“亨利小姐到哪儿去了？”一天我趁着返还给她批过的作业的机会问她。
'Elle est partie, monsieur.' 
“她走了，先生。”
'Partie! et pour combien de temps? Quand reviendra-t-elle?' 
“走了！要走多久呀？什么时候回来呢？”
'Elle est partie pour toujours, monsieur; elle ne reviendra plus.' 
“她永远地离开了，先生，她再也不会回来了。”
'Ah!' was my involuntary exclamation; then after a pause,— 
“啊！”我情不自禁地叫道，然后停了一会又问，
'En êtes-vous bien sure, Sylvie?' 
“你肯定吗，西尔维？”
'Oui, oui, monsieur, mademoiselle la directrice nous l' a dit elle- même il y a deux ou trois jours' 
“没错的，没错的，先生，校长前两天亲口对我们说的。”
And I could pursue my inquiries no further; time, place, and circumstances forbade my adding another word. I could neither comment on what had been said nor demand further particulars. A question as to the reason of the teacher's departure, as to whether it had been voluntary or otherwise, was indeed on my lips, but I suppressed it—there were listeners all round. An hour after, in passing Sylvie in the corridor as she was putting on her bonnet, I stopped short and asked,— 
我不能再问下去了；时间、地点以及环境都不允许我再多说一个字。我既不能对她所说的加以评价，也不能再进一步询问细节。我还想问问关于她离开的原因，是出于自愿还是其他原因，这些问题确实都到嘴边了，但我还是咽了下去——四周到处都有人在听着。一小时之后，碰见西尔维在走廊上戴帽子，我停了一下，问道：
'Sylvie, do you know Mdlle. Henri's address? I have some books of hers, 'I added carelessly,' and I should wish to send them to her.' 
“西尔维，你知道亨利小姐住在哪儿吗？我这里还有她的几本书，”我随意地加了一句，“我想把它们还给她。”
'No, monsieur,' replied Sylvie; 'but perhaps Rosalie, the portress, will be able to give it you.' 
“我不知道，先生，”西尔维回答道，“但是也许门房罗莎莉能告诉你。”
Rosalie's cabinet was just at hand. I stepped in and repeated the inquiry. Rosalie, a smart French grisette, looked up from her work with a knowing smile, precisely the sort of smile I had been so desirous to avoid exciting. Her answer was prepared. She knew nothing whatever of Mdlle. Henri's address—had never known it. Turning from her with impatience—for I believed she lied and was hired to lie—I almost knocked down some one who had been standing at my back. It was the directress. My abrupt movement made her recoil two or three steps. I was obliged to apologize, which I did more concisely than politely. No man likes to be dogged, and in the very irritable mood in which I then was the sight of Mdlle. Reuter thoroughly incensed me. At the moment I turned her countenance looked hard, dark, and inquisitive; her eyes were bent upon me with an expression of almost hungry curiosity. I had scarcely caught this phase of physiognomy ere it had vanished; a bland smile played on her features; my harsh apology was received with good-humoured facility.
罗莎莉的房间就在附近。我走进门，又问了一遍。罗莎莉是个精明的法国女工，她放下手中的活，抬头看了看我，会心一笑——正是我一直避免引起的那种微笑。她的回答是精心准备好的。她说她对亨利小姐的地址一无所知——从来也没有听说过。我不耐烦地转过身——因为我相信她在撒谎，而且是被收买而撒谎——差点撞倒一个一直站在我身后的人。那个人正是女校长。我突然的转身动作使她后退了两三步。我只好道歉；我的道歉与其说彬彬有礼，不如说是在敷衍搪塞。没有人喜欢被监视，而且我当时在气头上，看见勒泰小姐，更是觉得恼羞成怒。我转过身，只见她面容僵硬，脸色阴沉，还有几分打探之意；她的眼睛带着一种近乎渴望的好奇神情盯着我，我几乎还没有捕捉到那个神情，它便消失了；此时她又堆出一副笑盈盈的面孔，一派温和地接受了我不甚由衷的道歉。
'Oh, don't mention it, monsieur; you only touched my hair with your elbow; it is no worse, only a little dishevelled.' She shook it back, and passing her fingers through her curls, loosened them into more numerous and flowing ringlets. Then she went on with vivacity,— 
“哦，没关系，先生，你的手肘只碰到了我的头发，不要紧，只是把头发弄得有点乱。”她向后甩了甩头发，又用手指卷了卷，放开后又显出更多流畅的波纹。接着她又故作轻松地说，——
'Rosalie, I was coming to tell you to go instantly and close the windows of the salon. The wind is rising, and the muslin curtains will be covered with dust.' 
“罗莎莉，我是来告诉你，赶快去把客厅的窗子关上。刮风了，平纹布窗帘上会沾到灰尘的。”
Rosalie departed. 'Now,' thought I, 'this will not do. Mdlle. Reuter thinks her meanness in eavesdropping is screened by her art in devising a pretext, whereas the muslin curtains she speaks of are not more transparent than this same pretext.' An impulse came over me to thrust the flimsy screen aside, and confront her craft boldly with a word or two of plain truth. 'The roughshod foot treads most firmly on slippery ground,' thought I; so I began,— 'Mademoiselle Henri has left your establishment, been dismissed, I presume?' 'Ah, I wished to have a little conversation with you, monsieur,' replied the directress, with the most natural and affable air in the world. 'but we cannot talk quietly here. Will monsieur step into the garden a minute?' And she preceded me, stepping out through the glass door I have before mentioned.
罗莎莉离开了。“这可不行”我想。勒泰小姐以为找个借口就可以将她偷听别人说话的卑劣行径掩盖过去，更何况，让平纹布窗帘这借口也假得太过分了。我突然有一种冲动，想戳穿这低劣的谎言，大胆地说几句实话来对抗她的伎俩。“在滑地上行走，穿着带防滑钉铁蹄的鞋最稳当。”我想，于是我开始说：“亨利小姐已经离开了您的学校。我想她是被开除的吧？”“啊，我想跟你谈一谈，先生，”女校长用世界上最为自然友善的声音回答道，“但是在这里无法安静地交谈。请先生到公园去一会儿好吗？”于是她便在我前面，走出了我之前提到的那个玻璃门。
'There,' said she, when we had reached the centre of the middle alley, and when the foliage of shrubs and trees, now in their summer pride, closing behind and around us, shut out the view of the house, and thus imparted a sense of seclusion even to this little plot of ground in the very core of a capital—‘there one feels quiet and free when there are only pear-trees and rose-bushes about one. I dare say you, like me, monsieur, are sometimes tired of being eternally in the midst of life, of having human faces always round you, human eyes always upon you, human voices always in your ear. I am sure I often wish intensely for liberty to spend a whole month in the country at some little farmhouse, bien gentille, bien propre tout entouree de champs et de bois; quelle vie charmante que la vie champetre! N'est-ce pas, monsieur?' 
“就这儿吧。”当我们走到那条小路的正中时，她说道。时值仲夏，园中树木枝叶茂盛，所过之处都被灌木丛包围着，使我们看不见任何房屋，因此尽管这一块小地方处于城市的中心地带，但仍给人一种世外桃源的感觉。“在这里，一个人置身于梨树与玫瑰花丛之中，会感到静谧与自由。我敢说，先生您跟我一样，有时也会厌倦无休止地处于生活之中，厌倦于一张张总是在你周围的面孔，一双双总是盯着你的眼睛，一阵阵充斥你耳朵的人声。我总是很希望能有空到乡下的某个小农舍里无拘无束地住上一个月，环境优美，空气清新，周围都是原野或树林，那种田园生活多么地美妙啊！不是吗，先生？”
'Cela depend, mademoiselle.' 
“那得看情况，小姐。”
'Que le vent est bon et frais!' continued the directress; and she was right there, for it was a south wind, soft and sweet. I carried my hat in my hand, and this gentle breeze, passing through my hair, soothed my temples like palm. Its refreshing effect, however, penetrated no deeper than the mere surface of the frame; for as I walked by the side of Mdlle. Reuter my heart was still hot within me, and while I was musing the fire burned. Then spoke I with my tongue,— 
“好凉爽的风啊！”女校长继续说道，这回她倒说得没错，因为确实一阵柔和的南风拂过，沁人心脾。我把帽子拿在手里，这阵微风掠过我的头发，如同手掌一样轻抚着我的两鬓。然而这清爽的感觉只限于身体表面，因为当我和勒泰小姐并肩而行时，我的心中仍是愤愤不平，当我在深思的时候那把怒火仍在熊熊燃烧。然后我硬着舌头说：
'I understand Mdlle. Henri is gone from hence, and will not return?' 
“我知道亨利小姐已经走了，而且不会回来了，是吧？”
'Ah, true! I meant to have named the subject to you some days ago, but my time is so completely taken up I cannot do half the things I wish. Have you never experienced what it is, monsieur, to find the day too short by twelve hours for your numerous duties?' 
“啊，是这样！我本准备几天前跟您说这件事的，但是我的时间总是被占得满满的，我想做的事一半都没做到。事情太多，总是觉得白天十二个小时不够用，先生，您有过这样的经历吗？”
'Not often. Mdlle. Henri's departure was not voluntary, I presume? If it had been, she would certainly have given me some intimation of it, being my pupil.' 
“不常有。我想，亨利小姐的离开并不是自愿的吧？如果是自愿的话，作为我的学生她应该会知会我一声的。”
'Oh, she did not tell you? That was strange. For my part, I never thought of adverting to the subject; when one has so many things to attend to, one is apt to forget little incidents that are not of primary importance.' 
“哦，她没告诉你吗？那是有些奇怪了。至于我，我从来没想起来要讲这件事。当人有太多事情忙的时候，很容易忘记一些无关痛痒的小事。”
'You consider Mdlle. Henri's dismission, then, as a very insignificant event?' 
“您认为亨利小姐被解雇是一件鸡毛蒜皮的小事？”
'Dismission? Ah! she was not dismissed, I can say with truth, monsieur, that since I became the head of this establishment no master or teacher has ever been dismissed from it.' 
“被解雇？啊！她不是被解雇的，我可以实话实说，先生，自从我担任这所学校的校长以来还没有哪个老师被解雇过。”
'Yet some have left it, mademoiselle?' 
“但是有些人不是离开了吗，小姐？”
'Many. I have found it necessary to change frequently. A change of instructors is often beneficial to the interests of a school; it gives life and variety to the proceedings; it amuses the pupils, and suggests to the parents the idea of exertion and progress.' 
“是很多人。我觉得时不时的变化是很有必要的。更换一下老师对于维护一所学校的利益是大有好处的，它可以使得各项讨论更多元，更具活力，使得学生有新鲜感，同时让家长感到这所学校在不断改善进步。”
'Yet when you are tired of a professor or maitresse you scruple to dismiss them?' 
“那么当您对某个教师感到厌烦并解聘他们的时候会有顾虑吗？”
'No need to have recourse to such extreme measures I assure you. Allons, monsieur le professeur—asseyons nous; je vais vous donner une petite lecon dans votre etat d'instituteur.' (I wish I might write all she said to me in French; it loses sadly by being translated into English.) We had now reached the garden-chair. The directress sat down, and signed to me to sit by her; but I only rested my knee on the seat, and stood leaning my head and arm against the embowering branch of a huge laburnum, whose golden flowers, blent with the dusky green leaves of a lilac-bush, formed a mixed arch of shade and sunshine over the retreat. Mdlle. Reuter sat silent a moment; some novel movements were evidently working in her mind, and they showed their nature on her astute brow. She was meditating some chef d'ouvre of policy. Convinced by several months' experience that the affectation of virtues she did not possess was unavailing to ensnare me—aware that I had read her real nature, and would believe nothing of the character she gave out as being hers—she had determined at last to try a new key, and see if the lock of my heart would yield to that; a little audacity, a word of truth a glimpse of the real. 'Yes, I will try,' was her inward resolve; and then her blue eye glittered upon me—it did not flash, nothing of flame ever kindled in its temperate gleam.
“完全没有必要采取这样极端的方式，这我可以向你保证。来，先生，我们坐着说吧，我想就您的教师身份给您一点小小的忠告。”（我希望能把她用法语对我所说的话都写下来；翻译成英语会面目全非的。）此时我们走到了花园长椅处。女校长坐了下来，示意让我坐到她旁边；但我只是一条腿搭在椅子上，另一条腿站着，头和胳膊靠在一棵巨大的金链花枝干上，上面长满了树叶；它金色的花朵与紫丁香暗绿的叶子彼此交错，在这幽静的角落构成一个光影婆娑的弧形顶盖。勒泰小姐默不作声地坐了一会；很明显，她脑袋里正在打着新算盘，这一点可以从她狡猾的眉毛上看出来。她在苦想着什么锦囊妙计。几个月的经验使我确信，她本不具有却硬要装出来的美德对我是没有用了——因为我已经看穿了她的本性，不会再相信她做出的任何姿态——最后她下定决心要用一把新钥匙，来试试看能不能打开我的心灵之锁，那就是一点厚颜无耻，一句真话和一段真相。“好吧，我试试看，”这是她内心的决定；接着，她那蓝眼睛朝我闪动起来——那里并没有闪现什么，在那有节制的一瞥中并没有任何点燃的激情。
'Monsieur fears to sit by me?' she inquired playfully.
“先生莫非不敢坐在我旁边？”她调侃似地问。
'I have no wish to usurp Pelet's place,' I answered, for I had got the habit of speaking to her bluntly—a habit begun in anger, but continued because I saw that, instead of offending it fascinated her. She cast down her eyes and dropped her eyelids. She sighed uneasily; she turned with an anxious gesture, as if she would give me the idea of a bird that flutters in its cage, and would fain fly from its jail and jailer, and seek its natural mate and pleasant nest.
“我并不想侵占珀莱的位置，”我回答道，我已经养成跟她直言不讳的习惯——一开始这样是因为愤怒，但是之所以我继续这样做，是因为我发现这非但没有使她生气，反而让她着迷。她收起目光，垂下眼帘。她心神不安地叹了口气，焦虑地转过身去，似乎想让我感觉正有一只小鸟在笼子里振翅，想冲出笼子，飞离主人，寻觅它自然的同伴和欢乐的鸟巢。
'Well, and your lesson?' I demanded briefly.
“好吧，那您的忠告呢？”我简短地问道。
'Ah!' she exclaimed, recovering herself, 'you are so young, so frank and fearless, so talented, so impatient of imbecility, so disdainful of vulgarity, you need a lesson. Here it is, then. Far more is to be done in this world by dexterity than by strength; but perhaps you knew that before, for there is delicacy as well as power in your character, policy as well as pride!' 
“啊！”她一下子回过神来，惊叫道，”您这么年轻，这么坦率，勇敢无惧，又这么有天分，看不上愚钝，又瞧不上庸俗，您确实需要一些忠告。我想跟您说的是，在这个世界上需要靠巧力来解决的事比需要靠蛮力解决的事要多得多；但是也许您以前就知道了，因为您的性格粗中有细，既有谋才又不失傲骨！”
'Go on,' said I; and I could hardly help smiling, the flattery was so piquant, so finely seasoned. She caught the prohibited smile, though I passed my hand over my mouth to conceal it: and again she made room for me to sit beside her. I shook my head, though temptation penetrated to my senses at the moment, and once more I told her to go on.
“说下去，”我说道，并情不自禁地笑了起来，这一番奉承听起来十分痛快，也恰如其分。尽管我用手去掩住那强忍的笑，她还是看到了；她再次腾出空间让我坐在她身边。我摇了摇头，尽管那种诱惑此时已经冲破了我的理性，我再一次让她继续说下去。
'Well, then, if ever you are at the head of a large establishment, dismiss nobody. To speak truth, monsieur (and to you I will speak truth), I despise people who are always making rows, blustering, sending off one to the right, and another to the left, urging and hurrying circumstances. I'll tell you what I like best to do, monsieur, shall I?' She looked up again; she had compounded her glance well this time—much archness, more deference, a spicy dash of coquetry, an unveiled consciousness of capacity. I nodded. She treated me like the great Mogul; so I became the great Mogul as far as she was concerned.
“好吧，倘若有朝一日您成为一所大学校的校长，请不要解雇任何人。说真话，先生（对您我要说真话），我鄙视那些总是吵吵嚷嚷，说话气势汹汹，今天解雇这个，明天辞退那个，不分青红皂白胡乱处理事情的人。让我告诉您我最喜欢什么，好吗先生？”她又抬起头看着我，这次她给的眼神搭配得很好——十分顽皮，也更为顺从，外加少许故作媚态的风骚，还有毫不掩饰的自负。我点了点头。她像对待莫卧儿大帝一样对待我；我估计在她眼里我就是莫卧儿大帝了。
'I like, monsieur, to take my knitting in my hands, and to sit quietly down in my chair. Circumstances defile past me. I watch their march. So long as they follow the course I wish, I say nothing and do nothing. I don't clap my hands, and cry out "Bravo! how lucky I am!" to attract the attention and envy of my neighbours—I am merely passive; but when events fall out ill—when circumstances become adverse—I watch very vigilantly. I knit on still, and still I hold my tongue; but every now and then, monsieur, I just put my toe out—so—and give the rebellious circumstance a little secret push, without noise which sends it the way I wish, and I am successful after all, and nobody has seen my expedient. So, when teachers or masters become troublesome and inefficient—when, in short, the interests of the school would suffer from their retaining their places—I mind my knitting; events progress, circumstances glide past; I see one which, if pushed ever so little awry will render untenable the post I wish to have vacated—the deed is done—the stumbling- block removed—and no one saw me; I have not made an enemy; I am rid of an encumbrance.' 
“先生，我喜欢手里拿着我的编织物，安静地坐在我的椅子上。各种事务列队一般从我身边经过。我看着它们前进。只要它们按着我所希望的路线行走，我就什么都不说什么也不做。我不会边鼓掌边说‘太棒了！我多幸运呐！’以此来吸引邻居们的关注与羡慕——我只会不动声色；可是当有事情脱轨——当情况不利时——我就会高度警惕地查看着。我还是会静静地编织，还是不说话，但是先生，我只需要伸出脚——就是这样——私下里悄悄地踹那个反叛者一脚，把他送回到我所希望的轨道上；而我终究是成功了，也没有人看到我的对策。所以，每当有老师变得调皮捣蛋并且效率低下——简而言之，当他们留在学校便会损坏学校的利益时——我专心织着我的衣服；事情发展，情况倒退；如果我发现有人不管怎样都推不上正轨，无法完成我交给他的工作时——我就会采取行动——绊脚石被搬开了——没有人看见我，我也没有树敌，只是解决掉了一个累赘。”
A moment since, and I thought her alluring; this speech concluded, I looked on her with distaste. 'Just like you,' was my cold answer. 'And in this way you have ousted Mdlle. Henri? You wanted her office, therefore you rendered it intolerable to her?' 
之前我以为她在引诱我，而当她说完时，我憎恶地看着她。“多么像你。”我冷冷地答道。“你就是用这种方法赶走亨利小姐的？你想解除她的职务，所以你便使她在这待不下去？”
'Not at all, monsieur; I was merely anxious about Mdlle. Henri's health. No; your moral sight is clear and piercing, but there you have failed to discover the truth. I took—I have always taken a real interest in Mdlle. Henri's welfare. I did not like her going out in all weathers; I thought it would be more advantageous for her to obtain a permanent situation: besides, I considered her now qualified to do something more than teach sewing. I reasoned with her; left the decision to herself She saw the correctness of my views, and adopted them.' 
“完全不是这样的，先生；我只是关心亨利小姐的健康。不，您一向明辨是非，但是这一次您没看清事实。我曾——我一直是真真正正地在为亨利小姐着想。我不想看到她不管刮风下雨都得外出，我想如果她能找到一个固定的职位对她会更有益；此外，我觉得现在她有能力做更多的事，不用非得教缝纫。我对她晓之以理，让她自己做决定。她知道我的观点是对的，于是便接受了。”
'Excellent! And now, mademoiselle, you will have the goodness to give me her address.' 
“太棒了！那现在，小姐，请您把她的地址给我吧。“
'Her address!' and a sombre and stony change came over the mien of the directress—‘her address? Ah! well, I wish I could oblige you, monsieur, but I cannot, and I will tell you why. Whenever I myself asked her for her address, she always evaded the inquiry. I thought—I may be wrong—but I thought her motive for doing so was a natural though mistaken reluctance to introduce me to some probably very poor abode. Her means were narrow, her origin obscure; she lives somewhere, doubtless, in the 'basse ville.'' 
“她的地址！”女校长的态度立即变得阴暗而冷酷——“她的地址？啊！好吧，我倒是很愿意告诉您，先生，但是我不能，我来告诉您原因。因为我自己每次问她住在哪儿时，她总是避而不谈。我觉得——当然我有可能是错的——我觉得她这么做虽然不对，但她的动机也合乎情理，她极有可能是不想让我看见她十分贫寒的住所。她收入不多，出身卑微，毋庸置疑，她一定是住在一个‘下层的居住区'。”
'I'll not lose sight of my best pupil yet,' said I, 'though she were born of beggars and lodged in a cellar; for the rest, it is absurd to make a bugbear of her origin to me. I happen to know that she was a Swiss pastor's daughter, neither more nor less; and as to her narrow means, I care nothing for the poverty of her purse so long as her heart overflows with affluence.' 
“我不会见不到我最好的学生的，”我说道，“即便她是出身于乞丐家庭，住在地下室里；另外，关于她的出身您欺骗我可太可笑了。我碰巧知道她刚好是一位瑞士牧师的女儿，至于她的贫寒，只要她拥有丰富的精神世界，再穷我也不在乎。”
'Your sentiments are perfectly noble, monsieur,' said the directress, affecting to suppress a yawn; her sprightliness was now extinct, her temporary candour shut up. The little, red-coloured, piratical-looking pennon of audacity she had allowed to float a minute in the air was furled, and the broad, sober-hued flag of dissimulation again hung low over the citadel. I did not like her thus, so I cut short the tete-a-tete and departed.
“先生，您的情操真是无比高尚。”女校长说道，并假装要打哈欠但又忍住的样子；如今她的活泼已经荡然无存，她片刻的直率已经无处可见。那面狭小的厚颜无耻的红色海盗三角旗在空中飘扬了一阵后被收起来了，取而代之的是虚伪欺骗的素色大旗，它再次低低地挂在城堡之上。我不喜欢她这样子，于是我中断了谈话，起身离去。
