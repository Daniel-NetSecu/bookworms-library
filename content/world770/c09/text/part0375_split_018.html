XIX  

第十九章  

Litvinov went quickly up the staircase of the Hôtel de l'Europe; a little girl of thirteen, with a sly little face of Kalmuck cast, who had apparently been on the look-out for him, stopped him, saying in Russian: "Come this way, please; Irina Pavlovna will be here directly." He looked at her in perplexity. She smiled, repeated: "Come along, come along," and led him to a small room, facing Irina's bedroom, and filled with traveling trunks and portmanteaus, then at once disappeared, closing the door very softly. Litvinov had not time to look about him, before the door was quickly opened, and before him in a pink ball-dress, with pearls in her hair and on her neck, stood Irina. She simply rushed at him, clutched him by both hands, and for a few instants was speechless; her eyes were shining, and her bosom heaving as though she had run up to a height.
李特维诺夫迅速走上欧罗巴旅馆的楼梯。一个长着一张卡尔梅克式的淘气的小脸、十三岁的小女孩——很明显，她一直在等着李特维诺夫，叫住了他，用俄语说道：“请往这边来，伊琳娜·巴甫洛夫娜马上就会来这里。”李特维诺夫困惑地看着她。她微笑着，又说道：“往这边来，这边来。”说着，把他领到伊琳娜卧室对面的一个堆满了运动裤和行李箱的小房间，轻轻地关上门就立即离开了。李特维诺夫还没来得及环顾四周，门就迅速地被打开了，伊琳娜站在了他面前，穿着一套粉红色的晚礼服，头发上和脖子上都戴着珍珠。她只是冲向他，紧紧地抓住他的双手，很久都没有说话。她的眼睛闪闪发光，胸脯起伏不断，好像跑上了高处一样的。
"I could not receive... you there," she began in a hurried whisper: "we are just going to a dinner party, but I wanted above everything to see you… That is your betrothed, I suppose, with whom I met you today?" 
“我不能在这里与您相见……”她开始急促地轻声说道，“我们现在马上要去赴晚宴，但我最想做的事就是见您……今天我所见到的和您在一起的那位，我想就是您的未婚妻吧？”
"Yes, that was my betrothed," said Litvinov, with emphasis on the word "was." 
“是的，她曾经是我的未婚妻。”李特维诺夫回答道，强调了“曾经”这个词。
"And so I wanted to see you for one minute, to tell you that you must consider yourself absolutely free, that everything that happened yesterday ought not to affect your plans…" 
“所以我才想和您短暂地见一面，告诉您一定不要有负担，昨天所发生的一切都不应该影响您所有的计划……”
"Irina!" cried Litvinov, "why are you saying this?"  He uttered these words in a loud voice. There was the note in them of unbounded passion. Irina involuntarily closed her eyes for a minute.
“伊琳娜！”李特维诺夫叫道，“您为什么要说这些？”他大声地说出这些话。声音中带着无法遏制的激动之情。伊琳娜不由自主地将双眼闭上了一会儿。
"Oh, my sweet one!" she went on in a whisper still more subdued, but with unrestrained emotion, "you don't know how I love you, but yesterday I only paid my debt, I made up for the past… Ah! I could not give you back my youth, as I would, but I have laid no obligations on you, I have exacted no promise of any sort of you, my sweet! Do what you will, you are free as air, you are bound in no way, understand that, understand that!" "But I can't live without you, Irina," Litvinov interrupted, in a whisper, now; "I am yours for ever and always, since yesterday… I can only breathe at your feet..." 
“啊，亲爱的！”她的声音压得更低了，但还是带着难以抑制的情感，“您不知道我有多么爱您，但是昨天我只是还了旧债，赎了以前的罪……啊！我不能像我原先期望的那样将我的青春给您，但我不需要您对我负任何责任，也不要您任何的承诺，亲爱的！您要怎样做就怎样做，您同空气一样自由，您没有任何束缚，您要明白这一点，明白这一点！”“但是没了您我就无法活下去啊，伊琳娜。”李特维诺夫轻声地打断了她，“自昨天之后，我就永远属于您、一直属于您了……只有在您的脚下我才能呼吸……”
He stooped down all in a tremble to kiss her hands. Irina gazed at his bent head.
他颤抖着俯身吻她的双手。伊琳娜凝视着他低垂的头。
"Then let me say," she said, "that I, too, am ready for anything, that I, too, will consider no one, and nothing. As you decide, so it shall be. I, too, am for ever yours... yours." 
“那么让我告诉您。”她说道，“我也一样，作好了一切准备，也和您一样，不再考虑其他任何人和任何事情。既然您决定了，那就这样吧。我也生生世世属于您……属于您。”
Some one tapped warily at the door. Irina stooped, whispered once more, "Yours... good-by!” Litvinov felt her breath on his hair, the touch of her lips. When he stood up, she was no longer in the room, but her dress was rustling in the corridor, and from the distance came the voice of Ratmirov: "Eh bien? Vous ne venez pas?" 
有人小心地敲了敲门。伊琳娜俯下身子，又一次轻声地说：“属于您……再见！”李特维诺夫在自己的头发中感觉到她的呼吸以及她双唇的触碰。当他站起来时，伊琳娜已经不在房间了，但还能听到她的裙子在走廊里发出的瑟瑟声，而且远处传来了拉特米罗夫的声音：“怎么啦？您不去了吗？”
Litvinov sat down on a high chest, and hid his face. A feminine fragrance, fresh and delicate, clung about him… Irina had held his hand in her hands. "It's too much, too much," was his thought. The little girl came into the room, and smiling again in response to his agitated glance, said:  
李特维诺夫坐在一个很高的箱子上，捂住脸。一种淡雅清新的、女性的香味萦绕在他身边……伊琳娜刚刚还握着他的手。“这已经足够了，这已足够了。”他想。那个小女孩来到房间，见李特维诺夫焦虑地瞥了自己一眼，又笑着说：
"Kindly come, now—" 
“赶紧走吧，现在——”
He got up, and went out of the hotel. It was no good even to think of returning home: he had to regain his balance first. His heart was beating heavily and unevenly; the earth seemed faintly reeling under his feet. Litvinov turned again along the Lichtenthaler Allee. He realized that the decisive moment had come, that to put it off longer, to dissemble, to turn away, had become impossible, that an explanation with Tatyana had become inevitable; he could imagine how she was sitting there, never stirring, waiting for him... he could foresee what he would say to her; but how was he to act, how was he to begin? He had turned his back on his upright, well-organized, orderly future; he knew that he was flinging himself headlong into a gulf... but that did not confound him. The thing was done, but how was he to face his judge? And if only his judge would come to meet him—an angel with a flaming sword; that would be easier for a sinning heart... instead of which he had himself to plunge the knife in… Infamous! But to turn back, to abandon that other, to take advantage of the freedom offered him, recognized as his.... No! better to die! No, he would have none of such loathsome freedom... but would humble himself in the dust, and might those eyes look down on him with love… 
他站起来，离开了旅馆。就算想到他即将要回去都让他感觉不好：他首先必须得恢复平静。他的心剧烈、不均地跳动着，脚下的大地似乎也在微微地颤动。李特维诺夫转身再次沿着李赫顿泰勒林阴道走去。他意识到决定性的时刻到了,拖延、掩饰、逃避都已经不可能了，向塔吉娅娜解释已经在所难免。他可以想象她正坐在那里、安安静静地等着他……他可以预见自己会跟她说什么，但是他该怎么做，从何说起呢？他已经背叛了自己合乎正道、井然有序的未来，他知道他在一头陷入一个漩涡……但他并不因此而困惑。事已至此，但是要如何面临自己的判官呢？如果他真的要去见判官——一个带着火焰之剑的天使,那么他那颗罪恶的心也会舒服一点……因为不需要自己将刀插进去……真是太无耻了！但是如果回去，放弃另外的一切，享受他接受并认可的自由……不！他宁愿去死！不，他是不会要这种肮脏的自由的……他宁愿化作卑微的尘土，也要得到充满爱意的双眼的关注……
"Grigory Mihalitch," said a melancholy voice, and some one's hand was laid heavily upon Litvinov.
“格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇！”一个忧郁的声音响起，接着有人重重地拍了下李特维诺夫。
He looked round in some alarm and recognized Potugin.
他有些惊慌地环顾四周，认出是波图金。
"I beg your pardon, Grigory Mihalitch," began the latter with his customary humility, "I am disturbing you, perhaps, but, seeing you in the distance, I thought… However if you're not in the humor..."  "On the contrary I'm delighted," Litvinov muttered between his teeth.
“真是抱歉，格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇。”波图金以他惯有的谦虚礼貌的口吻说道，“也许我打扰到您了，但是我远远地看到了您，我就想……不过要是您的心情不好的话……”“哦，不，我很开心。”李特维诺夫喃喃地说。
Potugin walked beside him.
波图金跟他并肩走着。
"What a lovely evening!" he began, "so warm! Have you been walking long?" 
“多么美好的夜晚啊！”他开始说道，“如此温暖！您走了很长时间吗？”
"No, not long." 
“不，不是很久。”
"Why do I ask, though; I've just seen you come out of the Hôtel de l'Europe." 
“其实我又何必问这个呢，因为我刚刚见到您从欧巴罗旅馆出来。”
"Then you've been following me?" 
“那您一直在跟踪我了？”
"Yes." 
“是的。”
"You have something to say to me?" 
“您有话对我说？”
"Yes," Potugin repeated, hardly audibly. Litvinov stopped and looked at his uninvited companion. His face was pale, his eyes moved restlessly; his contorted features seemed overshadowed by old, long-standing grief. "What do you specially want to say to me?" Litvinov said slowly, and he moved forward.
“是的。”波图金重复了一遍，声音小得几乎听不见。李特维诺夫停下脚步，看着这位不速之客。他的脸色苍白，眼睛不安地转动着，扭曲的五官似乎因为过去的、持久的悲痛而显得更加阴郁。“您有什么特别事要说给我听呢？”李特维诺夫缓慢地说道，一边继续往前走着。
"Ah, with your permission... directly. If it's all the same to you, let us sit down here on this seat. It will be most convenient." "Why, this is something mysterious," Litvinov declared, seating himself near him. "You don't seem quite yourself, Sozont Ivanitch." 
“嗯，既然您允许了，那我就直说了……如果您觉得合适，我们就在这边的椅子上坐下吧。那会方便很多。”“啊，这事好神秘。”李特维诺夫说道，并坐在他身旁。“您看上去有点儿不舒服，索松特·伊凡内奇。”
"No; I'm all right; and it's nothing mysterious either. I specially wanted to tell you... the impression made on me by your betrothed... she is betrothed to you, I think?... well, anyway, by the girl to whom you introduced me to-day. I must say that in the course of my whole existence I have never met a more attractive creature. A heart of gold, a really angelic nature." 
“不，我很好，也没有什么神秘的事情。我只是想特地告诉您……我对您未婚妻的印象……我想她是您的未婚妻吧？呃，就是今天您介绍给我认识的那位女孩。我必须得说她是我这一生中见过的最有魅力的女人。她有着一颗金子般的心，真正像天使一样的本性。”
Potugin uttered all these words with the same bitter and mournful air, so that even Litvinov could not help noticing the incongruity between his expression of face and his speech.
说这些话时，波图金依然是一脸的沉痛与悲伤，所以就连李特维诺夫也不禁注意到了他面部表情和言语的不协调。
"You have formed a perfectly correct estimate of Tatyana Petrovna," Litvinov began, "though I can't help being surprised, first that you should be aware of the relation in which I stand to her; and secondly, that you should have understood her so quickly. She really has an angelic nature; but allow me to ask, did you want to talk to me about this?" 
“您对塔吉娅娜·彼得罗夫娜的评价非常贴切。”李特维诺夫开始说道，“但我还是不禁感到十分吃惊，第一，您应该知道我和她的关系，第二，您竟能如此迅速地了解她。是的，她确实有着像天使一样的本性，但是请允许我问一下，您想要和我谈的就是这个吗？”
"It's impossible not to understand her at once," Potugin replied quickly, as though evading the last question. "One need only take one look into her eyes. She deserves every possible happiness on earth, and enviable is the fate of the man whose lot it is to give her that happiness! One must hope he may prove worthy of such a fate." 
“想不马上了解她根本不可能啊。”波图金马上答道，似乎是想避开他最后那个问题。“只要看一看她的眼睛足够了。她值得拥有这个世界上的所有快乐，那位能给她带来这些快乐的男人真是让人艳羡，命太好了！希望他能证明自己值得拥有这等好命。”
Litvinov frowned slightly.
李特维诺夫轻轻地皱了眉头。
"Excuse me, Sozont Ivanitch," he said, "I must confess our conversation strikes me as altogether rather original… I should like to know, does the hint contained in your words refer to me?" 
“很抱歉，索松特·伊凡内奇。”他说，“我必须得承认我觉得我们的谈话很独特……我想知道您的言外之意是不是指我？”
Potugin did not at once answer Litvinov; he was visibly struggling with himself.
波图金并没有马上回答李特维诺夫，很显然他内心正在挣扎不已。
"Grigory Mihalitch," he began at last, "either I am completely mistaken in you, or you are capable of hearing the truth, from whomsoever it may come, and in however unattractive a form it may present itself. I told you just now, that I saw where you came from." 
“格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇。”他终于说道，“不管是我完全误会了您，还是您能听得进去真话，无论是谁告诉您的，无论它是以一种多么不愉快的方式呈现出来。我刚刚跟您说过了，我看到您从哪里出来。”
"Why, from the Hôtel de l'Europe. What of that?" 
“怎么了，我就是从欧巴罗旅馆出来的。那又怎样？”
"I know, of course, whom you have been to see there." 
“当然，我还知道您在那里跟谁见了面。”
"What?" 
“什么？”
"You have been to see Madame Ratmirov." 
“您去见拉特米罗夫夫人了。”
"Well, I have been to see her. What next?"  "What next?... You, betrothed to Tatyana Petrovna, have been to see Madame Ratmirov, whom you love... and who loves you." 
“是的，我是去见了她。然后呢？”“然后又怎样呢？……您已经是塔吉娅娜·彼得罗夫娜的未婚夫，却和拉特米罗夫夫人约会，还爱着她……她也爱着您。”
Litvinov instantly got up from the seat; the blood rushed to his head. "What's this?" he cried at last, in a voice of concentrated exasperation: "stupid jesting, spying? Kindly explain yourself." 
李特维诺夫一下子从椅子上跳了起来，热血冲上了他的大脑。“什么啊！”他大喊，声音充满了愤怒，“您开什么蠢玩笑，您在监视我吗？恳请您解释清楚！”
Potugin turned a weary look upon him.
波图金疲惫地看着他。
"Ah! don't he offended at my words. Grigory Mihalitch, me you cannot offend. I did not begin to talk to you for that, and I'm in no joking humor now." "Perhaps, perhaps. I'm ready to believe in the excellence of your intentions; but still I may be allowed to ask you by what right you meddle in the private affairs, in the inner life, of another man, a man who is nothing to you; and what grounds you have for so confidently giving out your own... invention for the truth?" 
“哎！不要因为我的话生气。格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇，您不要生我的气。我跟您说这些不是为了惹您生气，我也没心思开玩笑。”“也许吧，也许吧！那我就相信您的良苦用心吧，但是我还是想问您，您有什么权利干涉别人的私事、别人的私生活，而且这个人跟您一点关系也没有，您有什么理由这么自信地说出您自己……瞎编乱造的事实？”
"My invention! If I had imagined it, it should not have made you angry; and as for my right, well I never heard before that a man ought to ask himself whether he had the right to hold out a hand to a drowning man." 
“我瞎编乱造！如果我是在胡说八道，那您就不该生气。至于说到我的权利，我是从未听说过，当有人落水了，一个人还会问自己是否有权利伸出援助之手。”
"I am humbly grateful for your tender solicitude," cried Litvinov passionately, "but I am not in the least in need of it, and all the phrases about the ruin of inexperienced young men wrought by society women, about the immorality of fashionable society, and so on, I look upon merely as stock phrases, and indeed in a sense I positively despise them; and so I beg you to spare your rescuing arm, and to let me drown in peace." 
“我真是感谢您对我的这份心意！”李特维诺夫激动地说，“但是我根本就不需要，什么被社交圈的女子毁掉的涉世未深的年轻男子，什么上流社会的伤风败俗，诸如此类的话我觉得全都是老调重弹，也确实极其鄙视这类话，所以请您收起您的援助之手，让我平静地淹死吧！”
Potugin again raised his eyes to Litvinov. He was breathing hard, his lips were twitching.
波图金抬起眼睛看着李特维诺夫。他喘着粗气，嘴唇在抽搐着。
"But look at me, young man," broke from him at last, and he clapped himself on the breast: "can you suppose I have anything in common with the ordinary, self-satisfied moralist, a preacher? Don't you understand that simply from interest in you, however strong it might be, I would never have let fall a word, I would never have given you grounds for reproaching me with what I hate above all things—indiscretion, intrusiveness? Don't you see that this is something of a different kind altogether, that before you is a man crushed, utterly obliterated by the very passion, from the results of which he would save you, and... and for the same woman!” 
“但是请看着我，年轻人。”他最终说道，还拍着胸脯，“您觉得我和那些平庸自负的说教者、传教士有什么共同之处吗？难道您没明白，我只是在关心您吗？不管我是如何强烈地想关心您，我都不允许、也不会给您任何理由说出这样的话来责怪我，这些我最讨厌的话——说我鲁莽、插手别人的事！您没有看出来，这完全是两码事吗？在您的面前也是一个同样被同一种感情摧残着，甚至是被毁灭的人，正因如此，他才向您伸出了援手，更何况……更何况是为了同一个女人！”
Litvinov stepped back a pace.
李特维诺夫向后退了一步。
"Is it possible? What did you say?... You… you... Sozont Ivanitch? But Madame Byelsky... that child?" 
“这有可能吗？您在说什么？……您……您……索松特·伊凡内奇？可是别里斯卡娅女士怎么回事……还有那个孩子？”
"Ah, don't cross-examine me... Believe me! That is a dark, terrible story, and I'm not going to tell you it. Madame Byelsky I hardly knew, that child is not mine, but I took it all upon myself... because... she wished it, because it was necessary for her. Why am I here in your hateful Baden? And, in fact, could you suppose, could you for one instant imagine, that I'd have brought myself to caution you out of sympathy for you? I'm sorry for that sweet, good girl, your fiancée, but what have I to do with your future, with you both?... But I am afraid for her... for her." 
“哎，不要再盘问我了……相信我吧！这是一个阴暗可怕的故事，我不想跟您讲。我对别里斯卡娅娜女士几乎一无所知，那个孩子也不是我的，但是我把这些责任都扛在自己身上……那是因为……因为她希望我这样做，这对于她很有必要。我为什么会呆在这令您厌恶的巴登呢？而且，事实上，您想一下吧，您就想一下，我来这里警告您难道会是因为同情您吗？我很同情您那个可爱、善良的未婚妻，但你们两个人的未来、你们两个人又关我什么事呢？但我为她担惊受怕……为她担惊受怕。”
"You do me great honor, Mr. Potugin," began Litvinov, "but since, according to you, we are both in the same position, why is it you don't apply such exhortations to yourself, and ought I not to ascribe your apprehensions to another feeling?" 
“您真是让我倍感荣幸，波图金先生。”李特维诺夫开始说道，“但是，正如您所说，我们的处境是一样的，您为何不用同样的话来劝说您自己，还有，我是不是不该把您的担心归结于另一种情感呢？”
"That is to jealousy, you mean? Ah, young man, young man, it's shameful of you to shuffle and make pretences, it's shameful of you not to realize what a bitter sorrow is speaking to you now by my lips! No, I am not in the same position as you! I, I am old, ridiculous, an utterly harmless old fool—but you! But there's no need to talk about it! You would not for one second agree to accept the position I fill, and fill with gratitude! Jealousy? A man is not jealous who has never had even a drop of hope, and this is not the first time it has been my lot to endure this feeling. I am only afraid... afraid for her, understand that. And could I have guessed when she sent me to you that the feeling of having wronged you—she owned to feeling that—would carry her so far?"
