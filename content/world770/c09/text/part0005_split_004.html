V  

五  

I was seldom at home in the evening, for when I attempted to occupy myself in my apartments the lamplight brought in a swarm of noxious insects, and it was too hot for closed windows.
我晚上的时候很少在家，因为当我试图呆在自己的房间时，灯光会招来一群害虫，而关窗又太热。
Accordingly I spent the late hours either on the water (the moonlight of Venice is famous), or in the splendid square which serves as a vast forecourt to the strange old basilica of Saint Mark.
因此，晚间时分，我要么在水上度过（威尼斯的月光很著名），要么在宽阔的广场上度过。这个广场充当了古老而独特的圣马克长方形会堂宽敞的前院。
I sat in front of Florian's cafe, eating ices, listening to music, talking with acquaintances: the traveler will remember how the immense cluster of tables and little chairs stretches like a promontory into the smooth lake of the Piazza. The whole place, of a summer's evening, under the stars and with all the lamps, all the voices and light footsteps on marble (the only sounds of the arcades that enclose it), is like an open-air saloon dedicated to cooling drinks and to a still finer degustation—that of the exquisite impressions received during the day.
我坐在弗洛里安咖啡馆前面，吃着冰品，听着音乐，和熟人聊天：旅行者都会记得这片如此密集的桌子和小椅子，它们像岬角一样，延伸进湖水般光滑的广场。夏夜里，繁星下，伴随着所有的灯光、所有的喧闹声和大理石面上轻轻的脚步声（环绕这块拱石的唯一声音），整个地方像是专卖冷饮的户外酒吧间，供人细细品尝——品尝日间得到的细腻印象。
When I did not prefer to keep mine to myself there was always a stray tourist, disencumbered of his Baedeker, to discuss them with, or some domesticated painter rejoicing in the return of the season of strong effects.
当我不愿独享的时候，总有懒得费神看旅行指南的迷路游客来和我讨论这些印象。或是遇到一些画通俗画的画家，他们正为这带给人强烈印象的季节的回归而欢欣。
The wonderful church, with its low domes and bristling embroideries, the mystery of its mosaic and sculpture, looking ghostly in the tempered gloom, and the sea breeze passed between the twin columns of the Piazzetta, the lintels of a door no longer guarded, as gently as if a rich curtain were swaying there.
低低的圆屋顶、丛立的装饰、神秘的马赛克和雕塑让雄伟的教堂在灰暗中显得阴森。海风轻柔地在小广场两根圆柱间穿过，轻柔得像是在摇曳昂贵的窗帘。门柱已经不再有守卫了。
I used sometimes on these occasions to think of the Misses Bordereau and of the pity of their being shut up in apartments which in the Venetian July even Venetian vastness did not prevent from being stuffy.
在那样的情形下，我有时会想起两位博尔德罗小姐，想到她们多可怜，关在自己的房间里。在七月的威尼斯，就算在空旷的地方都免不了闷热，更何况在她们的房间里。
Their life seemed miles away from the life of the Piazza, and no doubt it was really too late to make the austere Juliana change her habits.
她们的生活和这广场的生活有千里之遥，毫无疑问，要想让古板的朱莉安娜改变她的习惯实在是太晚了。
But poor Miss Tita would have enjoyed one of Florian's ices, I was sure; sometimes I even had thoughts of carrying one home to her.
不过，可怜的蒂塔小姐可能会喜欢弗洛里安的冰品，这我可以肯定。有时，我甚至想给她带一份回去。
Fortunately my patience bore fruit, and I was not obliged to do anything so ridiculous.
幸运的是，我的耐心结出了果实，我也没有必要做任何这样荒谬的事。
One evening about the middle of July I came in earlier than usual—I forget what chance had led to this—and instead of going up to my quarters made my way into the garden.
七月中的一个晚上，我比平时进家门要早——我忘了到底是什么原因导致我提前回来——我没有回房间，而是来到了花园。
The temperature was very high; it was such a night as one would gladly have spent in the open air, and I was in no hurry to go to bed.
气温非常高。这样的夜晚，人在室外呆着会比较舒心，而且我也不急着睡觉。
I had floated home in my gondola, listening to the slow splash of the oar in the narrow dark canals, and now the only thought that solicited me was the vague reflection that it would be pleasant to recline at one's length in the fragrant darkness on a garden bench.
我坐着平底小船划回家，在窄窄的、黑暗的运河里，听着船桨缓缓地拍打水面。我脑子里只有些模糊的想法，认为在芳香黑暗的花园长凳上摊开四肢躺下会很愉快。
The odor of the canal was doubtless at the bottom of that aspiration and the breath of the garden, as I entered it, gave consistency to my purpose.
运河的气息无疑是我最不渴望的。而花园的气味，我走进去时，和我的目的是吻合的。
It was delicious—just such an air as must have trembled with Romeo's vows when he stood among the flowers and raised his arms to his mistress's balcony.
那气息真是怡人——正是那种空气。当罗密欧站在花丛中，向阳台上的情人举起双臂，随着他的誓言而颤抖的，也是这样的空气。
I looked at the windows of the palace to see if by chance the example of Verona (Verona being not far off) had been followed; but everything was dim, as usual, and everything was still.
我望着宅子的窗户，看有没有人碰巧延续那个在维罗纳发生的故事（维罗纳离这里不远）。但是一切都像平时那么暗淡，一切都是静止的。
Juliana, on summer nights in her youth, might have murmured down from open windows at Jeffrey Aspern, but Miss Tita was not a poet's mistress any more than I was a poet. This however did not prevent my gratification from being great as I became aware on reaching the end of the garden that Miss Tita was seated in my little bower.
朱莉安娜，在她年轻时的夏夜里，也许从敞开的窗户向杰弗里·阿斯本低语，但蒂塔小姐不是一位诗人的情妇，我也不是一位诗人。但这不影响我强烈的满足感，因为当我走到花园尽头的时候，我发现蒂塔小姐坐在我的小凉亭里。
At first I only made out an indistinct figure, not in the least counting on such an overture from one of my hostesses; it even occurred to me that some sentimental maidservant had stolen in to keep a tryst with her sweetheart.
一开始，我只辨认出模糊的人形，一点儿也没有想过是我的房东之一。我甚至认为是某个多情的女仆，偷溜进这里和她的情人幽会。
I was going to turn away, not to frighten her, when the figure rose to its height and I recognized Miss Bordereau's niece.
我打算转身，不去惊扰她。当这个人影起身站直，我认出她是博尔德罗小姐的侄女。
I must do myself the justice to say that I did not wish to frighten her either, and much as I had longed for some such accident I should have been capable of retreating. It was as if I had laid a trap for her by coming home earlier than usual and adding to that eccentricity by creeping into the garden.
我要为自己辩解一下，我也不想惊扰她。尽管我一直渴望有这样的事件发生，我仍然能够保持镇定。这好像是我故意早回家，对她设了个圈套一样，更怪的是，我还是蹑手蹑脚地走进花园的。
As she rose she spoke to me, and then I reflected that perhaps, secure in my almost inveterate absence, it was her nightly practice to take a lonely airing.
她站起身和我说话。我猜想，也许，因为我晚上总不在家，她觉得安全，所以晚上独自呆在室外。
There was no trap, in truth, because I had had no suspicion.
其实，这里没有圈套，因为我没什么可怀疑的。
At first I took for granted that the words she uttered expressed discomfiture at my arrival; but as she repeated them—I had not caught them clearly—I had the surprise of hearing her say, "Oh, dear, I'm so very glad you've come!” She and her aunt had in common the property of unexpected speeches. She came out of the arbor almost as if she were going to throw herself into my arms.
开始的时候，我理所当然地认为，她要说，我的到来让她很尴尬。但当她重复地讲的时候——我刚开始没有听清楚——我很惊讶地听到她说：“哦，天哪，你来了，我真是太高兴了！”她和她的姑妈有个共同的特征，会冷不丁说些出人意料的话。她走出凉亭，简直像是要扑到我的怀里似的。
I hasten to add that she did nothing of the kind; she did not even shake hands with me. It was a gratification to her to see me and presently she told me why—because she was nervous when she was out-of-doors at night alone.
我得马上补充一句，她并没有做那样的事。她甚至都没有和我握手。见到我她很高兴，还立刻告诉了我原因——因为她害怕晚上独自一人呆在外面。
The plants and bushes looked so strange in the dark, and there were all sorts of queer sounds—she could not tell what they were—like the noises of animals.
花草和灌木在夜色里看起来很奇怪。到处都是各种奇怪的声音——她分不出那些是什么声音——像是动物的叫声。
She stood close to me, looking about her with an air of greater security but without any demonstration of interest in me as an individual.
她站在我旁边，东张西望，带着一种较为安心的神情，不过对我这个人没有表现出半点儿兴趣。
Then I guessed that nocturnal prowlings were not in the least her habit, and I was also reminded (I had been struck with the circumstance in talking with her before I took possession) that it was impossible to overestimate her simplicity.
于是我猜测，夜间游荡一点儿也不是她的习惯，同时我也提醒自己（和她谈话这情形让我吃惊不已，然后才定下神来）不能太过高估她的简单率直。
"You speak as if you were lost in the backwoods," I said, laughing. "How you manage to keep out of this charming place when you have only three steps to take to get into it is more than I have yet been able to discover. You hide away mighty well so long as I am on the premises, I know; but I had a hope that you peeped out a little at other times. You and your poor aunt are worse off than Carmelite nuns in their cells. Should you mind telling me how you exist without air, without exercise, without any sort of human contact? I don't see how you carry on the common business of life.” 
“你说话的样子，像是在偏远的树林里迷路了似的，”我笑着说，“你怎么能避开这么迷人的地方，你只要走两三步就行。这个我真是弄不明白。只要我在，你就藏得很好，这我知道。但我有的时候，希望你能偷偷往外看一眼。你和你可怜的姑妈比生活在卡梅尔派小隔间里的修女还清苦。你介不介意告诉我，没有新鲜空气，不锻炼，没有其他任何一种人际接触，你们怎么生活？我不知道你们怎么进行日常生活的事务。”
She looked at me as if I were talking some strange tongue, and her answer was so little of an answer that I was considerably irritated. "We go to bed very early—earlier than you would believe.” I was on the point of saying that this only deepened the mystery when she gave me some relief by adding, "Before you came we were not so private. But I never have been out at night." 
她看着我，好像我在讲外语。她答非所问，我对此相当恼怒。“我们睡觉很早——早得你都不相信。”我正想说这只会加深她们的神秘感。这时，她补充了一句，让我有了点儿安慰：“在你到来之前，我们没有这样遮遮掩掩。但我晚上的时候从没出来过。”
"Never in these fragrant alleys, blooming here under your nose?" 
“从来没有来过这芳香的花园小径？花就在你眼前开放着呢。”
"Ah," said Miss Tita, "they were never nice till now!" There was an unmistakable reference in this and a flattering comparison, so that it seemed to me I had gained a small advantage.
“哎！”蒂塔小姐说，“它们从来没有像现在这么好。”这话里有种明白无误的意味，还有一种讨好的比较，所以，看起来，我有了一点点进展。
As it would help me to follow it up to establish a sort of grievance I asked her why, since she thought my garden nice, she had never thanked me in any way for the flowers I had been sending up in such quantities for the previous three weeks.
我问她，既然她认为花园好，为什么在过去的三周里，她从来没有以任何一种方式感谢我为她送来了这么好的鲜花。我这样说，就好像说话带点儿委屈，会有助于我接下她的话头似的。
I had not been discouraged—there had been, as she would have observed, a daily armful; but I had been brought up in the common forms and a word of recognition now and then would have touched me in the right place.
我没有泄气——就像她看到的，每天一大束。但是，我是在普通礼节下长大的，偶尔一句感谢的话会让我的心里感到惬意。
"Why I didn't know they were for me!” 
“啊！我不知道那是送给我的！”
"They were for both of you. Why should I make a difference?" 
“它们是给两位的。我何必要区别对待呢？”
Miss Tita reflected as if she might by thinking of a reason for that, but she failed to produce one. Instead of this she asked abruptly, "Why in the world do you want to know us?" 
蒂塔小姐沉思着，她可能在思考原因，但是她却没有办法想出来一个。相反，她突然问道：“你究竟为什么想要结识我们？”
"I ought after all to make a difference," I replied. "That question is your aunt's; it isn't yours. You wouldn't ask it if you hadn't been put up to it.” 
“我的确该区分开，”我回答道，“这个问题是你姑妈的，不是你的。如果不是她要求，你不会问这个问题的。”
"She didn't tell me to ask you," Miss Tita replied without confusion; she was the oddest mixture of the shrinking and the direct.
“她没有叫我问你。”蒂塔小姐毫无疑问地回答。她既畏缩又直截了当，是个最为奇怪的混合体。
"Well, she has often wondered about it herself and expressed her wonder to you. She has insisted on it, so that she has put the idea into your head that I am insufferably pushing. Upon my word I think I have been very discreet. And how completely your aunt must have lost every tradition of sociability, to see anything out of the way in the idea that respectable intelligent people, living as we do under the same roof, should occasionally exchange a remark! What could be more natural? We are of the same country, and we have at least some of the same tastes, since, like you, I am intensely fond of Venice." 
“嗯，她常常自己思考，然后把她的想法表达给你听。她坚持这样做，这样就把她的想法灌输给你，认为我太自以为是，令人难以忍受。老实说，我觉得自己很谨慎的。你姑妈是如此完全地忘记了社交的传统，不能以一个有知识的人的行事方式那样来看待事物。像我们这样同住一个屋檐下，应该偶尔交谈一两句。还有什么比这更为自然的？我们来自同一个国家。既然，像你一样，我这么喜欢威尼斯，那至少我们的品味也是相同的。”
My interlocutress appeared incapable of grasping more than one clause in any proposition, and she declared quickly, eagerly, as if she were answering my whole speech: "I am not in the least fond of Venice. I should like to go far away!" 
我的聊友看起来没有办法领会这个说法里的半句话的意思。她快速急切地宣布，就像回答我先前所讲的一切：“我可一点儿都不喜欢威尼斯。我倒想走得远远的！”
"Has she always kept you back so?" I went on, to show her that I could be as irrelevant as herself.
“她总是这样阻止你？”我继续说。向她表明，我可以和她一样岔开话题。
"She told me to come out tonight; she has told me very often," said Miss Tita. "It is I who wouldn't come. I don't like to leave her.” 
“她今晚让我出来，她常常这样说，”蒂塔小姐说，“是我不想出来。我不愿意留下她一人。”
"Is she too weak, is she failing?" I demanded, with more emotion, I think, than I intended to show. I judged this by the way her eyes rested upon me in the darkness. It embarrassed me a little, and to turn the matter off I continued genially: "Do let us sit down together comfortably somewhere, and you will tell me all about her." 
“是她太虚弱了吗？她状况变遭了？我询问道。我觉得自己表现出的关切之情比我想要表现的还要多。”我发现这一点，是因为她的眼神在黑暗中停留在我身上。这让我有点儿尴尬，为了转换话题，我柔和地继续说道：“让我们找个地方一起舒服地坐下来，你再告诉我她的事情吧。”
Miss Tita made no resistance to this. We found a bench less secluded, less confidential, as it were, than the one in the arbor; and we were still sitting there when I heard midnight ring out from those clear bells of Venice which vibrate with a solemnity of their own over the lagoon and hold the air so much more than the chimes of other places.
蒂塔小姐没有拒绝。我们找到一个凳子，和凉亭下那个相比，这个地方不那么隐蔽，也不那么隐秘。当我们还坐在那里的时候，我听到威尼斯午夜清脆的钟声。从泻湖顶上传来的钟声自有一种庄重，比其他地方来的和音更为持久地停留在空气中。
We were together more than an hour, and our interview gave, as it struck me, a great lift to my undertaking.
我们在一起呆了一个多小时，我认为，我们的面谈对我的大计起到了极大的推动作用。
Miss Tita accepted the situation without a protest; she had avoided me for three months, yet now she treated me almost as if these three months had made me an old friend. If I had chosen I might have inferred from this that though she had avoided me she had given a good deal of consideration to doing so.
蒂塔小姐毫不反对地接受了这个情形。尽管她避开我三个多月，但她现在对待我的方式，就像是过去的三个月使得我成为了一位老朋友似的。如果我乐意，我可以这样看待这件事：虽然她避开我，但是她也思考了很久，想要和我交朋友。
She paid no attention to the flight of time—never worried at my keeping her so long away from her aunt. She talked freely, answering questions and asking them and not even taking advantage of certain longish pauses with which they inevitably alternated to say she thought she had better go in. It was almost as if she were waiting for something—something I might say to her—and intended to give me my opportunity.
她对于时间的流逝没有知觉——始终没有注意到我让她离开她姑妈已经太久了。她自由畅谈，回答问题，也提出问题。其中有相当长时间的沉默，但她没有利用这些机会说她最好要进去了。她简直就像是在等待——等我说些什么话——打算给我机会似的。
I was the more struck by this as she told me that her aunt had been less well for a good many days and in a way that was rather new.
但我更惊讶的是，她告诉我她姑妈好多天以来身体差了好多，情形和以前非常不同。
She was weaker; at moments it seemed as if she had no strength at all; yet more than ever before she wished to be left alone. That was why she had told her to come out—not even to remain in her own room, which was alongside; she said her niece irritated her, made her nervous.
她更虚弱了。有时候，她看起来几乎半天没有力气，但她比以往更愿意独自一个人。这就是为什么她要她出来的原因——甚至都不要蒂塔小姐呆在她自己的房间，也就是隔壁房间。她说侄女让自己很心烦，很紧张。
She sat still for hours together, as if she were asleep; she had always done that, musing and dozing; but at such times formerly she gave at intervals some small sign of life, of interest, liking her companion to be near her with her work.
她一连坐上几个小时，好像在睡觉似的。她总是那样，沉思，打瞌睡。但以前这样的时候，她时不时表现出些许生命迹象，表现出一些兴致，喜欢同伴呆在她身边做点儿自己的事情。
Miss Tita confided to me that at present her aunt was so motionless that she sometimes feared she was dead; moreover she took hardly any food—one couldn't see what she lived on.
蒂塔小姐向我透露，目前，她姑妈面无表情，她有时候害怕她已经死了。况且，她几乎不吃东西——简直猜不透她靠什么活下来。
The great thing was that she still on most days got up; the serious job was to dress her, to wheel her out of her bedroom. She clung to as many of her old habits as possible and she had always, little company as they had received for years, made a point of sitting in the parlor.
最好的事情就是，她多数的日子还是会起床。给她穿上衣服，推她出来，这是件重要的事。她尽可能保持自己的许多旧习惯，她总是，尽管这么多年来少有客人拜访，还是决意要坐在会客室里。
I scarcely knew what to think of all this—of Miss Tita's sudden conversion to sociability and of the strange circumstance that the more the old lady appeared to decline toward her end the less she should desire to be looked after.
我几乎不知道怎么看待这一切——蒂塔小姐突然变得善于交际；而老太太越是濒临死亡，越是不希望看护。这个情形很奇怪。
The story did not hang together, and I even asked myself whether it were not a trap laid for me, the result of a design to make me show my hand.
事情不相干地悬着，我甚至问自己，这是不是给我设的陷阱，是一个设计来让我自揭老底的圈套。
I could not have told why my companions (as they could only by courtesy be called) should have this purpose—why they should try to trip up so lucrative a lodger. At any rate I kept on my guard, so that Miss Tita should not have occasion again to ask me if I had an arriere-pensee. Poor woman, before we parted for the night my mind was at rest as to HER capacity for entertaining one.
我不知道我的同伴们（就礼节来讲，是该这样称呼她们）有没有这样的目的——为什么她们试图对这样一个有油水可捞的房客使绊子。无论如何我要保持警惕，以免蒂塔小姐再有机会问我有没有什么动机。可怜的女人，在我们临睡分手前，我已经对她本人招呼人的本领放心了。
She told me more about their affairs than I had hoped; there was no need to be prying, for it evidently drew her out simply to feel that I listened, that I cared.
她给我讲的比我希望听的还要多。我没有必要四处打听，因为很明显，只需要让她感觉到我在听，感觉到我在关心，她就会把话讲出来。
She ceased wondering why I cared, and at last, as she spoke of the brilliant life they had led years before, she almost chattered.
她不再考虑为什么我会关心这些。最后，在讲到很多年前她们过的辉煌生活的时候，她几乎喋喋不休。
It was Miss Tita who judged it brilliant; she said that when they first came to live in Venice, years and years before (I saw that her mind was essentially vague about dates and the order in which events had occurred), there was scarcely a week that they had not some visitor or did not make some delightful passeggio in the city.
蒂塔小姐认为那是很辉煌的。她说，当她们很多很多年以前，刚刚到威尼斯的时候（我发现她对于具体日期，事件发生的顺序的记忆基本上是模糊的），基本上没有一周她们是没有访客的，没有一周不去城里逛逛。
They had seen all the curiosities; they had even been to the Lido in a boat (she spoke as if I might think there was a way on foot); they had had a collation there, brought in three baskets and spread out on the grass.
她们看过所有稀奇古怪的事情。她们甚至还乘小船到利多岛去（她说得就像我还以为有一条路可以步行去那里似的）。她带了三篮子东西，在草地上铺开，在那里吃点心。
I asked her what people they had known and she said, Oh! Very nice ones—the Cavaliere Bombicci and the Contessa Altemura, with whom they had had a great friendship.
我问她她们结识了谁，她说，哦！很不错的人——邦比基骑士和阿尔特穆拉伯爵夫人，她们之间的关系很好。
Also English people—the Churtons and the Goldies and Mrs. Stock-Stock, whom they had loved dearly; she was dead and gone, poor dear. That was the case with most of their pleasant circle (this expression was Miss Tita's own), though a few were left, which was a wonder considering how they had neglected them.
还有英国人——丘顿一家、戈尔迪一家、和斯托克－斯托克夫人，她们很喜欢她，她已经去世了，可怜可亲的人。这就是她们最友好的圈子（这是蒂塔小姐本人的说法），尽管有不少已经去世了。这真是奇怪，因为这些人竟然如此地忽略她们。
She mentioned the names of two or three Venetian old women; of a certain doctor, very clever, who was so kind—he came as a friend, he had really given up practice; of the avvocato Pochintesta, who wrote beautiful poems and had addressed one to her aunt.
她提到了两三位威尼斯老妇的名字，提到某一位医生，非常机灵，说他很和气——他作为朋友来的，已经没有行医了。她还提到了波钦泰斯塔律师，诗写得好，还给她姑妈写过诗。
These people came to see them without fail every year, usually at the capo d'anno, and of old her aunt used to make them some little present—her aunt and she together: small things that she, Miss Tita, made herself, like paper lampshades or mats for the decanters of wine at dinner or those woolen things that in cold weather were worn on the wrists.
这些人每年必定来看她们，常常是元旦的时候。以前，老姑妈常常给他们一些小礼物——她姑妈和她一起：一些小物件是她，蒂塔小姐，亲自做的，比如纸做的灯罩，餐桌上玻璃酒瓶的垫子，或是冷天里护腕的毛纺织物。
The last few years there had not been many presents; she could not think what to make, and her aunt had lost her interest and never suggested. But the people came all the same; if the Venetians liked you once they liked you forever.
最近这些年，礼物就不多了。她想不出做什么，而姑妈也没有兴趣，也不要求了。但人们还是照样来。威尼斯人一旦喜欢你，就会一直喜欢你。
There was something affecting in the good faith of this sketch of former social glories; the picnic at the Lido had remained vivid through the ages, and poor Miss Tita evidently was of the impression that she had had a brilliant youth.
她对于以往的社交辉煌的真诚描述中，带有一些动人的东西。过了这么多年，利多岛上的野餐她依然清晰地记得，可怜的蒂塔小姐很显然相信她有个辉煌的青春。
She had in fact had a glimpse of the Venetian world in its gossiping, home-keeping, parsimonious, professional walks; for I observed for the first time that she had acquired by contact something of the trick of the familiar, soft-sounding, almost infantile speech of the place.
她事实上从那些说长道短，喜爱居家，吝啬节俭的专业人士们那里瞥见了威尼斯的世界。因为我一开始就观察到，她通过接触，学会了本地人那种亲密的、柔声而又幼稚的说话技巧。
I judged that she had imbibed this invertebrate dialect from the natural way the names of things and people—mostly purely local—rose to her lips.
我认为她吸收了这种柔若无骨的说话方式，那些人和事物都自然而然地——几乎全是地方腔调地——说了出来。
If she knew little of what they represented she knew still less of anything else.
如果说她对于这些名称所代表的意义知之甚少，那么她对其他的事情懂得就更少了。
Her aunt had drawn in—her failing interest in the table mats and lampshades was a sign of that—and she had not been able to mingle in society or to entertain it alone; so that the matter of her reminiscences struck one as an old world altogether.
她姑妈已经深居不待客——她不再对桌垫和灯罩感兴趣便是一种迹象——她再也不能在社交界来往，也不能独自招待客人。所以她所回忆的，让人觉得完全是个旧时代。
If she had not been so decent her references would have seemed to carry one back to the queer rococo Venice of Casanova.
如果不是因为她端庄正派，她讲的内容会把人引向卡萨诺瓦那个古怪的洛可可式的威尼斯。
I found myself falling into the error of thinking of her too as one of Jeffrey Aspern's contemporaries; this came from her having so little in common with my own.
我发现自己陷入了一种错误，把她也当成是杰弗里·阿斯本同时代的人。这是因为她和我本人没有什么共同点。
It was possible, I said to myself, that she had not even heard of him; it might very well be that Juliana had not cared to lift even for her the veil that covered the temple of her youth.
有可能，我告诉自己，她没有听说过他。也有可能朱莉安娜甚至对她侄女也不愿撩开那罩在她年轻时的神殿上的面纱。
In this case she perhaps would not know of the existence of the papers, and I welcomed that presumption—it made me feel more safe with her—until I remembered that we had believed the letter of disavowal received by Cumnor to be in the handwriting of the niece.
如果是这样，她可能不知道这些文稿的存在。而我欢迎这样的假设——这让我同她在一起时感觉到更安全——直到我想起来卡诺姆收到的谢绝信是由这位侄女手写的。
If it had been dictated to her she had of course to know what it was about; yet after all the effect of it was to repudiate the idea of any connection with the poet.
若是她听写的，她当然知道那是关于什么的。而毕竟，那封信的作用就是否认任何与诗人有关联的想法。
I held it probable at all events that Miss Tita had not read a word of his poetry. Moreover if, with her companion, she had always escaped the interviewer there was little occasion for her having got it into her head that people were "after" the letters. People had not been after them, inasmuch as they had not heard of them; and Cumnor's fruitless feeler would have been a solitary accident.
无论如何，我认为蒂塔小姐很有可能没有读过他的诗歌。再者，如果她，和她的同伴一起，总是避开访问者，那她就几乎没有机会认为人们是为了“追”那些信件而来。人们并没有探求这些信件，因为他们没有听说过这些信。卡诺姆无果的试探，只是偶然事件。
When midnight sounded Miss Tita got up; but she stopped at the door of the house only after she had wandered two or three times with me round the garden. "When shall I see you again?" I asked before she went in; to which she replied with promptness that she should like to come out the next night. She added however that she should not come—she was so far from doing everything she liked.
当午夜的钟声敲响时，蒂塔小姐站起身来。但她和我围着花园逛了两三圈后，才在房门前停住了。“我什么时候能再见你？”她进门前我问她。她马上回答了这个问题，说她明天晚上愿意出来。但她又说她不该出来——她不能想做什么就做什么。
"You might do a few things that I like," I said with a sigh.
“你也许可以做些我愿意做的事。”我叹了口气说。
"Oh, you—I don't believe you!" she murmured at this, looking at me with her simple solemnity.
“啊，你——我不相信你！”她自言自语地说，一本正经地看着我。
"Why don't you believe me?” 
“你为什么不相信我？”
"Because I don't understand you.” 
“因为我不了解你。”
"That is just the sort of occasion to have faith." I could not say more, though I should have liked to, as I saw that I only mystified her; for I had no wish to have it on my conscience that I might pass for having made love to her.
“这正是建立信任的时刻。”我无法多说，虽然我本想那样做。我看出来了，我只会让她更迷惑。因为这可能被看作是向她求爱，而我不希望为此而内疚。
Nothing less should I have seemed to do had I continued to beg a lady to "believe in me" in an Italian garden on a midsummer night.
在这个仲夏夜的意大利花园里，继续祈求一位女士“相信我”，我做所的看起来不过如此。
There was some merit in my scruples, for Miss Tita lingered and lingered: I perceived that she felt that she should not really soon come down again and wished therefore to protract the present.
我迟疑不开口有些好处，因为蒂塔小姐也就迟迟不离开。我认为，她觉得自己不应该再下楼来了，所以希望能拖延目前的时间。
She insisted too on making the talk between us personal to ourselves; and altogether her behavior was such as would have been possible only to a completely innocent woman.
她也坚持要把我俩的私下交谈保密。总的来说，只有极其单纯无知的妇女才会有这样的举动。
"I shall like the flowers better now that I know they are also meant for me." 
“现在知道这些花有我一份，我更喜欢它们了。”
"How could you have doubted it? If you will tell me the kind you like best I will send a double lot of them." 
“你怎么会怀疑这一点呢？要是把你喜欢的花告诉我，我会加倍送来。”
"Oh, I like them all best!" Then she went on, familiarly: "Shall you study—shall you read and write—when you go up to your rooms?” 
“好啊，我全都喜欢！”她接着亲热地说，“你要学习吗——读和写——当你回到你房间的时候？”
"I don't do that at night, at this season. The lamplight brings in the animals.” 
“这个季节的晚上，我不会的。灯光很招虫。”
"You might have known that when you came." 
“你来的时候应该就知道了吧。”
"I did know it!" 
“我的确知道！”
"And in winter do you work at night?" 
“那冬天的晚上，你会工作吗？”
"I read a good deal, but I don't often write.” 
“我会读很多书，但是不常写作。”
She listened as if these details had a rare interest, and suddenly a temptation quite at variance with the prudence I had been teaching myself associated itself with her plain, mild face.
她听着，就像这些细节有着罕见的趣味。突然，我觉得她朴实温和的脸上投射出一种魅力，和我平时对她的谨慎看法大不相同。
Ah yes, she was safe and I could make her safer!
哦，对。她是可靠的，而我可以让她更可靠！
It seemed to me from one moment to another that I could not wait longer—that I really must take a sounding.
我有时候觉得自己不能再等下去了——觉得自己真的有必要试探一下。
So I went on: "In general before I go to sleep—very often in bed (it's a bad habit, but I confess to it), I read some great poet.
于是，我接着说：“通常，我睡觉前——常常是在床上（是个坏习惯，而我承认这一点），会读些伟大诗人的作品。
In nine cases out of ten it's a volume of Jeffrey Aspern.” 
十有八九的时间，我读的是杰弗里·阿斯本的诗卷。”
I watched her well as I pronounced that name but I saw nothing wonderful. Why should I indeed—was not Jeffrey Aspern the property of the human race?
我一边说这个名字，一边观察她，但是没有发现什么让人振奋的神情。我为什么会有这样的期望呢——难道杰弗里·阿斯本不是人类的财富吗？
"Oh, we read him—we HAVE read him," she quietly replied.
“哦，我们也读他——我们读过他的作品。”她轻声回应。
"He is my poet of poets—I know him almost by heart.” 
“对我来说，他是诗人中的诗人——他的诗我熟记于心。”
For an instant Miss Tita hesitated; then her sociability was too much for her.
蒂塔小姐犹豫了一刻，然后她的社交心理让她按捺不住了。
"Oh, by heart—that's nothing!" she murmured, smiling. "My aunt used to know him—to know him”—she paused an instant and I wondered what she was going to say—"to know him as a visitor." 
“啊，熟记于心——那不算什么！”她微笑着，低声说道。“我姑妈曾经认识他——认识他的”——她暂停了一下。我在猜她接下来会说什么——“认识他，因为他曾来这里作客。”
"As a visitor?" I repeated, staring.
“来过这里作客？”我复述着，楞住了。
"He used to call on her and take her out." 
“他曾来见她，带她出去。”
I continued to stare. "My dear lady, he died a hundred years ago!" 
我继续瞪大眼睛看她。“我亲爱的小姐，他去世了有一百年了！”
"Well," she said mirthfully, "my aunt is a hundred and fifty." 
“对。”她开心地说道，“我的姑妈已经一百五十岁了。”
"Mercy on us!" I exclaimed, "why didn't you tell me before? I should like so to ask her about him.” 
“天哪！”我喊道，“你先前为什么没有告诉我？那样的话，我就要向她问问他了。”
"She wouldn't care for that—she wouldn't tell you," Miss Tita replied.
“她才不愿意呢——她不会告诉你的。”蒂塔小姐答道。
"I don't care what she cares for! She MUST tell me—it's not a chance to be lost.” 
“我不管她愿不愿意！她必须告诉我——我不能错过这样的机会。”
"Oh, you should have come twenty years ago: then she still talked about him.” 
“哦，你应该早二十年来：她那个时候还会谈起他。”
"And what did she say?" I asked eagerly.
“那她说什么？”我急切地问道。
"I don't know—that he liked her immensely.” 
“我不知道——比如他很喜欢她。”
"And she—didn't she like him?” 
“那她——她不喜欢他吗？”
"She said he was a god." Miss Tita gave me this information flatly, without expression; her tone might have made it a piece of trivial gossip. But it stirred me deeply as she dropped the words into the summer night; it seemed such a direct testimony.
“她说他是神。”蒂塔小姐平淡地告诉我这样的信息，没有表情。她的语气就像是在讲述一则无关紧要的流言。但我深受影响，因为她在这个夏夜，抛下如此的说法。这简直像是直接的表白。
"Fancy, fancy!" I murmured.
“惊人！太惊人了！”我低声说。
And then, "Tell me this, please—has she got a portrait of him? They are distressingly rare.” 
接着，“请告诉我——她有没有他的画像？那可是极其罕见的。”
"A portrait? I don't know," said Miss Tita; and now there was discomfiture in her face. "Well, good night!" she added; and she turned into the house.
“画像？我不知道。”蒂塔小姐说。这时，她脸上的表情看起来不自在了。“哦，晚安！”她又说，然后转身进房。
I accompanied her into the wide, dusky, stone-paved passage which on the ground floor corresponded with our grand sala.
我陪着她走进底楼这条杂乱的，满是灰尘的，石头铺成的过道。这条过道的上面就连着我们那个宏大的正厅。
It opened at one end into the garden, at the other upon the canal, and was lighted now only by the small lamp that was always left for me to take up as I went to bed. An extinguished candle which Miss Tita apparently had brought down with her stood on the same table with it.
它一面通向花园，另一面在运河之上。如今过道上只有一盏小灯照明，通常是留给我晚上上楼睡觉时带上去的。同一张桌子上还有一只蜡烛，已经灭了，很明显是蒂塔小姐带下来的。
"Good night, good night!" I replied, keeping beside her as she went to get her light. "Surely you would know, shouldn't you, if she had one?” 
“晚安，晚安！”我重复道，陪她取下她的蜡烛，“你一定知道，对不对？要是她有的话！”
"If she had what?" the poor lady asked, looking at me queerly over the flame of her candle.
“她有什么？”这可怜的女士问，越过她手上的烛光，奇怪地看着我。
"A portrait of the god. I don't know what I wouldn't give to see it.” 
“这位神明的画像。为了看到它，我不知道有什么是不可以放弃的。”
"I don't know what she has got. She keeps her things locked up.” And Miss Tita went away, toward the staircase, with the sense evidently that she had said too much.
“我不知道她有什么。她的东西都锁起来了。”蒂塔小姐朝着楼梯走去，她明显意识到，自己说得太多了。
I let her go—I wished not to frighten her—and I contented myself with remarking that Miss Bordereau would not have locked up such a glorious possession as that—a thing a person would be proud of and hang up in a prominent place on the parlor wall.
我让她走了——我希望没有吓到她——而且我自己很满意地认为博尔德罗小姐没有把这么珍贵的物品锁起来——这样的东西，人们会特别引以为傲，必定会挂在会客室的正墙上。
Therefore, of course she had not any portrait. Miss Tita made no direct answer to this and, candle in hand, with her back to me, ascended two or three stairs. Then she stopped short and turned round, looking at me across the dusky space.
因此，她肯定没有什么画像。蒂塔小姐没有正面回答这个问题，她拿着蜡烛，背对着我，上了两三级楼梯。然后她突然停住，转过身，透过灰蒙蒙的空气看着我。
"Do you write—do you write?” There was a shake in her voice—she could scarcely bring out what she wanted to ask.
“你是作家吗——你是作家吗？”她的声音有些颤抖——她简直快要问不出想要问的话了。
"Do I write? Oh, don't speak of my writing on the same day with Aspern's!” 
“我是作家？哦，不要拿我的作品和阿斯本的相提并论！”
"Do you write about HIM—do you pry into his life?” 
“你在写他——在探查他的生活？”
"Ah, that's your aunt's question; it can't be yours!" I said, in a tone of slightly wounded sensibility.
“啊，那是你姑妈的问题。绝不是你想问的！”我说，语气里有点点情感受伤的意味。
"All the more reason then that you should answer it. Do you, please?" 
“那么你更有理由要回答我了。能回答我吗，请问？”
I thought I had allowed for the falsehoods I should have to tell; but I found that in fact when it came to the point I had not. Besides, now that I had an opening there was a kind of relief in being frank. Lastly (it was perhaps fanciful, even fatuous), I guessed that Miss Tita personally would not in the last resort be less my friend. So after a moment's hesitation I answered, "Yes, I have written about him and I am looking for more material. In heaven's name have you got any?” 
我以为，我不得不说谎话的时候到了，但我发现，事实上，当事情发生的时候，我没有说谎。另外，我既然已经打开了局面，坦诚以待让我比较放心。最后（可能是出于幻想，甚至是出于愚昧），我猜测蒂塔小姐最终是最不可能不与我为善的人。于是，我犹豫了一阵之后，回答道：“是的，我写过他，而且我在寻找更多的材料。你到底有没有呢？”
"Santo Dio!" she exclaimed, without heeding my question; and she hurried upstairs and out of sight. I might count upon her in the last resort, but for the present she was visibly alarmed. The proof of it was that she began to hide again, so that for a fortnight I never beheld her. I found my patience ebbing and after four or five days of this I told the gardener to stop the flowers.
“天哪！”她惊呼道，没有回答我的问题，就匆匆上楼，消失了。可能我最终是可以依靠她的，但是当时，她明显受惊了。证据就是，她又开始躲我了。这样，我整整两周没有看到她。我发现自己耐心变差了，四五天后，我告诉园丁让他停止送花。
