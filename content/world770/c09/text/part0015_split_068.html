COUNT CAVOUR.  

卡武尔伯爵  

Wednesday, 29th.

星期三，29日

You are to make a description of the monument to Count Cavour. You can do it. But who was Count Cavour? You cannot understand at present. For the present this is all you know: he was for many years the prime minister of Piemont. It was he who sent the Piemontese army to the Crimea to raise once more, with the victory of the Cernaia, our military glory, which had fallen with the defeat at Novara; it was he who made one hundred and fifty thousand Frenchmen descend from the Alps to chase the Austrians from Lombardy; it was he who governed Italy in the most solemn period of our revolution; who gave, during those years, the most potent impulse to the holy enterprise of the unification of our country,—he with his luminous mind, with his invincible perseverance, with his more than human industry.
你们要写一篇卡武尔伯爵的纪念碑记。你能行的。但卡武尔伯爵是怎样的一个人呢？恐怕你现在并不了解。现在你只知道：他做了很多年的皮埃蒙特总理大臣。是他派遣皮埃蒙特军队到克里米亚，在瑟耐亚大获全胜，使我们的军队在诺瓦拉败北之后重振雄威；是他从阿尔卑斯山撤下十五万法国军队去驱逐伦巴第的奥军；是他统领着意大利走过了革命中最黑暗的时期；是他在那些年里强有力地推进了意大利统一的神圣事业——全靠他睿智的头脑、不可战胜的坚韧和过人的勤勉。
Many generals have passed terrible hours on the field of battle; but he passed more terrible ones in his cabinet, when his enormous work might suffer destruction at any moment, like a fragile edifice at the tremor of an earthquake. Hours, nights of struggle and anguish did he pass, sufficient to make him issue from it with reason distorted and death in his heart. And it was this gigantic and stormy work which shortened his life by twenty years. Nevertheless, devoured by the fever which was to cast him into his grave, he yet contended desperately with the malady in order to accomplish something for his country. "It is strange," he said sadly on his death-bed, "I no longer know how to read; I can no longer read." 
许多将军都在战场上经历过危难的时刻，但他在内阁经历了更糟糕的时刻，他的伟业就像地震中一座脆弱的大厦，随时都有遭到毁灭的可能。他日夜在挣扎和痛苦中度过，这些挣扎和痛苦足以使他思维混乱、心如死灰。就是这宏伟而几经波折的事业使他的寿命缩短了二十年。然而，尽管受到高烧的折磨，即将丧失生命，他还在同疾病拼死抗争，好为他的国家做些事情。“真奇怪，”他临终前悲伤地说，“我再也不能阅读了，我现在连阅读也不会了。”
While they were bleeding him, and the fever was increasing, he was thinking of his country, and he said imperiously: "Cure me; my mind is clouding over; I have need of all my faculties to manage important affairs." When he was already reduced to extremities, and the whole city was in a tumult, and the king stood at his bedside, he said anxiously, "I have many things to say to you, Sire, many things to show you; but I am ill; I cannot, I cannot;" and he was in despair.
当人们给他进行放血治疗时，他的高烧却愈加恶化。但他还是在想着他的国家，他急切地说：“治好我，我的脑袋晕晕乎乎的。我需要找回我的全部精力来处理重要事情。”当他病入膏肓时，整座城市骚动不已。国王站在他的病床边的时候，他忧心忡忡地对国王说道：“我有许多事情要向您禀报，殿下，我有许多事情要呈给您过目。但是我生病了，我做不了，我做不了。”他绝望极了。
And his feverish thoughts hovered ever round the State, round the new Italian provinces which had been united with us, round the many things which still remained to be done. When delirium seized him, "Educate the children!" he exclaimed, between his gasps for breath,—"educate the children and the young people—govern with liberty!" 
他那狂热的思绪一直围绕着国家、新近同我们联合起来的意大利各省，以及诸多还未解决的事情。当他神志不清时，他会喘着粗气叫喊着：“教育儿童——教育儿童和青年——以自由治国！”
His delirium increased; death hovered over him, and with burning words he invoked General Garibaldi, with whom he had had disagreements, and Venice and Rome, which were not yet free: he had vast visions of the future of Italy and of Europe; he dreamed of a foreign invasion; he inquired where the corps of the army were, and the generals; he still trembled for us, for his people. His great sorrow was not, you understand, that he felt that his life was going, but to see himself fleeing his country, which still had need of him, and for which he had, in a few years, worn out the measureless forces of his miraculous organism. He died with the battle-cry in his throat, and his death was as great as his life. Now reflect a little, Enrico, what sort of a thing is our labor, which nevertheless so weighs us down; what are our griefs, our death itself, in the face of the toils, the terrible anxieties, the tremendous agonies of these men upon whose hearts rests a world! Think of this, my son, when you pass before that marble image, and say to it, "Glory!" in your heart.
他神经错乱得更厉害了，死神在他的头顶盘旋。他在胡言乱语中提到了曾经与他有过争执的加里波第将军，提到了尚未获得自由的威尼斯和罗马；他对意大利和欧洲的未来抱有远大的理想；他梦见一场外来侵略，并询问军队和指挥官在哪里。他仍然在为我们——为他的人民担忧。你知道的，最让他痛苦的不是他的生命即将走到尽头，而是眼看着自己逃离那个仍然如此需要他的祖国，那个在几年内耗尽他非凡机体内蕴藏着的无穷精力的祖国。他在呐喊中死去，他的死和他的生一样伟大。现在稍微想想吧，安利柯。我们的工作不算什么，却也使我们如此疲惫不堪。在这些心系世界的人们的艰苦工作、可怕的焦虑和巨大的痛苦面前，我们的痛苦和死亡算得了什么！要想想这些，我的儿子，当你从那大理石像前经过的时候，要在心中对它说：“伟大啊！”
Thy Father.
