CHAPTER XXIII  

第二十三章  

That Ruth had little faith in his power as a writer, did not alter her nor diminish her in Martin's eyes. In the breathing spell of the vacation he had taken, he had spent many hours in self-analysis, and thereby learned much of himself. He had discovered that he loved beauty more than fame, and that what desire he had for fame was largely for Ruth's sake. It was for this reason that his desire for fame was strong. He wanted to be great in the world's eyes; "to make good," as he expressed it, in order that the woman he loved should be proud of him and deem him worthy.
鲁思对他当一名作家的能力没什么信心，这没有改变或降低她在马丁眼中的地位。在他度过的这个短暂假期里，他花了好多时间来分析自己，因此对自己很了解了。他发现自己对美的热爱胜于对名的热爱，而他对名的渴望绝大部分还是为了鲁思。正是出于这个原因他对名的渴望才会那么强烈。为了让自己所爱的女人替他骄傲，将他视为才俊，他想成为世人眼里了不起的人，用他自己的话说就是“成功”。
As for himself, he loved beauty passionately, and the joy of serving her was to him sufficient wage. And more than beauty he loved Ruth. He considered love the finest thing in the world. It was love that had worked the revolution in him, changing him from an uncouth sailor to a student and an artist; therefore, to him, the finest and greatest of the three, greater than learning and artistry, was love. Already he had discovered that his brain went beyond Ruth's, just as it went beyond the brains of her brothers, or the brain of her father. In spite of every advantage of university training, and in the face of her bachelorship of arts, his power of intellect overshadowed hers, and his year or so of self-study and equipment gave him a mastery of the affairs of the world and art and life that she could never hope to possess.
至于他自己，他对美怀着强烈的喜爱，为美服务的喜悦对他而言就是足够的报酬。而他对鲁思的爱又胜过对美的爱。他把爱情当成世界上最美好的事物。是爱情让他发生了彻底的改变，让他从粗野的水手变成了一名学生，一位艺术家。因此，对他来说，三者之中最美好、最伟大的，比学习和艺术还要崇高的，就是爱情。他早就发现自己的思想已经超越了鲁思的，正如超越了她的兄弟和父亲的思想一样。尽管她享有大学教育的种种优势，但面对她的文学学士学位，他的才智之力仍使她黯然失色，他这一年左右的自学和知识装备给了他一种对世界、艺术和生活的深刻理解，这种理解她永远也不可能获得。
All this he realized, but it did not affect his love for her, nor her love for him. Love was too fine and noble, and he was too loyal a lover for him to besmirch love with criticism. What did love have to do with Ruth's divergent views on art, right conduct, the French Revolution, or equal suft sixhis he refrage? They were mental processes, but love was beyond reason; it was superrational. He could not belittle love. He worshipped it. Love lay on the mountain-tops beyond the valley-land of reason. It was a sublimates condition of existence, the topmost peak of living, and it came rarely. Thanks to the school of scientific philosophers he favored, he knew the biological significance of love; but by a refined process of the same scientific reasoning he reached the conclusion that the human organism achieved its highest purpose in love, that love must not be questioned, but must be accepted as the highest guerdon of life. Thus, he considered the lover blessed over all creatures, and it was a delight to him to think of "God's own mad lover," rising above the things of earth, above wealth and judgment, public opinion and applause, rising above life itself and "dying on a kiss."
这一切他都意识到了，但这既并不影响他对鲁思的爱，也不影响鲁思对他的爱。爱情是如此美好而崇高，他又是一位如此忠诚的恋人，以至于他不会让非难指责玷污了爱情。鲁思对艺术、正确的行为、法国大革命或是平等的选举权有不同的见解，但爱情跟这些又有什么关系呢？这些都是理性思考的过程，但爱情不在理智的范围之内，它是超越于理智的。他不能小看爱情。他对爱情顶礼膜拜。爱情处在理性谷地之外的山峰之巅。它是生命存在的升华形式，是生活的顶峰。它罕有出现。多亏他钟爱的那些采用科学研究派哲学家，他懂得了爱情的生物学意义。但是借由同样科学细致的推理过程，他得出了一个结论，就是人类有机体在爱情当中实现了其最高目标，爱情是不可质疑的，而必须被当成生活的最高奖励来接受。因此，他把恋人当成万物之上的赐福，想到“上帝自己的恋人也疯狂”，她高于一切尘世之物，高于财富和理性判断，高于公众舆论和赞美，高于生命本身，会“为了一个吻而甘愿死去”，他就感到快乐。
Much of this Martin had already reasoned out, and some of it he reasoned out later. In the meantime he worked, taking no recreation except when he went to see Ruth, and living like a Spartan. He paid two dollars and a half a month rent for the small room he got from his Portuguese landlady, Maria Silva, a virago and a widow, hard working and harsher tempered, rearing her large brood of children somehow, and drowning her sorrow and fatigue at irregular intervals in a gallon of the thin, sour wine that she bought from the corner grocery and saloon for fifteen cents. From detesting her and her foul tongue at first, Martin grew to admire her as he observed the brave fight she made. There were but four rooms in the little house—three, when Martin's was subtracted. One of these, the parlor, gay with an ingrain carpet and dolorous with a funeral card and a death-picture of one of her numerous departed babes, was kept strictly for company. The blinds were always down, and her barefooted tribe was never permitted to enter the sacred precinct save on state occasions. She cooked, and all ate, in the kitchen, where she likewise washed, starched, and ironed clothes on all days of the week except Sunday; for her income came largely from taking in washing from her more prosperous neighbors. Remained the bedroom, small as the one occupied by Martin, into which she and her seven little ones crowded and slept. It was an everlasting miracle to Martin how it was accomplished, and from her side of the thin partition he heard nightly every detail of the going to bed, the squalls and squabbles, the soft chattering, and the sleepy, twittering noises as of birds. Another source of income to Maria were her cows, two of them, which she milked night and morning and which gained a surreptitious livelihood from vacant lots and the grass that grew on either side the public side walks, attended lksrooms inalways by one or more of her ragged boys, whose watchful guardianship consisted chiefly in keeping their eyes out for the poundmen.
其中大部分马丁早就找到答案了，而另一些则是他后来才明白的。同时他还要工作，除了去看望鲁思，他没有任何消遣活动，活得像斯巴达人一样清苦。他每月交两块半美元的房租，从一位葡萄牙女房东那里租到一间小屋子。女房东名叫玛丽亚·席尔瓦，是个剽悍的寡妇，她工作卖力，脾气比较暴躁，不知怎地养了一大群孩子。为了忘却悲伤和疲劳，她时不时灌上一加仑又稀又酸的葡萄酒，这酒是她用十五美分从街角的杂货店兼酒吧买来的。从一开始厌恶她和她那污秽不堪的舌头，马丁渐渐敬佩起她来，因为他看到了她的勇敢拼搏。那小房子里只有四间屋子，去掉马丁租走的那间还剩三间。其中一间是客厅，一条生染的地毯使其显出几分欢快，而一张白贴和她众多孩子中死去的那个的遗像又让客厅显出几分哀伤。客厅严格规定是用来接待客人的。百叶窗总是拉下的，她那群赤脚的孩子从未被允许进入客厅这个神圣的区域，除非遇上全国性的大事。她做饭，大家在厨房吃饭，除了星期天以外的所有日子，她还在那里洗衣服、浆衣服、熨衣服，因为她的大部分收入都来自帮那些更宽裕的邻居们洗衣服。剩下一间卧室，跟马丁住的那间一样狭小，她和她那七个小家伙就挤在这个小房间里睡觉。他们是如何做到的，对于马丁来说，这永远是个谜。透过一层薄薄的隔板墙他每天夜晚都听到她那边的每一个细节：上床、孩子的哭喊和争吵、轻声细语的唠叨，还有鸟儿们带着睡意吱吱叫的声音。玛丽亚的另一个收入来源是她那几头奶牛，共有两头，她每天早晚都要挤奶。这两头牛是偷偷靠着那几块空地和长在公共人行道两侧的青草活下来的，照看它们的总是她那几个穿得破破烂烂的孩子里头的一两个，他们警觉地看守着，主要就是留意围场的巡逻员。
In his own small room Martin lived, slept, studied, wrote, and kept house. Before the one window, looking out on the tiny front porch, was the kitchen table that served as desk, library, and type-writing stand. The bed, against the rear wall, occupied two-thirds of the total space of the room. The table was flanked on one side by a gaudy bureau, manufactured for profit and not for service, the thin veneer of which was shed day by day. This bureau stood in the corner, and in the opposite corner, on the table's other flank, was the kitchen—the oil-stove on a dry-goods box, inside of which were dishes and cooking utensils, a shelf on the wall for provisions, and a bucket of water on the floor. Martin had to carry his water from the kitchen sink, there being no tap in his room. On days when there was much steam to his cooking, the harvest of veneer from the bureau was unusually generous. Over the bed, hoisted by a tackle to the ceiling, was his bicycle. At first he had tried to keep it in the basement; but the tribe of Silva, loosening the bearings and puncturing the tires, had driven him out. Next he attempted the tiny front porch, until a howling southeaster drenched the wheel a night-long. Then he had retreated with it to his room and slung it aloft.
马丁在他自己的那间小屋子里生活、睡觉、学习、写作、做家务。在仅有的那一扇窗前，对着小小的前门廊，有一张厨房用的桌子，既当书桌，又当图书馆，还用来充当打字机的台架。床靠着后墙，占去了整个房间三分之二的空间。桌子的一侧放着个花哨的抽屉柜，做出来是为了盈利而不是为了实用，上面那层薄薄的镶饰每天都在脱落。这个抽屉柜放在一个角落里，对面的另一个角v heightbed, aga，也就是桌子的另一侧就是厨房——杂物箱上放着煤油炉子，箱内装着盘子和烹调用具，墙上有一个存放食物的架子，地板上放着一桶水。马丁必须得到厨房的水槽里去打水，他的房间里没有水龙头。在没有足够柴火供他做饭的那些日子里，从抽屉柜上剥落下来的镶饰板就是一种特大丰收。床铺上方悬着他的自行车，它被一个滑轮链子吊到了天花板。起初他想把它放在地下室里，但是席尔瓦的那帮小家伙弄松了车上的轴承，扎破了车胎，将他赶了出去。后来他试图把自行车放在那个小门廊里，直到一场东南方向袭来的狂风暴雨将车轮子浸泡了整整一夜。然后他把车搬回自己的房间里，悬挂在顶上。
A small closet contained his clothes and the books he had accumulated and for which there was no room on the table or under the table. Hand in hand with reading, he had developed the habit of making notes, and so copiously did he make them that there would have been no existence for him in the confined quarters had he not rigged several clothes-lines across the room on which the notes were hung. Even so, he was crowded until navigating the room was a difficult task. He could not open the door without first closing the closet door, and vice versa. It was impossible for him anywhere to traverse the room in a straight line. To go from the door to the head of the bed was a zigzag course that he was never quite able to accomplish in the dark without collisions. Having settled the difficulty of the conflicting doors, he had to steer sharply to the right to avoid the kitchen. Next, he sheered to the left, to escape the foot of the bed; but this sheer, if too generous, brought him against the corner of the table. With a sudden twitch and lurch, he terminated the sheer and bore off to the right along a sort of canal, one bank of which was the bed, the other the table. When the one chair in the room was at its usual place before the table, the canal was unnavigable. When the chair was not in use, it reposed on top of the bed, though sometimes he sat on the chair when cooking, reading a book while the water boiled, and even becoming skilful enough to manage a paragraph or two while steak was frying. Also, so small was the little corner that constituted the kitchen, he was able, sitting down, to reach anything he needed. In fact, it was expedient to cook sitting down; standing up, he was too often in his own way.
一个小衣橱里装着他的衣服以及他积存起来的书籍，因为无论桌上还是桌下都腾不出放书的空间来。在阅读的同时，他养成了记笔记的习惯。他做了那么多的笔记，要不是他拉起了几根晾衣绳来挂笔记，这狭小的屋子里就没有他可容身的空间了。即便如此，屋里还是挤得连他穿行走动都十分困难。不先关上衣橱门他就没法打开房门，反过来也是一样。无论如何他要直线穿过房间是完全不可能的。从房门走到床头是一条弯来折去的路线，他从来都做不到在黑暗里走完这条路线而不发生碰撞。解决了门扇间相互碰撞的难题后，他必须急转向右绕开厨房。接着，他又转向左边以避开床脚，但是这一转若转得幅度太大又会撞上桌角。倏地一倾一倒，他终止了拐弯，躺在一个有点像运河结构的右侧。“运河”的一岸是床，另一岸是桌子。当房间里仅有的一张椅子放在桌前通常放的地方时，这“运河”根本不能通航。椅子不用的时候就置在床上，尽管做饭的时候，他偶尔也坐到椅子上——一边看书一边煮开水。他甚至变得有技巧到能够一边煎牛排一边写下一两个段落。再者，构成厨房的那个小角落是那么狭窄，他能够坐着就伸手拿到任何他想要的东西。事实上，坐着做饭反而方便；站起来时，他总是自己碍着自己的；"置偓。
In conjunction with a perfect stomach that could digest anything, he possessed knowledge of the various foods that were at the same time nutritious and cheap. Pea-soup was a common article in his diet, as well as potatoes and beans, the latter large and brown and cooked in Mexican style. Rice, cooked as American housewives never cook it and can never learn to cook it, appeared on Martin's table at least once a day. Dried fruits were less expensive than fresh, and he had usually a pot of them, cooked and ready at hand, for they took the place of butter on his bread. Occasionally he graced his table with a piece of round-steak, or with a soup-bone. Coffee, without cream or milk, he had twice a day, in the evening substituting tea; but both coffee and tea were excellently cooked.
他有一个能消化任何东西的理想肠胃，同时他还知道各式各样既有营养又便宜的食物。豌豆汤是他菜谱上常见的一道菜，土豆和大豆也是一样，后者大大，是黄褐色，可做成墨西哥风味。大米至少每天在马丁的餐桌上出现一次。美国家庭主妇从来不像他那样做米饭，而且永远也学不来那样做米饭。干果比新鲜水果便宜，他通常都有一罐干果，做好了的，在手头备着，因为它们代替着他面包上的黄油。偶尔，他会给他的餐桌添块圆形牛排或者一碗排骨汤。不加奶油和牛奶的咖啡，他每天喝两次，晚上用来代替茶。不过，无论咖啡还是茶他都煮得特别好。
There was need for him to be economical. His vacation had consumed nearly all he had earned in the laundry, and he was so far from his market that weeks must elapse before he could hope for the first returns from his hack-work. Except at such times as he saw Ruth, or dropped in to see his sister Gertude, he lived a recluse, in each day accomplishing at least three days' labor of ordinary men. He slept a scant five hours, and only one with a constitution of iron could have held himself down, as Martin did, day after day, to nineteen consecutive hours of toil. He never lost a moment. On the looking-glass were lists of definitions and pronunciations; when shaving, or dressing, or combing his hair, he conned these lists over. Similar lists were on the wall over the oil-stove, and they were similarly conned while he was engaged in cooking or in washing the dishes. New lists continually displaced the old ones. Every strange or partly familiar word encountered in his reading was immediately jotted down, and later, when a sufficient number had been accumulated, were typed and pinned to the wall or looking-glass. He even carried them in his pockets, and reviewed them at odd moments on the street, or while waiting in butcher shop or grocery to be served.
他必须得节省。他的假期已经差不多花光了他在洗衣店挣到的所有的钱，而他的“市场”还遥不可及，在几个星期过去之前他是没有希望收到他那些卖文作品的报酬的。除了去看望鲁思或者拜访姐姐格特鲁德之外，他都过着隐居生活，每天要完成普通人花至少三天才能完成的工作。他睡觉睡不足五小时，只有体格如钢铁般强健的人才能比得过他，因为马丁每天都是连续苦干十九个小时。他从未浪费过一丁点儿的时间。镜子上贴着一些定义和发音的列单；刮胡子、穿衣服，或是梳头的时候，他就默记这些列单。煤油炉子上方的墙上也贴着这样的列单，他忙着做饭或洗刷碗碟的时候同样也会默记这些列单。新的列单不断替换着旧的列单。在阅读中遇到的每一个陌生或不太熟悉的单词，他都会马上记下来。然后，等积累了足够的数量，他就把它们打印出来，固定到墙上或者镜子上。他甚至把它们放在口袋里，利用上街时的零碎时间或在肉铺、杂货店等睭睳货ber 买东西的时间来复习它们。
He went farther in the matter. Reading the works of men who had arrived, he noted every result achieved by them, and worked out the tricks by which they had been achieved—the tricks of narrative, of exposition, of style, the points of view, the contrasts, the epigrams; and of all these he made lists for study. He did not ape. He sought principles. He drew up lists of effective and fetching mannerisms, till out of many such, culled from many writers, he was able to induce the general principle of mannerism, and, thus equipped, to cast about for new and original ones of his own, and to weigh and measure and appraise them properly. In similar manner he collected lists of strong phrases, the phrases of living language, phrases that bit like acid and scorched like flame, or that glowed and were mellow and luscious in the midst of the arid desert of common speech. He sought always for the principle that lay behind and beneath. He wanted to know how the thing was done; after that he could do it for himself. He was not content with the fair face of beauty. He dissected beauty in his crowded little bedroom laboratory, where cooking smells alternated with the outer bedlam of the Silva tribe; and, having dissected and learned the anatomy of beauty, he was nearer being able to create beauty itself.
他进一步深入到事物的实质。读到那些成功作家的作品时，他记下他们取得的每一样成就，并找出他们是用了怎样的技巧来实现的——叙述的技巧、说明的技巧、风格上的技巧、观点上的技巧、对比手法和妙语警句。他把这些也都做成单子以便学习。他不是光学个样子。他寻找的是那些原理。他从众多作家当中挑出那些有效的、吸引人的独特风格列成单子，直到他能够在诸多这样的单子里归纳出一个普适性的原理。如此准备之后，他要赶紧找出全新的、他自己独有的风格，并对它们进行恰当的权衡、比较和评价。他还用同样的方式收集感染力强的短语的列单，那些短语都是鲜活的语言，它们像酸一样刺痛人又像火一样灼伤人，或者在日常语言的荒漠之中发出融融的光芒，馥郁芬芳。他总是寻觅着那藏在背后的深处的原则。他想要知道好文章是如何写出来的。这样以后他就能自己写出好文章来了。光是美的精致表面并不能让他满足。他把自己那拥挤的小卧室当成实验室来解剖美，屋里弥漫着做饭的油烟味，屋外是席尔瓦家那帮孩子的吵闹声。解剖和研习了美的结构之后，他离获得创造美的能力就更近了。
He was so made that he could work only with understanding. He could not work blindly, in the dark, ignorant of what he was producing and trusting to chance and the star of his genius that the effect produced should be right and fine. He had no patience with chance effects. He wanted to know why and how. His was deliberate creative genius, and, before he began a story or poem, the thing itself was already alive in his brain, with the end in sight and the means of realizing that end in his conscious possession. Otherwise the effort was doomed to failure. On the other hand, he appreciated the chance effects in words and phrases that came lightly and easily into his brain, and that later stood all tests of beauty and power and developed tremendous and incommunicable connotations. Before such he bowed down and marvelled, knowing that they were beyond the deliberate creation of any man. And no matter how much he dissected beauty in search of the principles that underlie beauty and make beauty possible, he was aware, always, of the innermost mystery of beauty to which he did not penetrate and to which no man had ever penetrated. He knew full well, from his Spencer, that man can never attain ultimate knowledge of anything, and that the mystery of beauty was nf band to whio less than that of life—nay, more that the fibres of beauty and life were intertwisted, and that he himself was but a bit of the same nonunderstandable fabric, twisted of sunshine and star-dust and wonder.
他有这样的天性，以至于他只能在理解的前提下才能动手去做。他不能在黑暗中盲目工作，不知道自己在创造什么，把创作结果的好坏对错交托给运气和自己的天才福星。他对投机得到的结果没有耐心。他想要知道为什么和怎么做。他靠的是谨慎而富有创造力的天才，在他开始写故事或诗歌之前，故事或诗歌本身就已经存在于他的脑海之中了。他看得到结尾，而且实现那个结尾的方法也已经在他意识掌控之中。若非如此，那些努力是注定要失败的。另一方面，他欣赏单词之间、短语之间偶然出现的效果，它们轻盈自在地进入他脑子里，后来经受住了所有美和力的考验，发展出极多难以言传的隐含意义。面对这些，他俯首弯腰，惊讶万分。他知道任何人的精心创作都创造不出这些。他寻找那些藏在美的深处，使美成为可能的原理。然而，无论在此过程中他对美有怎样深入的剖析，他始终意识到美最深处的奥秘是他所无法参透的，也没有哪一个前人参透过它。他从斯宾塞的作品里充分地了解到，人永远都不能获得任何一件事物的终极知识，美的奥秘并不亚于生命的奥秘——不，美和生命两者的纤维往往是互相交织在一起的，而他自己不过是这一团无法领悟的纤维中的一小撮而已。
In fact, it was when filled with these thoughts that he wrote his essay entitled "Star-dust," in which he had his fling, not at the principles of criticism, but at the principal critics. It was brilliant, deep, philosophical, and deliciously touched with laughter. Also it was promptly rejected by the magazines as often as it was submitted. But having cleared his mind of it, he went serenely on his way. It was a habit he developed, of incubating and maturing his thought upon a subject, and of then rushing into the type-writer with it. That it did not see print was a matter a small moment with him. The writing of it was the culminating act of a long mental process, the drawing together of scattered threads of thought and the final generalizing upon all the data with which his mind was burdened. To write such an article was the conscious effort by which he freed his mind and made it ready for fresh material and problems. It was in a way akin to that common habit of men and women troubled by real or fancied grievances, who periodically and volubly break their long-suffering silence and "have their say" till the last word is said.
其实，就是带着这样的思索他写了一篇题为《星尘》的文章，里头他激情澎湃地表达了对那些重要批评家的指摘，而不是对批评原理的指摘。这篇文章写得才华横溢、深刻透彻、富有哲理，而且巧妙地融入了幽默诙谐的元素。跟往常一样，文章一寄出去就立刻被各个杂志社退回来了。但他已经想清楚了这一切，他要从容地走自己的路。他养成了一个习惯，就一个主题先酝酿思考，等自己想法成熟之后再冲到打字机前把它一口气写出来。这一作品没能见报的事实他并没有太在意。这篇文章是长期思考过程的最终成果，是散乱思想线索的整合，是深埋在他脑海里的所有资料的最后归纳。写下这样一篇文章是他有意识的努力。通过这样的努力，他解放了自己的思想，使它准备好去接受新鲜的材料和问题。这跟那些男人女人一种普遍的习惯很相似。他们被真实或假想的悲伤所困扰，定期打破他们沉默，滔滔不绝地倾吐长期以来所遭受的痛苦，“发表他们的言论”，直到说完最后一个字。
