CHAPTER XXXV  

第三十五章  

Brissenden gave no explanation of his long absence, nor did Martin pry into it. He was content to see his friend's cadaverous face opposite him through the steam rising from a tumbler of toddy.
布里森登对自己长时间的销声匿迹并没有什么解释，马丁也没有向他打听。透过那杯甜酒所升腾起的蒸汽，看见对面朋友那憔悴的脸，他感到满足。
"I, too, have not been idle," Brissenden proclaimed, after hearing Martin's account of the work he had accomplished.
“我也没偷懒。”听见马丁说到他已经完成的作品之后布里森登宣布道。
He pulled a manuscript from his inside coat pocket and passed it to Martin, who looked at the title and glanced up curiously.
他从里面一件衣服的口袋里掏出一篇稿子递给马丁，马丁看着那个题目便好奇地浏览起来。
"Yes, that's it," Brissenden laughed. "Pretty good title, eh? 'Ephemera'—it is the one word. And you're responsible for it, what of your man, who is always the erected, the vitalized inorganic, the latest of the ephemera, the creature of temperature strutting his little space on the thermometer. It got into my head and I had to write it to get rid of it. Tell me what you think of it."
“是的，就是它，”布里森登大笑，“相当漂亮的题目吧，啊？‘蜉蝣’——就是这么一个词。就是从你那里来的，跟你同类的人，永远直立，有活力的无机物，最新形式的蜉蝣，在温度表的微小空间里趾高气扬地走着的那有体温的生物。它走进了我的头脑中，我只得把它写出来才能摆脱它。你觉得怎么样，告诉我吧。”
Martin's face, flushed at first, paled as he read on. It was perfect art. Form triumphed over substance, if triumph it could be called where the last conceivable atom of substance had found expression in so perfect construction as to make Martin's head swim with delight, to put passionate tears into his eyes, and to send chills creeping up and down his back. It was a long poem of six or seven hundred lines, and it was a fantastic, amazing, unearthly thing. It was terrific, impossible; and yet there it was, scrawled in black ink across the sheets of paper. It dealt with man and his sopul-gropings in their ultimate terms, plumbing the abysses of space for the testimony of remotest suns and rainbow spectrums. It was a mad orgy of imagination, wassailing in the skull of a dying man who half sobbed under his breath and was quick with the wild flutter of fading heart-beats. The poem swung in majestic rhythm to the cool tumult of interstellar conflict, to the onset of starry hosts, to the impact of cold suns and the flaming up of nebular in the darkened void; and through it all, unceasing and faint, like a silver shuttle, ran the frail, piping voice of man, a querulous chirp amid the screaming of planets and the crash of systems.
马丁的脸先是发红，一读下去就变得苍白了。这是完美的艺术。形式击败了内容，如果可以叫做胜利的话，在那里可以想象得到的内容的每一个细节都能够用最完美的结构表达出来，以至于马丁的头脑中充满了喜悦，眼中溢满了激情的泪水，又有一阵阵寒气在他的脊背上起伏。这是一首长诗，有六七百行，是一部奇妙的、惊人的、不染尘俗的作品。它精彩至极，几乎不可能存在；然而它就在那里，用黑色的墨水写在一张张白纸上。它抒写的是人及人的灵魂在其终极意义上的探索，测探着空间的深渊，以证实最遥远的恒星和彩虹光谱。它是想象力的狂欢宴会，在一个垂死者的头颅里痛饮，垂死者无声地啜泣着，衰弱的心脏却在狂跳。那首诗以壮丽的旋律徘徊于星际冲突阴冷的骚乱中，徘徊于星群的攻击中，徘徊于黑暗的太空中冰冷的恒星的冲击和星云的燃烧中；而透过这一切，人类微弱细小的声音，像一只银梭无力地不停穿行着，在行星的呼啸声和天体的撞击声中不过是个哀怨的啁啾声。
"There is nothing like it in literature," Martin said, when at last he was able to speak. "It's wonderful!—wonderful! It has gone to my head. I am drunken with it. That great, infinitesimal question—I can't shake it out of my thoughts. That questing, eternal, ever recurring, thin little wailing voice of man is still ringing in my ears. It is like the dead-march of a gnat amid the trumpeting of elephants and the roaring of lions. It is insatiable with microscopic desire. I now I'm making a fool of myself, but the thing has obsessed me. You are—I don't know what you are—you are wonderful, that's all. But how do you do it? How do you do it?"
“在文学中，像这样的东西是没有的，”当马丁终于能说话的时候，他说道，“太了不起了！——了不起！它印入了我的头脑里。我陶醉其中了。那个伟大的而又极小的问题——我无法让它摆脱我的思想。人类那探求的、永恒的、反复的、微弱细小的哭号声依然在我耳边响着。这好像是在大象的吼声和狮子的咆哮当中，一只蚊虫的死亡进行曲。微小的欲望无法满足它。此刻，我让自己变成一个傻瓜，可是那东西已经迷惑了我。你是——我不知道你是什么——你是了不起的，就是这样。可是你是怎么写成它的呢？你是怎么办到的呢？”
Martin paused from his rhapsody, only to break out afresh.
马丁暂停了他狂热的言辞，但又重新开始说。
"I shall never write again. I am a dauber in clay. You have shown me the work of the real artificer-artisan. Genius! This is something more than genius. It transcends genius. It is truth gone mad. It is true, man, every line of it. I wonder if you realize that, you dogmatist. Science cannot give you the lie. It is the truth of the sneer, stamped out from the black iron of the Cosmos and interwoven with mighty rhythms of sound into a fabric of splendor and beauty. And now I won't say another word. I am overrd. I amwhelmed, crushed. Yes, I will, too. Let me market it for you."
“我永远不再写作了。我只会在泥土里乱画。你已经给我看了真正的巧匠的作品了。天才！这是比天才还高明的呀。它胜过天才。它是疯狂了的真理。它是真实的，老兄，每一行都是。我真想知道你是否晓得这个呢，你这个独断论者！科学骗不了你。这是冷笑的真理，是从宇宙的黑铁模印出来的，用有力的声音韵律织入一幅光彩美丽的织物。现在，我说不出别的话了。我被征服了，粉碎了。是的，我也一定会的。我替你把它卖出去吧。”
Brissenden grinned. "There's not a magazine in Christendom that would dare to publish it—you know that.”
布里森登咧嘴笑了。“在基督教的天下中，没有一家杂志敢刊登它——你知道的。”
"I know nothing of the sort. I know there's not a magazine in Christendom that wouldn't jump at it. They don't get things like that every day. That's no mere poem of the year. It's the poem of the century."
“我不懂那类事情。我只知道，在基督教的天下中，没有一家杂志不抢它。它们不是每天都能获得你这样的作品的。这不仅仅是年度之作。它是世纪之作。”
"I'd like to take you up on the proposition."
“就这一说法我愿意和你打赌。”
"Now don't get cynical," Martin exhorted. "The magazine editors are not wholly fatuous. I know that. And I'll close with you on the bet. I'll wager anything you want that 'Ephemera' is accepted either on the first or second offering."
“好了，别又愤世嫉俗了，”马丁劝他，“杂志编辑不是完全蠢笨的。我知道的。我愿意跟你打赌。你要什么我就赌什么，我赌《蜉蝣》第一次或者第二次寄出去就会被接受的。”
"There's just one thing that prevents me from taking you." Brissenden waited a moment. "The thing is big—the biggest I've ever done. I know that. It's my swan song. I am almighty proud of it. I worship it. It's better than whiskey. It is what I dreamed of—the great and perfect thing—when I was a simple young man, with sweet illusions and clean ideals. And I've got it, now, in my last grasp, and I'll not have it pawed over and soiled by a lot of swine. No, I won't take the bet. It's mine. I made it, and I've shared it with you."
“只是有一件事情我不能接受你的意见。”布里森登停了一会儿。“这篇作品是很重要的——我写过的最重要的作品。我知道的。它是我的天鹅之歌。我为它极其骄傲。我爱慕它。它比威士忌还要好。它是我梦想着的——伟大而完美的作品——当我还是一个单纯的青年，充满着甜蜜的幻想和纯洁的理想的时候。现在，我用最后一点力气得到了它，我绝不能让它被那一群猪猡蹂躏和玷污。不，我能打赌。它是我的。我创造出它来，我已经跟你分享它啦。”
"But think of the rest of the world," Martin protested. "The function of beauty is joy-making.”
“可是，为世界上其余的人想想吧，”马丁抗议道，“美的功能在于创造快乐。”
"It's my beauty."
“它是我的美。”
"Don't be selfish."
“别自私啊。”
"I'm no"2em">"It selfish." Brissenden grinned soberly in the way he had when pleased by the thing his thin lips were about to shape. "I'm as unselfish as a famished hog."
“我不自私。”布里森登冷静地笑了，他一高兴，他那薄嘴唇就会是那个样子。“我像一头饿极的公猪一样不自私呢。”
In vain Martin strove to shake him from his decision. Martin told him that his hatred of the magazines was rabid, fanatical, and that his conduct was a thousand times more despicable than that of the youth who burned the temple of Diana at Ephesus. Under the storm of denunciation Brissenden complacently sipped his toddy and affirmed that everything the other said was quite true, with the exception of the magazine editors. His hatred of them knew no bounds, and he excelled Martin in denunciation when he turned upon them.
马丁竭力想要他放弃那个决定，却没有结果。马丁告诉他他对那些杂志的憎恨是偏激狂热的，他的行为比放火烧掉以弗所的戴安娜神庙的青年还要卑劣一千倍。处在谴责的暴风雨之下，布里森登满意地啜着他的甜酒，认定对方所说的一切是十分正确的，除了关于杂志编辑的是一个例外。他对他们的憎恨是无止境的，一说到他们，他斥责的声音便超过了马丁。
"I wish you'd type it for me," he said. "You know how a thousand times better than any stenographer. And now I want to give you some advice." He drew a bulky manuscript from his outside coat pocket. "Here's your Shame of the Sun.' I've read it not once, but twice and three times—the highest compliment I can pay you. After what you've said about 'Ephemera' I must be silent. But this I will say: when The Shame of the Sun is published, it will make a hit. It will start a controversy that will be worth thousands to you just in advertising.”
“我希望你用打字机帮我打出来，”他说，“你懂得的比任何速记员都要好过一千倍。现在我要给你一点儿忠告了。”他从外衣口袋里掏出一叠厚厚的稿子。“这是你的《太阳的耻辱》。我不止读了一遍，而是两遍，三遍——我可以给你最高的恭维。而你对《蜉蝣》说了那一番话之后，我就得保持沉默了。但是我还要说一点：《太阳的耻辱》刊登出来的时候一定会获得成功。它将惹起一场争论，而这在你恰恰等于大登广告。”
Martin laughed. "I suppose your next advice will be to submit it to the magazines."
马丁大笑。“我看你的第二个忠告就是要我把它寄给杂志了。”
"By all means no—that is, if you want to see it in print. Offer it to the first-class houses. Some publisher's reader may be mad enough or drunk enough to report favorably on it. You've read the books. The meat of them has been transmuted in the alembic of Martin Eden's mind and poured into The Shame of the Sun, and one day Martin Eden will be famous, and not the least of his fame will rest upon that work. So you must get a publisher for it—the sooner the better.”
“无论如何也不要——就是说，如果你想看到它被印出来的话。把它寄到一流的出版公司吧。某个出版公司的审稿人可能会为它沉迷为它陶醉的，会做出有利的审稿意见。你是读过书的。它们的精华已经在马丁·伊登的蒸馏器里得到提炼吸收，并被注入到《太阳的耻辱》里，有一天马丁·伊登会成名的，而他的成名就是靠着这一部作品。所以你一定要找一家出版公司——越快越好。”

那晚布里森登很晚才回家去；当他刚刚迈步上车的时候，突然朝马丁转过来，把一个皱皱巴巴的小纸团塞到马丁手里。
"Here, take this," he said. "I was out to the races to-day, and I had the right dope.”
“来，拿着，”他说，“我今天去赌赛马了，得到可靠情报啦。”
The bell clanged and the car pulled out, leaving Martin wondering as to the nature of the crinkly, greasy wad he clutched in his hand. Back in his room he unrolled it and found a hundred-dollar bill.
铃响了，车开动了，剩下马丁在那里猜想着他拿在手里的那个折皱的纸团是什么东西。回到房间里，他摊开了它，发现是一张一百元的支票。
He did not scruple to use it. He knew his friend had always plenty of money, and he knew also, with profound certitude, that his success would enable him to repay it. In the morning he paid every bill, gave Maria three months' advance on the room, and redeemed every pledge at the pawnshop. Next he bought Marian's wedding present, and simpler presents, suitable to Christmas, for Ruth and Gertrude. And finally, on the balance remaining to him, he herded the whole Silva tribe down into Oakland. He was a winter late in redeeming his promise, but redeemed it was, for the last, least Silva got a pair of shoes, as well as Maria herself. Also, there were horns, and dolls, and toys of various sorts, and parcels and bundles of candies and nuts that filled the arms of all the Silvas to overflowing.
他毫不犹豫地拿去用了。他知道他的朋友总是有很多钱，也深信他的成功会帮他还清这笔钱。早上他付清了所有的帐，预付了玛丽亚三个月的房租，赎回了当铺里所有的东西。接着他给玛丽安买了结婚礼物，给鲁思和格特鲁德买了简单而得体的圣诞礼物。最后他带着剩下的钱率领着席尔瓦一家来到了奥克兰。说到履行诺言，他已经迟了一个冬天，但是最终还是履行了，至少每一个席尔瓦都得到了一双鞋子，玛丽亚也有一双。另外还有号角，娃娃和形形色色的玩具，一包包一束束的糖果和坚果塞满了每一个席尔瓦的怀抱。
It was with this extraordinary procession trooping at his and Maria's heels into a confectioner's in quest if the biggest candy-cane ever made, that he encountered Ruth and her mother. Mrs. Morse was shocked. Even Ruth was hurt, for she had some regard for appearances, and her lover, cheek by jowl with Maria, at the head of that army of Portuguese ragamuffins, was not a pretty sight. But it was not that which hurt so much as what she took to be his lack of pride and self-respect. Further, and keenest of all, she read into the incident the impossibility of his living down his working-class origin. There was stigma enough in the fact of it, but shamelessly to flaunt it in the face of the world—her world—was going too far. Though her engagement to Martin had been kept secret, their long intimacy had not been unproductive of gossip; and in the shop, glancing covertly at her lover and his following, had been several of her acquaintances. She lacked the easy largeness of Martin and could not rise superior to her environment. She had been hurt to the quick, and her sensitive nature was quivering with the shame of it. So it was, when Martin arrived later in the day, that he kept her present in his breast-pocket, deferring the giving of it to a more propitious occasion. Ruth in tears—passionate, angry tears—was a revelation to him. The spectacle of her suffering er suffeconvinced him that he had been a brute, yet in the soul of him he could not see how nor why. It never entered his head to be ashamed of those he knew, and to take the Silvas out to a Christmas treat could in no way, so it seemed to him, show lack of consideration for Ruth. On the other hand, he did see Ruth's point of view, after she had explained it; and he looked upon it as a feminine weakness, such as afflicted all women and the best of women.
正当这一支特殊的队伍跟在他和玛丽亚的身后大踏步地走进一家糖果店寻找最大的手杖糖时，他遇见了鲁思和她的母亲。莫尔斯太太大吃一惊。甚至鲁思也感到不快，因为她是有点儿爱面子的，而她的爱人跟玛丽亚亲密地在一起，带领着一支葡萄牙小叫花的队伍，真是不体面。但是更叫她伤心的是他的不自尊和不自爱。而且，最令人难受的是，从这次邂逅当中，她看出了他要摆脱他工人阶级的出身是不可能的。这事情本身就够丢脸的了，他还恬不知耻地炫耀于全世界——她的世界——未免太过分。尽管她和马丁的婚约一直在保密，他们长期的亲密来往不会不引起闲话的；在店里就有几个她的熟人偷偷瞥着她的爱人和跟着他的那帮人。她缺乏马丁那种广阔的胸襟，不能超越她的环境。她被伤及要害，她敏感的天性正因这耻辱而颤抖。马丁当天晚些时候来到的时候，就是这样的情形，他只好把要送她的礼物留在胸前的口袋里，送礼物这事就推迟到一个合适的场合再说。鲁思淌着眼泪——激动而生气的眼泪——这是对他的启示。看到她痛苦，他更确信自己是个野兽，然而他的灵魂却不明白是怎么回事，为的是什么。他永远不会想到会以他认识的人为耻，而带着席尔瓦们出去买些圣诞礼物在他看来也并不是对鲁思的不体贴。另一方面，在鲁思说明了自己的观点之后，他是明白的；他把这个看成是女性的弱点，它使所有女人苦恼，包括最优秀的女人。
