CHAPTER XX  

第二十章  

A competency was what I wanted; a competency it was now my aim and resolve to secure, but never had I been farther from the mark. With August the school year (l'année scolaire) closed, the examinations concluded, the prizes were adjudged, the schools dispersed, the gates of all colleges, the doors of all pensionnats shut, not to be reopened till the beginning or middle of October. The last day of August was at hand, and what was my position? Had I advanced a step since the commencement of the past quarter? On the contrary, I had receded one. By renouncing my engagement as English master in Mdlle. Reuter's establishment I had voluntarily cut off £20 from my yearly income; I had diminished my £60 per annum to £40, and even that sum I now held by a very precarious tenure.
我所需要的是丰厚的待遇；丰厚的待遇就是我现在的目标，我下定决心要得到的东西，但是它从来没像现在这样离我如此遥远。随着八月的来临，学年结束了，已经考完试，派发完奖金了，学校的人都已离开，所有学院和寄宿学校的大门都关闭了，一直到十月初或者中旬才会再次打开。八月的最后一天即将来临，我的地位怎么样了呢？从上个季度初到现在我向前迈进一步了吗？刚好相反，我向后退了一步。因为放弃了在勒泰小姐学校的英语老师工作，我就自动减去了自己每年二十英镑的收入，这样年收入就从每年六十英镑降到四十英镑，即使这个数目现在也很不稳定。
It is some time since I made any reference to M. Pelet. The moonlight walk is, I think, the last incident recorded in this narrative where that gentleman cuts any conspicuous figure. The fact is, since that event a change had come over the spirit of our intercourse. He, indeed, ignorant that the still hour, a cloudless moon, and an open lattice had revealed to me the secret of his selfish love and false friendship, would have continued smooth and complaisant as ever; but I grew spiny as a porcupine and inflexible as a black- thorn cudgel. I never had a smile for his raillery, never a moment for his society. His invitations to take coffee with him in his parlour were invariably rejected, and very stiffly and sternly rejected too. His jesting allusions to the directress (which he still continued) were heard with a grim calm very different from the petulant pleasure they were formerly wont to excite. For a long time Pelet bore with my frigid demeanour very patiently. He even increased his attentions; but finding that even a cringing politeness failed to thaw or move me, he at last altered too. In his turn he cooled; his invitations ceased; his countenance became suspicious and overcast, and I read in the perplexed yet brooding aspect of his brow a constant examination and comparison of premises, and an anxious endeavour to draw thence some explanatory inference. Ere long, I fancy, he succeeded, for he was not without penetration; perhaps, too, Mdlle. Zoraéde might have aided him in the solution of the enigma.
我已经有段时间没提过珀莱先生了。我想，那次月下散步是本故事中最后一次对这位绅士形象的显著描写了。事实上，自从那件事情之后我们之间交往的性质就有了变化。他还真不知道那个寂静的时刻，一轮毫无遮掩的皓月和一扇打开的格子窗已经向我暴露了他那自私的爱情和虚伪的友谊，所以他还像以前那么圆滑殷勤，但我已变得像一头豪猪一样浑身是刺，像一根带黑刺的短棍一样不屈不挠。对于他的逗趣我从来不笑，也一刻都不愿跟他多呆。他请我去他家客厅和他喝咖啡的邀请，无一不被我拒绝，而且拒绝得生硬坚决。过去他拿女校长打趣（现在也还是那么做），总会逗得我大乐，如今大不相同，我只会冷冷地静静听着。对我的冷漠行为，珀莱非常有耐心地忍受了很长一段时间。他甚至更加殷勤，但是当他发现对我礼貌到阿谀奉承的地步还是不能打动我、使我态度缓和时，他终于也改变了态度。轮到他对我冷淡了。他也不再邀请我，脸色变得多疑而阴沉。我从他眉宇间沉思困惑的样子看出，在不断的自我检查与比较之下，他还是急切地想尽力对所有事情得出一个解释性的结论。我想不久他就成功了，因为他并不缺乏洞察力，也许佐蕾德小姐也在帮助他解开这个难题。
At any rate, I soon found that the uncertainty of doubt had vanished from his manner; renouncing all pretence of friendship and cordiality, he adopted a reserved, formal, but still scrupulously polite deportment. This was the point to which I had wished to bring him, and I was now again comparatively at my ease. I did not, it is true, like my position in his house. But being freed from the annoyance of false professions and double-dealing I could endure it, especially as no heroic sentiment of hatred or jealousy of the director distracted my philosophical soul. He had not, I found, wounded me in a very tender point, the wound was so soon and so radically healed, leaving only a sense of contempt for the treacherous fashion in which it had been inflicted, and a lasting mistrust of the hand which I had detected attempting to stab in the dark.
不论怎样，我不久就发现他那疑心重重的举动已经消失了；他干脆放弃了对我友好热情的伪装，换上一副沉默不语的正经样子，但仍小心翼翼，彬彬有礼。这就是我想要在他身上达到的目的，如此一来，我又重新感到相对自在了。我实在不喜欢我在他家的位置。但是既然摆脱了假情假意和口是心非给我带来的烦恼，我还是能够忍受的，尤其是校长对我的过分仇视和嫉妒并没有扰乱我达观的心绪。我发现他并没有刺伤我的敏感处，伤口很快便彻底愈合了，只剩下对他一贯奸诈嘴脸的蔑视，也永远不再信任那只我查明了企图暗中刺伤我的手。
This state of things continued till about the middle of July, and then there was a little change. Pelet came home one night, an hour after his usual time, in a state of unequivocal intoxication; a thing anomalous with him, for if he had some of the worst faults of his countrymen, he had also one at least of their virtues—that is, sobriety. So drunk, however, was he upon this occasion, that after having roused the whole establishment (except the pupils, whose dormitory, being over the classes in a building apart from the dwelling-house, was consequently out of the reach of disturbance) by violently ringing the hall bell and ordering lunch to be brought in immediately—for he imagined it was noon, whereas the city bells had just tolled midnight—after having furiously rated the servants for their want of punctuality, and gone near to chastise his poor old mother, who advised him to go to bed, he began raving dreadfully about 'le maudit Anglais, Creemsvort.' I had not yet retired; some German books I had got hold of had kept me up late. I heard the uproar below, and could distinguish the director's voice exalted in a manner as appalling as it was unusual. Opening my door a little, I became aware of a demand on his part for 'Creemsvort' to be brought down to him, that he might cut his throat on the hall table and wash his honour, which he affirmed to be in a dirty condition, in infernal British blood. 'He is either mad or drunk,' thought I, 'and in either case the old woman and the servants will be the better of a man's assistance;' so I descended straight to the hall. I found him staggering about, his eyes in a fine frenzy rolling. A pretty sight he was—a just medium between the fool and the lunatic.
这种状态一直持续到七月中旬，然后发生了一点小变化。一天晚上珀莱比他以往晚回来一个小时，明显显得喝多了，这对他来说有点反常，因为如果他有着几样他的同胞们最坏的缺点，那至少他还保留着他们的一样美德——那就是，饮酒节制。然而这一次他却喝得烂醉如泥，他在大厅里疯狂地摇铃，惊动了整个学校（除了学生们，因为他们宿舍不在住宅楼，而在另一栋楼的教室上面，所以免受干扰），又叫仆人马上把午饭送过来——因为他以为当时是中午，实际上城市的午夜钟声刚刚敲过——他狂怒地斥责过仆人开饭不准时后，又跑去责骂他那可怜的老母亲；老太太劝他去睡觉，他却疯疯癫癫地大骂起“那个该死的英国人克里姆斯沃斯”来。当时我还没有睡觉，因为我手头有几本德语书，最近总是读到很晚。我听见楼下的骚动声，并辨出是校长在大喊大叫，声音异样而又十分骇人。我打开了一点门缝，听见他在叫人把“克里姆斯沃斯”给他带来，他要在大厅的桌子上割断他的喉咙，洗净他的人格。他断定克里姆斯沃斯的人格肮脏，带有可憎的英国血统。“他要不就是疯了，要不就是醉了，”我暗想，“无论哪种情况，那个老太太和仆人们都很需要一个男人的帮助。”于是我径直下楼来到大厅。我看见他步履蹒跚，眼珠快速地狂转着。他那半傻半疯的模样倒还挺有意思的。
'Come, M. Pelet,' said I, 'you had better go to bed,' and I took hold of his arm. His excitement, of course, increased greatly at sight and touch of the individual for whose blood he had been making application. He struggled and struck with fury. But a drunken man is no match for a sober one; and, even in his normal state, Pelet's worn-out frame could not have stood against my sound one. I got him upstairs, and, in process of time, to bed. During the operation he did not fail to utter comminations which, though broken, had a sense in them; while stigmatizing me as the treacherous spawn of a perfidious country, he in the same breath anathematized Zoraéde Reuter. He termed her 'femme sotte et vicieuse,' who, in a fit of lewd caprice, had thrown herself away on an unprincipled adventurer, directing the point of the last appellation by a furious blow, obliquely aimed at me. I left him in the act of bounding elastically out of the bed into which I had tucked him; but as I took the precaution of turning the key in the door behind me, I retired to my own room, assured of his safe custody till the morning, and free to draw undisturbed conclusions from the scene I had just witnessed.
“好了，珀莱先生，”我说道，“你还是去睡觉吧。”我抓住他的胳膊。他刚刚还在嚷嚷着要给我洗血，现在看见了我本人，又被我抓着，当然就更激动了。他挣扎着，对我愤怒地拳打脚踢。但是一个醉汉哪里打得过一个清醒的人；就算在正常状态下，珀莱那单薄的身躯也不可能与我健壮的体魄对抗。我将他扶上楼，又花了好长时间才把他弄上床。在这期间，他一直在嘟囔着一些话，尽管断断续续，但是有一定的含义；他骂我是一个背信弃义国家里的奸诈小人，同时又诅咒着佐蕾德·勒泰。他称她为“愚蠢的坏女人”，骂她是个水性杨花的烂货，堕落得一头钻进了无耻投机者的怀抱，最后那句“投机者”显然愤怒地将矛头间接转向了我。我把他放在床上安顿好，在这期间他不断地从床上弹起来；但直到我小心翼翼地用钥匙锁上他房间的门回到自己的房间，才确定他会在里面安安分分地睡到天亮，并能够不受影响地从刚刚目睹的情景中得出结论。
Now, it was precisely about this time that the directress, stung by my coldness, bewitched by my scorn, and excited by the preference she suspected me of cherishing for another, had fallen into a snare of her own laying—was herself caught in the meshes of the very passion with which she wished to entangle me. Conscious of the state of things in that quarter, I gathered, from the condition in which I saw my employer, that his ladylove had betrayed the alienation of her affections—inclinations, rather, I would say; affection is a word at once too warm and too pure for the subject—had let him see that the cavity of her hollow heart, emptied of his image, was now occupied by that of his usher. It was not without some surprise that I found myself obliged to entertain this view of the case. Pelet, with his old-established school, was so convenient, so profitable a match—Zoraïde was so calculating, so interested a woman—I wondered mere personal preference could, in her mind, have prevailed for a moment over worldly advantage. Yet it was evident from what Pelet said that not only had she repulsed him, but had even let slip expressions of partiality for me. One of his drunken exclamations was, 'And the jade dotes on your youth, you raw blockhead, and talks of your noble deportment, as she calls your accursed English formality, and your pure morals, forsooth! des m¦urs de Caton a-t-elle dit—sotte!' Hers, I thought, must be a curious soul, where, in spite of a strong natural tendency to estimate unduly advantages of wealth and station, the sardonic disdain of a fortuneless subordinate had wrought a deeper impression than could he imprinted by the most flattering assiduities of a prosperous chef d'institution. I smiled inwardly; and, strange to say, though my amour propre was excited not disagreeably by the conquest, my better feelings remained untouched. Next day, when I saw the directress, and when she made an excuse to meet me in the corridor, and besought my notice by a demeanour and look subdued to Helot humility, I could not love, I could scarcely pity her. To answer briefly and dryly some interesting inquiry about my health, to pass her by with a stern bow, was all I could. Her presence and manner had then, and for some time previously and consequently, a singular effect upon me. They sealed up all that was good, elicited all that was noxious in my nature; sometimes they enervated my senses, but they always hardened my heart. I was aware of the detriment done, and quarrelled with myself for the change. I had ever hated a tyrant; and, behold, the possession of a slave, self-given, went near to transform me into what I abhorred! There was at once a sort of low gratification in receiving this luscious incense from an attractive and still young worshipper, and an irritating sense of degradation in the very experience of the pleasure. When she stole about me with the soft step of a slave, I felt at once barbarous and sensual as a pasha. I endured her homage sometimes; sometimes I rebuked it. My indifference or harshness served equally to increase the evil I desired to check.
如今那位女校长正好被我的冷漠激怒，对我的轻蔑感到迷惑，又因怀疑我爱上另一个女人而深受刺激，此时她恰恰已经坠入她自己设置的圈套——她陷入了自己编织的情网，而她原本是想用那张网缠住我的。联想起那天在花园发生的事情，我从校长刚刚的表现可以推测出，他的情人泄露了她对他感情的疏远——或者我该说是意向的转移，感情这词在这里显得太过温情也太过纯洁——这使他发现她那空洞的心房中已没有了他，如今被他的职员占据着。当我发觉自己对此事有这样的想法时，不禁吃了一惊。珀莱拥有这样一所历史悠久的学校，是一位十分适宜又有利可图的佳偶——而佐蕾德是一个精于算计又很自私自利的女人——我很惊讶她心中纯粹的个人感情竟会在一时间压过世俗的利益。但是从珀莱所说的话中显然可以知道，她不但拒绝了他，还无意中说出了对我的偏爱。他的醉话里有一句是这样的，“那个女人看上了你的年轻，你这个没进化好的笨蛋，提到你那该死的英国礼节时还说你如贵族般风度翩翩，说你道德高尚——去你的吧！还说有卡顿那样的品质——笑话！”我想她的心态一定很古怪，尽管在那种心态之下她很自然地将财产和地位看得过重，但一个无财无权的下属对她的讽刺和轻蔑所产生的作用竟然远胜那位前途一片光明的校长的大肆奉承与殷勤。我暗暗地笑了笑；这也真是奇怪，尽管征服校长不合时宜地满足了我的自尊心，但我更美好的感情丝毫未被触动。次日，我见到了女校长，她借故和我在走廊上见了一面，她以一种如希洛人般谦卑的行为和表情想博得我的注意，可是我不能爱她，我甚至对她同情不起来。当她饶有兴趣地问及我的健康状况时，我所能做的只是简短而冷漠地回答几句，然后生硬地鞠了个躬离开。她那时的仪态和举止——在过去的很长一段时间也是这样——使我产生一种特别的印象。它们封锁了一切善的东西，引诱出我天性里一切恶的东西；有时它们能削弱我的感官，但却总是使我的心肠变得坚硬。我意识到它所造成的危害，内心也斗争过是否要改变。我素来痛恨专制之人；可是看，我也有了一个主动送上的奴隶，我本人也快要成为一个自己都憎恨的专制者了！在接受这位仍然年轻貌美的女崇拜者的赤裸裸地诱惑时，我心里有一种低贱的满足感，可就在享受这种乐趣时又有一种令人愤怒的堕落感。当她像个奴隶一样轻悄悄地走到我的身边，我立即会像个帕夏一样产生一种野蛮的肉欲。我有时忍受她的崇拜；有时又很排斥。我的冷淡和严肃却也同样地助长了我本想抑制的邪恶。
'Que le lui sied bien!' I once overheard her say to her mother. 'Il est beau comme Apollon quand il sourit de son air hautain.' 
有一次我无意听到她对她的母亲说：“他是多么地高傲啊！他高傲地微笑时就像太阳神阿波罗一样漂亮。“
And the jolly old dame laughed, and said she thought her daughter was bewitched; for I had no point of a handsome man about me, except being straight and without deformity. 'Pour moi,' she continued, 'il me fait tout l'effet d'un chathuant, avec ses bésicles.' 
这位快乐的老夫人哈哈笑起来，说她觉得她的女儿着了魔；因为我除了品德端正、四肢健全之外，一点都不具备美男的特征。“在我看来，”她继续说道，“他戴上眼镜就像一只猫头鹰。”
Worthy old girl! I could have gone and kissed her had she not been a little too old, too fat, and too red-faced. Her sensible, truthful words seemed so wholesome, contrasted with the morbid illusions of her daughter.
多么可敬的老姑娘啊！如果她不是有点太老太胖、脸色又过于通红的话，我就会走过去亲亲她。与她女儿那病态的幻觉相比，她明智而真实的评价多么全面。
When Pelet awoke on the morning after his frenzy fit, he retained no recollection of what had happened the previous night, and his mother fortunately had the discretion to refrain from informing him that I had been a witness of his degradation. He did not again have recourse to wine for curing his griefs, but even in his sober mood he soon showed that the iron of jealousy had entered into his soul. A thorough Frenchman, the national characteristic of ferocity had not been omitted by nature in compounding the ingredients of his character. It had appeared first in his access of drunken wrath when some of his demonstrations of hatred to my person were of a truly fiendish character, and now it was more covertly betrayed by momentary contractions of the features, and flashes of fierceness in his light blue eyes when their glance chanced to encounter mine. He absolutely avoided speaking to me; I was now spared even the falsehood of his politeness. In this state of our mutual relations my soul rebelled, sometimes almost ungovernably, against living in the house and discharging the service of such a man; but who is free from the constraint of circumstances? At that time I was not. I used to rise each morning eager to shake off his yoke, and go out with my portmanteau under my arm, if a beggar, at least a freeman; and in the evening, when I came back from the pensionnat de demoiselles, a certain pleasant voice in my ear; a certain face, so intelligent yet so docile, so reflective yet so soft, in my eyes; a certain cast of character, at once proud and pliant, sensitive and sagacious, serious and ardent, in my head; a certain tone of feeling, fervid and modest, refined and practical, pure and powerful, delighting and troubling my memory—visions of new ties I longed to contract, of new duties I longed to undertake, had taken the rover and the rebel out of me, and had shown endurance of my hated lot in the light of a Spartan virtue.
发酒疯的次日早晨，珀莱醒来，已经记不得前一天晚上发生的事情了，所幸她的母亲很谨慎，没有跟他说我亲眼目睹他出尽的洋相。他没有再借酒浇愁，但即使是清醒的，他也很快表现出那嫉妒的铁块已坠入了他的心灵。他是一个彻头彻尾的法国人，在他性格的各种构成要素中，法兰西民族的凶残特点绝对少不了。这种特点在他酒后的愤怒行为中第一次展现出来的，当时他对我个人充满仇恨的威胁体现了他恶魔似的本性，如今这种本性表现得较为隐蔽些，在他看到我时脸部肌肉会短暂地收缩，而当他的浅蓝色眼睛与我的相遇时便会闪耀着凶猛的光芒。他彻底避开了同我讲话，现在连假客气也一并省了。对于眼下我们之间的关系，我已经很厌烦，有时候几乎到了不可控制的地步，不想住在这间房子里，为这样一个人卖力；但是谁能挣脱环境的束缚呢？在那个时候，我不能。每天早晨起床我都想摆脱他的束缚，恨不得夹着我的皮箱离开这里，即便是当一个乞丐，至少也是自由的；晚上，当我从小姐的寄宿学校回来，就有一个愉快的声音回荡在我的耳边，一张机敏而又温顺、沉思而又温和的面孔出现在我的眼前，一个骄傲而又顺从、敏感而又聪慧、严肃而又热情的身影出现在我的脑海，一种热烈而又谦卑、高贵而又踏实、纯洁而又有力的情感使我的回忆既愉快又伤感——我能建起新的关系，承担新的义务，这使我不想再漂泊，再反叛，而应该用斯巴达人那样的品德去宽恕我所记恨的那些人。
But Pelet's fury subsided; a fortnight sufficed for its rise, progress, and extinction. In that space of time the dismissal of the obnoxious teacher had been effected in the neighbouring house, and in the same interval I had declared my resolution to follow and find out my pupil, and upon my application for her address being refused I had summarily resigned my own post. This last act seemed at once to restore Mdlle. Reuter to her senses. Her sagacity, her judgment, so long misled by a fascinating delusion, struck again into the right track the moment that delusion vanished. By the right track I do not mean the steep and difficult path of principle—in that path she never trod—but the plain highway of common sense, from which she had of late widely diverged. When there she carefully sought, and having found, industriously pursued the trail of her old suitor, M. Pelet. She soon overtook him. What arts she employed to soothe and blind him I know not, but she succeeded both in allaying his wrath and hoodwinking his discernment, as was soon proved by the alteration in his mien and manner. She must have managed to convince him that I neither was nor ever had been a rival of his, for the fortnight of fury against me terminated in a fit of exceeding graciousness and amenity, not unmixed with a dash of exulting self-complacency, more ludicrous than irritating. Pelet's bachelor life had been passed in proper French style with due disregard to moral restraint and I thought his married life promised to be very French also. He often boasted to me what a terror he had been to certain husbands of his acquaintance; I perceived it would not now be difficult to pay him back in his own coin.
但是珀莱的愤怒已经平息，两个星期的时间足够它产生、发展，然后消亡。在那段时间隔壁学校解雇了一位讨厌的老师，同时我也下定决心追寻我的学生，由于女校长拒绝告诉我女学生的地址，我立刻辞掉了我在她学校的工作。我的最后一招似乎立即使得勒泰小姐恢复了理智。她的洞察力和判断力长期被蛊惑人的幻象误导着，当幻象消失时，它们立即又回到了正确的轨道。我所说的正确轨道并不意味着陡峭艰难的原则之路——她从来没踏上过那条路——而是常识的大路，在这条路上她最近偏离得很厉害。她在常识这条路上仔细搜寻着，终于发现并不遗余力地追求起她旧时的求婚者珀莱先生；不久她就征服了他。我不知道她用了什么手段去安慰、迷惑他，但是她的确成功缓和了他的愤怒，并蒙蔽了他的双眼，这一点不久就被珀莱仪表和态度上的变化所证明。她肯定使他相信我从前不是，现在也不是他的对手，因为两周来他针对我的狂怒以一种过分和平友好的方式结束了，其中还颇有一些沾沾自喜的成分，让我在感到恼怒之余，更觉可笑。珀莱的单身生活是以地道的法国方式度过的，即无视道德的约束，我想他的婚后生活肯定也会是非常法国式的。他经常向我吹嘘他的那些已婚的老朋友是多么地害怕他；我猜想现在他们不难以其人之道还治其人之身了。
The crisis drew on. No sooner had the holidays commenced than note of preparation for some momentous event sounded all through the premises of Pelet. Painters, polishers, and upholsterers were immediately set to work, and there was talk of 'la chambre de madame,' 'le salon de madame.' Not deeming it probable that the old duenna at present graced with that title in our house had inspired her son with such enthusiasm of filial piety as to induce him to fit up apartments expressly for her use, I concluded, in common with the cook, the two housemaids, and the kitchen-scullion, that a new and more juvenile madame was destined to be the tenant of these gay chambers.
变化仍在继续。刚一放暑假我便注意到珀莱家里开始忙起一些重要的事情。粉刷匠、打磨匠、家具商都立即开始工作，到处谈论着“夫人的卧室”、“夫人的客厅”。我想那位老女人不可能一下子身价倍增，居然让她儿子孝心大发，专门为她装修起房间来；我和厨师、两个女仆以及管厨房的人断定将有一位年轻的新夫人将要入住这座漂亮的房子了。
Presently official announcement of the coming event was put forth. In another week's time M. François Pelet, directeur, and Mdlle. Zoraïde Reuter, directrice, were to be joined together in the bonds of matrimony. Monsieur, in person heralded the fact to me, terminating his communication by an obliging expression of his desire that I should continue, as heretofore, his ablest assistant and most trusted friend, and a proposition to raise my salary by an additional two hundred francs per annum. I thanked him, gave no conclusive answer at the time, and, when he had left me, threw off my blouse put on my coat, and set out on a long walk outside the Porte de Flandre, in order, as I thought, to cool my blood, calm my nerves, and shake my disarranged ideas into some order. In fact, I had just received what was virtually my dismissal. I could not conceal, I did not desire to conceal, from myself the conviction that, being now certain that Mdlle. Reuter was destined to become Madame Pelet, it would not do for me to remain a dependent dweller in the house which was soon to be hers. Her present demeanour towards me was deficient neither in dignity nor propriety; but I knew her former feeling was unchanged. Decorum now repressed, and policy masked it, but opportunity would be too strong for either of these—temptation would shiver their restraints.
不久后，这件即将到来的大事便被正式公开了。再过一个星期，弗朗索瓦·珀莱校长先生将与佐蕾德·勒泰小姐喜结连理。校长先生亲自通知了我这件事，最后亲切地向我表示他希望我一如既往地继续做他最得力的助手和最信赖的朋友，还说要给我加工资，每年再加二十法郎。我谢过了他，当时没给他明确答复。他离开之后，我脱下衬衫穿上外套，向弗朗德尔门外走去，我走了很远，想以此来冷却我的血液，平静我的神经，好把凌乱的思想理出一个头绪。事实上，我刚刚所收到的根本就是解聘通知。我不能也不想对自己隐瞒这种想法，勒泰小姐现在已经注定会成为珀莱夫人，我继续住在这个即将属于她的房子里也不合适。现在她对我的态度既尊重又颇为得体，但是我知道她之前的感情并没有改变。如今感情被礼节压抑，被策略遮掩，但是时机一到一切都会化为乌有——诱惑可以让束缚战栗。
I was no pope—I could not boast infallibility: in short, if I stayed, the probability was that in three months' time a practical modern French novel would be in full process of concoction under the roof of the unsuspecting Pelet. Now, modern French novels are not to my taste, either practically or theoretically. Limited as had yet been my experience of life, I had once had the opportunity of contemplating, near at hand, an example of the results produced by a course of interesting and romantic domestic treachery. No golden halo of fiction was about this example; I saw it bare and real, and it was very loathsome. I saw a mind degraded by the practice of mean subterfuge, by the habit of perfidious deception, and a body depraved by the infectious influence of the vice-polluted soul. I had suffered much from the forced and prolonged view of this spectacle; those sufferings I did not now regret, for their simple recollection acted as a most wholesome antidote to temptation. They had inscribed on my reason the conviction that unlawful pleasure, trenching on another's rights, is delusive and envenomed pleasure; its hollowness disappoints at the time, its poison cruelly tortures afterwards, its effects deprave for ever.
我不是教皇——我不能自诩绝无过失：简而言之，如果我继续住下去，很可能头三个月就会在没有戒心的珀莱的屋檐下上演一部活色生香的现实版法国现代小说。然而，法国现代小说无论从实践上还是理论上都不对我的胃口。尽管我的人生经验还有限，但我还是有幸目睹了身边一个有趣而浪漫的家庭背叛实例。这个实例没有被小说里的金色光晕萦绕，我看见它赤裸而真切地暴露着，十分可憎。我看到一个机关算尽、惯于行骗的人是多么堕落，也看到一个身体慢慢被邪恶的灵魂侵蚀掉是多么腐败。我曾因被迫长时间注视这种景象而感到十分痛苦；但我现在并不后悔，因为这种简单的回忆是抵制诱惑最有效的解毒剂。这种痛苦在我的理性上刻下了这样的信条：靠侵犯别人的权利而获得的不正当快乐是虚幻且有毒的，它的空虚会在当时使人失望，它的毒素会在日后使人痛苦，它的后果就是使人永世堕落。
From all this resulted the conclusion that I must leave Pelet's, and that instantly. 'But,' said Prudence, 'you know not where to go nor how to live.' And then the dream of true love came over me. Frances Henri seemed to stand at my side, her slender waist to invite my arm, her hand to court my hand. I felt it was made to nestle in mine; I could not relinquish my right to it, nor could I withdraw my eyes for ever from hers, where I saw so much happiness, such a correspondence of heart with heart; over whose expression I had such influence; where I could kindle bliss, infuse awe, stir deep delight, rouse sparkling spirit, and sometimes waken pleasurable dread. My hopes to win and possess, my resolutions to work and rise rose in array against me; and here I was about to plunge into the gulf of absolute destitution; 'and all this,' suggested an inward voice, 'because you fear an evil which may never happen!' 'It will happen; you know it will,' answered that stubborn monitor Conscience. 'Do what you feel is right. Obey me, and even in the sloughs of want I will plant for you firm footing.' And then, as I walked fast along the road there rose upon me a strange, inly-felt idea of some Great Being, unseen, but all-present, who in His beneficence desired only my welfare, and now watched the struggle of good and evil in my heart, and waited to see whether I should obey His voice, heard in the whispers of my conscience, or lend an ear to the sophisms by which His enemy and mine, the Spirit of Evil, sought to lead me astray. Rough and steep was the path indicated by divine suggestion, mossy and declining the green way along which temptation strewed flowers; but whereas, methought, the Deity of Love, the Friend of all that exists, would smile well pleased were I to gird up my loins and address myself to the rude ascent, so, on the other hand, each inclination to the velvet declivity seemed to kindle a gleam of triumph on the brow of the man- hating, God-defying demon. Sharp and short I turned round, fast I retraced my steps; in half an hour I was again at M. Pelet's. I sought him in his study; brief parley, concise explanation sufficed; my manner proved that I was resolved; he, perhaps, at heart approved my decision. After twenty minutes' conversation I re-entered my own room, self-deprived of the means of living, self-sentenced to leave my present home, with the short notice of a week in which to provide another.
从这一切我得出结论，那就是我必须离开珀莱家，而且立即离开。“但是，”谨慎告诉我，“你既不知道去哪也不知道怎么生存。”这时真爱之梦朝我走来。亨利小姐似乎是站在我这边，她的纤腰邀请我的手臂去拥抱，她的小手请求着我的大手去握住。我觉得她的手就是为了让我抓在手里而存在的；我既不能放弃我的权利，也永远不能避开她的眼神，在她眼里我看到了满满的幸福，看到了两心相印；我对她的表情有着很大的影响力，我能够在她体内燃起欢乐，灌注敬畏，搅动心底的愉悦，唤起闪光的灵魂，有时弄醒那令人愉快的恐惧。成功与富有的希望，工作与升迁的决心一个个站起来反对我，这里我几乎要坠入彻底贫困的深渊；“这一切，”我内心里一个声音说道，“都是因为你害怕一种可能根本不会发生的邪恶！”，“它会发生，你知道它会。”固执的良心回答说。“做你感觉是对的事吧。听我的话，即使陷入贫穷的泥潭中我也会让你站稳脚跟。”然后，当我快速地沿着这条路走时，内心里突然出现一个陌生的庞然大物，虽然看不见，但它是存在的。在它仁慈时，我只想到我的幸福安宁，但现在它却注视着我内心正与邪的斗争，等着看我是否会遵循它的话，听着我良心里的窃窃私语，或者偷听着我跟它共同的敌人——邪恶的幽灵——试图将我引上迷途。万能的理智给我暗示的是一条陡峭不平的上坡道，而下坡之路却长满青苔，沿着绿色的道路洒满了诱惑的鲜花；但是我想只要我鼓足勇气，勇敢地踏上崎岖的上坡道，那么身为万物之友的爱神将会极为满意地微笑；反过来说，一旦我踏上铺满天鹅绒的下坡，就会使得身为人神公敌的恶魔的眉角露出欣喜之情。我急忙转过身匆忙地往回跑；半个小时后我又回到了珀莱先生的家。在他的书房里我找到了他，简短的会谈、简单的解释便已足够，我的方式表明我已下定决心，也许他从心底里是赞同我的决定的。二十分钟的谈话后我又回到自己的房间，我就这样剥夺了自己的谋生手段，判决自己离开现在的家，简单地通知了他我会在一个星期之内找到地方搬走。
