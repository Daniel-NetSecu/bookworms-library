CHAPTER IX  

第九章  

Emil, who had continued to stand with his face to the window, even after Sanin's invitation to him to sit down, turned round directly his future kinsman had gone out, and with a childish pout and blush, asked Sanin if he might remain a little while with him. "I am much better today", he added, "but the doctor has forbidden me to do any work." 
埃米尔，甚至在萨宁跟他说了“请坐”后，仍然面窗而立。他那个未来的亲戚刚走，他便转过身来，孩子气地撅着嘴、微红着脸问道，是否可以和萨宁再呆会儿。“我今天感觉好多了，可是医生不允许我做任何事情。”埃米尔又说道。
"Stay by all means! You won't be in the least in my way," Sanin cried at once. Like every true Russian he was glad to clutch at any excuse that saved him from the necessity of doing anything himself.
“只管呆着吧！一点儿都不会妨碍我的。”萨宁马上高声说道。像每个俄国人一样，只要自己不费力气，他就会欣然接受任何请求。
Emil thanked him, and in a very short time he was completely at home with him and with his room; he looked at all his things, asked him about almost every one of them, where he had bought it, and what was its value. He helped him to shave, observing that it was a mistake not to let his moustache grow; and finally told him a number of details about his mother, his sister, Pantaleone, the poodle Tartaglia, and all their daily life. Every semblance of timidity vanished in Emil; he suddenly felt extraordinarily attracted to Sanin—not at all because he had saved his life the day before, but because he was such a nice person! He lost no time in confiding all his secrets to Sanin. He expatiated with special warmth on the fact that his mother was set on making him a shopkeeper, while he knew, knew for certain, that he was born an artist, a musician, a singer; that Pantaleone even encouraged him, but that Herr Kléber supported mamma, over whom he had great influence; that the very idea of his being a shopkeeper really originated with Herr Kléber, who considered that nothing in the world could compare with trade! To measure out cloth—and cheat the public, extorting from it "Narren—oder Russen Preise" (fools' —or Russian prices)—that was his ideal! [Footnote: In former days—and very likely it is not different now—when, from May onwards, a great number of Russians visited Frankfort, prices rose in all the shops, and were called "Russians'," or, alas! "fools' prices."] 
埃米尔道了谢，呆了不一会儿，他就和萨宁熟悉了，也熟悉了他的房间。他仔细打量着萨宁所有的东西，几乎每件东西他都会问在哪儿买的，值多少钱。他还帮萨宁刮胡子，并且说萨宁不蓄须是个错误。接着，他告诉了萨宁不少关于他妈妈、姐姐、潘塔莱昂纳以及狮子狗塔尔塔利阿的详细情况，以及他们的日常生活。这会儿，埃米尔脸上再也看不到任何拘谨的神色了，他忽然觉得和萨宁如此地亲近——不是因为萨宁前天救了他的命，而是因为，他觉得萨宁是个很亲切的人！他迫不及待地把自己的所有秘密都告诉了萨宁。他热切地向萨宁细细讲述着：事实上，他妈妈一定要让他去做个商人，可是他自己非常清楚，自己天生就是个艺术家、音乐家和歌手。对此，潘塔莱昂纳还曾经鼓励他。但是克吕贝尔先生却支持他妈妈的意见，并且对他妈妈的影响极大。让他做商人，本来就是克吕贝尔的主意，因为他认为世上再也没有什么职业可以和商人相媲美了！量出布匹——欺骗顾客，从中牟利（愚人的或俄国人的价格），这就是他这个商人的理想！[脚注：以前（如今大概也没有变），从五月份开始，大批的俄国人会涌到法兰克福来旅游，各个商店趁机抬高价格，由此叫做“俄国人的”——或者，唉！——“愚人的价格”。]
"Come! now you must come and see us!" he cried. Directly Sanin had finished his toilet and written his letter to Berlin.
“走吧！现在您必须跟我去我家里！”他大声说道。正好萨宁上完厕所，写好了寄往柏林的信。
"It s early yet," observed Sanin.
“还早呢。”萨宁说道。
"That's no matter," replied Emil caressingly. "Come along! We'll go to the post—and from there to our place. Gemma will be so glad to see you! You must have lunch with us... You might say a word to mamma about me, my career…” 
“没关系啦，”埃米尔亲切地回答道，“一起走吧！我们先去邮局，然后从那儿直接去我家。吉玛见到您一定会很高兴的。您一定要和我们一起吃午饭……您还可以和我妈妈谈谈我，还有我的职业……”
"Very well, let's go," said Sanin, and they set off.
“那也不错，我们走吧。”萨宁说完，他们便动身了。
