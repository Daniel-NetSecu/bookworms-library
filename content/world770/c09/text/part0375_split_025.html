XXVI  

第二十六章  

We happened once to go into the hut of a peasant-woman who had just lost her only, passionately loved son, and to our considerable astonishment we found her perfectly calm, almost cheerful. "Let her be," said her husband, to whom probably our astonishment was apparent, "she is gone numb now." And Litvinov had in the same way "gone numb." The same sort of calm came over him during the first few hours of the journey. Utterly crushed, hopelessly wretched as he was, still he was at rest, at rest after the agonies and sufferings of the last few weeks, after all the blows which had fallen one after another upon his head. They had been the more shattering for him that he was little fitted by nature for such tempests. Now he really hoped for nothing, and tried not to remember, above all not to remember. He was going to Russia... he had to go somewhere; but he was making no kind of plans regarding his own personality. He did not recognize himself, he did not comprehend his own actions, he had positively lost his real identity, and, in fact, he took very little interest in his own identity. Sometimes it seemed to him that he was taking his own corpse home, and only the bitter spasms of irremediable spiritual pain passing over him from time to time brought him back to a sense of still being alive. At times it struck him as incomprehensible that a man—a man!—could let a woman, let love, have such power over him ... "Ignominious weakness!" he muttered, and shook back his cloak, and sat up more squarely; as though to say, the past is over, let's begin fresh... a moment, and he could only smile bitterly and wonder at himself. He fell to looking out of the window. It was gray and damp; there was no rain, but the fog still hung about; and low clouds trailed across the sky. The wind blew facing the train; whitish clouds of steam, some singly, others mingled with other darker clouds of smoke, whirled in endless file past the window at which Litvinov was sitting. He began to watch this steam, this smoke. Incessantly mounting, rising and falling, twisting and hooking on to the grass, to the bushes as though in sportive antics, lengthening out, and hiding away, clouds upon clouds flew by... they were for ever changing and stayed still the same in their monotonous, hurrying, wearisome sport! Sometimes the wind changed, the line bent to right or left, and suddenly the whole mass vanished, and at once reappeared at the opposite window; then again the huge tail was flung out, and again it veiled Litvinov's view of the vast plain of the Rhine. He gazed and gazed, and a strange reverie came over him… He was alone in the compartment; there was no one to disturb him. "Smoke, smoke," he repeated several times; and suddenly it all seemed as smoke to him, everything, his own life, Russian life—everything human, especially everything Russian. All smoke and steam, he thought; all seems for ever changing, on all sides new forms, phantoms flying after phantoms, while in reality it is all the same and the same again; everything hurrying, flying towards something, and everything vanishing without a trace, attaining to nothing; another wind blows, and all is dashing in the opposite direction, and there again the same untiring, restless—and useless gambols! He remembered much that had taken place with clamor and flourish before his eyes in the last few years… "Smoke," he whispered, "smoke; "he remembered the hot disputes, the wrangling, the clamor at Gubaryov's, and in other sets of men, of high and low degree, advanced and reactionist, old and young.... "Smoke," he repeated, "smoke and steam"; he remembered, too, the fashionable picnic, and he remembered various opinions and speeches of other political personages—even all Potugin's sermonizing… "Smoke, smoke, nothing but smoke." And what of his own struggles and passions and agonies and dreams? He could only reply with a gesture of despair.
有一次，我们碰巧走进一位农妇的小木屋里。她刚刚失去她深爱的独子，但是让我们非常吃惊的是，我们发现她非常平静，简直很愉快。“随她去吧，”她丈夫说，或许我们对他的话更为吃惊，“她现在已经变得麻木了。”李特维诺夫也同样“变得麻木”了。在旅途开始时的几个小时里，他也是这样的平静。在过去几周的痛苦与折磨之后，在打击一个接一个落在他头上之后，他已经被彻底打倒、可怜地绝望了，但他还是那么平静，那么平静。他生性不擅长抵挡这样的风暴，因此就更加不堪一击了。现在，他真的是绝望了。他努力不去想起往事，他最想做的就是不去想起往事。他要去俄国……他必须去某个地方，但是他没有根据自己的情况作任何打算。他认不清自己，无法理解自己的行为，他已经完全失去了真实的“自我”，实际上，他对自我没多大兴趣。有时候，他觉得他是在将自己的尸体带回家，只有在那令人痛苦的、无法愈合的精神创伤不时袭遍全身时，他才能感觉到自己还活着。他总是觉得无法理解——一个男人——一个堂堂的男人怎么会被一个女人、被爱情操控……“丢人的懦弱啊！”他轻声念道，一边将大衣理到身后，坐得更直一些，似乎是在说，过去的一切已经结束了，重新开始吧……在这个时候，他只能苦笑，惊讶于自己的想法。他身体向后仰着以便看到窗外。外面灰暗潮湿，虽然没有下雨，但是大雾仍然没有消散，压低的云层挡住了整个天空。火车逆风而上，一团团白色的水蒸气，有时候夹杂着火车漆黑的滚滚浓烟，无止境地飞速掠过李特维诺夫座位旁边的窗户。他开始看着这蒸气、这烟雾。蒸气、烟雾不断地增加、起起落落，时而萦绕在树木之间，时而卧躺在草丛之上，好像在做滑稽的运动一样。渐渐地它们四处延伸，藏了起来，一团团飞逝……它们就这样不断变化着，但还是做着那单调、匆忙、乏味的运动！有时候，风会变化，风向偏向右边或左边；整一团烟雾会突然消失不见，然后又立刻出现在对面的窗户外。接着，烟雾会猛然甩出硕大的尾巴，再一次挡住李特维诺夫的视线，使他无法观赏到窗外莱茵河畔广袤的平原。他一直看啊看，一种奇怪的幻想向他袭来……车厢里只有他一个人，没有任何人来打扰他。“烟，烟。”重复了几遍。忽然，他觉得似乎一切都成了烟雾，所有的一切，他自己的生活，在俄国的生活——人世间的一切，尤其在俄国的一切都化成了一缕青烟。他觉得所有的烟雾、蒸气似乎都在以各种各样的形式、一个幻影接着一个幻影地不断变化着，然而实际上一切如故，不曾变化。一切都在匆忙地飞往其他事物，一切都在悄无声息地消逝，没有得到任何东西。又有一阵风刮过来，一切都往相反的方向冲去，接着又是同样不知疲倦、躁动不安、毫无意义的游戏！他想起了近几年来发生在他眼前的许多轰轰烈烈的事情……“烟。”他轻声念道，“烟。”他想起了古巴寥夫家中以及其他人——有地位高的也有地位低的，有进步主义者也有守旧主义者，有老也有少——那里的激烈争论、争吵和喧嚣，……“烟啊。”他重复说着，“烟啊，蒸气啊。”他还想起了那时髦的野餐，想起了其他政客的各类观点和言辞——甚至还想起了波图金的那些说教……“烟，烟，都是烟而已。”那么，他自己的挣扎、激情、痛苦和梦想又是什么呢？他只能报之以绝望的手势。
And meanwhile the train dashed on and on; by now Rastadt, Carlsruhe, and Bruchsal had long been left far behind; the mountains on the right side of the line swerved aside, retreated into the distance, then moved up again, but not so high, and more thinly covered with trees... The train made a sharp turn... and there was Heidelberg. The carriage rolled in under the cover of the station; there was the shouting of newspaper-boys, selling papers of all sorts, even Russian; passengers began bustling in their seats, getting out on to the platform, but Litvinov did not leave his corner, and still sat on with downcast head. Suddenly some one called him by name: he raised his eyes; Bindasov's ugly phiz was thrust in at the window; and behind him—or was he dreaming, no, it was really so—all the familiar Baden faces; there was Madame Suhantchikov, there was Voroshilov, and Bambaev, too; they all rushed up to him, while Bindasov bellowed: 
同时，火车隆隆地冲往前方；此时，拉什塔特、卡尔斯鲁厄和勃罗赫扎里早已远远地被抛在了后面，铁路右边的山脉突然转到了一边，退到了远处，接着又重新涌起来，但不如之前那么高，树木也比之前稀疏……火车来了个急转弯……到达了海德堡。列车驶进车站了。车站里充斥着报童的叫卖声，兜售各种报纸，连俄国的报纸都有；乘客们开始在座位上忙碌起来，走到了外面的站台上，但是李特维诺夫没有离开自己的角落，还低着头坐在那里。忽然有人叫着他的名字：他抬起头，看到宾达索夫将他那张丑陋的脸挤进了车窗内。而他身后的——是他在做梦吗，不，是真的——是来自巴登的那些熟悉的面孔，苏哈契科娃女士、沃罗希洛夫还有庞巴耶夫。他们都向他奔去，宾达索夫则大声叫喊着：
"But where's Pishtchalkin? We were expecting him; but it's all the same, hop out, and we'll be off to Gubaryov's."  "Yes, my boy, yes, Gubaryov's expecting us," Bambaev confirmed, making way for him, "hop out." 
“比夏尔金在哪儿？我们都在等着他呢；不过也一样，您快下来吧，和我们一起去古巴寥夫那里。”“是的，是的，小子，古巴廖夫在等着我们呢，”庞巴耶夫也这么说，一边给他让道。“快下来吧！”
Litvinov would have flown into a rage, but for a dead load lying on his heart. He glanced at Bindasov and turned away without speaking. "I tell you Gubaryov's here," shrieked Madame Suhantchikov, her eyes fairly starting out of her head.
若不是因为胸口沉重的负担，李特维诺夫早就火冒三丈了。他瞟了一眼宾达索夫，默默地把头扭开了。“我说古巴寥夫在这里，您听见了没有啊。”苏哈契科娃女士尖声叫道，眼珠子简直都要爆出来了。
Litvinov did not stir a muscle.
李特维诺夫纹丝不动。
"Come, do listen, Litvinov," Bambaev began at last, "there's not only Gubaryov here, there's a whole phalanx here of the most splendid, most intellectual young fellows, Russians—and all studying the natural sciences, all of the noblest convictions! Really you must stop here, if it's only for them. Here, for instance, there's a certain… there, I've forgotten his surname, but he's a genius! simply!” "Oh, let him be, let him be, Rostislav Ardalionovitch," interposed Madame Suhantchikov, "let him be! You see what sort of a fellow he is; and all his family are the same. He has an aunt; at first she struck me as a sensible woman, but the day before yesterday I went to see her here—she had only just before gone to Baden and was back here again before you could look round—well, I went to see her; began questioning her... Would you believe me, I couldn't get a word out of the stuck-up thing. Horrid aristocrat!” 
“别这样，听着，李特维诺夫！”庞巴耶夫终于说道，“这里不仅有古巴寥夫，还有那些最出色、最聪明的年轻人、俄国人——他们都在研究自然科学，他们都有着崇高的信仰！真的，您必须留在这里，哪怕只是为了他们。比如说，这里有一位……有一位，我忘了他的姓，但是他是个天才！简直就是个天才”“噢，别理他，随他去吧！罗斯基科拉夫·阿尔达里奥纳奇。”苏哈契科娃女士插嘴说道，“不要理他！你们也看到了他是什么样的人，他们全家人都一样！他有一位姑姑，刚开始我还以为她是个明智的人，但是前天我去找她——她刚去过巴登，还没等您游览一番，她就又回到这里了——于是，我去找她，开始问她一些事……你们能相信吗？我居然从这个高傲的女人嘴里问不出半句话。讨厌的贵族！”
Poor Kapitolina Markova an aristocrat! Could she ever have anticipated such a humiliation?
可怜的卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜居然变成了一位贵族！她何曾料到过会有这样的羞辱呢？
But Litvinov still held his peace, turned away, and pulled his cap over his eyes. The train started at last.
但是李特维诺夫还是那么平静，他扭过头，将帽子拉下来盖住了眼睛。火车终于开动了。
"Well, say something at parting at least, you stony-hearted man!" shouted Bambaev, "this is really too much!" 
“呃，至少在分别时说点什么啊！您这个铁石心肠的男人！” 庞巴耶夫喊道，“真是太过分了！”
"Rotten milksop!" yelled Bindasov. The carriages were moving more and more rapidly, and he could vent his abuse with impunity. "Niggardly stick-in-the-mud." Whether Bindasov invented this last appellation on the spot, or whether it had come to him second-hand, it apparently gave great satisfaction to two of the noble young fellows studying natural science, who happened to be standing by, for only a few days later it appeared in the Russian periodical sheet, published at that time at Heidelberg under the title: A tout venant je crache!1 or, "We don't care a hang for anybody!” 
“堕落又懦弱的家伙！”宾达索夫大叫道。车厢移动得越来越快，因此他可以无所顾忌地破口大骂。“真是个固执的小气鬼！”不管最后这个称呼是宾达索夫当场创造出的还是从别人那里学来的，总之，它显然让碰巧站在旁边研究自然科学的两位年轻贵族相当满意。因为没过几天这个称呼就被出版在当时的海德堡，题目是《历史事实》【1】 （又称为《上帝不语，猪猡不食》）。
But Litvinov repeated again, "Smoke, smoke, smoke! Here," he thought, "in Heidelberg now are over a hundred Russian students; they're all studying chemistry, physics, physiology—they won't even hear of anything else... but in five or six years' time there won't be fifteen at the lectures by the same celebrated professors; the wind will change, the smoke will be blowing... in another quarter... smoke... smoke!"2 
但是李特维诺夫还是一遍一遍地念着：“烟、烟、烟！”他想：“海德堡现在有一百多个俄国学生；他们全都在学习化学，物理还有生理学——其他任何东西他们连听都听不进去……但是五六年之后，还去听那些著名教授上课的学生将不足十五个。风向会改变，烟也会吹向……另外一个地区……烟…… 烟！”【2】
Towards nightfall he passed by Cassel. With the darkness intolerable anguish pounced like a hawk upon him, and he wept, burying himself in the corner of the carriage. For a long time his tears flowed, not easing his heart, but torturing him with a sort of gnawing bitterness; while at the same time, in one of the hotels of Cassel, Tatyana was lying in bed feverishly ill. Kapitolina Markovna was sitting beside her. "Tanya," she was saying, "for God's sake, let me send a telegram to Grigory Mihalitch, do let me, Tanya!” 
夜幕降临的时候，火车经过了凯塞尔。黑暗之中，令人难以忍受的痛苦像鹰一样扑向他的头，他把自己埋到车厢一角，哭了起来。他的泪水流了很长时间，但没有舒缓他的心情，反使他被一种如刀绞一般的痛苦所折磨，而与此同时，在凯塞尔的一家旅馆里，塔吉娅娜正发着高烧躺在床上。卡比托里娜·马尔科夫娜坐在她身边。“塔尼娅，”她说道，“看在上帝的份上，让我发一份电报给格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇吧！就让我这样做吧！塔尼娅！”
"No, aunt," she answered; "you mustn't; don't be frightened, give me some water: it will soon pass." 
“不，姑姑。”她回答道，“您不能这样做，别害怕，给我倒点水，很快就会过去的。”
And a week later she did, in fact, recover, and the two friends continued their journey.
一周之后，她确实恢复了。我们的这两个朋友继续她们的旅行。
[1]A historical fact.
【1】历史性事件。
[2]Litvinov's presentiments came true. In 1866 there were in Heidelberg thirteen Russian students entered for the summer, and twelve for the winter session.
【2】李特维诺夫的预感应验了。1886年夏天，在海德堡只有十三个俄国学生，到冬天只剩下十二个了。
