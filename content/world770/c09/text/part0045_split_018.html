Chapter 19  

第十九章  

In he first three letters, my father expressed his concern for my silence and asked the reason for it. In he last, he made it clear that he had been informed of my changed way of life, and announced his arrival in the very near future.
在前三封信里，父亲对我没有回信感到担心，并问我是什么原因。在最后一封信里，他明确告诉我，他已经知道了我生活中的一些变化，并说再过不久就要到巴黎来。
I have always felt great respect and a genuine affection for my father. So I wrote back saying that the reason for my silence was that I had been away travelling for a while, and I asked him to let me know on which day he proposed to arrive so that I could be there to meet him.
我一向很尊敬我的父亲，始终对他怀有很深的感情。因此我回信说，我之所以不回信是因为我之前去旅行了，我又请他提前把到达的日期告诉我，以便我去接他。
I gave my servant my country address and left orders that he was to bring the first letter that came postmarked C. Then I set off again immediately for Bougival.
我把我乡下的地址告诉了仆人，并嘱咐他一接到有C城邮戳的信就送去给我。然后，我又马上回到布吉瓦尔。
Marguerite was waiting for me at the garden gate.
玛格丽特在花园门口等着我。
Her look was anxious. She threw her arms around my neck and could not stop herself asking: 'Did you see Prudence?'
她看起来很焦虑。她搂住我的脖子，情不自禁地问我：“你见到普鲁登斯了吗？”
'No.'
“没有。”
'Why did you stay so long in Paris?'
“你怎么在巴黎呆了那么久？”
'I found some letters from my father which I had to answer.'
“我收到了父亲的几封信，我必须给他回信。”
A few moments after this, Nanine came in. She was out of breath. Marguerite stood up, went over and spoke to her softly.
没多久，纳尼娜进来了。她气喘吁吁，上气不接下气。玛格丽特站起来，走过去跟她低声说了几句。
When Nanine had gone, Marguerite sat down beside me once more and, taking my hand, said:
纳尼娜出去后，玛格丽特重新坐回我身边，握住我的手对我说：
'Why did you deceive me? You went to Prudence's, didn't you?'
“你为什么骗我？你去过普鲁登斯那里了，不是吗？”
'Who told you?'
“谁告诉你的？”
'Nanine.'
“纳尼娜。”
'And who told her?'
“那又是谁告诉她的？”
'She followed you.'
“她跟着你去的。”
'So you told her to follow me?'
“是你叫她跟着我的吗？”
'Yes. I thought there must have been a very good reason to make you go up to Paris like that. You've not left my side for four months. I was afraid that something awful had happened or that perhaps you were going to see another woman.'
“是的。我想，你那么着急回巴黎肯定有什么重要原因。你已经四个月没离开过我了。我害怕你出什么意外，又害怕你是去见其他的女人。”
'Silly girl!'
“傻孩子！”
'My mind's easy now. I know what you did, but I still don't know what you were told.'
“现在我放心了。我知道你都做了些什么，但我还是不知道别人都对你说了些什么。”
I showed Marguerite my father's letters.
我拿出父亲的信给玛格丽特看。
'That's not what I asked. What I'd like to know is why you called on Prudence.'
“我不是问这个。我想知道的是你为什么到普鲁登斯那儿去。”
'To see her.'
“去探望她。”
'You're lying, my dear.'
“你在撒谎，我亲爱的。”
'All right then. I went to ask her if the horse was better, and if she'd finished with your shawl and your jewels.'
“好吧。我去她那儿问问你的马好点儿了没，还有你的披肩和首饰她用完了没。”
Marguerite flushed, but said nothing.
玛格丽特的脸红了起来，但她什么都没说。
'And,' I continued, 'I found out to what use you'd put the horses, shawls and diamonds.'
“然后，”我继续说，“我就知道了你到底拿你的马、披肩和首饰干了什么。”
'And you're angry with me?'
“你怪我了是吗？”
'I'm angry with you for not thinking of asking me for whatever you needed.'
“我怪你为什么不找我要你需要的东西。”
In affairs like ours, as long as the woman has something of her self-respect left, she must shoulder any number of sacrifices herself rather than ask her lover for money and in so doing taint her love with mercenary motives. You love me, I know you do, but you have no idea just how weak are the ties that bind the love men have for girls like me. Who knows? Perhaps one day, when you were short of money or feeling annoyed, you'd have come round to thinking that our affair was a carefully worked-out plot! Prudence talks too much. I didn't need those horses! I've saved myself money by selling them: I can manage without, and now I don't have to spend anything on them. As long as you love me, that's all I ask. And you can love me just as much without horse and shawls and diamonds.'
“像我们这样的关系，只要这个女人还有一点点儿的自尊心，她就必须承担任何牺牲，而不向她的情人要钱，否则她的爱情就跟卖淫没什么区别。你爱我，这点我完全明白，但你却不知道，男人对我这种女人的爱的约束力有多么脆弱。谁又知道呢？也许你哪天缺钱了，或者心情不好，你就会把我们的爱情当作一场精心策划的阴谋。普鲁登斯总喜欢多嘴。我根本不需要那些马！把它们卖掉后我省了不少钱：没有这些马我也能过日子，而且也不用再在它们身上花钱。只要你爱我，这是我唯一要求的事情。即使没有那些马、披肩和首饰，你也会一样爱我。”
She said all this in so natural a tone of voice that there were tears in my eyes as I listened.
她说这些时语气是那么自然，我听着听着眼泪就在眼眶里打转了。
'But, my sweet Marguerite,' I answered, lovingly pressing my mistress's hands in mine, 'you must have known that some day I'd find out about your sacrifice, and that the day I did find out, I'd never have allowed it.'
“但是，我亲爱的玛格丽特，”我深情地握住我情人的手回答说，“你很清楚我总有一天会知道你的这些牺牲，到那时候，我是绝不会允许你做这些牺牲的。”
'And why not?'
“为什么不允许呢？”
'Because, dearest girl, I do not intend that the affection you truly feel for me should leave you the poorer by even a single piece of jewelry. Like you, I don't ever want you to think, when things are hard or you're feeling angry, that such bad times would never have happened if you'd lived with somebody else. Nor can I stand the thought that you should ever regret living with me, even for a moment. A few days from now, your horses, your diamonds and your shawls will be returned to you. You need them as much as life needs air. It may be ridiculous, but I'd rather have you lavish than frugal.'
“亲爱的姑娘，因为，我不想你因为对我的感情而变得贫穷，哪怕少一件珠宝都不行。跟你一样，我不希望你在遇到困难或心情不好时会想到，如果你跟别人在一起的话，就不会经历这些糟糕的事了。我也无法忍受你因为跟我在一起而感到后悔，哪怕一分钟也不行。几天之后，你的马、首饰和披肩都会回到你身边。你需要这些东西，就像生命需要空气一样。这听起来也许很可笑，但我宁愿你过得奢侈些，也比节俭来得好。”
'Which is to say you don't love me any more.'
“这么说，你不再爱我了。”
'Don't be silly!'
“别傻了！”
'If you really loved me, you'd let me love you in my own way. But you persist in thinking of me as though I'm some girl who can't live without all this luxury, someone you still think you have to pay. You are ashamed to accept proof that I love you. In you heart, you're thinking of leaving me some day, and you're being very careful to put you scruples beyond suspicion. You're quite right, my dear, but I had expected better.'
“如果你真的爱我，就让我用我自己的方式来爱你。但你一直把我当成那种奢侈成性的姑娘，那种你不得不花钱养活的姑娘。你羞于接受我爱你的证据。在你心里，你一直在想哪天总会离开我，一直在小心地用不安来掩饰你的疑心。你是对的，我亲爱的，只是我以前期待的太多了。”
And Marguerite stirred, as though she were about to get up. I held her back a moment, saying: I want you to be happy. I don't want there to be anything that you can reproach me for. That's all.'
玛格丽特动了一下，似乎想站起来。我拉住她，对她说：“我希望你能快乐。我不希望有任何原因能让你埋怨我。就这样。”
'Even so, we shall go our separate ways!'
“即便这样，我们也还是分手吧！”
'Why, Marguerite? Who can separate us?' I exclaimed.
“为什么，玛格丽特？谁能把我们分开？”我大声说道。
'You. You won't take me into your confidence by saying exactly where you stand, and you're vain enough to want to keep me in my place. You want to keep me in the luxury to which I was accustomed, but you also want to maintain the moral distance between us. You're the one. You don't consider that my feelings are sufficiently disinterested to want to share that money you have with me so that we could live happily together. No, you'd sooner ruin yourself. A slave to a stupid prejudice, that's what you are. Do you really think I compare a carriage and bits of jewelry with your love? Do you imagine I think happiness consists of those empty pleasures which people make do with when they've got nothing to love, but which seem so unimportant when they have? You'll pay my debts, you' ll sign away all you have and you'll be my keeper! And how long will that last? Two or three months — and then it'll be too late to start the life I'm offering you, for then you'd be kept by me, and that's something which no self-respecting man could accept. Whereas at the moment, you've got eight or ten thousand francs a year on which we can manage. I'll sell everything I don' t need, and by investing the proceeds I'd have a steady two thousand a year. We'll rent a nice little apartment and live there together. In summer, we'll come down to the country, not to a house like this, but to something smaller, just big enough for two. You've no ties, I'm free, and we're young. For heaven's sake, Armand, don't make me go back to the life I had to lead once!'
“是你啊。你不肯对我诉说心事，不告诉我你的处境，而且要我像过去那样生活，以此来满足自己的虚荣心。你想让我继续过去那种奢侈的生活，你想继续维持我们道德上的距离。你就是这样。你不认为我会愿意和你分享你那点钱来一起幸福地生活。不，你宁愿让自己倾家荡产。被愚蠢的成见所奴役，那就是你。你真以为那些马车和首饰可以跟你的爱相比吗？你知道吗？我认为，人们在没有爱情的时候才会把空虚的玩乐当幸福，而他们一旦拥有了爱情，这些东西就变得毫无价值了。你要替我还债，你把自己的钱都花光，然后来养我！这又能维持多久呢？两三个月？到那时再想按我说的方法去生活就太晚了，因为到那时你就要被我养着，这是任何一个有自尊心的男人都不愿接受的。而现在，你每年有八千到一万法郎的收入，我们可以靠这笔钱过下去。我把用不到的东西都卖掉，然后用卖得的钱来投资，这样我们每年又会有两千利弗尔的稳定收入。我们可以租一套漂亮的小公寓，一起在那生活。夏天的时候，我们可以到乡下玩，我们不要现在这种房子，只要一栋足够我们两人住的小房子就行了。你没有牵绊，我也自由自在，而且我们年纪还轻。看在老天的份上，阿尔芒，别再让我回到从前那种不得不过的日子里去了。”
I could not answer. My eyes brimmed over with tears of gratitude and love, and I threw myself into Marguerite' s arms.
我什么话都说不出来。我的双眼因感激和对玛格丽特的爱而充满泪水，我扑到了玛格丽特的怀里。
'I wanted,' she went on, 'to arrange everything without telling you. I wanted to pay my debts and get my new apartment ready. In October we would have returned to Paris and it would have been too late to say no. But since Prudence has told you everything, you'll have to agree before and not after. Do you love me enough to say yes?'
“我原本想，”她接着说，“瞒着你把一切都安排好。我想把我的债还清，再叫人把我们的新家布置好。十月份我们就会回到巴黎，那时你就来不及拒绝了。但既然普鲁登斯都告诉你了，你现在就得事先同意而不是事后认同了。你能因为爱我而答应我吗？”
I could not hold out against such devotion. I kissed Marguerite's hands with great feeling and told her:
我无法抗拒这样真挚的感情。我激动地吻着玛格丽特的手对她说：
'I shall do whatever you want.'
“我一切都听你的。”
And so what she had decided was agreed between us.
她的计划就这么定下了。
Then she became wildly exhilarated. She danced, she sang, she went into raptures about how homely her new apartment would be, and was already asking me in what part of Paris it should be and how it should be laid out.
她高兴得像疯了似的。她跳啊、唱啊，为自己温暖舒适的新家欣喜若狂，甚至已经开始问我想在巴黎哪个街区买房子，里面又想如何布置了。
I could see she was happy and very proud of this arrangement which seemed as though it would bring us together for good.
我看得出来，她对自己的这个主意既高兴又骄傲，似乎这样一来我们就可以永远在一起。
Which was why I had no wish to be any less keen than she was.
因此，我也不愿意白白接受她的好意。
In a moment, I decided what course my life was to take. I worked out how I stood financially, and made over to Marguerite the income from my mother' s estate, though it did not seem anything like an adequate return for the sacrifice which I was accepting.
我马上为我之后的生活做好了打算。我把我的财产重新做了安排，把我从我母亲的遗产中获得的那笔收入给了玛格丽特，虽然这还远远无法报答她为我做出的牺牲。
There remained the allowance of five thousand francs which my father made me and, however things turned out, this annual allowance would always be enough to live on.
剩下的就是我父亲给我的每年五千法郎的津贴，不管以后发生什么，这笔钱也足够过日子用了。
I did not tell Marguerite what I had decided, for I was quite convinced that she would refuse to accept my deed of gift.
我还要赠予玛格丽特一笔钱，而我相信她肯定会拒绝，所以没有把这个决定告诉她。
The money in question derived from a mortgage of sixty thousand francs on a house which I had never even seen. All I knew was that each quarter, my father's solicitor, an old family friend, handed over seven hundred and fifty francs against my signature.
这笔钱来自一栋六万法郎的房子的抵押金，我从没见过这栋房子。我只知道每一个季度，我父亲的公证人——我们家的一个老朋友——都会凭我的收据给我七百五十法郎。
The day Marguerite and I came to Paris to look at apartments, I called at his office and asked him how I should set about transferring this income to another party.
我跟玛格丽特回巴黎找房子的那天，我去他办公室拜访了他，问他怎样才能把这笔钱转让给另外一个人。
The good man thought that I was ruined, and asked me questions about why I had decided to take such a step. Now, since I was going to have to tell him sooner or later in whose favour I was making the deed of gift, I decided to confess the truth there and then.
这个善良的人以为我破产了，就问我这么做的原因。我知道迟早都要把这笔钱的受益人告诉他，于是决定把事实当场告诉他。
He did not raise any of the objections which his position as solicitor and friend entitled him to make, and he assured me that he would see that everything was arranged for the best.
作为一位公证人或者说是一位朋友，他本可以提出反对，但他什么也没说，只是向我保证他会尽量把事情办到最好。
Of course, I urged him to the greatest discretion with regard to my father, and left him to join Marguerite who was waiting for me at Julie Duprat's, where she had preferred to stay rather than go and be lectured by Prudence.
当然，我叮嘱他千万不要把事情告诉我父亲，然后就告辞去找玛格丽特了，她在朱莉·迪普拉家里等我，她宁愿在那儿呆着也不想去听普鲁登斯的说教。
We started looking for apartments. Marguerite found all the ones we saw too expensive, and I thought them too ordinary. Even so, we did agree in the end, and, in one of the quietest parts of Paris, decided on a modest lodge which was situated at a good distance from the main house.
我们开始找房子。玛格丽特觉得我们看过的房子都太贵了，我却觉得太简陋。不过，我们最后终于达成一致，决定在巴黎最清静的一个街区租一栋普通的小房子，这栋房子离主楼有些距离。
Behind this small lodge there was a delightful garden which was part of the property. It was enclosed by walls high enough to separate us from our neighbours, but not so high that they restricted the view.
在这栋房子后面有一个漂亮的小花园，也属于这栋房子。它被围墙包围着，那些围墙的高度刚刚好，既能把我们跟周围的邻居隔开，又不会妨碍到我们的视线。
It was better than we had hoped for.
这比我们想象得要好。
While I went back to my apartment to arrange to vacate the premises, Marguerite went to see a dealer who, she said, had already done for one of her friends what she was now going to ask him to do for her.
趁着我回去把我之前的房子退掉的时间，玛格丽特到一个经纪人那儿去了，她说他以前为她的一个朋友办过一些她现在要请他办的事。
She came for me in the rue de Provence, quite delighted. The man had promised to pay all her debts, give her a receipt in full, and let her have around twenty thousand francs in exchange for relinquishing all her furniture.
她之后回到普罗旺斯大街找我，看起来相当高兴。那个人同意替她还清所有债务，并给她一张全数收清条据，又给了她两万法郎，作为放弃所有家具的补偿。
You can see from the sum realized by the auction that this good man of business stood to make upwards of thirty thousand francs out of his client.
你可以从拍卖的价钱中看出，这个老实人大概从他的主顾身上赚到了三万法郎。
We set off back to Bougival in high spirits. As we went, we continued telling each other about our plans for the future which, with the help of our thoughtlessness but especially our love, we saw in the rosiest of lights.
我们高高兴兴地出发回布吉瓦尔。一路上，我们继续讨论着将来的计划，由于此刻没什么好顾虑的，再加上我们又是那么相爱，我们都觉得未来一片光明。
A week later, we were having lunch when Nanine came in and told me that my servant was asking for me.
一个星期之后，正当我们吃午饭的时候，纳尼娜进来告诉我说我的仆人要见我。
I told her to show him in.
我让她叫他进来。
Sir,' he said, 'your father has arrived in Paris, and asks you to return to your apartment at once. He's waiting for you there.'
“先生，”他说，“您父亲已经到巴黎了，他让您马上回您的公寓。他在那儿等您。”
The news was the simplest thing imaginable, and yet, as we took it in, Marguerite and I exchanged looks.
这本是最平常的事，但我和玛格丽特听到后却面面相觑。
We scented trouble in this turn of events.
我们猜到有麻烦要来了。
Which was why, though she did not intimate to me anything of her reaction which I shared, I responded by holding out my hand to her: There's nothing to be afraid of.'
因此，尽管她没有把我们心里所想的说出来，我还是把手伸给她，说：“没什么好怕的。”
'Come back as soon as you can,' murmured Marguerite as she kissed me. I'll be waiting by the window.'
“你尽早回来。”玛格丽特吻着我喃喃地说。“我会在窗边等你。”
I sent Joseph on ahead to let my father know I was on my way.
我先派约瑟夫回去告诉我父亲我已经上路了。
And two hours later, I was in my apartment in the rue de Provence.
两个小时以后，我来到了我在普罗旺斯街的公寓。
