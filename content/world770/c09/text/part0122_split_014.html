Chapter 15  HESTER AND PEARL  

第十五章  赫斯特和珠儿  

So Roger Chillingworth — a deformed old figure, with a face that haunted men's memories longer than they liked — took leave of Hester Prynne, and went stooping away along the earth. He gathered here and there an herb, or grubbed up a root, and put it into the basket on his arm. His grey beard almost touched the ground, as he crept onward. Hester gazed after him a little while, looking with a half-fantastic curiosity to see whether the tender grass of early spring would not be blighted beneath him, and show the wavering track of his footsteps, sere and brown, across its cheerful verdure. She wondered what sort of herbs they were, which the old man was so sedulous to gather. Would not the earth, quickened to an evil purpose by the sympathy of his eye, greet him with poisonous shrubs, of species hitherto unknown, that would start up under his fingers? Or might it suffice him that every wholesome growth should be converted into something deleterious and malignant at his touch? Did the sun, which shone so brightly everywhere else, really fall upon him? Or was there, as it rather seemed, a circle of ominous shadow moving along with his deformity, whichever way he turned himself? And whither was he now going? Would he not suddenly sink into the earth, leaving a barren and blasted spot, where, in due course of time, would be seen deadly nightshade, dogwood, henbane, and whatever else of vegetable wickedness the climate could produce, all flourishing with hideous luxuriance? Or would he spread bat's wings and flee away, looking so much the uglier, the higher he rose towards heaven?
就这样，罗杰·奇林沃斯离开了赫斯特·普林，弓着身子沿泥路走去。他身材畸形、老态龙钟，那张脸总像鬼魅般萦绕在人们的记忆里，让人想忘都忘不了。他这儿采些那儿采些，或是把他们连根拔起，然后放入手上挽着的篮子里。他慢慢地前进着，灰白的胡须几乎要碰到地面。赫斯特怀着一种异乎寻常的好奇，凝视了会儿他的背影，想看看早春的嫩草是否会在他脚下枯萎，那欣欣向荣的葱绿上，会不会随着他的足迹留下一条枯黄的曲径小道。她寻思着，那该是种什么药草呢，这老人竟如此潜心地采摘。这土地是否会因受了他眼神的感应而心生恶意，凡手指所碰之处，都用不知名的有毒灌木来迎接他呢？或者，要是每一种良草益木经他触碰都会变成毒木莠草，他是否会感到满足呢？这普照的阳光真能落在他身上吗？或者，是否无论他走到哪里，都有一个不祥的阴影跟随着他畸形的身躯？他现在又将去往哪里？他会突然陷入这土地，留下一块不毛之地吗？随着时间的推移，在特定的气候里，这里会长满致命的龙葵、山茱萸、天仙子等毒草，可怕地蔓延滋生。或者，他是否会插上蝙蝠的翅膀飞走，越飞越高，也越来越丑陋呢？
"Be it sin or no, " said Hester Prynne, bitterly, as she still gazed after him, "I hate the man!"
“不管是不是罪过，”赫斯特依旧盯着他的背影，愤愤的说道，“我恨这个男人！”
She upbraided herself for the sentiment, but could not overcome or lessen it. Attempting to do so, she thought of those long-past days, in a distant land, when he used to emerge at eventide from the seclusion of his study, and sit down in the firelight of their home, and in the light of her nuptial smile. He needed to bask himself in that smile, he said, in order that the chill of so many lonely hours among his books might be taken off the scholar's heart. Such scenes had once appeared not otherwise than happy; but now, as viewed through the dismal medium of her subsequent life, they classed themselves among her ugliest remembrances. She marvelled how such scenes could have been! She marvelled how she could ever have been wrought upon to marry him! She deemed it her crime most to be repented of that she had ever endured, and reciprocated, the lukewarm grasp of his hand, and had suffered the smile of her lips and eyes to mingle and melt into his own. And it seemed a fouler offence committed by Roger Chillingworth, than any which had since been done him, that, in the time when her heart knew no better, he had persuaded her to fancy herself happy by his side.
她为这样的情绪而自责，却不能自制。她试着克服这样的情绪，想起了很久以前的那些日子：在遥远的土地上，每每日落，他从清幽的书房出来，坐在家中的壁炉旁，沉浸在她贤惠的笑容里。他曾说过，他需要这笑容来取暖，驱除他心中因独自久居书卷而带来寒气。从前，她只要想起这样的情景就会感到幸福；而现在，透过之后的那些苦楚，她把这些幸福归到了记忆中最不堪入目的部分。她对那样的场景感到不可思议。她诧异自己竟会嫁给他！她觉得自己最该懊悔的是，自己当初竟然忍受并回握了他不冷不热的手，竟能忍受让自己嘴唇和眉眼的笑意融入他的笑意。看来，比之人们之后对他犯下的罪过，罗杰·奇林沃斯犯下的罪过更为严重：在她不谙世事时，他要她相信，跟了他便会幸福。
"Yes, I hate him!" repeated Hester, more bitterly than before. "He betrayed me! He has done me worse wrong than I did him!"
“是的，我恨他！”赫斯特重复着，口气更狠了。“他辜负了我！他对我的伤害胜过我对他的！”
Let men tremble to win the hand of woman, unless they win along with it the utmost passion of her heart! Else it may be their miserable fortune, as it was Roger Chillingworth's, when some mightier touch than their own may have awakened all her sensibilities, to be reproached even for the calm content, the marble image of happiness, which they will have imposed upon her as the warm reality. But Hester ought long ago to have done with this injustice. What did it betoken? Had seven long years, under the torture of the scarlet letter, inflicted so much of misery, and wrought out no repentance?
让他们男人颤抖着去赢取女人的婚姻吧！除非他们能同时赢得女人最大的芳心，否则这将是他们的悲剧。就像罗杰·奇林沃斯那样，当更强烈的触动唤醒女人的情感时，那些以温暖和现实之名强加给她们的满足和幸福，即使平静，即使坚如磐石，都将受到指责。而赫斯特早就该对这种不公处之泰然了。但这又能证明什么呢？难道那红字长达七年的折磨和痛苦，还不能带来一丝忏悔之意？
The emotions of that brief space, while she stood gazing after the crooked figure of old Roger Chillingworth, threw a dark light on Hester's state of mind, revealing much that she might not otherwise have acknowledged to herself.
她站在那里，望着老罗杰•奇林沃斯驼背的样子，那转瞬即逝的情绪在她心里投下了暗影，一些平时不肯承认的念头在心头滋生。
He being gone, she summoned back her child.
等他走远，她把孩子叫了回来。
"Pearl! Little Pearl! Where are you?"
“珠儿！小珠儿！你在哪里？”
Pearl, whose activity of spirit never flagged, had been at no loss for amusement while her mother talked with the old gatherer of herbs. At first, as already told, she had flirted fancifully with her own image in a pool of water, beckoning the phantom forth, and — as it declined to venture — seeking a passage for herself into its sphere of impalpable earth and unattainable sky. Soon finding, however, that either she or the image was unreal, she turned elsewhere for better pastime. She made little boats out of birch-bark, and freighted them with snailshells, and sent out more ventures on the mighty deep than any merchant in New England; but the larger part of them foundered near the shore. She seized a live horse-shoe by the tail, and made prize of several five-fingers, and laid out a jelly-fish to melt in the warm sun. Then she took up the white foam, that streaked the line of the advancing tide, and threw it upon the breeze, scampering after it with winged footsteps, to catch the great snowflakes ere they fell. Perceiving a flock of beach-birds, that fed and fluttered along the shore, the naughty child picked up her apron full of pebbles, and, creeping from rock to rock after these small sea-fowl, displayed remarkable dexterity in pelting them. One little grey bird with a white breast, Pearl was almost sure, had been hit by a pebble, and fluttered away with a broken wing. But then the elf-child sighed, and gave up her sport; because it grieved her to have done harm to a little being that was as wild as the sea-breeze, or as wild as Pearl herself.
珠儿向来劲头十足，在她妈妈和老采药者讲话那会儿，她玩得可带劲了。一开始，就像前面提到的那样，她异想天开地和自己在水塘里的倒影嬉戏，招呼她出来玩。由于那倒影不肯出来，她便要寻找一条途径，通往那不可捉摸的天地。然而，她很快发现她和那映像总有一个是不真实的，于是就转身去玩更有意思的游戏。她用桦树皮做了很多小船，把蜗牛壳装在船里，让它们漂在海上，数量之多胜过新英格兰任何一个商人的货物，但是大部分离岸不远就沉没了。她抓住了一条鲎鱼的尾巴，捕到了几只海星，还把水母放在温暖的阳光下融化。然后她捧起沿着潮头前进的白泡沫，抛在风中，像脚上长了翅膀似的追着它跑，想在这大大的雪片掉落前就接住。她看见一群海鸟在岸边飞来飞去地觅食，这调皮的孩子就捡了一围裙小石子，沿着岩石爬向海鸟，动作敏捷地朝着它们扔石子。她几乎可以确定，一只有着灰色羽毛、白色肚皮的小鸟，被一粒小石子击中了，拍着受伤的翅膀飞走了。但这精灵般的孩子叹了口气，为自己打伤了那小家伙而难过，不再这样玩了。这小鸟和珠儿自己一样，都似海风般狂野。
Her final employment was to gather sea-weed of various kinds, and make herself a scarf, or mantle, and a head-dress, and thus assume the aspect of a little mermaid. She inherited her mother's gift for devising drapery and costume. As the last touch to her mermaid's garb, Pearl took some eel-grass, and imitated, as best she could, on her own bosom, the decoration with which she was so familiar on her mother's. A letter — the letter A — but freshly green, instead of scarlet! The child bent her chin upon her breast, and contemplated this device with strange interest; even as if the one only thing for which she had been sent into the world was to make out its hidden import.
最后，她采起了各种海草，为自己做了条围巾——或者说是披肩——还有一圈头饰，把自己扮成小美人鱼的模样。她遗传了母亲设计服装衣饰的天赋。珠儿取了些鳗草放在胸前，尽量模仿着母亲胸前那熟悉的装饰，为她的人鱼装束做最后的点缀。是一个字母——字母"A"——不是深红色的，而是清新的绿色！这孩子把下巴抵在胸口，怀着奇妙的兴致端详着这玩意儿，好像她来到这个世界的唯一目的就是弄清这字母背后的含义。
"I wonder if mother will ask me what it means?" thought Pearl.
“不晓得妈妈会不会问我它的意思？”珠儿想。
Just then, she heard her mother's voice, and flitting along as lightly as one of the little sea-birds, appeared before Hester Prynne, dancing, laughing, and pointing her finger to the ornament upon her bosom.
就在这时，她听到了妈妈的喊声，于是像小鸟般轻快地跑到赫斯特•普林的跟前，手舞足蹈，乐呵呵地指着自己胸前的装饰。
"My little Pearl!" said Hester, after a moment's silence, "the green letter, and on thy childish bosom, has no purport. But dost thou know, my child, what this letter means which thy mother is doomed to wear?"
“我的小珠儿！”赫斯特沉默了会儿，说道，“这绿色的字母在你那小胸脯上，没有任何意义。但我的孩子，你知道妈妈非戴不可的这字母有什么含义吗？”
"Yes, mother, " said the child. "It is the great letter A. Thou hast taught me in the horn-book. "
“是的，妈妈。”孩子回答，“那是大写的A字。你在识字书上教过我的。”
Hester looked steadily into her little face; but, though there was that singular expression which she had so often remarked in her black eyes, she could not satisfy herself whether Pearl really attached any meaning to the symbol. She felt a morbid desire to ascertain the point.
赫斯特紧紧地凝视着那张小脸，尽管那黑眼睛中的表情如往常一样独特，但她还是不知道珠儿是否由那字母联想到了什么。她感到有一种病态的欲望想弄清楚这一点。
"Dost thou know, child, wherefore thy mother wears this letter?"
“孩子，你知道妈妈为什么要戴上这字母吗？”
"Truly do I!" answered Pearl, looking brightly into her mother's face. "It is for the same reason that the minister keeps his hand over his heart!"
“我当然知道了！”珠儿答道，用亮闪闪的眸子看着她母亲的脸。“这和牧师用手捂住心口是同一个原因！”
"And what reason is that?" asked Hester, half smiling at the absurd incongruity of the child's observation; but, on second thoughts, turning pale. "What has the letter to do with any heart, save mine?"
“是什么原因呢？”赫斯特问道，半带微笑地听着孩子这番荒诞不经的话，可转念一想，她的脸变得刷白了。“除了我的心之外，那字母怎么还和别人的心有关系呢？”
"Nay, mother, I have told all I know, " said Pearl more seriously than she was wont to speak. "Ask yonder old man whom thou hast been talking with! It may be he can tell. But in good earnest now, mother dear, what does this scarlet letter mean? — and why dost thou wear it on thy bosom? — and why does the minister keep his hand over his heart?"
“那我就不知道了，妈妈，我知道的我全说了。”珠儿的口气比平常严肃了些。“问问刚才和你说话的那老人吧！说不定他知道呢。不过说真的，好妈妈，那红字是什么意思呢？为什么你要戴它在胸口呢？为什么牧师要用手捂住心口呢？”
She took her mother's hand in both her own, and gazed into her eyes with an earnestness that was seldom seen in her wild and capricious character. The thought occurred to Hester that the child might really be seeking to approach her with childlike confidence, and doing what she could, and as intelligently as she knew how, to establish a meeting-point of sympathy. It showed Pearl in an unwonted aspect. Heretofore, the mother, while loving her child with the intensity of a soul affection, had schooled herself to hope for little other return than the waywardness of an April breeze, which spends its time in airy sport, and has its gusts of inexplicable passion, and is petulant in its best of moods, and chills oftener than caresses you, when you take it to your bosom; in requital of which misdemeanours, it will sometimes, of its own vague purpose, kiss your cheek with a kind of doubtful tenderness, and play gently with your hair, and then be gone about its other idle business, leaving a dreamy pleasure at your heart. And this, moreover, was a mother's estimate of the child's disposition. Any other observer might have seen few but unamiable traits, and have given them a far darker colouring. But now the idea came strongly into Hester's mind, that Pearl, with her remarkable precocity and acuteness, might already have approached the age when she could be made a friend, and entrusted with as much of her mother's sorrows as could be imparted, without irreverence either to the parent or the child. In the little chaos of Pearl's character, there might be seen emerging — and could have been, from the very first — the steadfast principles of an unflinching courage — an uncontrollable will — a sturdy pride, which might be disciplined into self-respect — and a bitter scorn of many things, which, when examined, might be found to have the taint of falsehood in them. She possessed affections, too, though hitherto acrid and disagreeable, as are the richest flavours of unripe fruit. With all these sterling attributes, thought Hester, the evil which she inherited from her mother must be great indeed, if a noble woman do not grow out of this elfish child.
她双手握着母亲的手，用那轻狂个性中少有的一本正经直视着母亲。赫斯特突然闪过一个念头：这孩子兴许真的试图在用稚气的信心接近她，尽其所能，以她所知的最聪明的方式来建立一种情感共识。这展现了珠儿不同以往的一面。在这之前，她虽然全身心地深爱着孩子，不求别的回报，只要孩子能像任性的四月之风就好。那风无忧无虑地游戏，有股不可捉摸的激情，心情好时也会使使性子，揽她入怀还时会冻到你而不是爱抚你。为弥补那种过失，它有时会出于模糊的目的，用难以置信的温柔亲你的脸颊，轻轻地拨弄你的头发，然后跑开闲玩去了，在你心头留下梦幻般的愉悦。这更是一位母亲对孩子脾性的判断。旁观者或许不会看出什么好的脾性，只会给出更坏的评价。现在赫斯特强烈地感到，珠儿有异乎寻常的早熟和敏锐，或许早已到了可以和她作伴的年龄，可以尽其所能地分担母亲的忧愁，也不用担心会没大没小。珠儿的脾性虽有些小糟糕，却开始显现出——或许一开始就有——一种坚定不移的勇气、一种不受束缚的意志、一种可以造就自尊的桀骜不驯和一种对事物的极度不屑。这些要是细究起来，或许会有虚伪的印迹。她还拥有丰富的情感，虽然那情感至今还像未成熟的果实般，酸涩而难以入口。赫斯特寻思着：这孩子有那么多纯良的禀赋，要是她不能长成一位高贵的女人，那就是从她母亲身上遗传了太多的邪恶。
Pearl's inevitable tendency to hover about the enigma of the scarlet letter seemed an innate quality of her being. From the earliest epoch of her conscious life, she had entered upon this as her appointed mission. Hester had often fancied that Providence had a design of justice and retribution, in endowing the child with this marked propensity; but never, until now, had she bethought herself to ask, whether, linked with that design, there might not likewise be a purpose of mercy and beneficence. If little Pearl were entertained with faith and trust, as a spirit messenger no less than an earthly child, might it not be her errand to soothe away the sorrow that lay cold in her mother's heart, and converted it into a tomb? —     and to help her to overcome the passion, once so wild, and even yet neither dead nor asleep, but only imprisoned within the same tomblike heart?
珠儿对这红字之谜的一味纠缠，大概是天性使然。自打她有意识起，她就把这当成使命一样对待。赫斯特以前常想，上天是出于因果报应的意图才赋予孩子这种明显的习性。可到了如今，她终于开始问自己，有没有可能上天也是出于仁慈才有了如此的设计。如果小珠儿能被信任，能被当作精神的使者，而不仅仅是个尘世的孩子，难道她就不能抚平母亲的忧伤，使她的心不再冰冷，不再深埋于墓穴吗？难道她就不能帮母亲制服那曾经似火的激情吗？这激情如今虽未沉寂或死去，却仍禁锢在那颗坟墓般的心里。
Such were some of the thoughts that now stirred in Hester's mind, with as much vivacity of impression as if they had actually been whispered into her ear. And there was little Pearl, all this while holding her mother's hand in both her own, and turning her face upward, while she put these searching questions, once, and again, and still a third time.
正是这样的想法纠缠着赫斯特，其印象如此鲜活，仿佛有人在她耳边低语诉说着这些。而小珠儿此时正双手握着母亲的手，抬着头，一而再、再而三地问着这些问题，急于知道答案。
"What does the letter mean, mother? —     and why dost thou wear it? —     and why does the minister keep his hand over his heart?"
“这字母是什么意思呀，妈妈？你为什么要戴着它？牧师为什么把手放在心口？”
"What shall I say?" thought Hester to herself. "No! If this be the price of the child's sympathy, I cannot pay it."
“我该说些什么呢？”赫斯特心想。“不行！如果这是换取孩子感情上支持的代价，我不能支付。”
Then she spoke aloud.
于是她开口说话了。
"Silly Pearl, " said she, "what questions are these? There are many things in this world that a child must not ask about. What know I of the minister's heart? And as for the scarlet letter, I wear it for the sake of its gold thread."
“傻珠儿，”她说，“这都是些什么问题呀？这世界上很多问题是小孩子不该问的。我哪会知道关于牧师的心那件事呢？至于这红字嘛，我是因为它有金色的线才戴着的。”
In all the seven bygone years, Hester Prynne had never before been false to the symbol on her bosom. It may be that it was the talisman of a stern and severe, but yet a guardian spirit, who now forsook her; as recognising that, in spite of his strict watch over her heart, some new evil had crept into it, or some old one had never been expelled. As for little Pearl, the earnestness soon passed out of her face.
在这过去的七年里，赫斯特•普林从来没就这胸前的徽记说过谎。也许，这红字不仅是个苛刻严厉的辟邪物，也是个守护神，可如今守护神却抛弃了她。因为它发现，尽管它严格地守在她心口，但一些新的邪恶已经趁虚而入，或是那些旧的邪恶从来就没有被驱除出去。至于小珠儿呢，脸上那一本正经的表情很快消失了。
But the child did not see fit to let the matter drop. Two or three times, as her mother and she went homeward, and as often at suppertime, and while Hester was putting her to bed, and once after she seemed to be fairly asleep, Pearl looked up, with mischief gleaming in her black eyes.
但这孩子还不善罢甘休。回家的路上，她又问了两三遍。吃晚饭时，以及赫斯特送她上床时，她又问了两三遍。在她仿佛已睡着的时候，珠儿抬起头来又问了一遍，黑眼睛里闪着调皮的神色。
"Mother, " said she, "what does the scarlet letter mean?"
“妈妈，”她说道，“这红字是什么意思呢？”
And the next morning, the first indication the child gave of being awake was by popping up her head from the pillow and making that other inquiry, which she had so unaccountably connected with her investigations about the scarlet letter —
第二天一早，这孩子醒来的第一个反应就是，从枕头上猛地仰起头来，又问了另外那个问题。不知为什么，她总把那问题跟对红字的调查联系起来。
"Mother! — mother! — why does the minister keep his hand over his heart?"
“妈妈！妈妈！牧师为什么要用手捂着心口？”
"Hold thy tongue, naughty child!" answered her mother, with an asperity that she had never permitted to herself before. "Do not tease me, else I shall shut thee into the dark closet!"
“闭嘴，捣蛋鬼！”她母亲应道，她从前可不允许自己那么粗鲁。“别缠着我，不然我把你关到暗房里去！”
