The Maison Tellier  

泰利耶公馆  

Part I

一

They went there every evening about eleven o'clock, just as they would go to the club. Six or eight of them; always the same set, not fast men, but respectable tradesmen, and young men in government or some other employ, and they would drink their Chartreuse, and laugh with the girls, or else talk seriously with Madame Tellier, whom everybody respected, and then they would go home at twelve o'clock! The younger men would sometimes stay later.
每晚十一点左右，他们都会到那个地方，就如同他们要去俱乐部一样。在那个地方相聚的有六到八个人；而且始终是那几个，他们不是放荡不羁的人，而是有名望的商人、政府或其他行业的年轻人。他们一边喝着察吐士酒，一边和姑娘们调情，或者跟深受大家敬重的泰利耶夫人一本正经地聊天，然后他们在十二点钟就会回家！年轻人有时会留下来多呆一会儿。
It was a small, comfortable house painted yellow, at the corner of a street behind Saint Etienne's Church, and from the windows one could see the docks full of ships being unloaded, the big salt marsh, and, rising beyond it, the Virgin's Hill with its old gray chapel.
这所房子小而舒适，漆成了黄色，坐落在圣艾蒂安教堂背后的街道拐角处。从房子的窗口，人们可以看到码头上满是正在卸货的船舶，看到大片的盐碱滩，以及盐碱滩后面的圣母山和山坡上古老的灰色教堂。
Madame Tellier, who came of a respectable family of peasant proprietors in the Department of the Eure, had taken up her profession, just as she would have become a milliner or dressmaker. The prejudice which is so violent and deeply rooted in large towns, does not exist in the country places in Normandy. The peasant says: "It is a paying-business," and he sends his daughter to keep an establishment of this character just as he would send her to keep a girls' school.
泰利耶夫人出生在厄尔省一个有名望的自耕农家庭。她从事这一行，就和她成为女帽商或者女装裁缝师没什么两样。城里人对于这个行当持有强烈而又根深蒂固的偏见，但这个偏见在诺曼底的农村却是不存在的。农民会说：“这个行当不错。”于是，他就让女儿去开妓院了，就像让他的女儿去开设女子寄宿学校一样。
She had inherited the house from an old uncle, to whom it had belonged. Monsieur and Madame Tellier, who had formerly been innkeepers near Yvetot, had immediately sold their house, as they thought that the business at Fecamp was more profitable, and they arrived one fine morning to assume the direction of the enterprise, which was declining on account of the absence of the proprietors. They were good people enough in their way, and soon made themselves liked by their staff and their neighbors.
这座房子是她从原来的业主，一位年老的舅舅手中继承来的。以前在伊沃托附近开客店的泰利耶先生和夫人，认为费康的生意更加有利可图，就立即卖了他们的房子。一个晴朗的早晨，他们赶到了费康，接管了这家因为没有老板而每况愈下的妓馆。他们为人正直，很快就得到手下和邻居们的喜爱。
Monsieur died of apoplexy two years later, for as the new place kept him in idleness and without any exercise, he had grown excessively stout, and his health had suffered. Since she had been a widow, all the frequenters of the establishment made much of her; but people said that, personally, she was quite virtuous, and even the girls in the house could not discover anything against her. She was tall, stout and affable, and her complexion, which had become pale in the dimness of her house, the shutters of which were scarcely ever opened, shone as if it had been varnished. She had a fringe of curly false hair, which gave her a juvenile look, that contrasted strongly with the ripeness of her figure. She was always smiling and cheerful, and was fond of a joke, but there was a shade of reserve about her, which her occupation had not quite made her lose. Coarse words always shocked her, and when any young fellow who had been badly brought up called her establishment a hard name, she was angry and disgusted.
两年之后，先生中风去世了，因为这个新职业让他过着懒散的生活，加上平时不进行任何锻炼，他变得过度肥胖，身体也就这么毁了。自从她成为寡妇以后，妓馆的常客都对她垂涎三尺；但是据说，她这个人非常规矩，就连馆里的姑娘们也从未发现过她有不检点的行为。她高大，肥胖，亲切友善。她住在这所整天关着百叶窗的阴暗的房子里，面色变得苍白，但又闪着光泽，好像上了一层清漆似的。额前卷曲的假刘海让她看起来很年轻，但与她那成熟的体型又形成鲜明的对比。她总是笑呵呵，快快乐乐的，又喜欢开玩笑，不过她很有分寸，她的新行当并没有使她失去这点分寸。粗鲁的话总是令她震惊，如果哪个教养不好的年轻小伙子用难听的名称称呼她掌管的这家妓馆，她立即就板起脸发起脾气来。
In a word, she had a refined mind, and although she treated her women as friends, yet she very frequently used to say that "she and they were not made of the same stuff." 
总之，她不落俗套，尽管她把那些姑娘当朋友看，她还是常常说“她和她们不属于同一类人。”
Sometimes during the week she would hire a carriage and take some of her girls into the country, where they used to enjoy themselves on the grass by the side of the little river. They were like a lot of girls let out from school, and would run races and play childish games. They had a cold dinner on the grass, and drank cider, and went home at night with a delicious feeling of fatigue, and in the carriage they kissed Madame' Tellier as their kind mother, who was full of goodness and complaisance.
在周末以外的日子里，她有时会雇一辆马车，带一些姑娘们去郊外，在那里小河边的草地上，她们常常会玩得很开心。她们像一群逃出寄宿学校的女学生，相互追逐嬉戏，还玩一些儿童游戏。她们坐在草地上吃冷餐，喝苹果酒，直到天黑，她们才带着舒服愉悦的疲劳感回到家。在马车上，她们会像吻一位善良、随和的母亲一样吻泰利耶太太。
The house had two entrances. At the corner there was a sort of tap-room, which sailors and the lower orders frequented at night, and she had two girls whose special duty it was to wait on them with the assistance of Frederic, a short, light-haired, beardless fellow, as strong as a horse. They set the half bottles of wine and the jugs of beer on the shaky marble tables before the customers, and then urged the men to drink.
这所房子有两个入口。在拐角处有一个酒吧间，水手和下等阶层的人们晚上经常去那儿。她有两个姑娘专门负责招待他们，弗雷德里克当她们的助手，他个子矮小，一头金发，没有胡子，身体结实得像一头牛。他们把半瓶半瓶的葡萄酒和小罐小罐的啤酒放在酒客前面摇摇晃晃的大理石桌上，接着就劝他们喝酒。
The three other girls—there were only five of them—formed a kind of aristocracy, and they remained with the company on the first floor, unless they were wanted downstairs and there was nobody on the first floor. The salon de Jupiter, where the tradesmen used to meet, was papered in blue, and embellished with a large drawing representing Leda and the swan. The room was reached by a winding staircase, through a narrow door opening on the street, and above this door a lantern inclosed in wire, such as one still sees in some towns, at the foot of the shrine of some saint, burned all night long.
另外三个姑娘——妓馆里只有五个姑娘——构成一种贵族阶级，她们专门在二楼陪客。除非楼下点名要她们，而楼上又没有客人，她们才下楼。朱庇特大厅是商人通常聚集的地方，这个大厅糊着蓝色的墙纸，而且还用一幅很大的画做装饰，画上是勒达和天鹅。到这个房间需要走一条蜿蜒的楼梯，楼梯口是一扇临街的窄门。门上有个安了铁网的壁洞，彻夜点着一盏小灯，就像现在有些城镇的圣人神龛下面点的那种小灯。
The house, which was old and damp, smelled slightly of mildew. At times there was an odor of eau de Cologne in the passages, or sometimes from a half-open door downstairs the noisy mirth of the common men sitting and drinking rose to the first floor, much to the disgust of the gentlemen who were there. Madame Tellier, who was on friendly terms with her customers, did not leave the room, and took much interest in what was going on in the town, and they regularly told her all the news. Her serious conversation was a change from the ceaseless chatter of the three women; it was a rest from the obscene jokes of those stout individuals who every evening indulged in the commonplace debauchery of drinking a glass of liqueur in company with common women.
这所房子，既古老又潮湿，还带着轻微的霉味。有时，过道里飘过一股古龙香水的香味，有时从楼下半掩着的小门里传来坐在底层喝酒的男人聒噪的笑声，这使得二楼的绅士们非常反感。泰利耶夫人把顾客当成她的朋友，从来不离开房间，她对城里发生的事十分感兴趣，她的顾客们也会经常将城里的新闻全都告诉她。她严肃的谈话对于那三个姑娘叽叽喳喳不间断的谈话来说，是个小插曲；对于那些大腹便便的人来说，是调情谈笑中的一次小休息。这些人每天晚上都来这儿有所节制地放荡一下，让妓女陪他们喝一杯利口酒。
The names of the girls on the first floor were Fernande, Raphaele, and Rosa, the Jade. As the staff was limited, madame had endeavored that each member of it should be a pattern, an epitome of the feminine type, so that every customer might find as nearly as possible the realization of his ideal. Fernande represented the handsome blonde; she was very tall, rather fat, and lazy; a country girl, who could not get rid of her freckles, and whose short, light, almost colorless, tow-like hair, like combed-out hemp, barely covered her head.
在二楼的那三个姑娘名叫费尔南德、拉斐尔和泼妇罗莎。由于人员有限，夫人就尽量让每个人都成为一种样品，成为一类女人的典型代表，这样每位顾客就都能找到近乎他们理想的对象。费尔南德代表的是金发美女：她个子高，相当胖，而且很懒；她原是农家姑娘，脸上的雀斑怎么样也没法去掉，头发很短，颜色还浅，像是梳理过的大麻，稀稀拉拉地盖住了她的脑袋。
Raphaele, who came from Marseilles, played the indispensable part of the handsome Jewess, and was thin, with high cheekbones, which were covered with rouge, and black hair covered with pomatum, which curled on her forehead. Her eyes would have been handsome, if the right one had not had a speck in it. Her Roman nose came down over a square jaw, where two false upper teeth contrasted strangely with the bad color of the rest.
拉斐尔，是个马赛人，充当着不可缺少的犹太美女的角色。她身材瘦削，高高的颧骨上涂着一层厚厚的胭脂，卷在前额的黑色头发上抹着润发油。要是她的右眼没有长白翳，她那双眼睛会很美。她的鹰钩鼻垂到宽大的下巴上，上面两颗假门牙与其他颜色暗淡的旧牙齿形成了鲜明的对比。
Rosa was a little roll of fat, nearly all body, with very short legs, and from morning till night she sang songs, which were alternately risque or sentimental, in a harsh voice; told silly, interminable tales, and only stopped talking in order to eat, and left off eating in order to talk; she was never still, and was active as a squirrel, in spite of her embonpoint and her short legs; her laugh, which was a torrent of shrill cries, resounded here and there, ceaselessly, in a bedroom, in the loft, in the cafe, everywhere, and all about nothing.
罗莎整个身子像个小肉球，腿很短。她从早到晚不停地用她那刺耳的嗓音唱着时而轻佻、时而感伤的歌；她爱讲无聊又冗长的故事，只有在要吃东西的时候她才不再唠叨，但一吃完东西，她就又开始喋喋不休；她一刻也没闲过，始终在动，尽管身体肥胖，腿又短，但她还是像松鼠一样灵活；她笑声不断，听上去像是一连串的尖叫，不管是在哪儿，在卧室、在阁楼，还是在咖啡馆，都能听到她那无缘无故连续不断的爆笑声。
The two women on the ground floor, Louise, who was nicknamed Hen, and Flora, whom they called Seesaw, because she limped a little, the former always dressed as the Goddess of Liberty, with a tricoloured sash, and the other as a Spanish woman, with a string of copper coins in her carroty hair, which jingled at every uneven step, looked like cooks dressed up for the carnival. They were like all other women of the lower orders, neither uglier nor better looking than they usually are.
楼下的两个姑娘：路易丝，绰号叫“老母鸡”；弗洛拉，腿有点瘸，人们就叫她“跷跷板”。前一个腰上总是扎着一条三色彩带，打扮成“自由女神”的样子；另一个打扮成西班牙女郎，她一瘸一拐地走着，红色头发上吊的那串铜质的硬币状的小东西也随之叮当叮当响，样子像过狂欢节的厨娘一样。她们和所有下层妇女一样，既不会比她们美，也不会比她们丑。
They looked just like servants at an inn, and were generally called "the two pumps." 
她们就像客店里女佣人的模样，人们给她们起了个绰号叫“两个水泵”。
A jealous peace, which was, however, very rarely disturbed, reigned among these five women, thanks to Madame Tellier's conciliatory wisdom, and to her constant good humor, and the establishment, which was the only one of the kind in the little town, was very much frequented. Madame Tellier had succeeded in giving it such a respectable appearance, she was so amiable and obliging to everybody, her good heart was so well known, that she was treated with a certain amount of consideration. The regular customers spent money on her, and were delighted when she was especially friendly toward them, and when they met during the day, they would say: "Until this evening, you know where," just as men say: "At the club, after dinner." In a word, Madame Tellier's house was somewhere to go to, and they very rarely missed their daily meetings there.
这五个女人相互嫉妒，但彼此又相处得很和睦，多亏了泰利耶夫人善于从中调解，也多亏了她一贯的好脾气，这种和睦才很少被扰乱。这个妓馆是这个小城里仅有的一家，因此门庭若市。泰利耶夫人把这个妓馆装修得像模像样，她自己待人又是那么和蔼可亲，对所有人都殷勤体贴，她善良的心远近闻名，因此受到各方的敬重。那些常客千方百计地讨好她，只要她对他们格外亲近一点，他们就会洋洋得意。当他们白天相遇时，他们总会说“今天晚上，老地方见”，就如同说“吃过晚饭，上俱乐部”一样。总之，泰利耶夫人的妓馆是个好去处，极少有人错过他们每天在那儿的约会。
One evening toward the end of May, the first arrival, Monsieur Poulin, who was a timber merchant, and had been mayor, found the door shut. The lantern behind the grating was not alight; there was not a sound in the house; everything seemed dead. He knocked, gently at first, but then more loudly, but nobody answered the door. Then he went slowly up the street, and when he got to the market place he met Monsieur Duvert, the gunmaker, who was going to the same place, so they went back together, but did not meet with any better success. But suddenly they heard a loud noise, close to them, and on going round the house, they saw a number of English and French sailors, who were hammering at the closed shutters of the taproom with their fists.
五月底的一天晚上，第一个来的是从前的市长，木材商普兰先生，他发现妓馆的门紧闭着。铁网后面的那盏小灯没有点亮；房子里一点动静也没有，死气沉沉的。他先轻轻地敲了门，后来又使劲地敲，但还是没人应声。于是他沿着街道缓步往回走，当他走到市场那儿时，遇到了要去同一地方的枪炮制造商迪韦尔先生，两个人就一起折了回去，但还是没人开门。但是他们突然听到不远处传来喧哗声，于是他们绕过房子，看到关闭着的酒吧间门前，一群英国和法国的水手，正在用拳头捶打门板。
The two tradesmen immediately made their escape, but a low "Pst!" stopped them; it was Monsieur Tournevau, the fish curer, who had recognized them, and was trying to attract their attention. They told him what had happened, and he was all the more annoyed, as he was a married man and father of a family, and only went on Saturdays. That was his regular evening, and now he should be deprived of this dissipation for the whole week.
两个商人赶忙溜走，但是一个低声的“嘘！”声，让他们停了下来；他们一看，原来是咸鱼腌制商图尔纳沃先生，他认出了他们，正在向他们打招呼。他们向他介绍了情况，他听了很恼火，因为他是个已婚的人，有儿有女，他只有星期六才能到这儿来。这天晚上正好是他来的日子，这样一来，他就被剥夺了这个一星期才一次的放纵机会。
The three men went as far as the quay together, and on the way they met young Monsieur Philippe, the banker's son, who frequented the place regularly, and Monsieur Pinipesse, the collector, and they all returned to the Rue aux Juifs together, to make a last attempt. But the exasperated sailors were besieging the house, throwing stones at the shutters, and shouting, and the five first-floor customers went away as quickly as possible, and walked aimlessly about the streets.
这三个人一起一直走到码头，半路遇见了银行家的儿子——年轻的菲利普先生，他是泰利耶妓馆的常客；还遇见了税务官皮尼佩斯先生。于是他们就一起回到犹太人街，想最后再试一次。但是怒气冲冲的水手们正在围攻那所房子，边往百叶窗上扔石头，边大喊大叫；那五个二楼的客人赶紧离开，漫无目的地在街上走着。
Presently they met Monsieur Dupuis, the insurance agent, and then Monsieur Vasse, the Judge of the Tribunal of Commerce, and they took a long walk, going to the pier first of all, where they sat down in a row on the granite parapet and watched the rising tide, and when the promenaders had sat there for some time, Monsieur Tournevau said: "This is not very amusing!" 
他们不久又遇见了保险代理人迪皮伊先生，以及商事法庭法官瓦斯先生。他们一起走了很长一段路，先是去了码头，并排坐在那里的花岗石护墙上，望着起伏的波浪。这群散步的人在那里坐了一会儿后，图尔纳沃先生说道：“这真没意思！”
"Decidedly not," Monsieur Pinipesse replied, and they started off to walk again.
“的确如此，”皮尼佩斯先生应声道。于是，他们又开始走了。
After going through the street alongside the hill, they returned over the wooden bridge which crosses the Retenue, passed close to the railway, and came out again on the market place, when, suddenly, a quarrel arose between Monsieur Pinipesse, the collector, and Monsieur Tournevau about an edible mushroom which one of them declared he had found in the neighborhood.
他们穿过了山坡旁的那条街道后，又从横跨水库的木桥折了回来，从铁路附近经过，重新走到了市场上。这时候，税务官皮尼佩斯先生和咸鱼腌制商图尔纳沃先生，突然为了某种食用蘑菇吵起来，他们中有一个人咬定自己曾经在附近一带看到过。
As they were out of temper already from having nothing to do, they would very probably have come to blows, if the others had not interfered. Monsieur Pinipesse went off furious, and soon another altercation arose between the ex-mayor, Monsieur Poulin, and Monsieur Dupuis, the insurance agent, on the subject of the tax collector's salary and the profits which he might make. Insulting remarks were freely passing between them, when a torrent of formidable cries was heard, and the body of sailors, who were tired of waiting so long outside a closed house, came into the square. They were walking arm in arm, two and two, and formed a long procession, and were shouting furiously. The townsmen hid themselves in a doorway, and the yelling crew disappeared in the direction of the abbey. For a long time they still heard the noise, which diminished like a storm in the distance, and then silence was restored. Monsieur Poulin and Monsieur Dupuis, who were angry with each other, went in different directions, without wishing each other good-by.
由于无事可做，他们火气很大。要不是其他人劝解，他们很有可能已经大打出手了。皮尼佩斯先生一气之下走了。紧接着，前市长普兰先生和保险代理人迪皮伊先生又为了税务官的薪俸和可能得到的外快问题发生了争执。骂人的话一句连着一句，他们两个互不相让。此时，忽然传来一阵可怕的叫喊声，声音不绝于耳。原来是那群水手在关了门的房子前等那么久，等得不耐烦了，跑到了广场上。他们两个两个地走着，相互挽着胳膊，组成一支很长的队伍，正疯狂地叫骂着。这伙城里人躲到一户人家的门口，望着那群闹哄哄的水手消失在去修道院的方向。过了很久，他们还是可以听到那群人的喧闹声，不过声音逐渐减弱，如同移到远处的暴风雨，最后又恢复了平静。普兰先生和迪皮伊先生还在生彼此的气，没有向对方告辞，就朝着各自的方向走了。
The other four set off again, and instinctively went in the direction of Madame Tellier's establishment, which was still closed, silent, impenetrable. A quiet, but obstinate drunken man was knocking at the door of the lower room, and then stopped and called Frederic, in a low voice, but finding that he got no answer, he sat down on the doorstep, and waited the course of events.
其他四个接着往前走，他们不自觉地往泰利耶妓馆的方向走去。门仍然紧闭，悄无声息，没法进去。一个醉汉，不声不响地还顽固地坚守在那儿，他正敲着底层酒吧间的门。后来，他停了下来，轻声叫着弗雷德里克，却发现没有人回应他。他便坐在门前的台阶上，等着看会有什么情况发生。
The others were just going to retire, when the noisy band of sailors reappeared at the end of the street. The French sailors were shouting the "Marseillaise," and the Englishmen "Rule Britannia." There was a general lurching against the wall, and then the drunken fellows went on their way toward the quay, where a fight broke out between the two nations, in the course of which an Englishman had his arm broken and a Frenchman his nose split.
那几个人正要走，忽然看见那帮闹哄哄的水手又出现在街口。法国水手们高唱《马赛曲》，英国水手们则唱着《统治吧，大不列颠》。这帮人都倚着墙壁，随后这些醉汉们涌向码头。到了码头，两国的水手就打起来了，在搏斗中，一个英国人的手臂被打折了，一个法国人的鼻子被打裂了。
The drunken man who had waited outside the door, was crying by that time, as drunken men and children cry when they are vexed, and the others went away.
这时，那个在门口等着的醉汉突然哭了，哭得就像受了委屈的酒鬼和孩子一样。那几个绅士最后也走了。
By degrees, calm was restored in the noisy town; here and there, at moments, the distant sound of voices could be heard, and then died away in the distance.
吵闹的城镇渐渐平静了下来；不过，时而从这里或哪里还可以听见远处的声音，随后那声音又在远处逐渐消逝了。
One man only was still wandering about, Monsieur Tournevau, the fish curer, who was annoyed at having to wait until the following Saturday, and he hoped something would turn up, he did not know what; but he was exasperated at the police for thus allowing an establishment of such public utility, which they had under their control, to be closed.
只有一个人还在路上徘徊，就是咸鱼腌制场主图尔纳沃先生，他想到要一直等到下个星期六才能过来就十分生气。他希望事情有所转机，虽然他不知道是什么事；但是他又对警察们很恼火，因为他们竟然允许一个在他们管制之下的公益场所关门。
He went back to it and examined the walls, trying to find out some reason, and on the shutter he saw a notice stuck up. He struck a wax match and read the following, in a large, uneven hand: "Closed on account of the Confirmation." 
他回到那儿，仔细地查看墙壁，想找出一些原因来，接着便发现百叶窗上贴着一张告示。他赶忙点着一根蜡制火柴，看到上面写着几个歪歪扭扭的大字：“因坚信礼，暂停营业。”
Then he went away, as he saw it was useless to remain, and left the drunken man lying on the pavement fast asleep, outside that inhospitable door.
他明白他再等下去也没有用，于是就离开了，留下那个醉汉横躺在紧闭着的大门前酣睡着。
The next day, all the regular customers, one after the other, found some reason for going through the street, with a bundle of papers under their arm to keep them in countenance, and with a furtive glance they all read that mysterious notice: "Closed on account of the Confirmation." 
第二天，所有的老顾客一个接着一个设法经过那条街，为了装样子，他们还在腋下夹着一捆文件。每个人都偷偷地看了一眼那张神秘的告示：“因坚信礼，暂停营业。”

Part II

二

Madame Tellier had a brother, who was a carpenter in their native place, Virville, in the Department of Eure. When she still kept the inn at Yvetot, she had stood godmother to that brother's daughter, who had received the name of Constance—Constance Rivet; she herself being a Rivet on her father's side. The carpenter, who knew that his sister was in a good position, did not lose sight of her, although they did not meet often, for they were both kept at home by their occupations, and lived a long way from each other. But as the girl was twelve years old, and going to be confirmed, he seized that opportunity to write to his sister, asking her to come and be present at the ceremony. Their old parents were dead, and as she could not well refuse her goddaughter, she accepted the invitation. Her brother, whose name was Joseph, hoped that by dint of showing his sister attention, she might be induced to make her will in the girl's favor, as she had no children of her own.
泰利耶夫人有个弟弟，在家乡厄尔省的维里维尔当木匠。当她还在伊沃托开客店的时候，她当了她这个弟弟的女儿的教母，这个女孩名字叫康斯坦丝——康斯坦丝·里韦；泰利耶夫人自己娘家的姓氏就是里韦。木匠知道姐姐境况不错，尽管他们都很忙，而且住的地方又离得很远，彼此之间很少见面，但是他一直和她保持着联系。小姑娘已经十二岁了，并且就要接受坚信礼了，于是他就抓住这次机会，写信邀请她姐姐过来参加仪式。他们年迈的父母已经去世了，因为她不能拒绝她教女的事，所以就接受了邀请。他的弟弟名叫约瑟夫，他希望对她多献献殷勤，说不定这样能促使她立下有利于小姑娘的遗嘱，因为她自己没有孩子。
His sister's occupation did not trouble his scruples in the least, and, besides, nobody knew anything about it at Virville. When they spoke of her, they only said: "Madame Tellier is living at Fecamp," which might mean that she was living on her own private income. It was quite twenty leagues from Fecamp to Virville, and for a peasant, twenty leagues on land is as long a journey as crossing the ocean would be to city people. The people at Virville had never been further than Rouen, and nothing attracted the people from Fecamp to a village of five hundred houses in the middle of a plain, and situated in another department; at any rate, nothing was known about her business.
他一点也不介意她姐姐的职业，再说当地的人对此也一无所知。说起他姐姐时，他们只会说：“泰利耶夫人住在费康城里。”这意思就是她靠着她个人的收入过日子。从费康到维里维尔，足足有二十里格，对于一个乡下人来说，二十里格就像城里人漂洋过海的距离一样长。维里维尔的人们从没去过比鲁昂更远的地方，当然也不会有什么能吸引费康的人来到这坐落在平原中的、只有五百户人家的小村庄，而且还是另一个省份的小村庄。总而言之，别人对她的职业全然不知。
But the Confirmation was coming on, and Madame Tellier was in great embarrassment. She had no substitute, and did not at all care to leave her house, even for a day; for all the rivalries between the girls upstairs and those downstairs would infallibly break out. No doubt Frederic would get drunk, and when he was in that state, he would knock anybody down for a mere word. At last, however, she made up her mind to take them all with her, with the exception of the man, to whom she gave a holiday until the next day but one.
但是，坚信礼的日子一天一天临近了，泰利耶夫人感到十分为难。她没有可代替她的人，哪怕是离开她的妓馆一天，她也不放心，因为楼上和楼下的姑娘们之间的积怨准会爆发出来。弗雷德里克很可能喝醉酒，一喝醉，他就会为了芝麻小事大打出手。最后她决定除了弗雷德里克以外，把所有人都带走。至于弗雷德里克，她给他放了一天假。
When she asked her brother, he made no objection, but undertook to put them all up for a night, and so on Saturday morning the eight-o'clock express carried off Madame Tellier and her companions in a second-class carriage. As far as Beuzeville they were alone, and chattered like magpies, but at that station a couple got in. The man, an old peasant, dressed in a blue blouse with a turned-down collar, wide sleeves tight at the wrist, ornamented with white embroidery, wearing an old high hat with long nap, held an enormous green umbrella in one hand, and a large basket in the other, from which the heads of three frightened ducks protruded. The woman, who sat up stiffly in her rustic finery, had a face like a fowl, with a nose that was as pointed as a bill. She sat down opposite her husband and did not stir, as she was startled at finding herself in such smart company.
她征求了她弟弟的意见，他对此毫无异议，而且还答应招待她们住一晚上。就这样，星期六早晨，泰利耶夫人和她的同伴们赶上八点钟的快车，坐上二等车厢出发了。一直到伯兹维尔，车厢里就只有她们几个人，她们像喜鹊似的唧唧喳喳地谈个不停。但是到了这一站，上来了一对夫妇。男的是个老农，穿着一件翻领蓝罩衫，袖口紧缩，宽松的袖子上绣着小白花，头上戴着一顶老式的长绒高顶礼帽。他一只手拿着一把绿色大伞，另一只手拿着个大篮子，篮子里三只神色惊慌的鸭子探出了它们的脑袋。女的是乡下人打扮，直挺挺地坐着，长着一副“母鸡脸”，鼻子尖得像鸟嘴。她坐在丈夫的对面，看到自己处在一群时髦女子的中间，大吃一惊，坐在那儿一动也不敢动。
There was certainly an array of striking colors in the carriage. Madame Tellier was dressed in blue silk from head to foot, and had on a dazzling red imitation French cashmere shawl. Fernande was puffing in a Scotch plaid dress, of which her companions had laced the bodice as tight as they could, forcing up her full bust, that was continually heaving up and down. Raphaele, with a bonnet covered with feathers, so that it looked like a bird's nest, had on a lilac dress with gold spots on it, and there was something Oriental about it that suited her Jewish face. Rosa had on a pink skirt with largo flounces, and looked like a very fat child, an obese dwarf; while the two Pumps looked as if they had cut their dresses out of old flowered curtains dating from the Restoration.
这节车厢里确实是花花绿绿，令人眼花缭乱。泰利耶夫人全身上下都是蓝色的绸缎，上面披着一条红色的仿制法国羊绒披肩，红得耀眼。费尔南德被身穿的苏格兰彩格呢连衣裙包得直喘气。她的女伴们尽力把她的上身束紧，将她丰满的胸部往上兜，以至于她的胸部不停地上下晃动着。拉斐尔戴一顶插着羽毛的帽子，那帽子看上去像个鸟窝；身穿一身淡紫色衣裙，上面装饰着金色的亮片，颇有东方情调，同她的犹太人相貌很相称。罗莎穿着宽荷叶边的粉红裙子，样子像个胖小孩，或者是肥胖的侏儒；那“两个水泵”看起来好像穿着用复辟时期的旧花窗帘裁制而成的衣服。
As soon as they were no longer alone in the compartment, the ladies put on staid looks, and began to talk of subjects which might give others a high opinion of them. But at Bolbeck a gentleman with light whiskers, a gold chain, and wearing two or three rings, got in, and put several parcels wrapped in oilcloth on the rack over his head. He looked inclined for a joke, and seemed a good-hearted fellow.
车厢里一有其他人以后，这些姑娘们就一副沉着的表情，为了给人一个好印象，她们开始谈论一些高雅的话题。但是到了博尔贝克，一位蓄着浅色胡须的先生上来了。他戴了一条金链和两三个戒指，他把几个漆布包裹放在头顶的行李架上。看上去，他是个爱开玩笑的人，脾气似乎很随和。
"Are you ladies changing your quarters?" he said, and that question embarrassed them all considerably. Madame Tellier, however, quickly regained her composure, and said sharply, to avenge the honor of her corps: "I think you might try and be polite!" 
“女士们，你们调换防地吗？”他说道，这句话弄得她们一个个满面羞愧，尴尬不已。但是泰利耶夫人很快镇定下来，为了挽回她们的面子，她尖锐地说道：“我想你应该讲点礼貌！”
He excused himself, and said: "I beg your pardon, I ought to have said your nunnery." 
他道歉说：“请原谅，我的意思是说修道院。”
She could not think of a retort, so, perhaps thinking she had said enough, madame gave him a dignified bow and compressed her lips.
夫人无言以对，也许她认为对方这样的回答令她满意，于是她抿着嘴，尊敬地行了个礼。
Then the gentleman, who was sitting between Rosa and the old peasant, began to wink knowingly at the ducks whose heads were sticking out of the basket, and when he felt that he had fixed the attention of his public, he began to tickle them under the bills and spoke funnily to them to make the company smile.
接着，那位先生坐到了罗莎和老农的中间，开始故意对着那三只探出脑袋的鸭子眨眼睛。他觉得自己已经吸引了周围人的注意力后，就开始伸手胳肢这几只鸭子的脖子。为了让大伙高兴，他还用滑稽可笑的声调和鸭子说话。
"We have left our little pond, quack! quack! to make the acquaintance of the little spit, qu-ack! qu-ack!” 
“咱们离开了小水塘，嘎！嘎！为的是去认识烤肉叉，嘎！嘎！”
The unfortunate creatures turned their necks away, to avoid his caresses, and made desperate efforts to get out of their wicker prison, and then, suddenly, all at once, uttered the most lamentable quacks of distress. The women exploded with laughter. They leaned forward and pushed each other, so as to see better; they were very much interested in the ducks, and the gentleman redoubled his airs, his wit and his teasing.
可怜的动物扭转脖子，躲避他的抚摸，而且拼命挣扎，想从柳条编的牢笼里逃出来。后来，这三只鸭子突然同时惨叫：“嘎！嘎！嘎！”那几个女人哄然大笑。她们都探身过去，你推我挤，想看得更清楚些；她们对鸭子很感兴趣。那位先生则加倍地献殷勤，卖弄聪明，媚态百出。
Rosa joined in, and leaning over her neighbor's legs, she kissed the three animals on the head, and immediately all the girls wanted to kiss them, in turn, and as they did so the gentleman took them on his knee, jumped them up and down and pinched their arms. The two peasants, who were even in greater consternation than their poultry, rolled their eyes as if they were possessed, without venturing to move, and their old wrinkled faces had not a smile, not a twitch.
罗莎也跟着凑合，她俯在旁边那个男人的大腿上，吻了三只鸭子的脑袋。随即，所有的姑娘都想要吻它们，而当她们轮流吻的时候，那位先生让她们坐在他的膝盖上，颠着她们，还拧她们的手臂。两个乡下人比他们的鸭子还惊慌，眼睛像着了魔似的直转，身子完全不敢动；那两张满是皱纹的脸没有一丝微笑，也没有颤动一下。
Then the gentleman, who was a commercial traveller, offered the ladies suspenders by way of a joke, and taking up one of his packages, he opened it. It was a joke, for the parcel contained garters. There were blue silk, pink silk, red silk, violet silk, mauve silk garters, and the buckles were made of two gilt metal cupids embracing each other. The girls uttered exclamations of delight and looked at them with that gravity natural to all women when they are considering an article of dress. They consulted one another by their looks or in a whisper, and replied in the same manner, and Madame Tellier was longingly handling a pair of orange garters that were broader and more imposing looking than the rest; really fit for the mistress of such an establishment.
那位先生是个旅行推销员，他开玩笑说，有背带要给她们看，于是取下一个包裹并打开。这是个玩笑话，因为包裹里装的是袜带。这些丝袜带，有蓝的、粉红的、大红的、紫色的，还有淡紫的，袜带的金属带扣是两个拥抱在一起的镀金小爱神。姑娘们都高兴地叫起来，接着仔细地看货样，流露出任何女人在研究服饰用品时都会有的自然庄重的表情。她们交换眼色，或者嘀咕一句来互相询问、商量。泰利耶夫人拿着一双橙色的袜带，爱不释手。这双袜带比其他袜带要宽些，看起来也更庄重一些，十分适合老板娘用。
The gentleman waited, for he had an idea.
那位先生等着，脑子里有了个主意。
"Come, my kittens," he said, "you must try them on." 
“来吧，我的小猫们，”他说道，“你们应该试穿一下啊。”
There was a torrent of exclamations, and they squeezed their petticoats between their legs, but he quietly waited his time and said: "Well, if you will not try them on I shall pack them up again." 
他的话引起了一片惊叫声。她们用双腿紧紧夹住衬裙。那位先生却沉住气，等待时机，然后说：“如果你们不愿意试，那我就包起来了。”
And he added cunningly: "I offer any pair they like to those who will try them on." 
接着他又狡猾地说：“谁愿意试，我就把她中意的那双送给她。”
But they would not, and sat up very straight and looked dignified.
但是她们都不愿意，都一本正经地挺直身子。
But the two Pumps looked so distressed that he renewed his offer to them, and Flora, especially, visibly hesitated, and he insisted: "Come, my dear, a little courage! Just look at that lilac pair; it will suit your dress admirably." 
但是“两个水泵”看上去可怜巴巴的，所以他又重新提议了一遍。特别是弗洛拉，明显在犹豫不决。他便催促她：“来吧，我的姑娘，拿出点勇气！瞧瞧，淡紫色的这双，正好配你的裙子。”
That decided her, and pulling up her dress she showed a thick leg fit for a milkmaid, in a badly fitting, coarse stocking. The commercial traveller stooped down and fastened the garter. When he had done this, he gave her the lilac pair and asked: "Who next?" 
于是她下定决心，撩起她的裙子，露出一条挤奶女工才有的粗腿，上面裹着松垮的劣质袜子。那位旅行推销员弯下腰，帮她系上袜带。他系好之后，就把淡紫色的那双送给了她，又问：“谁再来？”
"I! I!" they all shouted at once, and he began on Rosa, who uncovered a shapeless, round thing without any ankle, a regular "sausage of a leg," as Raphaele used to say.
“我来！我来！”她们都立即叫嚷起来，于是，他从罗莎开始。她露出一条不匀称的、圆滚滚的腿，看不见踝骨，正如拉斐尔所说，是一个不折不扣的“香肠腿”。
Lastly, Madame Tellier herself put out her leg, a handsome, muscular Norman leg, and in his surprise and pleasure, the commercial traveller gallantly took off his hat to salute that master calf, like a true French cavalier.
最后，泰利耶夫人自己也伸出腿，一条诺曼人的腿，既漂亮又强健。推销员见了，又惊又喜，像个真正的法兰西骑士那样，殷勤地脱下帽子，朝着这美腿鞠躬致敬。
The two peasants, who were speechless from surprise, glanced sideways out of the corner of one eye, and they looked so exactly like fowls that the man with the light whiskers, when he sat up, said: "Co—co—ri—co" under their very noses, and that gave rise to another storm of amusement.
两个农民目瞪口呆，只用一只眼斜瞅着，他们的那副模样活像两只鸡，引得那个蓄浅色胡须的人直坐起身来，有意地学公鸡叫道：“喔——喔——喔！”于是，又爆发了一阵暴风雨般的笑声。
The old people got out at Motteville with their basket, their ducks and their umbrella, and they heard the woman say to her husband as they went away: "They are no good and are off to that cursed place, Paris." 
两个老人带着他们的篮子、鸭子和伞在莫特维尔下车。大家听到那个妇人边走边对她的丈夫说：“这帮下贱货，她们准是去巴黎那该死的地方。”
The funny commercial traveller himself got out at Rouen, after behaving so coarsely that Madame Tellier was obliged sharply to put him in his right place, and she added, as a moral: "This will teach us not to talk to the first comer." 
那个搞笑的旅行推销员也在鲁昂下了车，下车前他的举动太粗俗了以至于泰利耶夫人不得不狠狠地教训他，叫他安分点。她还引以为戒，说：“这事叫我们明白，不能随便跟人说话。”
At Oissel they changed trains, and at a little station further on Monsieur Joseph Rivet was waiting for them with a large cart with a number of chairs in it, drawn by a white horse.
她们在瓦塞勒换车，到了下一小站的站头上，看到约瑟夫·里韦先生在那儿等着她们。他赶了一辆套着一匹白马、里面摆满椅子的大车。
The carpenter politely kissed all the ladies and then helped them into his conveyance.
木匠很有礼貌地和这些女士行礼，然后扶着她们上了马车。
Three of them sat on three chairs at the back, Raphaele, Madame Tellier and her brother on the three chairs in front, while Rosa, who had no seat, settled herself as comfortably as she could on tall Fernande's knees, and then they set off.
有三个人坐在后面的三把椅子上，拉斐尔、泰利耶夫人和她的弟弟坐在前面的三把椅子上，罗莎没有座位，将就坐在高个子费尔南德的膝盖上，接着他们就出发了。
But the horse's jerky trot shook the cart so terribly that the chairs began to dance and threw the travellers about, to the right and to the left, as if they were dancing puppets, which made them scream and make horrible grimaces.
那匹马迈着不平稳的步子跑着，使得马车摇晃得非常厉害，连椅子也开始跟着跳动起来。这些乘客们仿佛是跳动的木偶，被来回甩着，左右晃动，她们被吓得直叫，脸上还露出惊慌失措的表情。
They clung on to the sides of the vehicle, their bonnets fell on their backs, over their faces and on their shoulders, and the white horse went on stretching out his head and holding out his little hairless tail like a rat's, with which he whisked his buttocks from time to time.
她们抓住车沿，帽子落到了背上、脸上、肩膀上。那匹白马伸长脖子，一直奔跑，那条像老鼠尾巴似的无毛小尾巴直挺挺的，还不时拍打着屁股。
Joseph Rivet, with one leg on the shafts and the other doubled under him, held the reins with his elbows very high, and kept uttering a kind of clucking sound, which made the horse prick up its ears and go faster.
约瑟夫·里韦一条腿踏在车辕上，另一条腿盘在身子底下，胳膊肘高高抬起，紧握住缰绳，嗓子里不停地发出咯咯的声音，马听了竖起耳朵，加快步伐。
The green country extended on either side of the road, and here and there the colza in flower presented a waving expanse of yellow, from which arose a strong, wholesome, sweet and penetrating odor, which the wind carried to some distance.
绿油油的田野在大路两边伸展开来，到处都是大片大片的高低起伏的黄澄澄的油菜花，散发出一股浓郁的、有益健康的香味，这股香味甘甜而沁人心脾，随风传到很远很远的地方。
The cornflowers showed their little blue heads amid the rye, and the women wanted to pick them, but Monsieur Rivet refused to stop.
在一片黑麦田中，矢车菊露出天蓝色的小脑袋。姑娘们本想去摘，但是里韦先生不肯停车。
Then, sometimes, a whole field appeared to be covered with blood, so thick were the poppies, and the cart, which looked as if it were filled with flowers of more brilliant hue, jogged on through fields bright with wild flowers, and disappeared behind the trees of a farm, only to reappear and to go on again through the yellow or green standing crops, which were studded with red or blue.
有时候一整块田地像被血淹没了，原来是长满了罂粟花。在野花烂漫的原野上，白马小跑着，而那辆大车仿佛满载着更加绚丽多彩的花朵，一会儿消失在农庄的大树后面，一会儿又出现，继续穿行在由红色或蓝色点缀的黄色或绿色的田地里。
One o'clock struck as they drove up to the carpenter's door. They were tired out and pale with hunger, as they had eaten nothing since they left home. Madame Rivet ran out and made them alight, one after another, and kissed them as soon as they were on the ground, and she seemed as if she would never tire of kissing her sister-in-law, whom she apparently wanted to monopolize. They had lunch in the workshop, which had been cleared out for the next day's dinner.
到木匠家门口时，正好一点钟。她们个个筋疲力竭，饿得脸色发白。自从动身到现在，她们一点东西也没吃。里韦夫人奔过来，扶她们一个一个下车，她们脚一着地，她就亲吻她们。她看起来好像会不厌其烦地吻她的大姑子，似乎想要独占她。他们就在作坊里吃午饭，为了给第二天的筵席腾场地，作坊里的东西已经挪空了。
The capital omelet, followed by boiled chitterlings and washed down with good hard cider, made them all feel comfortable.
煎蛋卷美味可口，接着是煮熟的油炸猪小肠，她们一边吃着，一边还喝着上等的苹果酒，这让她们都感觉很舒服。
Rivet had taken a glass so that he might drink with them, and his wife cooked, waited on them, brought in the dishes, took them out and asked each of them in a whisper whether they had everything they wanted. A number of boards standing against the walls and heaps of shavings that had been swept into the corners gave out a smell of planed wood, a smell of a carpenter's shop, that resinous odor which penetrates to the lungs.
里韦喝下一杯以向大家祝酒，他的妻子在底下伺候，做菜、端菜、撤菜，还低声问每一位在场的女士：“还想再要点什么吗？”一摞摞木板靠墙放着，一堆堆刨花堆在屋角，还散发出新刨的木料香味和细木作坊常有的那种沁人心脾的树脂气味。
They wanted to see the little girl, but she had gone to church and would not be back again until evening, so they all went out for a stroll in the country.
她们本想看看那个小姑娘，但是她去教堂了，晚上才能回来。于是大伙就到附近走走。
It was a small village, through which the highroad passed. Ten or a dozen houses on either side of the single street were inhabited by the butcher, the grocer, the carpenter, the innkeeper, the shoemaker and the baker.
这是一个小村子，一条大路从中间穿过。十多所房子沿着这条仅有的街道排列着，里面住的全是当地的买卖人，有肉店老板、食品杂货店商人、木匠师傅、小旅馆老板、鞋匠师傅和面包店老板。
The church was at the end of the street and was surrounded by a small churchyard, and four immense lime-trees, which stood just outside the porch, shaded it completely. It was built of flint, in no particular style, and had a slate-roofed steeple. When you got past it, you were again in the open country, which was varied here and there by clumps of trees which hid the homesteads.
教堂在街道的一头，被一片狭小的墓地围着，门前有四株高大的欧椴树，把整个教堂都遮住了。教堂是用燧石砌的，风格没什么特别的，顶上有一个盖着板岩的尖塔。穿过教堂，又可以看到广阔的田野了，田野上分散着许多树丛，树丛里隐藏着农庄。
Rivet had given his arm to his sister, out of politeness, although he was in his working clothes, and was walking with her in a dignified manner. His wife, who was overwhelmed by Raphaele's gold-striped dress, walked between her and Fernande, and roly-poly Rosa was trotting behind with Louise and Flora, the Seesaw, who was limping along, quite tired out.
里韦虽然身穿工作服，但出于礼貌，还是让姐姐挽着他的手臂，神态威严地陪着她散步。他的妻子，被拉斐尔那镶着金丝的裙子迷住了，走在她和费尔南德中间。矮胖的罗莎、路易丝和一瘸一拐、筋疲力尽的“跷跷板”弗洛拉快步跟在后面走。
The inhabitants came to their doors, the children left off playing, and a window curtain would be raised, so as to show a muslin cap, while an old woman with a crutch, who was almost blind, crossed herself as if it were a religious procession, and they all gazed for a long time at those handsome ladies from town, who had come so far to be present at the confirmation of Joseph Rivet's little girl, and the carpenter rose very much in the public estimation.
村民们来到门口，孩子们停止了玩耍。窗帘掀起，露出了一个戴着印花布小帽的脑袋。一个拄着拐杖的老妇人，眼睛几乎瞎了，她用手划着十字，就好像面前经过的是宗教仪式的队伍。每个人都久久地盯着这些城里来的漂亮妇人。她们远道而来，为的是参加约瑟夫·里韦的小女儿的坚信礼仪式。这使得大家都对木匠刮目相看。
As they passed the church they heard some children singing. Little shrill voices were singing a hymn, but Madame Tellier would not let them go in, for fear of disturbing the little cherubs.
经过教堂时，她们听见孩子们在唱歌。尖细的嗓音在唱着圣歌，但是泰利耶夫人怕打搅这些小天使，就不让大家进去。
After the walk, during which Joseph Rivet enumerated the principal landed proprietors, spoke about the yield of the land and the productiveness of the cows and sheep, he took his tribe of women home and installed them in his house, and as it was very small, they had to put them into the rooms, two and two.
在乡间散步的过程中，约瑟夫·里韦谈到了当地有哪几个主要的地主，土地有多少收成，牲畜有多少产量。之后，他把这群女客人领回家，并安排她们住下。由于地方非常小，她们必须两个人住一间。
Just for once Rivet would sleep in the workshop on the shavings; his wife was to share her bed with her sister-in-law, and Fernande and Raphaele were to sleep together in the next room. Louise and Flora were put into the kitchen, where they had a mattress on the floor, and Rosa had a little dark cupboard to herself at the top of the stairs, close to the loft, where the candidate for confirmation was to sleep.
里韦暂时睡在作坊里的刨花堆上；他的妻子和他的姐姐睡一张床；费尔南德和拉斐尔一起睡在她们隔壁的房间。路易丝和弗洛拉被安排在厨房里，睡在就地铺上的那床褥子上。罗莎单独住在楼梯上面的一间暗黑的小屋里，紧挨着小阁楼，要接受坚信礼的小姑娘就睡在那里。
When the little girl came in she was overwhelmed with kisses; all the women wished to caress her with that need of tender expansion, that habit of professional affection which had made them kiss the ducks in the railway carriage.
小姑娘回家的时候，被接二连三的亲吻弄得不知所措。每个女人都想爱抚她，这是她们发泄情感的需要，是她们职业性的习惯，也正是这一习惯使得她们在车厢里去亲吻鸭子。
They each of them took her on their knees, stroked her soft, light hair and pressed her in their arms with vehement and spontaneous outbursts of affection, and the child, who was very good and religious, bore it all patiently.
她们每一个人都把她抱到膝盖上，轻抚着她柔软的浅色头发。在强烈的感情冲动下，她们情不自禁地把她搂在怀里。而这个很乖而又虔诚信教的孩子，则耐心地忍受着这一切。
As the day had been a fatiguing one for everybody, they all went to bed soon after dinner. The whole village was wrapped in that perfect stillness of the country, which is almost like a religious silence, and the girls, who were accustomed to the noisy evenings of their establishment, felt rather impressed by the perfect repose of the sleeping village, and they shivered, not with cold, but with those little shivers of loneliness which come over uneasy and troubled hearts.
一天下来每个人都很累了，晚餐后她们很快就去睡觉了。乡下这种充满虔诚气氛的寂静，笼罩着整个村子。那些姑娘们，过惯了妓院喧嚣的夜生活，沉睡了的乡村的沉寂让她们感觉很不自在。她们打寒颤，并不是因为冷，而是因为孤独，那是从惶恐不安的内心深处发出的孤独的寒颤。
As soon as they were in bed, two and two together, they clasped each other in their arms, as if to protect themselves against this feeling of the calm and profound slumber of the earth. But Rosa, who was alone in her little dark cupboard, felt a vague and painful emotion come over her.
她们每两个人睡一张床，一上床就紧紧地抱在一起，就像是为了不让她们自己感觉到大地那安静和深沉的沉寂。但是独自睡在小黑屋的罗莎，心里隐隐约约有种痛苦的感觉。
She was tossing about in bed, unable to get to sleep, when she heard the faint sobs of a crying child close to her head, through the partition. She was frightened, and called out, and was answered by a weak voice, broken by sobs. It was the little girl, who was always used to sleeping in her mother's room, and who was afraid in her small attic.
她在床上辗转反侧，无法入眠，忽然听见她床头隔板的那一边，有孩子微弱的呜咽声。她吓了一跳，就叫喊了一声，一个呜咽着的断断续续的声音轻轻地回应了她。原来是那个小姑娘，她向来睡在她母亲的房间里，现在独自一个人睡在小阁楼里感到很害怕。
Rosa was delighted, got up softly so as not to awaken any one, and went and fetched the child. She took her into her warm bed, kissed her and pressed her to her bosom, lavished exaggerated manifestations of tenderness on her, and at last grew calmer herself and went to sleep. And till morning the candidate for confirmation slept with her head on Rosa's bosom.
罗莎很高兴，她从床上起来，怕吵醒其他人，就蹑手蹑脚地走过去找那个小孩子。她把她带到自己暖和的床上，吻她，紧紧地把她搂在怀里，以各种夸张的方式爱抚她，最后她自己也平静了下来，睡着了。一直到天亮，那个要接受坚信礼的小姑娘都把头枕在罗莎的胸口上睡着。
At five o'clock the little church bell, ringing the Angelus, woke the women, who usually slept the whole morning long.
五点钟，教堂响起的奉告祈祷的钟声，吵醒了这些通常要睡到中午的姑娘们。
The villagers were up already, and the women went busily from house to house, carefully bringing short, starched muslin dresses or very long wax tapers tied in the middle with a bow of silk fringed with gold, and with dents in the wax for the fingers.
村民们都起来了，妇女们挨家挨户地忙碌着，有的小心谨慎地捧着像上了浆似的细布短连衣裙，有的捧着很长的蜡烛，蜡烛的腰上扎着一个镶有金线的蝴蝶结，在手握的地方还刻着凹痕。
The sun was already high in the blue sky, which still had a rosy tint toward the horizon, like a faint remaining trace of dawn. Families of fowls were walking about outside the houses, and here and there a black cock, with a glistening breast, raised his head, which was crowned by his red comb, flapped his wings and uttered his shrill crow, which the other cocks repeated.
太阳已经在蔚蓝的天空高高升起了，而地平线附近仍有一点淡红色，像是朝霞留下的痕迹。一群群的鸡在各自的窝前走来走去。不时有一只胸脯亮闪闪的黑公鸡抬起它戴红冠的头，拍打着翅膀，发出嘹亮的啼声，周围的那些公鸡也跟着叫起来。
Vehicles of all sorts came from neighboring parishes, stopping at the different houses, and tall Norman women dismounted, wearing dark dresses, with kerchiefs crossed over the bosom, fastened with silver brooches a hundred years old.
好些马车从附近的教区来了，停在各个家门口，从车上下来的是高大的诺曼妇女，她们穿着深色的裙子，胸前都搭着用古式银胸针扣住的大方巾。
The men had put on their blue smocks over their new frock-coats or over their old dress-coats of green-cloth, the two tails of which hung down below their blouses. When the horses were in the stable there was a double line of rustic conveyances along the road: carts, cabriolets, tilburies, wagonettes, traps of every shape and age, tipping forward on their shafts or else tipping backward with the shafts up in the air.
男人们则在新的双排扣长礼服或者旧的绿呢燕尾服外面罩上蓝罩衫，两条燕尾露在罩衫下面。马都牵进马厩里，沿着大路摆着两排农村的交通工具：大车、篷车、轻便二轮马车、四轮轻便马车、各种式样各种年代的双轮轻便马车，有的车头向前歪着，有的则向后歪着，车辕还悬在空中。
The carpenter's house was as busy as a beehive. The women, in dressing-jackets and petticoats, with their thin, short hair, which looked faded and worn, hanging down their backs, were busy dressing the child, who was standing quietly on a table, while Madame Tellier was directing the movements of her battalion. They washed her, did her hair, dressed her, and with the help of a number of pins, they arranged the folds of her dress and took in the waist, which was too large.
木匠的家里忙忙碌碌，像个蜂窝。几位女客人穿着短上衣和短裙，头发披在背上，又稀又短，看上去就像是褪色了并被磨损过似的。她们正忙着在泰利耶夫人的指挥下，给安静地站在桌上的孩子穿衣服。她们给她洗脸、梳头、穿衣服，还用许多别针给她的裙子打出裙褶，收紧过于宽松的腰身。
Then, when she was ready, she was told to sit down and not to move, and the women hurried off to get ready themselves.
打扮好后，她们让小姑娘坐下不许乱动，随后这些女人又赶紧去打扮自己。
The church bell began to ring again, and its tinkle was lost in the air, like a feeble voice which is soon drowned in space. The candidates came out of the houses and went toward the parochial building, which contained the two schools and the mansion house, and which stood quite at one end of the village, while the church was situated at the other.
教堂又开始敲钟了，那口小钟的叮当声很快就消失在空气中，如同微弱的人声，很快就淹没在无边无际的空间里。接受坚信礼的孩子们从家里出来，朝着教区建筑的方向走去。那儿有两所学校和村政府大楼，它们静静地坐落在村子的一头，而另一头便是那所教堂。
The parents, in their very best clothes, followed their children, with embarrassed looks, and those clumsy movements of a body bent by toil.
家长们都穿着最好的衣裳，带着不自然的神情和因常年弯腰干活而形成的那种笨拙动作，跟在孩子后面走。
The little girls disappeared in a cloud of muslin, which looked like whipped cream, while the lads, who looked like embryo waiters in a cafe and whose heads shone with pomatum, walked with their legs apart, so as not to get any dust or dirt on their black trousers.
小姑娘们淹没在一片看上去像搅得起了沫的奶油似的平纹细布里。而那些男孩子们，看起来像小小的咖啡馆伙计，头上因为抹了发油而闪闪发光，走起路来两腿叉开，生怕弄脏身上的黑裤子。
It was something for a family, to be proud of, when a large number of relatives, who had come from a distance, surrounded the child, and the carpenter's triumph was complete.
能有一大帮亲戚从远处赶来围着自己的孩子，这对一个家庭来说，是件光荣的事，因此木匠完全胜利了。
Madame Tellier's regiment, with its leader at its head, followed Constance; her father gave his arm to his sister, her mother walked by the side of Raphaele, Fernande with Rosa and Louise and Flora together, and thus they proceeded majestically through the village, like a general's staff in full uniform, while the effect on the village was startling.
由老板娘率领的泰利耶部队跟在康斯坦丝后面走着；她的父亲让他姐姐挽着胳膊，母亲和拉斐尔并肩走着，费尔南德陪着罗莎，路易丝和弗洛拉一起走。队伍庄严地穿过村子，就像穿着军服的将军的士兵。这让整个村子的人都为之震惊。
At the school the girls ranged themselves under the Sister of Mercy and the boys under the schoolmaster, and they started off, singing a hymn as they went. The boys led the way, in two files, between the two rows of vehicles, from which the horses had been taken out, and the girls followed in the same order; and as all the people in the village had given the town ladies the precedence out of politeness, they came immediately behind the girls, and lengthened the double line of the procession still more, three on the right and three on the left, while their dresses were as striking as a display of fireworks.
在学校里，女孩子们在慈光会修女的领导下排队，男孩子们则在男校长的领导下排队，然后他们唱着圣歌出发了。男孩子在前，排成两列纵队，走在两行卸了牲口的车辆中间；女孩子以相同的队形跟随着；而全体村民出于礼貌，让城里来的女士们先走，于是她们紧跟在女孩子后面，三个在左，三个在右，让那两列队伍更长了。她们的装扮如烟火一样光彩夺目。
When they went into the church the congregation grew quite excited. They pressed against each other, turned round and jostled one another in order to see, and some of the devout ones spoke almost aloud, for they were so astonished at the sight of those ladies whose dresses were more elaborate than the priest's vestments.
当她们走进教堂时，教堂里立刻就炸开了锅。教徒们一个个都转过身看她们，你推我挤的。有些虔诚的教徒甚至高声说话，因为看到这些女士们的衣裳比牧师的圣衣还要精致，他们感到十分惊奇。
The mayor offered them his pew, the first one on the right, close to the choir, and Madame Tellier sat there with her sister-in-law, Fernande and Raphaele. Rosa, Louise and Flora occupied the second seat, in company with the carpenter.
镇长把自己坐的长凳，也就是右边靠近唱诗席的第一张凳子让了出来，泰利耶夫人和她的弟媳，还有费尔南德和拉斐尔坐在了这张长凳上。罗莎、路易丝和弗洛拉在木匠的陪同下坐在后面的第二张长凳上。
The choir was full of kneeling children, the girls on one side and the boys on the other, and the long wax tapers which they held looked like lances pointing in all directions, and three men were standing in front of the lectern, singing as loud as they could.
教堂的唱诗席里跪满了孩子，女孩在一边，男孩在另一边，他们手中拿着的长蜡烛看上去像是东倒西歪的长矛。有三个人立在读经台前高声地唱着。
They prolonged the syllables of the sonorous Latin indefinitely, holding on to "Amens" with interminable "a-a's," which the reed stop of the organ sustained in a monotonous, long-drawn-out tone.
他们尽量拖长拉丁文中一些响亮的音节，唱到“阿门”的时候，“阿······”唱个不停，同时管风琴的簧管音栓也在以一种单调的无限延长的音调附和着。
A child's shrill voice took up the reply, and from time to time a priest sitting in a stall and wearing a biretta got up, muttered something and sat down again, while the three singers continued, their eyes fixed on the big book of plain chant lying open before them on the outstretched wings of a wooden eagle.
孩子的尖细的嗓音在答唱。一个坐在高坛上、头戴法冠的牧师，不时站起来，叽里咕噜地念一番，又重新坐下。那三个唱经的则继续唱着，眼睛盯着放在他们面前的那本厚厚的素歌乐谱。打开的乐谱摊在那木制雄鹰伸展的翅膀上。
Then silence ensued and the service went on. Toward the close Rosa, with her head in both hands, suddenly thought of her mother, her village church and her first communion. She almost fancied that that day had returned, when she was so small anti was almost hidden in her white dress, and she began to cry.
后来全场肃静，祭礼继续进行。此时，罗莎将额头贴在双手上，她突然想起她的母亲、村里的教堂还有她自己的首次领受圣餐仪式。她几乎以为自己又回到了那一天。她那时还是个小姑娘，整个人裹在那件雪白裙子里。她开始哭了起来。
First of all she wept silently, and the tears dropped slowly from her eyes, but her emotion in creased with her recollections, and she began to sob. She took out her pocket handkerchief, wiped her eyes and held it to her mouth, so as not to scream, but it was in vain. A sort of rattle escaped her throat, and she was answered by two other profound, heartbreaking sobs, for her two neighbors, Louise and Flora, who were kneeling near her, overcome by similar recollections, were sobbing by her side, amid a flood of tears; and as tears are contagious, Madame Tellier soon in turn found that her eyes were wet, and on turning to her sister-in-law, she saw that all the occupants of her seat were also crying.
起初哭声很轻，泪珠从她的眼睛滑了下来，但随着她不断回忆，情绪越来越激动，她开始呜咽了。为了不至于号啕大哭，她掏出手绢擦眼泪，又用手绢捂住嘴巴，可是这还是没有用。一种格格声从她的喉咙传出来，另外还有两个令人心碎的长长的呜咽声在应和着她，原来是跪在她身旁的路易丝和弗洛拉，她们也被类似的回忆压抑着。她们在她身旁哭泣着，泪如泉涌。眼泪是具有感染力的，泰利耶夫人很快也感觉眼圈湿了。她转头看了下弟媳，看到和她同坐一条长凳的人都在哭。
Soon, throughout the church, here and there, a wife, a mother, a sister, seized by the strange sympathy of poignant emotion, and affected at the sight of those handsome ladies on their knees, shaken with sobs was moistening her cambric pocket handkerchief and pressing her beating heart with her left hand.
很快，教堂里不时会有做妻子的、做母亲的或做姐姐的，出于一种奇怪的同情，也被悲伤的情绪所支配。她们看到那些漂亮女士跪在地上抽泣，哭得浑身颤抖，也变得激动起来，她们的眼泪浸湿了麻纱手绢，还不断用左手使劲地捂住怦怦直跳的胸口。
Just as the sparks from an engine will set fire to dry grass, so the tears of Rosa and of her companions infected the whole congregation in a moment. Men, women, old men and lads in new smocks were soon all sobbing, and something superhuman seemed to be hovering over their heads—a spirit, the powerful breath of an invisible and all powerful Being.
正像星星之火可以燎原一样，罗莎和她同伴的眼泪一时间感染了全体教徒。男人、女人、老人、穿着新罩衫的年轻人，一下子全都跟着哭起来。在他们的头顶仿佛盘旋着一种超人类的东西——一个灵魂，一股无形的全能者强有力的气息。
Suddenly a species of madness seemed to pervade the church, the noise of a crowd in a state of frenzy, a tempest of sobs and stifled cries. It came like gusts of wind which blow the trees in a forest, and the priest, paralyzed by emotion, stammered out incoherent prayers, without finding words, ardent prayers of the soul soaring to heaven.
突然间一种疯狂的骚动传遍了整个教堂，那是人们处在狂热状态时的喧闹声，是暴风雨般的呜咽声和压抑的叫喊声。那像是森林里刮弯树枝的一阵阵狂风。牧师激动得浑身无力，找不到合适的词句，结结巴巴，不连贯地祷告着，但这是从直冲入天堂的灵魂发出的热情的祷告。
The people behind him gradually grew calmer. The cantors, in all the dignity of their white surplices, went on in somewhat uncertain voices, and the reed stop itself seemed hoarse, as if the instrument had been weeping; the priest, however, raised his hand to command silence and went and stood on the chancel steps, when everybody was silent at once.
他背后的教徒们渐渐平静下来。那些穿着白祭披的庄严的唱经者，又开始唱起来，不过音调不够准确，连簧管音栓也似乎沙哑了，仿佛这乐器也哭过似的；这时，牧师举起双手，要大家肃静，然后他走过去，站在高坛的台阶上，大家便立即安静了下来。
After a few remarks on what had just taken place, and which he attributed to a miracle, he continued, turning to the seats where the carpenter's guests were sitting; "I especially thank you, my dear sisters, who have come from such a distance, and whose presence among us, whose evident faith and ardent piety have set such a salutary example to all. You have edified my parish; your emotion has warmed all hearts; without you, this great day would not, perhaps, have had this really divine character. It is sufficient, at times, that there should be one chosen lamb, for the Lord to descend on His flock." 
他对刚才发生的事，那被他称之为奇迹的事发表了辞说后，便转向木匠的客人坐的那两条长凳，说：“特别要感谢你们，远道而来的亲爱的姐妹们。你们的光临，你们强烈的信仰、无比的虔诚，为每个人树立了一个有益的榜样。你们感化了我的教区；你们的感情温暖了每个人的心灵；没有你们，也许这个伟大的日子就不会具有这种真正的神圣性质。有时，只要有一只优秀的羔羊，就足以促使上帝降临到羊群里来。”
His voice failed him again, from emotion, and he said no more, but concluded the service.
他再次激动得说不出话来，他没再说什么，只是宣布仪式结束。
They now left the church as quickly as possible; the children themselves were restless and tired with such a prolonged tension of the mind. The parents left the church by degrees to see about dinner.
现在大家都急着要离开教堂；小孩们精神紧张了那么长时间，他们自己也焦躁不安并且已经疲倦了。他们的父母亲为了准备午餐，也纷纷离开了教堂。
There was a crowd outside, a noisy crowd, a babel of loud voices, where the shrill Norman accent was discernible. The villagers formed two ranks, and when the children appeared, each family took possession of their own.
门外十分拥挤，闹哄哄的，一片嘈杂的喧嚷声，其中尖利的诺曼口音清晰可辨。村民形成两道人墙，等孩子们出来的时候，每个人都涌到了自己的孩子身边。
The whole houseful of women caught hold of Constance, surrounded her and kissed her, and Rosa was especially demonstrative. At last she took hold of one hand, while Madame Tellier took the other, and Raphaele and Fernande held up her long muslin skirt, so that it might not drag in the dust; Louise and Flora brought up the rear with Madame Rivet; and the child, who was very silent and thoughtful, set off for home in the midst of this guard of honor.
康斯坦丝被她家里的所有妇人抓住，她们围住她，吻她。罗莎的拥抱尤其热烈。最后，她牵着她的一只手，泰利耶夫人牵另一只手。拉斐尔和费尔南德为了不让她的细布长裙拖在地上，为她拎着那长裙；路易丝和弗洛拉则由里韦夫人陪着走在后面。这孩子默默地沉思着，在这支仪仗队的护送下朝家里走去。
Dinner was served in the workshop on long boards supported by trestles, and through the open door they could see all the enjoyment that was going on in the village. Everywhere they were feasting, and through every window were to be seen tables surrounded by people in their Sunday best, and a cheerful noise was heard in every house, while the men sat in their shirt-sleeves, drinking glass after glass of cider.
筵席摆在作坊里，用由架子撑着的长木板当桌子。家家户户都敞开大门，整个村子都弥漫着快乐的气氛。到处都在摆筵席。从每个窗口望进去，都可以看见桌子旁边坐着穿着节日盛装的人，每间屋子都传来喜悦的叫嚷声。那些只穿着衬衫的男人们一杯接着一杯地喝着苹果酒。
In the carpenter's house the gaiety maintained somewhat of an air of reserve, the consequence of the emotion of the girls in the morning, and Rivet was the only one who was in a jolly mood, and he was drinking to excess. Madame Tellier looked at the clock every moment, for, in order not to lose two days running, they must take the 3:55 train, which would bring them to Fecamp by dark.
在木匠家里，欢乐之中带着些许拘谨气氛，这是由女孩子们上午的情绪所致。只有里韦一个人兴高采烈，而且他喝酒喝得有点过量了。泰利耶夫人不停地看时间，因为她不愿意连着休业两天，她们必须赶上三点五十五分的火车，这样傍晚就可以到费康了。
The carpenter tried very hard to distract her attention, so as to keep his guests until the next day, but he did not succeed, for she never joked when there was business on hand, and as soon as they had had their coffee she ordered her girls to make haste and get ready, and then, turning to her brother, she said: "You must put in the horse immediately," and she herself went to finish her last preparations.
木匠千方百计地转移她的注意力，设法使他的客人留到次日，但他并没有成功。因为有关买卖上的事，泰利耶夫人绝不会闹着玩的。他们刚喝完咖啡，她就吩咐姑娘们快点准备，接着转身对她弟弟说：“你马上去准备套车。”她自己便去做最后的准备工作了。
When she came down again, her sister-in-law was waiting to speak to her about the child, and a long conversation took place, in which, however, nothing was settled. The carpenter's wife was artful and pretended to be very much affected, and Madame Tellier, who was holding the girl on her knee, would not pledge herself to anything definite, but merely gave vague promises—she would not forget her, there was plenty of time, and besides, they would meet again.
当她重新下楼时，她的弟媳正等着她，想跟她谈孩子的事。谈话时间很长，但是没有得出任何结论。木匠的妻子很有心计，假装非常感动，泰利耶夫人把孩子抱在膝盖上，却什么保证也不肯做，只是含糊地答应——她不会忘记小姑娘的，以后有的是时间，况且她们还会再见面。
But the conveyance did not come to the door and the women did not come downstairs. Upstairs they even heard loud laughter, romping, little screams, and much clapping of hands, and so, while the carpenter's wife went to the stable to see whether the cart was ready, madame went upstairs.
但是车子还没来，姑娘们也还没下楼。甚至还可以听见楼上有大笑声、嬉闹声、短促的叫喊声，还有许多拍手声。于是，趁木匠妻子去马厩里看车子是否准备好了的时候，泰利耶夫人也上楼去看看。
Rivet, who was very drunk, was plaguing Rosa, who was half choking with laughter. Louise and Flora were holding him by the arms and trying to calm him, as they were shocked at his levity after that morning's ceremony; but Raphaele and Fernande were urging him on, writhing and holding their sides with laughter, and they uttered shrill cries at every rebuff the drunken fellow received.
喝得烂醉的里韦，正在纠缠罗莎，她笑得都差点要窒息了。路易丝和弗洛拉由于刚参加完上午的宗教仪式，对他的轻率举动非常反感，她们抓住他的手臂，想让他平静下来；但是拉斐尔和费尔南德却在一旁怂恿他，她们笑弯了腰，乐得捧着肚子，醉汉每次下手不成功，她们就发出刺耳的尖叫声。
The man was furious, his face was red, and he was trying to shake off the two women who were clinging to him, while he was pulling Rosa's skirt with all his might and stammering incoherently.
他恼羞成怒，脸都涨红了，他一边拼命地挣脱那两个抓住他的女人，一边还使出全身力气拉罗莎的裙子，嘴里还不连贯地、结结巴巴地说着话。
But Madame Tellier, who was very indignant, went up to her brother, seized him by the shoulders, and threw him out of the room with such violence that he fell against the wall in the passage, and a minute afterward they heard him pumping water on his head in the yard, and when he reappeared with the cart he was quite calm.
可是泰利耶夫人，非常愤怒，走上前去抓住她弟弟的肩膀，把他推出去，推得很猛，以致于他撞到了走廊的墙上。一分钟后，她们听到从院子里传来他抽水冲头的声音。等他赶着马车重新出现的时候，他已经很平静了。
They started off in the same way as they had come the day before, and the little white horse started off with his quick, dancing trot. Under the hot sun, their fun, which had been checked during dinner, broke out again. The girls now were amused at the jolting of the cart, pushed their neighbors' chairs, and burst out laughing every moment.
像前一天她们来的时候一样，她们上了车，那匹小白马迈着它那摇晃的轻快步伐出发了。吃饭时克制住的愉快心情，在火辣辣的阳光下又宣泄出来了。姑娘们现在觉得车子一颠一簸很有趣，甚至还去推旁边人的椅子，她们每时每刻都在大笑。
There was a glare of light over the country, which dazzled their eyes, and the wheels raised two trails of dust along the highroad. Presently, Fernande, who was fond of music, asked Rosa to sing something, and she boldly struck up the "Gros Cure de Meudon," but Madame Tellier made her stop immediately, as she thought it a very unsuitable song for such a day, and she added: "Sing us something of Béranger's.” And so, after a moment's hesitation, Rosa began Béranger's song "The Grandmother" in her worn-out voice, and all the girls, and even Madame Tellier herself, joined in the chorus: "How I regret My dimpled arms, My nimble legs, And vanished charms." "That is first rate," Rivet declared, carried away by the rhythm, and they shouted the refrain to every verse, while Rivet beat time on the shaft with his foot, and with the reins on the back of the horse, who, as if he himself were carried away by the rhythm, broke into a wild gallop, and threw all the women in a heap, one on top of the other, on the bottom of the conveyance.
刺眼的阳光照射在田野上，使她们眼花缭乱。大路上，车轮掀起了两股尘土。不久，酷爱音乐的费尔南德要求罗莎唱点什么，罗莎就大胆地唱起了《默冬的胖神父》，但是泰利耶夫人让她立刻停止。因为她认为这首歌不适合在这个日子里唱，她还说：“给我们唱个贝朗瑞的歌吧。”于是，迟疑了一会儿之后，罗莎开始用她那嘶哑的嗓子唱起了贝朗热的《老祖母》。所有的姑娘们，甚至连泰利耶夫人，也齐声合唱起来：“我多么怀念我那肥胖的胳膊，我那灵活的大腿，和我那逝去的青春。”“棒极了！”里韦喊道。这节奏让他痴迷。每到诗节的叠句部分，她们就高声地呼喊，而里韦则会伴着节奏用脚踏着车辕，还把缰绳拍到马背上。那匹马，似乎也被这节奏迷住了，它疯狂地飞奔着，把所有的姑娘耍成了一堆，一个压着另一个，堆在马车的底部。
They got up, laughing as if they were mad, and the Gong went on, shouted at the top of their voices, beneath the burning sky, among the ripening grain, to the rapid gallop of the little horse, who set off every time the refrain was sung, and galloped a hundred yards, to their great delight, while occasionally a stone-breaker by the roadside sat up and looked at the load of shouting females through his wire spectacles.
她们一边起来，一边像疯子一样笑着。她们继续唱着歌曲，在烈日炎炎的天空下，在成熟的庄稼地里，在那匹小马飞快的步伐中，她们声嘶力竭地喊着。让她们无比兴奋的是，每唱一次叠句，那匹小马都要来个一百码的狂跑。路边时不时有个敲石头的人站起来，隔着铁丝网面罩望着这群狂吼的妇人。
When they got out at the station, the carpenter said: "I am sorry you are going; we might have had some good times together." But Madame Tellier replied very sensibly: "Everything has its right time, and we cannot always be enjoying ourselves." And then he had a sudden inspiration: "Look here, I will come and see you at Fecamp next month." And he gave Rosa a roguish and knowing look.
她们在车站前下车时，木匠说：“你们要走了真可惜，不然我们可以好好闹闹。”泰利耶夫人则通情达理地答道：“任何事情都有一定的限度，我们总不能一直玩玩乐乐。”这时，里韦突然闪过一个念头：“好，那我下个月去费康看你们。”他用不怀好意又心照不宣的眼神看着罗莎。
"Come," his sister replied, "you must be sensible; you may come if you like, but you are not to be up to any of your tricks." 
“得啦，”他姐姐说，“要规矩点；你想来就来，不过来了可不准干蠢事。”
He did not reply, and as they heard the whistle of the train, he immediately began to kiss them all. When it came to Rosa's turn, he tried to get to her mouth, which she, however, smiling with her lips closed, turned away from him each time by a rapid movement of her head to one side. He held her in his arms, but he could not attain his object, as his large whip, which he was holding in his hand and waving behind the girl's back in desperation, interfered with his movements.
他没有回答，这时火车的汽笛响了，他连忙和大家拥抱吻别。等轮到罗莎时，他拼命地想亲她的嘴唇，不过她总抿着嘴笑着，每一次都迅速地把头转向另一边，躲开了。他把她搂在怀里，但他就是达不到目的，因为他手里握着的长鞭很碍事，他一用力，长鞭就在罗莎的背后使劲摇晃。
"Passengers for Rouen, take your seats!" a guard cried, and they got in. There was a slight whistle, followed by a loud whistle from the engine, which noisily puffed cut its first jet of steam, while the wheels began to turn a little with a visible effort, and Rivet left the station and ran along by the track to get another look at Rosa, and as the carriage passed him, he began to crack his whip and to jump, while he sang at the top of his voice: "How I regret My dimpled arms, My nimble legs, And vanished charms." And then he watched a white pocket-handkerchief, which somebody was waving, as it disappeared in the distance.
“去鲁昂的旅客，请上车！”一位列车员叫着，于是她们上车了。先是一声细长的哨子声，紧接着火车头发出强劲的汽笛声，呼呼地喷出第一股蒸汽。同时，车轮开始慢慢地、明显吃力地转动了。里韦离开站台，沿着轨道跑了一下，想再看一眼罗莎。当那节车厢在他面前经过时，他开始啪啪地甩响鞭子，同时一边跳，一边使出全身力气唱着：“我多么怀念我那肥胖的胳膊，我那灵活的大腿，和我那逝去的青春。”然后他看到有人在挥动着一块白手绢，那画面渐渐消失在远方。

Part III

三

They slept the peaceful sleep of a quiet conscience, until they got to Rouen, and when they returned to the house, refreshed and rested, Madame Tellier could not help saying: "It was all very well, but I was longing to get home." 
路上她们一直睡觉，就像良心上得到安宁的人那样，睡得很踏实，直到到了鲁昂。等她们回到家里，一个个精神饱满，体力充沛。泰利耶夫人忍不住说：“不管怎么说，我早就想回家了。”
They hurried over their supper, and then, when they had put on their usual evening costume, waited for their regular customers, and the little colored lamp outside the door told the passers-by that Madame Tellier had returned, and in a moment the news spread, nobody knew how or through whom.
她们匆匆地吃了晚饭，然后换上她们常穿的晚礼服，等候老主顾上门。门口那盏有颜色的小灯点亮了，像是通知过往的行人，泰利耶夫人已经回来了。这消息马上就传开了，没有人知道是怎么传开的，也不知道是通过谁传开的。
Monsieur Philippe, the banker's son, even carried his friendliness so far as to send a special messenger to Monsieur Tournevau, who was in the bosom of his family.
银行家的儿子菲利普先生还一番好意，特地派人捎信给呆在家中的图尔纳沃先生。
The fish curer had several cousins to dinner every Sunday, and they were having coffee, when a man came in with a letter in his hand. Monsieur Tournevau was much excited; he opened the envelope and grew pale; it contained only these words in pencil: "The cargo of cod has been found; the ship has come into port; good business for you. Come immediately." 
咸鱼腌制商每逢星期天，都要请几位表兄弟吃饭，他们正在喝咖啡，突然来了一个人，手里还拿着一封信。图尔纳沃先生极其激动；他拆开信封，脸色变得煞白；信里只有这些用铅笔写的文字：“载运的鳕鱼已经找到；货船已进港；对您是桩好买卖。请速来。”
He felt in his pockets, gave the messenger twenty centimes, and suddenly blushing to his ears, he said: "I must go out." He handed his wife the laconic and mysterious note, rang the bell, and when the servant came in, he asked her to bring him has hat and overcoat immediately. As soon as he was in the street, he began to hurry, and the way seemed to him to be twice as long as usual, in consequence of his impatience.
他摸了摸口袋，掏出二十分钱给送信人。接着，他的脸一下子红到了耳根，说：“我得出去一趟。”他把那封简洁又神秘的短信递给了他的妻子，打了铃，等女仆来了以后，就让她立刻拿来他的帽子和大衣。他一到街上就跑起来，由于心急如焚，他觉得路比平常远了一倍。
Madame Tellier's establishment had put on quite a holiday look. On the ground floor, a number of sailors were making a deafening noise, and Louise and Flora drank with one and the other, and were being called for in every direction at once.
泰利耶妓馆充满了节日的气氛。在楼下，一群水手吵吵闹闹，声音震耳欲聋。路易丝和弗洛拉陪这个喝又陪那个喝，四面八方的人都同时召唤着她们。
The upstairs room was full by nine o'clock. Monsieur Vasse, the Judge of the Tribunal of Commerce, Madame Tellier's regular but Platonic wooer, was talking to her in a corner in a low voice, and they were both smiling, as if they were about to come to an understanding.
刚九点钟，楼上就满了。商事法庭法官瓦斯先生一直是泰利耶夫人的求爱者，但他是那种追求纯精神的柏拉图式求爱者。他和泰利耶夫人在角落里小声交谈，两个人都面带笑容，好像就要达到某种默契了。
Monsieur Poulin, the ex-mayor, was talking to Rosa, and she was running her hands through the old gentleman's white whiskers.
前市长普兰先生，正和罗莎聊天，她还用手抚摸着这位老先生的白胡须。
Tall Fernande was on the sofa, her feet on the coat of Monsieur Pinipesse, the tax collector, and leaning back against young Monsieur Philippe, her right arm around his neck, while she held a cigarette in her left hand.
高个子费尔南德坐在沙发上，两只脚搭在税务官皮尼佩斯的上衣上，上身则倚着年轻的菲利普，右手搂着他的脖子，左手夹着一支香烟。
Raphaele appeared to be talking seriously with Monsieur Dupuis, the insurance agent, and she finished by saying: "Yes, I will, yes." 
拉斐尔好像在认真地和保险代理商迪皮伊先生谈判，她用这句话结束了谈判：“好吧，好吧，我愿意。”
Just then, the door opened suddenly, and Monsieur Tournevau came in, and was greeted with enthusiastic cries of "Long live Tournevau!" And Raphaele, who was dancing alone up and down the room, went and threw herself into his arms. He seized her in a vigorous embrace and, without saying a word, lifted her up as if she had been a feather.
接着，门突然开了，图尔纳沃先生来了，大家热烈欢呼道：“图尔纳沃万岁！”此时，拉斐尔正独自旋转飞舞着，正好倒在他的怀里。他紧紧地搂住她，一句话也没说，就把她托起来，像托根羽毛似的。
Rosa was chatting to the ex-mayor, kissing him and puffing; both his whiskers at the same time, in order to keep his head straight.
罗莎正挑逗着前市长，不停地吻他，同时拉他的胡须，使他的脑袋保持笔直的姿势。
Fernanae and Madame Tellier remained with the four men, and Monsieur Philippe exclaimed: "I will pay for some champagne; get three bottles, Madame Tellier." And Fernande gave him a hug, and whispered to him: "Play us a waltz, will you?" So he rose and sat down at the old piano in the corner, and managed to get a hoarse waltz out of the depths of the instrument.
剩下费尔南德和泰利耶夫人陪着四位男客。菲利普先生嚷道：“我请客喝香槟；泰利耶夫人，请叫人拿三瓶来。”费尔南德搂住他，在他耳边低声说：“为我们弹段华尔兹吧，好吗？”他站起来，在角落里的老式钢琴前坐下，于是一段声音沙哑的华尔兹舞曲从钢琴里面传出。
The tall girl put her arms round the tax collector, Madame Tellier let Monsieur Vasse take her round the waist, and the two couples turned round, kissing as they danced. Monsieur Vasse, who had formerly danced in good society, waltzed with such elegance that Madame Tellier was quite captivated.
高个子姑娘搂住税务官的脖子，泰利耶夫人则由瓦斯先生搂着腰，这两对在跳着舞，旋转着，吻着。瓦斯先生以前在上流社会跳过舞，舞姿十分优美，泰利耶夫人被他深深吸引住了。
Frederic brought the champagne; the first cork popped, and Monsieur Philippe played the introduction to a quadrille, through which the four dancers walked in society fashion, decorously, with propriety, deportment, bows and curtsies, and then they began to drink.
弗雷德里克送来了香槟；第一瓶的瓶塞砰地飞了出去，菲利普先生弹奏着一首方阵舞曲的序曲。两对跳舞的人仿照上流社会的方式，彬彬有礼而又端庄恭敬地鞠躬敬礼。接着他们开始喝酒。
Monsieur Philippe next struck up a lively polka, and Monsieur Tournevau started off with the handsome Jewess, whom he held without letting her feet touch the ground. Monsieur Pinipesse and Monsieur Vasse had started off with renewed vigor, and from time to time one or other couple would stop to toss off a long draught of sparkling wine, and that dance was threatening to become never-ending, when Rosa opened the door.
菲利普先生很快又弹了一首轻快的波尔卡舞曲。图尔纳沃先生和他刚刚托起的犹太美女开始翩翩起舞了，他抱起她不让她的脚碰到地。皮尼佩斯先生和瓦斯先生又兴致勃勃地跳起来。时不时有一对人停下来，将一杯发泡的酒一饮而尽。这个舞看来要永远地跳下去了。突然，罗莎打开了门。
"I want to dance," she exclaimed. And she caught hold of Monsieur Dupuis, who was sitting idle on the couch, and the dance began again.
她喊道：“我要跳舞。”然后她抓住闲坐在沙发上的迪皮伊先生，舞蹈又重新开始了。
But the bottles were empty. "I will pay for one," Monsieur Tournevau said. "So will I," Monsieur Vasse declared. "And. I will do the same," Monsieur Dupuis remarked.
但是几瓶酒已经喝完了。“我请大家喝一瓶。”图尔纳沃说。“我也请。”瓦斯先生跟着说。“恩，我也请大家喝。”迪皮伊最后也说。
They all began to clap their hands, and it soon became a regular ball, and from time to time Louise and Flora ran upstairs quickly and had a few turns, while their customers downstairs grew impatient, and then they returned regretfully to the tap-room. At midnight they were still dancing.
大家都鼓掌欢迎。这里很快就变成了一个真正的舞会。路易丝和弗洛拉时不时匆匆跑上楼，跳几圈华尔兹舞。当楼下的客人等得不耐烦时，她们又恋恋不舍地跑回酒吧间。到了午夜，他们还在跳舞。
Madame Tellier let them amuse themselves while she had long private talks in corners with Monsieur Vasse, as if to settle the last details of something that had already been settled.
泰利耶夫人让他们各自娱乐，自己则和瓦斯先生在角落里密谈，仿佛在商榷一件已经谈妥的事情的最后细节。
At last, at one o'clock, the two married men, Monsieur Tournevau and Monsieur Pinipesse, declared that they were going home, and wanted to pay. Nothing was charged for except the champagne, and that cost only six francs a bottle, instead of ten, which was the usual price, and when they expressed their surprise at such generosity, Madame Tellier, who was beaming, said to them: "We don't have a holiday every day.” 
最后，到了一点钟，两位有家室的人，图尔纳沃先生和皮尼佩斯先生说他们得回去了，想把账结了。这次只算了香槟的钱，而且每瓶只算六法郎，平常是十法郎。他们对这样的慷慨感到十分惊讶，泰利耶夫人则满面春风地说道：“难得像今天这么高兴呀！”
