XVI  

第十六章  

Litvinov did not sleep all night, and did not undress. He was very miserable. As an honest and straightforward man, he realized the force of obligations, the sacredness of duty, and would have been ashamed of any double dealing with himself, his weakness, his fault. At first he was overcome by apathy; it was long before he could throw off the gloomy burden of a single half-conscious, obscure sensation; then terror took possession of him at the thought that the future, his almost conquered future, had slipped back into the darkness, that his home, the solidly-built home he had only just raised, was suddenly tottering about him....
李特维诺夫彻夜未眠，连衣服都没有脱。他万分痛苦。作为一个诚实直率的人，他明白义务的威力和责任的神圣。他本来是会为自己的虚伪、软弱与过失感到羞愧的。起初，他心里只有冷漠。过了好久他才摆脱那种半醉半醒、模糊灰暗的感情重担。当他想到自己的未来时，心中顿生恐惧，原本几乎成竹在胸的前途现在已经跌入黑暗之中，还有他的家，那个刚刚建立的稳固家庭也突然在他四周动摇了……
He began reproaching himself without mercy, but at once checked his own vehemence. "What feebleness!" he thought. "It's no time for self-reproach and cowardice; now I must act. Tanya is my betrothed, she has faith in my love, my honor, we are bound together for life, and cannot, must not, be put asunder." He vividly pictured to himself all Tanya's qualities, mentally he picked them out and reckoned them up; he was trying to call up feeling and tenderness in himself. "One thing's left for me," he thought again, "to run away, to run away directly, without waiting for her arrival, to hasten to meet her; whether I suffer, whether I am wretched with Tanya—that's not likely—but in any case to think of that, to take that into consideration is useless; I must do my duty, if I die for it! But you have no right to deceive her," whispered another voice within him. "You have no right to hide from her the change in your feelings; it may be that when she knows you love another woman, she will not be willing to become your wife? Rubbish! rubbish!" he answered, "that's all sophistry, shameful, double-dealing, deceitful conscientiousness; I have no right not to keep my word, that's the thing. Well, so be it… Then I must go away from here, without seeing the other..." 
他开始无情地责怪自己，但是又马上抑制了这种冲动。“真是个懦夫！”他心想，“这根本不是自责和怯懦的时候，现在我必须得行动。塔尼娅是我的未婚妻，她信任我的爱、我的忠诚，我们已经永远结合在了一起，不能也不可以被拆散。”他生动地回忆起了塔尼娅的各种品质，并在脑海里对这些品质进行挑选、计算，竭力地让自己回想起感觉与柔情。“只有一件事可做。”他又想，“离开，马上离开，不等她来就赶快去见她；无论自己是否会受折磨或是因为和塔尼娅在一起而痛苦——这是不大可能的——但无论如何，想这些，考虑这些一点用也没有；我必须履行自己的职责，即便是死也要这么做！　但是您没有权利骗她。”他心里的另外一个声音向他低语。“您没有权利向她隐瞒您变心的事实。当她知道您爱着另外一个女人时，也许她就不愿意跟您结婚了呢？胡说八道！胡说八道！” 他反驳道，“全都是诡辩、可耻的欺骗、口是心非、骗人的责任心，我无权不履行诺言，就是这样！好，就这样吧……那么，我必须得离开这里，不去见那个……”
But at that point Litvinov's heart throbbed with anguish, he turned cold, physically cold, a momentary shiver passed over him, his teeth chattered weakly. He stretched and yawned, as though he were in a fever. Without dwelling longer on his last thought, choking back that thought, turning away from it, he set himself to marveling and wondering in perplexity how he could again... again love that corrupt worldly creature, all of whose surroundings were so hateful, so repulsive to him. He tried to put to himself the question: "What nonsense, do you really love her?" and could only wring his hands in despair. He was still marveling and wondering, and suddenly there rose up before his eyes, as though from a soft fragrant mist, a seductive shape, shining eyelashes were lifted, and softly and irresistibly the marvelous eyes pierced him to the heart and a voice was singing with sweetness in his ears, and resplendent shoulders, the shoulders of a young queen, were breathing with voluptuous freshness and warmth...
但是此时李特维诺夫却是心如刀绞，他全身发凉，觉得好冷，一阵寒战刹那间袭遍他全身，牙齿也微微颤动着。他伸了下懒腰，打了个哈欠，像是得了风寒一般。他不再坚持刚刚那个想法了，打消了那个念头，并且回避它。他变得既惊讶又困惑,不明白他怎么会再次……会再次爱上那个堕落世俗的女人，连她身边的一切都是那么地让他讨厌，令他作呕。他竭力这样问自己：“简直太荒唐了，您真的爱她吗？” 但结果只能是绝望地拧自己的双手。他仍然困惑、迷茫。可是，就如同来自于轻柔芳香的薄雾，那个女人诱人的身姿突然浮现在他的眼前。她闪耀的睫毛抬起来，那双迷人的双眼直逼他的心魄，那甜美的嗓音如歌声般回荡在他的耳边，还有那美丽的双肩，就像女王的双肩一样，散发着撩人的清新和热情……
Towards morning a determination was at last fully formed in Litvinov's mind. He decided to set off that day to meet Tatyana, and seeing Irina for the last time, to tell her, since there was nothing else for it, the whole truth, and to part from her for ever.
快要天亮的时候，李特维诺夫心里最终拿定主意了。他决定当天就出发去见塔尼娅，并且去见伊琳娜最后一面，既然没有其他任何可说的，那就将整个真相告诉她，然后永远地离开她。
He set in order and packed his things, waited till twelve o'clock, and started to go to her. But at the sight of her half-curtained windows Litvinov's heart fairly failed him ... he could not summon up courage to enter the hotel. He walked once or twice up and down Lichtenthaler Allee. "A very good day to Mr. Litvinov!" he suddenly heard an ironical voice call from the top of a swiftly-moving "dogcart." Litvinov raised his eyes and saw General Ratmirov sitting beside Prince M., a well-known sportsman and fancier of English carriages and horses. The prince was driving, the general was leaning over on one side, grinning, while he lifted his hat high above his head. Litvinov bowed to him, and at the same instant, as though he were obeying a secret command, he set off at a run towards Irina's.
他把一切准备就绪，整理好行李，一直等到十二点才出发去见她。但是一看到伊琳娜那半掩着的窗户，李特维诺夫简直就心软了……他没有勇气进入旅馆。他沿着李赫登泰勒林荫道来回走了好几趟。“您好呀！李特维诺夫先生！”他突然听到一种带嘲讽味的声音从一辆疾驰的马车上传来。李特维诺夫抬眼，看见到了拉特米罗夫将军，还有坐在他旁边的M.公爵。M.公爵是个有名的运动员，同时也是英式马车和赛马的爱好者。公爵驾驶着马车，而将军靠在一边，咧嘴笑着，并把帽子高举在头顶之上。李特维诺夫向他鞠躬行礼，同时也像执行密令似的，马上跑向伊琳娜家。
She was at home. He sent up his name; he was at once received. When he went in, she was standing in the middle of the room. She was wearing a morning blouse with wide open sleeves; her face, pale as the day before, but not fresh as it had been then, expressed weariness; the languid smile with which she welcomed her visitor emphasized that expression even more clearly. She held out her hand to him in a friendly way, but absent-mindedly.
她在家中。他报上自己的姓名后，马上有人接待了他。当他进去的时候，伊琳娜正站在房间的中央。她穿着一件袖口宽大的晨衣，面容还如前天那样苍白，可是一脸倦容，不像之前那样清新。这种疲惫的笑容在她迎接来客的时候更为明显。她友善地向他伸出手，但是却有些心不在焉。
"Thanks for coming," she began in a plaintive voice, and she sank into a low chair. "I am not very well this morning; I spent a bad night. Well, what have you to say about last night? Wasn't I right?" 
“谢谢您能过来。” 她说，声音带着一丝悲伤，然后倒进一张低矮的椅子里。“今天早上我感觉不太舒服，昨天晚上没睡好。嗯，关于昨天晚上您有什么要说的吗？我没说错吧？”
Litvinov sat down.
李特维诺夫坐了下来。
"I have come to you, Irina Pavlovna," he began.
“我是过来找您的，伊琳娜·巴甫洛夫娜。”他开始说道。
She instantly sat up and turned round; her eyes simply fastened upon Litvinov.
她立即正了正身子，转过脸来，仅仅是直愣愣地盯着李特维诺夫。
"What is it," she cried. "You're pale as death, you're ill. What's the matter with you?"  
“怎么啦？”她叫喊道，“您脸色惨白，生病了吧。您是怎么啦？”
Litvinov was confused.
李特维诺夫一头雾水。
"With me, Irina Pavlovna?" 
“您是指我吗，伊琳娜·巴甫诺夫？”
"Have you had bad news? Some misfortune has happened, tell me, tell me—" 
“您知道了什么坏消息吗？肯定是有什么不好的事发生了，告诉我，赶快告诉我——”
Litvinov in his turn looked at Irina. "I have had no bad news," he brought out not without effort, "but a misfortune has certainly happened, a great misfortune ... and it has brought me to you." 
这下子轮到李特维诺夫看着伊琳娜了。“我并没有什么坏消息。” 他费劲地说，“但是确实有不幸的事发生了，很大的不幸……正是因为如此，我才过来找您的。”
"A misfortune? What is it?" 
“不幸？是什么？”
"Why ... that—" 
“啊……就是——”
Litvinov tried to go on ... and could not. He only pinched his hands together so that his fingers cracked. Irina was bending forward and seemed turned to stone.
李特维诺夫努力继续说下去……但是做不到。他只是压紧自己的双手，太过用力连手指都咯咯作响了。伊琳娜的身子向前倾，像块石头似的僵住了。
"Oh! I love you!" broke at last with a low groan from Litvinov's breast, and he turned away, as though he would hide his face.
“噢！我爱您！”李特维诺夫最后终于从胸腔里发出了这一低沉的呻吟。他转过身去了，似乎是想隐藏他的脸。
"What? Grigory Mihalitch, you"... Irina, too, could not finish her sentence, and leaning back in her chair, she put both her hands to her eyes. "You. love me?" "Yes... yes... yes," he repeated with bitterness, turning his head further and further away.
“什么？格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇，您—— ”伊琳娜也无法把话继续说下去，她靠回到椅子上，双手蒙住眼睛。“您——爱我？” “是的……是的……是的。” 他痛苦地重复着，将脸别得越来越远了。
Everything was silent in the room; a butterfly that had flown in was fluttering its wings and struggling between the curtain and the window.
房间里寂静无声。先前飞进房间的蝴蝶此时正在窗户和窗帘之间拍打着翅膀，挣扎着。
The first to speak was Litvinov. "That, Irina Pavlovna," he began, "that is the misfortune, which... has befallen me, which I ought to have foreseen and avoided, if I had not now just as in the Moscow days been carried off my feet at once. It seems fate is pleased to force me once again through you to suffer tortures, which one would have thought should not be repeated again… It was not without cause I struggled… I tried to struggle; but of course there's no escaping one's fate. And I tell you all this to put an end at once to this... this tragic farce," he added with a fresh outburst of shame and bitterness. Litvinov was silent again; the butterfly was struggling and fluttering as before. Irina did not take her hands from her face.
李维特诺夫首先打破了沉默。“那个，伊琳娜·巴甫洛夫娜。”他开始说话了，“这就是不幸……发生在我身上的不幸。假如这次没有像上次在莫斯科那样对您一见倾心、不可自拔的话，这种不幸我是可以预见并且避免的。命运之神似乎很高兴见我再次因您而受苦，谁也不会想到这种事会重演……我挣扎反抗并不是没有原因的……我努力挣扎，但是当然了，谁都逃不过命运的安排。我告诉您这些，就是想马上结束这一切……这个可悲的闹剧！” 他继续说道，羞愧和痛苦再次爆发。李特维诺夫再次沉默下来，但那窗边的蝴蝶还依然在振翅挣扎。伊琳娜没有把手从她的脸上拿开。
"And you are not mistaken?" her whisper sounded from under those white, bloodless-looking hands. "I am not mistaken," answered Litvinov in a colorless voice. "I love you, as I have never loved any one but you. I am not going to reproach you; that would be too foolish; I'm not going to tell you that perhaps nothing of all this would have happened if you yourself had behaved differently with me.... Of course, I alone am to blame, my self-confidence has been my ruin; I am deservedly punished, and you could not have anticipated it. Of course you did not consider that it would have been far less dangerous for me if you had not been so keenly alive to your wrong... your supposed wrong to me; and had not wished to make up for it... but what's done can't be undone. I only wanted to make clear my position to you; it's hard enough as it is… But at least there will be, as you say, no misunderstanding, while the openness of my confession will soften, I hope, the feeling of offense which you cannot but feel." 
“您没有弄错吧？”她微弱的声音从那白皙却毫无血色的手指间传出来。“我没有弄错。”李特维诺夫冷淡地说，“我爱您，除了您我从未爱过任何人。我是不会责怪您的，那样就太愚蠢了。我也不想说假如您以另外一种态度对我，这所有的一切都不会发生……当然了，应受到谴责的只有我，我过分的自信毁掉了我，我是罪有应得啊，您不可能预料到这一点。当然，您没有想到，如果您没有因您的错而倍感煎熬，如果您不曾误以为愧对于我，如果您不曾想弥补您的过错，那么我所面临的危险就会小很多……但事已至此，已经无法挽回了。我只是想向您说明我目前的处境，现在已经够艰难的了……但至少如您所说，我们之间不再有误解了。我希望我敞开心扉的表白能够减少您内心无法排遣的罪恶感。”
Litvinov spoke without raising his eyes, but even if he had glanced at Irina, he could not have seen what was passing in her face, as she still as before kept her hands over her eyes. But what was passing over her face meanwhile would probably have astounded him; both alarm and delight were apparent on it, and a kind of blissful helplessness and agitation; her eyes hardly glimmered under their overhanging lids, and her slow, broken breathing was chill upon her lips, that were parted as though with thirst...
李特维诺夫说话的时候眼皮都没抬一下，即便是他看一眼伊琳娜，也察觉不出她脸上表情有任何变化，因为她仍然如之前一样用手蒙住眼睛。但与此同时，她脸上的表情也许会使他无比震惊。她脸上既惊慌又欣喜的表情很明显，其中还夹杂着一种幸福的无助和激动;她的双眼在她低垂的眼帘下微微闪烁着，同时她断断续续、缓慢的喘息声将她那像是渴得离开的双唇冻得冰凉……
Litvinov was silent, waiting for, a response, some sound… Nothing!
李特维诺夫又陷入沉默，等待着她的回应，她的声音……可是什么都没有！
"There is one thing left for me," he began again, "to go away; I have come to say good-by to you." 
“我还有一件事情要说。”他又开始说道，“我要离开这里了，我是过来跟您道别的。”
Irina slowly dropped her hands on to her knees.
伊琳娜慢慢地将双手垂放在膝盖上。
"But I remember, Grigory Mihalitch," she began, "that... that person of whom you spoke to me, she was to have come here? You are expecting her?" 
“可是我记得，格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇……”她开始说道，“那个人——您曾向我提起的那个人，要来这里吧？您是在等她吧？”
"Yes; but I shall write to her... she will stop somewhere on the way... at Heidelberg, for instance." 
“是的，但是我会写信给她的……让她在途中某个地方停留的……比如说在海德堡。”
"Ah! Heidelberg… Yes… It's nice there. But all this must upset your plans. Are you perfectly certain, Grigory Mihalitch, that you are not exaggerating, et que ce n'est pas une fausse alarme?" 
“啊！海德堡……是呀……那里挺美的。但是这一切肯定会打乱您的计划。格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇，您真的非常确定您没有夸大其辞，这不是虚惊一场？”
Irina spoke softly, almost coldly, with short pauses, looking away towards the window. Litvinov made no answer to her last question.
伊琳娜近乎冷淡地轻声说着，稍稍停顿之后，她朝窗外望去。李特维诺夫没有回答她最后那个问题。
"Only, why did you talk of offense?" she went on. "I am not offended... oh, no! and if one or other of us is to blame, in any case it's not you; not you alone... Remember our last conversations, and you will be convinced that it's not you who are to blame." "I have never doubted your magnanimity," Litvinov muttered between his teeth, "but I should like to know, do you approve of my intention?" 
“只是，您为什么要提一些‘冒犯’之类的话呢？”她继续说道，“我并没有觉得被冒犯啊……噢！不！假如真的要责罚我们两之中的谁，无论如何也不是您，不单是您……想想我们俩最后的几次谈话，您就会确信这不是您的过错。”“我从未怀疑过您的宽宏大量。” 李特维诺夫喃喃自语，“但是我想知道，您是否同意我离开？”
"To go away?" 
“离开？”
"Yes." 
“是的。”
Irina continued to look away.
伊琳娜还是看着窗外。
"At the first moment, your intention struck me as premature… but now I have thought over what you have said... and if you are really not mistaken, then I suppose that you ought to go away. It will be better so... better for us both." 
“刚开始，您的这一打算确实令我震惊……但是现在我仔细考虑了您所说的话……假若您真的没有弄错的话，我想您应该离开这里。这样会更好……对我们都更好。”
Irina's voice had grown lower and lower, and her words, too, came more and more slowly. "General Ratmirov, certainly, might notice," Litvinov was beginning… 
伊琳娜的声音变得越来越低沉，话也越说越慢。“拉特米罗夫将军肯定会觉察到什么的。”李特维诺夫开始说道。
Irina's eyes dropped again, and something strange quivered about her lips, quivered and died away.
伊琳娜又再次垂下眼帘，双唇周围闪过一种奇怪的东西，渐渐消失不见。
"No; you did not understand me," she interrupted him. "I was not thinking of my husband. Why should I? And there is nothing to notice. But I repeat, separation is necessary for us both." 
“不，您还是没有明白我的意思。” 她打断了他的话，“我并不是在考虑我的丈夫。我为什么要这样做呢？没有什么好发现的。但是，我还是重复一遍，分开对我们两个来说是有必要的。”
Litvinov picked up his hat, which had fallen on the ground.
李特维诺夫捡起了掉在地上的帽子。
"Everything is over," he thought, "I must go. And so it only remains for me to say good-by to you, Irina Pavlovna," he said aloud, and suddenly felt a pang, as though he were preparing to pronounce his own sentence on himself. "It only remains for me to hope that you will not remember evil against me, and... and that if we ever—" 
“现在一切都结束了。”他想，“我得走了。那么现在我就只剩下跟您道别这件事了，伊琳娜·巴甫洛夫娜。”他大声说道，忽然感到一阵心痛，像是要准备给自己宣读审判书一样。“我只是希望您不要记恨我……而如果……如果我们能——”
Irina again cut him short.
伊琳娜再次打断了他。
"Wait a little, Grigory Mihalitch, don't say good-by to me yet. That would be too hurried." 
“等等，格里高利·米哈伊洛维奇，不要跟我道别。这也太仓促了。”
Something wavered in Litvinov, but the burning pain broke out again and with redoubled violence in his heart.
李特维诺夫的内心在踌躇着，但是那种灼热的剧痛再次爆发而且愈加强烈地灼烧着他的心。
"But I can't stay," he cried. "What for? Why prolong this torture?" 
“但是我不能留下来。”他哭喊着，“为什么？为什么还要继续折磨我？”
"Don't say good-by to me yet," repeated Irina. "I must see you once more… Another such dumb parting as in Moscow again—no, I don't want that. You can go now, but you must promise me, give me your word of honor that you won't go away without seeing me once more."  
“先不要跟我道别！”伊琳娜重复道。“我必须再见您一次……难道我们又要像在莫斯科那样默默地忍痛别离吗——不，我不要这样。您现在可以走，但是您必须得答应我，以您的名誉向我保证，没有再见我一面就决不离开。”
"You wish that?" 
“您希望这样吗？”
"I insist on it. If you go away without saying good-by to me, I shall never forgive it, do you hear, never! Strange!" she added as though to herself, "I cannot persuade myself that I am in Baden… I keep feeling that I am in Moscow… Go now." 
“我坚决要求这样。如果您就这样不辞而别，我永远都不会原谅您的！您听清楚了吗？是永远！真奇怪！”她加了一句，好像在自言自语，“我真是说服不了自己现在是在巴登……我一直觉得我还在莫斯科……您走吧。”
Litvinov got up.
李特维诺夫起身了。
"Irina Pavlovna," he said, "give me your hand." 
“伊琳娜·巴甫洛夫娜。”他说，“把您的手伸给我。”
Irina shook her head.
伊琳娜摇了摇头。
"I told you that I don't want to say good-by to you..." 
“我已经告诉过您，我不想跟您说再见……”
"I don't ask it for that." 
“我不是想要跟您说再见。”
Irina was about to stretch out her hand, but she glanced at Litvinov for the first time since his avowal, and drew it back.
伊琳娜正想把手伸出来，但是她看了李特维诺夫一眼（这是自他表白以来，伊琳娜第一次看他) 就又把手缩了回去。
"No, no," she whispered, "I will not give you my hand. No... no. Go now." 
“不，不行。”她低声说道，“我是不会跟您握手道别的。不……不！您走吧。”
Litvinov bowed and went away. He could not tell why Irina had refused him that last friendly handshake… He could not know what she feared. He went away, and Irina again sank into the armchair and again covered her face.
李特维诺夫鞠了一躬，离开了。他不明白为什么伊琳娜会拒绝这最后一次友好的握手……他不知道她在害怕什么。他走了，伊琳娜再一次无力地倒进扶手椅里，又用双手蒙住了脸。
