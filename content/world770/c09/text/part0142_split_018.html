CHAPTER XIX  

第十九章  

Novelists should never allow themselves to weary of the study of real life. If they observed this duty conscientiously, they would give us fewer pictures chequered with vivid contrasts of light and shade； they would seldom elevate their heroes and heroines to the heights of rapture, still seldomer sink them to the depths of despair; for if we rarely taste the fullness of joy in this life, we yet more rarely savour the acrid bitterness of hopeless anguish. Unless, indeed, we have plunged like beasts into sensual indulgence—abused, strained, stimulated, again overstrained, and at last destroyed our faculties for enjoyment; then, truly, we may find ourselves without support, robed of hope. Our agony is great, and how can it end? We have broken the spring of our powers. Life must be all suffering—too feeble to conceive faith; death must be darkness; God, spirits, religion can have no place in our collapsed minds, where linger only hideous and polluting recollections of vice; and time brings us on to the brink of the grave, and dissolution flings us in—a rag eaten through and through with disease, wrung together with pain, stamped into the churchyard sod by the inexorable heel of despair.
小说家们绝不应该让自己对现实生活的研究感到厌烦。如果他们认真履行了这种职责，就不会向我们呈现出那么多光明与明暗交织，对比如此鲜明的画面了。他们很少让男女主人公提升到狂喜的状态，更不会让他们陷入绝望的境地，因为如果我们很少体会到生活中充满欢乐，那就更难品尝到绝望痛苦的辛酸。的确，除非我们像野兽一样沉浸于声色犬马——肆意挥霍，强弩精神，深受刺激，过度劳累，最终毁掉了我们享乐的能力；那时我们会发现自己真的失去了支撑，丧失了希望。我们的痛苦是巨大的，然而它会怎样结束呢？我们破坏了力量的源泉。活着就是要受尽折磨——生命太虚弱而难以产生信念；死亡就是一片黑暗；上帝、精神和宗教在我们垮掉的心灵上没有立足之地，那上面只有对罪恶那丑恶肮脏的回忆；时间把我们带到坟墓的边缘，腐尸将我们裹挟进去——如同一块破布，被疾病反复咀嚼，被痛苦一次次折磨，而后被无情的绝望之蹄践踏，踩烂到教堂庭院的草皮之中。
But the man of regular life and rational mind never despairs. He loses his property—it is a blow—he staggers a moment; then his energies, roused by the smart, are at work to seek a remedy; activity soon mitigates regret. Sickness affects him; he takes patience—endures what he cannot cure. Acute pain racks him—his writhing limbs know not where to find rest; he leans on Hope's anchor. Death takes from him what he loves; roots up, and tears violently away the stem round which his affections were twined—a dark, dismal time, a frightful wrench. But some morning Religion looks into his desolate house with sunrise, and says that in another world, another life, he shall meet his kindred again. She speaks of that world as a place unsullied by sin, of that life as an era unembittered by suffering; she mightily strengthens her consolation by connecting with it two ideas—which mortals cannot comprehend, but on which they love to repose—Eternity, Immortality; and the mind of the mourner, being filled with an image, faint yet glorious, of heavenly hills all light and peace—of a spirit resting there in bliss—of a day when his spirit shall also alight there, free and disembodied—of a reunion perfected by love, purified from fear—goes out to encounter then necessities and discharges the duties of life; and, though sadness may never lift her burden from his mind, Hope will enable him to support it.
但是，生活有规律而且头脑清醒的人永远不会绝望。他失去了他的财产——这是当头一棒——他蹒跚了一会儿，然后那被痛苦所激起的能量便开始寻找拯救的方案；行动会立即减轻懊悔。疾病折磨他，他却耐心忍受着那无法治愈的疾病。剧烈的疼痛撕扯着他——扭曲的四肢不知该倚靠何处，他只能依偎在希望的铁锚上。死亡夺走了他的所爱；连根拔起并且残暴地撕裂了他情感与之共生的树干——那是一个黑暗阴沉的时刻，一种可怕的痛苦。但是一天清晨，信仰伴着朝霞探望了他荒凉的房子，说道，在另一个世界，另一个生命，他会与族亲重逢。信仰谈到那个世界是一个不曾被罪恶玷污的地方，那段生命不会受痛苦的折磨；她通过联系两种思想——两种凡人不能理解，但愿意依靠的思想——永恒与不朽极大地增强了她的安抚之意；哀悼者的内心被充满光明与安宁的天国山峦之景填满了，这景色模糊但却壮丽——一个受福佑的灵魂在那里安享——某一天他的灵魂也会升入那里，自由自在，没有羁绊——没有恐惧，只有爱的圆满的团聚——他走出去挺身面对必经的困难和生命的责任；虽然永远无法将悲伤的负担从心灵上卸下，但希望已经赋予他了承受的力量。
Well, and what suggested all this, and what is the inference to be drawn therefrom? What suggested it is the circumstance of my best pupil—my treasure—being snatched from my hands, and put away out of my reach. The inference to be drawn from it is, that being a steady, reasonable man, I did not allow the resentment, disappointment, and grief engendered in my mind by this evil chance to grow there to any monstrous size, nor did I allow them to monopolize the whole space of my heart; I pent them, on the contrary, in one strait and secret nook. In the daytime, too, when I was about my duties, I put them on the silent system; and it was only after I had closed the door of my chamber at night that I somewhat relaxed my severity towards these morose nurslings, and allowed vent to their language of murmurs; then, in revenge, they sat on my pillow, haunted my bed, and kept me awake with their long midnight cry.
那么，所有这些意味着什么，从中又该得到什么结论呢？这些意味着，我最好的学生——我的珍宝——被从我的手中夺去，并被抛到了我无从寻找的地方。从中可以得到的结论是，作为一个沉着稳重、通情达理的人，我既不能让怨恨、失望和悲伤趁虚而入，在我的心里扎根长成参天大树，也不能让它们霸占我的整个心灵；相反，我要将它们囚禁在一个狭窄而隐秘的角落里。白天工作时，我让它们沉默安静；只有到夜晚关上自己的房门后，我才稍稍放松对这些忧郁婴儿的严格管教，允许它们窃窃私语地发泄；于是，为了报复我，它们就坐在我的枕上，缠在我的床边，用幽长的夜半哭声吵得我无法入眠。
A week passed. I had said nothing more to Mdlle. Reuter. I had been calm in my demeanour to her, though stony cold and hard. 'Mademoiselle, will you have the goodness to give me the address of Frances Evans Henri?' 
一个星期过去了。我没有跟勒泰小姐说过一句话。尽管我对她没什么过激的举动，但态度冷酷无情，而且生硬。“小姐，您能否发发慈悲将弗朗西斯·埃文斯·亨利的住址给我？”
A little surprised, but not disconcerted, she smilingly disclaimed any knowledge of that address, adding, 'Monsieur has perhaps forgotten that I explained all about that circumstance before—a week ago?' 
她有些吃惊，但并没有慌乱，她微笑着说自己并不知道那个地址，还补充道：“先生难道忘了，关于这件事情我之前已经向您解释过了——就在一个星期之前？”
'Mademoiselle,' I continued, 'you would greatly oblige me by directing me to that young person's abode.' 
“小姐，”我接着说道，“如果您能告知我那个年轻人的住处，我将不胜感激。”
She seemed somewhat puzzled; and at last, looking up with an admirably counterfeited air of naivete, she demanded 'Does monsieur think I am telling an untruth?' 
她似乎有点迷惑，但是最终以伪装得十分完美的天真表情问道：“难道先生认为我在说谎吗？”
Still avoiding to give her a direct answer, I said, 'It is not, then, your intention, mademoiselle, to oblige me in this particular?' 
我还是避免正面回答她的问题，说道：“小姐，您的意思是您并不打算在这件事上帮我这个忙了？”
'But, monsieur, how can I tell you what I do not know?' 
“但是，先生，我怎么能告诉您我不知道的事情呢？”
'Very well. I understand you perfectly, mademoiselle, and now I have only two or three words to say. This is the last week in July; in another month the vacation will commence. Have the goodness to avail yourself of the leisure it will afford you to look out for another English master. At the close of August I shall be under the necessity of resigning my post in your establishment.' 
“非常好。我完全明白您的意思，小姐，现在我只想说两三句话。这是七月的最后一个星期，下个月假期就要开始了。劳烦您最好利用假期时间再找一位英语老师。在八月底我将不得已辞去在贵校的职位。”
I did not wait for her comments on this announcement, but bowed and immediately withdrew.
我没有等她对我的话发表评论便鞠了个躬，立即离开了。
That same evening, soon after dinner, a servant brought me a small packet. It was directed in a hand I knew, but had not hoped so soon to see again. Being in my own apartment and alone, there was nothing to prevent my immediately opening it. It contained four five-franc pieces, and a note in English.
当天晚上晚饭过后不久，一位仆人给了我一个小包裹。我很熟悉上面的笔迹，但是没想到这么快又见到它。独处在自己的公寓时，我迫不及待地打开了它。那里面有四枚五法郎的硬币和一张用英文写的便条。
'Monsieur,—I came to Mdlle. Reuter's house yesterday at the time when I knew you would be just about finishing your lesson, and I asked if I might go into the schoolroom and speak to you.
“先生，我昨天去了勒泰小姐房间，我知道当时您快要下课了，所以我就问我能否到教室跟您说几句话。
Mdlle. Reuter came out and said you were already gone. It had not yet struck four, so I thought she must be mistaken, but concluded it would be vain to call another day on the same errand. In one sense a note will do as well. It will wrap up the twenty francs, the price of the lessons I have received from you; and if it will not fully express the thanks I owe you in addition—if it will not bid you good-bye as I could wish to have done—if it will not tell you, as I long to do, how sorry I am that I shall probably never see you more—why, spoken words would hardly be more adequate to the task. Had I seen you, I should probably have stammered out something feeble and unsatisfactory—something belying my feelings rather than explaining them; so it is perhaps as well that I was denied admission to your presence. You often remarked, monsieur, that my devoirs dwelt a great deal on fortitude in bearing grief; you said I introduced that theme too often. I find indeed that it is much easier to write about a severe duty than to perform it, for I am oppressed when I see and feel to what a reverse fate has condemned me. You were kind to me, monsieur—very kind. I am afflicted—I am heart-broken to be quite separated from you; soon I shall have no friend on earth. But it is useless troubling you with my distresses. What claim have I on your sympathy? None. I will then say no more. —Farewell, monsieur.
勒泰小姐出来说您已经走了。那时还没到四点，所以我想她肯定弄错了，但是我想就同一件事改天再来拜访一次也徒劳无益。从某种意义上说留一张便条也是一样的。里面有二十法郎，是我上您的课应缴的费用；如果它不足以表达我对您的感谢，我会日后再补上——如果它不能如我所愿地向您告别——如果它不能如我长期所盼望的那样表达我可能再也见不到您的遗憾之情——那么，当面谈也无法充分表达我的心意。如果能见到您，或许我也只是结结巴巴地说一些软弱无力，无法令人满意的话——那些话只会掩饰我的情感，而不是解释它们；所以，也许我没能见到您倒也好。先生，您经常说，我的习作总是围绕着隐忍伤痛的坚韧来写，您说这个主题我写得太多了。我发现事实上去描写一种严苛的职责要比去经历它容易得多，因为每当我明白并感受到颠倒的命运对我的谴责时，我就感到十分压抑。先生，您对我很好——非常好。我很痛苦——与您分别令我极度伤心，不久我在这世上就彻底没有朋友了。但是为了我的痛苦去叨扰您是没有用的。我有什么权利要求您的同情呢？没有。我应就此罢笔。——再见，先生。
F. E. Henri.
F．E．亨利。”
I put up the note in my pocket-book; I slipped the five-franc pieces into my purse; then I took a turn through my narrow chamber.
我将这张便条夹在我的小笔记本里，将这几枚五法郎硬币塞进钱包里，然后便在我狭小的房间里转起圈来。
'Mdlle. Reuter talked about her poverty,' said I, 'and she is poor; yet she pays her debts and more. I have not yet given her a quarter's lessons, and she has sent me a quarter's due. I wonder of what she deprived herself to scrape together the twenty francs. I wonder what sort of a place she has to live in, and what sort of a woman her aunt is, and whether she is likely to get employment to supply the place she has lost. No doubt she will have to trudge about long enough from school to school, to inquire here and apply there, she rejected in this place, disappointed in that. Many an evening she'll go to her bed tired and unsuccessful. And the directress would not let her in to bid me good-bye? I might not have the chance of standing with her for a few minutes at a window in the schoolroom and exchanging some half-dozen of sentences—getting to know where she lived—putting matters in train for having all things arranged to my mind? No address on the note,' I continued, drawing it again from the pocket-book and examining it on each side of the two leaves. 'Women are women that is certain, and always do business like women. Men mechanically put a date and address to their communications. And these five-franc pieces' (I hauled them forth from my purse)—‘if she had offered me them herself instead of tying them up with a thread of green silk in a kind of Lilliputian packet, I could have thrust them back into her little hand, and shut up the small, taper fingers over them—so—and compelled her shame, her pride, her shyness, all to yield to a little bit of determined will. Now where is she? How can I get at her?' 
“勒泰小姐说过她的贫困，”我自言自语道，“她确实穷，但是她付了学费，而且还多付了。我还没有教够她一个季度的课程，她却给了我一个季度的报酬。我想知道她为了存够这二十法郎是怎么省吃俭用的。我想知道她住在什么样的地方，她的姑妈是个什么样的人，还想知道她是否能找到差事来替代所失去的这份工作。毋庸置疑，她不得不整日长途跋涉，从一所学校到另一所学校，这里问问那里找找，不是被这里拒绝就是对那里感到失望。很多个夜晚她会疲惫不堪，满心沮丧地爬上床。而校长甚至不肯让她来跟我道个别？我就不能有个机会跟她在教室的窗口前站上几分钟，说上几句话——弄清楚她住在哪里——搞清楚事情的来龙去脉，好让我心里也有个交待？便条上也没有地址。”想到这儿，我从小笔记本里又拿出它，把那两页纸翻来覆去看了又看。“女人终究还是女人，她们总是以女人的方式做事。男人写信都会顺手写上日期和地址。这几个硬币，”（我把它们从钱包里扒出来）——“如果她是亲手将它们交给我，而不是用绿丝带把它们扎在小人国似的袋子里，我就能够把钱塞回她的小手里，然后合上她那纤纤玉指——就这样——她的惭愧、骄傲、害羞，最后都不得不服从于我坚定的意志。她现在到底在哪儿？我怎么才能找到她？”
Opening my chamber door, I walked down into the kitchen.
我打开房间门，走到楼下的厨房。
'Who brought the packet?' I asked of the servant who had delivered it to me.
“谁送这个包裹过来的？”我问那个递给我包裹的仆人。
'Un petit commissionaire, monsieur.' 
“一个小信差，先生。”
'Did he say anything?' 
“他说什么了吗？”
'Rien.' 
“没有。”
And I wended my way up the back stairs, wondrously the wiser for my inquiries.
我便走回楼上，以便能好好思考这些事。
'No matter,' said I to myself, as I again closed the door—'no matter; I'll seek her through Brussels.' 
“没关系，”我再次关上房门自言自语道，“没关系，就算走遍布鲁塞尔我也要找到她。”
And I did. I sought her day by day, whenever I had a moment's leisure, for four weeks. I sought her on Sundays all day long; I sought her on the boulevards, in the Allée Verte, in the Park; I sought her in St. Gudule and St. Jacques; I sought her in the two Protestant chapels; I attended these latter at the German, French, and English services, not doubting that I should meet her at one of them. All my researches were absolutely fruitless; my security on the last point was proved by the event to be equally groundless with my other calculations. I stood at the door of each chapel after the service, and waited till every individual had come out scrutinizing every gown draping a slender form, peering under every bonnet covering a young head. In vain. I saw girlish figures pass me, drawing their black scarfs over their sloping shoulders, but none of them had the exact turn and air of Mdlle. Henri's. I saw pale and thoughtful faces encadrees in bands of brown hair, but I never found her forehead, her eyes, her eye- brows. All the features of all the faces I met seemed frittered away, because my eye failed to recognize the peculiarities it was bent upon—an ample space of brow and a large dark, and serious eye, with a fine but decided line of eyebrow traced above.
我真的这样做了。只要我有一小会的空闲时间，我便去找她，日复一日，找了四个星期。每逢周日我便整日地找她；我到大路上找，到林阴小道上找，到公园找；我到圣居迪勒教堂和圣雅克教堂找；到两所基督教礼拜堂找；到那两个专供德国人、法国人和英国人做礼拜的基督教堂找，我会在其中一个教堂遇见她，对此我从未怀疑过。我的所有找寻都是徒劳无功；我确定我会在那两个礼拜堂见到她，但这想法和我其他的估计一样，被事实证明是没有根据的。每当礼拜结束时，我便站在两个礼拜堂的门口，直到每个人都出来，我认真地审视着每件长袍盖住的苗条身姿，窥视着每个圆帽遮盖下的年轻脑袋。还是白费力气。我看着一个个少女走过我的身边，将她们的黑色披肩拉下来盖住倾斜的双肩，但是没有一个人有亨利小姐那样的气质和风度。我看着一张张苍白而又深思的脸蛋，配着褐色头发；但是我从来没有见到亨利小姐那样的前额，那样的双眼，那样的眉毛。我所见的所有的面部特征似乎都渐渐消失，因为我的眼睛没能找到我想要看到的特质——宽阔的眉间，大眼睛黝黑而严肃，以及眼睛上那线条分明的精致眉毛。
'She has probably left Brussels—perhaps is gone to England, as she said she would,' muttered I inwardly, as on the afternoon of the fourth Sunday I turned from the door of the chapel-royal, which the doorkeeper had just closed and locked, and followed in the wake of the last of the congregation, now dispersed and dispersing over the square. I had soon outwalked the couples of English gentlemen and ladies. (Gracious goodness! why don't they dress better? My eye is yet filled with visions of the high-flounced, slovenly, and rumbled dresses in costly silk and satin, of the large unbecoming collars in expensive lace, of the ill-cut coats and strangely fashioned pantaloons which every Sunday, at the English service, filled the choirs of the chapel-royal, and after it issuing forth into the square, came into disadvantageous contrast with freshly and trimly attired foreign figures, hastening to attend salut at the church of Coburg.) I had passed these pairs of Britons, and the groups of pretty British children, and the British footmen and waiting-maids; I had crossed the Place Royale, and got into the Rue Royale; thence I had diverged into the Rue de Louvain, an old and quiet street. I remember that, feeling a little hungry, and not desiring to go back and take my share of the gouter, now on the refectory table at Pelet's—to wit, pistolets and water—I stepped into a baker's and refreshed myself on a couc—it is a Flemish word, I don't know how to spell it—A Corinthe—anglice, a currant bun—and a cup of coffee; and then I strolled on towards the Porte de Louvain.
“很有可能她已经离开布鲁塞尔了——也许她去英国了，因为她说过她会去。”我心里自语道，在第四个周日的下午，我离开了皇家礼拜堂的大门，刚转过身，看门人就关门上锁，我跟在最后一批信徒后面。现在他们在广场上渐渐散开了。很快我就超过了那一对对英国男女们。（天啊！他们就不能穿得好看点吗？我满眼尽是用名贵绸缎做成的镶着多层荷叶边的邋遢衣服，尽是绣着昂贵花边的不得体的大领子，尽是裁剪糟糕的外套和形状古怪的裤子；每个周日英国人做礼拜时，皇家礼拜堂唱诗班都穿这种衣服，祈祷结束后，他们又涌入广场，与那些匆匆赶来参观科堡大教堂的外国人清新整洁的打扮形成鲜明对比，实在是有损英国人的形象。）我超过了这一对对英国人，超过了一群群可爱的英国小孩，超过了一个个英国的男侍从和女仆人；我穿过了皇室住宅区，来到了国王大街，从那里拐进了卢汶街，一条古老寂静的街道。我感到有点饿，现在才想起摆在珀莱家餐桌上的茶点——小奶油面包和水——但是我又不想回去，于是走进一家面包店，要了一份充饥的couc——这是一个佛兰芒词，我不知道怎么拼它——是科林斯语加英语构成的，意思就是一种内有葡萄干的圆形甜面包——还有一杯咖啡；吃完后我又朝卢汶门慢慢走去。
Very soon I was out of the city, and slowly mounting the hill, which ascends from the gate, I took my time; for the afternoon, though cloudy, was very sultry, and not a breeze stirred to refresh the atmosphere. No inhabitant of Brussels need wander far to search for solitude; let him but move half a league from his own city, and he will find her brooding still and blank over the wide fields, so drear though so fertile, spread out treeless and trackless round the capital of Brabant. Having gained the summit of the hill, and having stood and looked long over the cultured but lifeless campaign, I felt a wish to quit the highroad, which I had hitherto followed, and get in among those tilled grounds—fertile as the beds of a Brobdignagian kitchen-garden—spreading far and wide even to the boundaries of the horizon, where from a dusk green distance changed them to a sullen blue, and confused their tints with those of the livid and thunderous-looking sky. Accordingly I turned up a by-path to the right. I had not followed it far ere it brought me, as I expected, into the fields, amidst which, just before me, stretched a long and lofty white wall enclosing, as it seemed from the foliage showing above, some thickly planted nursery of yew and cypress, for of that species were the branches resting on the pale parapets, and crowding gloomily about a massive cross, planted doubtless on a central eminence and extending its arms, which seemed of black marble, over the summits of those sinister trees. I approached, wondering to what house this well-protected garden appertained; I turned the angle of the wall, thinking to see some stately residence. I was close upon great iron gates. There was a hut serving for a lodge near; but I had no occasion to apply for the key—the gates were open. I pushed one leaf back—rain had rusted its hinges, for it groaned dolefully as they revolved. Thick planting embowered the entrance. Passing up the avenue, I saw objects on each hand which, in their own mute language of inscription and sign, explained clearly to what abode I had made my way. This was the house appointed for all living; crosses, monuments, and garlands of everlastings announced, 'The Protestant Cemetery, outside the gate of Louvain.' 
我很快便走出了市区，慢慢登着山，那山从城门外就开始向上倾斜了，我不慌不忙地登着；因为是下午，所以虽然多云，但仍很闷热，没有一丝风来让空气清新一下。布鲁塞尔居民无须走太远去寻求宁静，只要走到城外半里格，他就会发现这座城正静静地、茫然地凝望着广阔的田野沉思，环绕着布拉班特首都的田野虽然肥沃，但也很沉闷，放眼望去，没有树木，也没有道路。我登上山顶，驻足良久，俯瞰着那虽已开垦却了无生气的田野，顿时有一种想法，就是离开来时走的公路踏进那些耕地——它们如同布罗布丁纳格菜地的菜一样肥沃——耕地向前方延伸开来，遥远而宽阔，直到地平线，它的颜色也由深绿变成暗蓝，与暴雨之前的铅灰色天空融为一体；于是，我拐入了右边上的一条岔道。没走多久，这小道就正如我所预料的那样把我带进了农田，一座高耸且长的白墙立在我面前的农田之中，从墙上方露出的叶子可以猜出，里面是一些种植着茂密的紫杉和柏树的苗圃，因为那些搭在白色低矮围墙上的正是这两种树的树枝。这些树阴沉地环绕着一个无疑是矗立在围墙中央的巨大十字架，同时十字架展开的黑色大理石双臂又凌驾在那些不祥的树冠之上。我向前走去，想知道这个守卫森严的花园是谁家的；我转过墙角，心想该会看到庄严宏伟的住宅。我来到大铁门前。那边有一间用作门房的小屋；不过我不需要钥匙——大门是开着的。我推开一扇门——门上的铰链已经被雨腐蚀了，因而门转动时铰链发出阴沉的吱吱声。入口被浓密的植物遮蔽着。我穿过林阴大道，看到在左右两边到处刻着铭文和标志，这些清楚地告诉我自己来到了什么地方。这是个为所有活着的人准备的房间，那些十字架，墓碑还有石刻花圈都说明这里是“卢汶门外基督教公墓”。
The place was large enough to afford half an hour's strolling without the monotony of treading continually the same path; and for those who love to peruse the annals of graveyards here was variety of inscription enough to occupy the attention for double or treble that space of time. Hither people of many kindreds, tongues, and nations had brought their dead for interment; and here, on pages of stone, of marble, and of brass, were written names, dates, last tributes of pomp or love, in English, in French, in German, and Latin. Here the Englishman had erected a marble monument over the remains of his Mary Smith or Jane Brown, and inscribed it only with her name. There the French widower had shaded the grave of his Elmire or Celestine with a brilliant thicket of roses, amidst which a little tablet rising bore an equally bright testimony to her countless virtues. Every nation, tribe, and kindred mourned after its own fashion; and how soundless was the mourning of all! My own tread, though slow and upon smooth-rolled paths, seemed to startle, because it formed the sole break to a silence, otherwise total. Not only the winds but the very fitful, wandering airs, were that afternoon, as by common consent, all fallen asleep in their various quarters: the north was hushed, the south silent, the east sobbed not, nor did the west whisper. The clouds in heaven were condensed and dull, but apparently quite motionless. Under the trees of this cemetery nestled a warm, breathless gloom, out of which the cypresses stood up straight and mute, above which the willows hung low and still; where the flowers, as languid as fair, waited listless for night-dew or thunder-shower; where the tombs, and those they hid, lay impassible to sun or shadow, to rain or drought.
这个地方很大，即使不走回头路也足够在里面转上半个小时；而对于那些喜欢研究墓场年鉴的人来说，这些各种各样的碑文足够他花上两到三倍的时间去研究。这里埋葬着不同血统、不同语言、不同国家的人；在那一块块用石头、大理石或者青铜做成的墓碑上，分别用英语、法语、德语、拉丁语刻着那些长眠者的姓名、生卒年月、以及献给他们的最后歌颂或敬爱之词。这里有一个英国人在玛丽·史密斯或者简·布朗的遗体上竖了一个大理石墓碑，上面仅仅刻着她的名字。在那边一个法国鳏夫在他的爱弥尔或塞莱斯蒂纳的坟前堆满了绚丽的玫瑰花丛，花丛中立着的碑上写着同样绚丽的话语，作为对她诉说不尽的美德的见证。每个国家，每个部落，每个家族都以自己的方式哀悼死者，而所有哀悼都如此无声无息！尽管小路的起伏很流畅，我自己的步子也放得很慢，但是脚步声听起来仍触目惊心，因为它是墓场肃杀气氛的唯一破坏者，或者说，全部破坏者。那天下午像是达成共识一般，不只是风，就连一阵阵飘荡的气流都躲到各处睡觉了：北边一片肃静，南边一片沉静，东边十分安静，西边也不再低语，沉静了。天上的云像被挤压了，毫无光彩，但也显然一动不动。公墓的树下萦绕着一种闷热且令人窒息的阴暗，沉默的柏树冲破了这阴暗，笔直耸立着，阴暗上方的柳枝也纹丝不动低垂着；花儿们懒散无力，无精打采地等着夜露或者雷雨；只有那些树阴下的坟墓及其掩盖的长眠者们无动于衷地躺在那里，不理会是艳阳还是阴暗，是雨水还是干旱。
Importuned by the sound of my own footsteps, I turned off upon the turf, and slowly advanced to a grove of yews. I saw something stir among the stems. I thought it might be a broken branch swinging—my short-sighted vision had caught no form, only a sense of motion—but the dusky shade passed on, appearing and disappearing at the openings in the avenue. I soon discerned it was a living thing, and a human thing. And drawing nearer, I perceived it was a woman, pacing slowly to and fro, and evidently deeming herself alone as I had deemed myself alone, and meditating as I had been meditating. Ere long she returned to a seat which I fancy she had but just quitted, or I should have caught sight of her before. It was in a nook, screened by a clump of trees. There was the white wall before her, and a little stone set up against the wall, and at the foot of the stone was an allotment of turf freshly turned up—a new- made grave. I put on my spectacles, and passed softly close behind her; glancing at the inscription on the stone, I read, 'Julienne Henri, died at Brussels, aged sixty, August 10th, 18--.' Having perused the inscription, I looked down at the form sitting bent and thoughtful just under my eyes, unconscious of the vicinity of any living thing. It was a slim, youthful figure in mourning apparel of the plainest black stuff, with a little, simple black crape bonnet. I felt, as well as saw, who it was; and moving neither hand nor foot, I stood some moments enjoying the security of conviction. I had sought her for a month, and had never discovered one of her traces, never met a hope or seized a chance of encountering her anywhere. I had been forced to loosen my grasp on expectation, and, but an hour ago, had sunk slackly under the discouraging thought that the current of life and the impulse of destiny had swept her for ever from my reach; and, behold, while bending sullenly earthward beneath the pressure of despondency—while following with my eyes the track of sorrow on the turf of a graveyard—here was my lost jewel dropped on the tear-fed herbage, nestling in the mossy and mouldy roots of yew trees.
因为不愿再让自己的脚步声打破这宁静，我离开小道走上了草地，慢慢地向一片紫杉林迈进。我看见有个东西在林中晃动。我原以为那可能是一根断枝在摆动——我眼睛近视，没看清楚形状，只是感觉到它在动弹——但那黑黝黝的形状在林荫道的空旷处若隐若现。不久我便发现那个东西是有生命的，而且是个人。直到靠近点，我才发现是个女人在那里慢慢地走来走去，而且显然和我一样以为自己是一个人在这儿，也和我一样在这里沉思。不久她便回到一个座位上，我猜想她刚刚才从那边站起来，或者说我应该刚刚就看到她在那坐着了。那是在一个角落里，被一丛丛的树掩蔽着。她面前是那座白墙，靠墙竖立着一块小石碑，石碑底下的草地是刚翻过的——这是一座新坟。我带上眼镜，轻轻地贴着她身后走过，瞥了一眼石头上刻的字，写道：“朱利安娜·亨利，卒于布鲁塞尔，享年六十，一八几几年八月十日。”细读完这些碑文，我把目光转向弯腰坐在我眼前思索的身影，她竟然对我这个近在咫尺的生命体毫无感觉。那是一个身材修长，十分年轻的身影，身穿最普通的黑布孝服，头戴一顶小巧简单的黑色孝帽。我感觉到，也看出来那是谁了；我在那站了一阵，从头到脚一动不动，享受着这种认出她的喜悦。我已经找了她一个月，从没发现过她的一丝行踪，我以为没有任何希望任何机会再见到她了。仅仅是一个小时前，我已经被迫放弃希望了，已经无力地坠入了令人沮丧的念头：生活的洪流与命运的冲力已将她永远地卷离我的身边；但没想到，当我被消沉的意志压得低下头——就在我用目光寻找墓场草皮上的悲哀脚印之时——我却在这找到了我丢失的珍宝，它就落在用眼泪灌溉的草地上，倚在生满苔藓腐烂发霉的杉树根下。
Frances sat very quiet, her elbow on her knee and her head on her hand. I knew she could retain a thinking attitude a long time without change. At last a tear fell. She had been looking at the name on the stone before her, and her heart had no doubt endured one of those constrictions with which the desolate living, regretting the dead, are at times so sorely oppressed. Many tears rolled down, which she wiped away again and again with her handkerchief; some distressed sobs escaped her, and then, the paroxysm over, she sat quiet as before. I put my hand gently on her shoulder; no need further to prepare her, for she was neither hysterical nor liable to fainting fits. A sudden push, indeed, might have startled her, but the contact of my quiet touch merely woke attention as I wished; and though she turned quickly, yet so lightning-swift is thought—in some minds especially—I believe the wonder of what, the consciousness of who it was that thus stole unawares on her solitude, had passed through her brain and flashed into her heart even before she had effected that hasty movement; at least Amazement had hardly opened her eyes and raised them to mine ere Recognition informed their irids with most speaking brightness. Nervous surprise had hardly discomposed her features ere a sentiment of most vivid joy shone clear and warm on her whole countenance. I had hardly time to observe that she was wasted and pale, ere called to feet a responsive inward pleasure by the sense of most full and exquisite pleasure glowing in the animated flush, and shining in the expansive light, now diffused over my pupil's face. It was the summer sun flashing out after the heavy summer shower; and what fertilizes more rapidly than that beam, burning almost like fire in its ardour?
弗朗西斯十分安静地坐在那里，胳膊撑在膝盖上，手托着脸。我知道她会长时间坐在那一动不动，一直思考着问题。最终一滴泪水夺眶而出。她一直盯着面前石碑上的名字，内心无疑一直忍受着那种生者追忆故人时所感到的被遗弃的痛苦。泪水不停滚落，她用手帕擦了又擦，却悲伤地呜咽起来，最后在一阵伤心的痛哭后，她再次安静地坐在那。我轻轻地将手搭在她的肩膀上，没有必要让她多做准备，因为她既不会歇斯底里，也不会吓昏到不省人事。我猛地推她一下，确实会使她大吃一惊；但是正如我想象的那样，我静静地碰碰她，仅仅唤起了她的注意，尽管她快速转过脸来——尤其，在某些人看来像是闪电一样快——但是我相信，在她做出那个突然的动作之前，她的脑海里已经闪过究竟是什么人偷偷闯进她的孤独，甚至已经闪入她的心房，至少，在她没有因认出我而眼中闪着最富表情的亮光之前，这惊讶没有使她睁开眼睛来看我。在她整张脸上露出清晰、热情、最生动的喜悦前，她并没有因为紧张而显得吃惊不已。我几乎没时间观察她那憔悴苍白的神色，便看见她整个脸上都泛起强烈的喜悦的光芒，涌出片片红晕，焕发着光芒，没等我对此表示由衷的高兴，那红晕已经在我学生的脸上蔓延开了。这是沉闷的夏天阵雨之后露出的第一缕阳光，还有什么比这束阳光发展得更快呢，那简直是像炽烈燃烧的火焰！
I hate boldness—that boldness which is of the brassy brow and insensate nerves; but I love the courage of the strong heart, the favour of the generous blood. I loved with passion the light of Frances Evans's clear haze eye when it did not fear to look straight into mine. I loved the tones with which she uttered the words,— 
我讨厌冒失——那种厚颜无耻的眉目传情和毫无情感的神经质的冒失；但是我喜欢坚强心灵的勇气，喜欢那种大方开朗的热情。我十分喜欢弗朗西斯·埃文斯那清澈的淡褐色眼睛毫不畏惧地直视我的眼睛时所闪烁的光芒。我喜欢她叫我时的声调：
'Mon maître! mon maître!' 
“老师！老师！”
I loved the movement with which she confided her hand to my hand. I loved her as she stood there, penniless and parentless; for a sensualist charmless, for me a treasure—my best object of sympathy on earth, thinking such thoughts as I thought, feeling such feelings as I felt; my ideal of the shrine in which to seal my stores of love; personification of discretion and forethought, of diligence and perseverance, of self-denial and self-control—those guardians, those trusty keepers of the gift I longed to confer on her—the gift of all my affections; model of truth and honour, of independence and conscientiousness—those refiners and sustainers of an honest life; silent possessor of a well of tenderness, of a flame, as genial as still, as pure as quenchless, of natural feeling, natural passion—those sources of refreshment and comfort to the sanctuary of home. I knew how quietly and how deeply the well bubbled in her heart; I knew how the more dangerous flame burned safely under the eye of reason; I had seen when the fire shot up a moment high and vivid, when the accelerated heat troubled life's current in its channels; I had seen reason reduce the rebel, and humble its blaze to embers.
我喜欢此时此刻她把手放在我手里。我喜欢她站在那的模样，身无分文，无父无母，对于好色之徒来说她毫无魅力，但对我来说她就是块珍宝——她是我这个世界上最同情的人，她想我所想，感我所感；她是我储藏爱的理想圣地；她是谨慎与远见、勤勉与毅力、克己与自制的化身——我渴望将我的礼物交给她，交给这样一个值得信赖的人看管——那就是我的全部感情；她是诚实、忠贞、独立和责任感的典范，这些品质能够维持一种真正的生活；她默默地拥有一口深井，充满了温柔和激情，她和蔼宁静，纯洁超然，天生感情丰富而强烈——这些对组成一个美好家庭都是必要的，是温馨舒适的来源。我知道她心里的井是多么地平静而深邃；我知道那危险的火焰在她理智的目光下燃烧得多么安全；我曾见过当火焰产生的热量扰乱生命之河时，火焰会在瞬间窜高迸裂；我也曾见过理智战胜反抗，将火焰泯为灰烬。
I had confidence in Frances Evans; I had respect for her, and as I drew her arm through mine and led her out of the cemetery, I felt I had another sentiment, as strong as confidence, as firm as respect, more fervid than either—that of love.
我对弗朗西斯·埃文斯有信心；我尊重她，当我挽着她的手臂带她走出那块墓地时，我有了另一种感情，如同信心一样强烈，同敬重一样坚定，又比两者都要热忱——那就是爱。
'Well, my pupil,' said I, as the ominous-sounding gate swung to behind us—'well, I have found you again. A month's search has seemed long, and I little thought to have discovered my lost sheep straying amongst graves.' 
“好了，我的学生，”当那发出刺耳声音的铁门在我们身后关上时，我说道：“这下，我又找到你了。我已经苦寻你一个月之久了，怎么也没想到我迷失的羔羊会在墓场里晃荡。”
Never had I addressed her but as 'mademoiselle' before, and to speak thus was to take up a tone new to both her and me. Her answer apprised me that this language ruffled none of her feelings, woke no discord in her heart.
我以前都是称呼她“小姐”，而现在这样说话，对我们来说都是一种崭新的方式。她的回答让我知道她对这种方式并不生气也不反感。
'Mon maitre,' she said, 'have you troubled yourself to seek me? I little imagined you would think much of my absence, but I grieved bitterly to be taken away from you. I was sorry for that circumstance when heavier troubles ought to have made me forget it.' 
“老师，”她说，“您真的这么费心找我？我从没想过您会这么关心我的去留，但是被人从您身边赶走我确实很伤心。对那件事我真的很抱歉，更加沉重的打击本该使我忘记它，但我忘不了。”
'Your aunt is dead?' 
“你姑妈过世了？”
'Yes, a fortnight since, and she died full of regret, which I could not chase from her mind. “小姐，拿一本英语书出来，因为雨还下得很大，我可能还得再呆半个小时。”她一下子松了一口气，站起身拿了一本书，转身坐在我给她在我旁边安排的椅子上。她从古典文学书架上选了一本《失乐园》，我猜她觉得宗教性质的书最适合礼拜天读。我让她从头读起，当她读到弥尔顿向神圣的缪斯祈祷，缪斯曾在“神秘的奥勒布山或西奈山山顶”向一位希伯来牧羊人讲述世界的概念是怎样从混沌的状态中产生并且成熟的，我完全沉浸于三重享受之中：一是她在我的身边，二是听见她的嗓音——在我耳边那甜美悦耳的嗓音——，三是不时欣赏一下她的面孔。最后一种享受我是通过纠正她语调、顿号或者重音等方式达到的，只要我坚持自己的意见时我便盯着她看，但也不至让她羞红脸。
'Enough,' said I, when she had gone through some half-dozen pages (a work of time with her, for she read slowly, and paused often to ask and receive information), 'enough; and now the rain is ceasing, and I must soon go.' For, indeed, at that moment, looking towards the window, I saw it all blue. The thunder-clouds were broken and scattered, and the setting August sun sent a gleam like the reflection of rubies through the lattice. I got up; I drew on my gloves.
“可以了，”当她读完六页左右我对她说（她读了很长时间，因为她读得很慢，还经常停下来问我问题，并听我给她讲解），“可以了；现在雨停了，我必须马上走了。”因为那时我朝窗外看，天空确实已经变蓝了。乌云已经四散开来，八月的夕阳透过窗格投入一束光芒，犹如红宝石上的反光。我站了起来，戴上了手套。

'Well, well, ere long I shall pay you another visit, Good-evening now,' and I left her rather abruptly. I had much ado to resist a strong inward impulse, urging me to take a warmer, a more expressive leave. What so natural as to fold her for a moment in a close embrace, to imprint one kiss on her cheek and forehead? I was not unreasonable; that was all I wanted. Satisfied in that point I could go away content. And Reason denied me even this. She ordered me to turn my eyes from her face and my steps from her apartment—to quit her as dryly and coldly as I would have quitted old Madame Pelet. I obeyed, but I swore rancorously to be avenged one day. I'll earn a right to do as I please in this matter, or I'll die in the contest. I have one object before me now—to get that Genevese girl for my wife; and my wife she shall be—that is, provided she has as much, or half as much, regard for her master as he has for her. And would she be so docile, so smiling, so happy under my instructions if she had not? Would she sit at my side, when I dictate or correct, with such a still, contented, halcyon mien? For I had ever remarked that, how- ever sad or harassed her countenance might be when I entered a room, yet after I had been near her, spoken to her a few words, given her some directions, uttered perhaps some reproofs, she would, all at once, nestle into a nook of happiness, and look up serene and revived. The reproofs suited her best of all. While I scolded she would chip away with her penknife at a pencil or a pen, fidgeting a little, pouting a little, defending herself by monosyllables; and when I deprived her of the pen or pencil, fearing it would be all cut away, and when I interdicted even the monosyllabic defence, for the purpose of working up the subdued excitement a little higher, she would at last raise her eyes and give me a certain glance, sweetened with gaiety, and pointed with defiance, which, to speak truth, thrilled me as nothing had ever done, and made me, in a fashion (though happily she did not know it), her subject, if not her slave. After such little scenes her spirits would maintain their flow, often for some hours, and, as, I remarked before, her health therefrom took a sustenance and vigour which, previously to the event of her aunt's death and her dismissal, had almost recreated her whole frame.
“好的，好的，不久后我会再来看你，现在晚安了。”就这样我突然地离开了她。我费了很大力气才抵制住内心的强烈欲望，那种欲望促使我想要跟她告别时更温情些，更意味深长些。如果将她紧紧拥入怀中一阵，再在她脸蛋和额头上亲吻一下，那也是很自然吧？我并非不切实际，那就是我想要的全部。那样的话我就可以心满意足地走了。然而理智甚至连这一点也没有允许我。它命令我从她脸上收回目光，从她的房间走出去——像离开珀莱夫人那样冷淡无情地离开她。我服从了，但是我满怀恨意地发誓终有一天要报复。在这场抗争中，我宁死也要赢得想干什么就干什么的权利。现在在我前方有一个目标——就是要娶到那位日内瓦姑娘为妻，只要她对我这个老师的关心跟我对她的关心一样或者只有一半，那我就要娶她为妻。如果她不是那样关心我，那在我教她学习时她怎么会那么温顺，那样微笑而且那么开心呢？当我口授或者改错时，她为何那么安静，那么满足又那么愉快地坐在我身边？我之前说过，当我走进房间时，不管之前她的脸色多么地伤心或者多么地苦闷，只要我走到她的身边，跟对她说上几句话，给她一点指导，或者责备她几句，她都会立刻陷入幸福之中，平静又振奋地抬头看着我。我的责备在她那儿最适用。当我责怪她时，她总是拿着一把铅笔刀削着铅笔或者钢笔，烦躁不安地噘着嘴巴，不时简短地为自己辩解几句。每当我因为怕她把笔都削完而夺走她的钢笔或铅笔时，每当我为了活跃气氛而打断她的辩解时，她就会抬起眼睛，以一种特殊的眼神瞥向我，说真话，那甜蜜快乐又带有挑战性的一瞥令我激动不已——从来没有什么能让我这样激动——并使我稀里糊涂地（幸亏她不知道这一点）即便不做她的奴隶，也甘愿臣服于她。在这些小事之后她的精神会很高涨，常常长达几个小时，而且正如我之前说过的，她的身体从此吸收了能量与活力，在她姑妈去世和她被解雇之前，这种能量与活力几乎使她的躯体获得了新生。
It has taken me several minutes to write these last sentences; but I had thought all their purport during the brief interval of descending the stairs from Frances' room. Just as I was opening the outer door I remembered the twenty francs which I had not restored. I paused—impossible to carry them away with me, difficult to force them back on their original owner. I had now seen her in her own humble abode, witnessed the dignity of her poverty, the pride of order, the fastidious care of conservatism, obvious in the arrangement and economy of her little home. I was sure she would not suffer herself to be excused paying her debts. I was certain the favour of indemnity would be accepted from no hand, perhaps least of all from mine. Yet these four five-franc pieces were a burden to my self- respect, and I must get rid of them. An expedient—a clumsy one no doubt, but the best I could devise—suggested itself to me. I darted up the stairs, knocked, re-entered the room as if in haste,—'Mademoiselle, I have forgotten one of my gloves. I must have left it here.' 
上面的几句话我是花了好几分钟才写出来的，但这一切都是我在从弗朗西斯房间出来下楼梯的短暂时间里想出的。就在我打开前门时，忽然想起我还没有还她那二十法郎。我停住了——我不可能再拿着那些钱，但是要强行把它们物归原主也很难。我现在已经见到了她那简陋的住所，目睹了她贫困中的傲骨，有序中的傲气，以及对于保守传统的讲究挑剔，更了解了她小家的布局与节俭。我确定，如果我还给她这学费，她会感到痛苦的。我确定她不会接受任何人的恩惠，尤其是从我的手中。然而这四枚五法郎硬币对我的自尊心也是一种负担，我必须摆脱。我忽然想出一个权宜之计——毫无疑问，这是个笨办法，但却是我能想到的最好的。我冲到楼上，敲了敲门，装作很急地又进了她的房间：“小姐，我忘了一只手套。肯定是落在这儿了。”

Brief as my impromptu return had been, it had afforded me time to pick up a heartache. I remarked that Frances had already removed the red embers of her cheerful little fire from the grate. Forced to calculate every item, to save in every detail, she had instantly on my departure retrenched a luxury too expensive, to be enjoyed alone.
尽管这次即兴的回访很短，但却让我有时间去掉一块心病。我看到弗朗西斯已经将烧得发红的煤块从跳跃着的炉火夹了下来。迫于要计较每个铜板，才会在每处细节上省钱，所以我一离开她就熄了火，她认为一个人烧那么旺的火太奢侈了。

Already the pavement was drying; a balmy and fresh breeze stirred the air, purified by lightning. I felt the west behind me, where spread a sky like opal; azure immingled with crimson. The enlarged sun, glorious in Tyrian tints, dipped his brim already. Stepping, as I was, eastward, I faced a vast bank of clouds, but also I had before me the arch of an evening rainbow—a perfect rainbow, high, wide, vivid. I looked long; my eye drank in the scene, and I suppose my brain must have absorbed it; for that night, after lying awake in pleasant fever a long time, watching the silent sheet-lightning, which still played among the retreating clouds, and flashed silvery over the stars. I at last fell asleep; and then in a dream were reproduced the setting sun, the bank of clouds, the mighty rain- bow. I stood, methought, on a terrace. I leaned over a parapeted wall. there was space below me, depth I could not fathom; but hearing an endless dash of waves, I believed it to be the sea—sea spread to the horizon, sea of changeful green and intense blue; all was soft in the distance, all vapour-veiled. A spark of gold glistened on the line between water and air, floated up, approached, enlarged, changed; the object hung midway between heaven and earth under the arch of the rainbow; the soft but dusk clouds diffused behind. It hovered as on wings; pearly, fleecy, gleaming air streamed like raiment round it; light, tinted with carnation, coloured what seemed face and limbs; a large star shone with still lustre on an angel's forehead; an upraised arm and hand, glancing like a ray, pointed to the bow overhead, and a voice in my heart whispered, 'Hope smiles on effort!'
