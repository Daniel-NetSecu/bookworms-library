Chapter 19  THE CHILD AT THE BROOK-SIDE  

第十九章  溪边的孩子  

Thou wilt love her dearly, " repeated Hester Prynne, as she and the minister sat watching little Pearl. "Dost thou not think her beautiful? And see with what natural skill she has made those simple flowers adorn her! Had she gathered pearls, and diamonds, and rubies, in the wood, they could not have become her better. She is a splendid child! But I know whose brow she has!"
“你会非常喜欢她的。”赫斯特·普林和牧师坐在一起望着小珠儿，重复道。“你不觉得她漂亮吗？看，她能用普通的花朵打扮自己，那真是生来的本事！就算她能在林子里采集到珍珠、钻石和红宝石，那些也不见得能把她点缀得更漂亮。她是个出色的孩子！但我清楚她的额头长得像谁！”
"Dost thou know, Hester, " said Arthur Dimmesdale, with an unquiet smile, "that this dear child, tripping about always at thy side, hath caused me many an alarm? Methought — O Hester, what a thought is that, and how terrible to dread it!—     that my own features were partly repeated in her face, and so strikingly that the world might see them! But she is mostly thine!"
“你知道么，赫斯特，”阿瑟·丁梅斯代尔微笑着说，却有些不安，“这个总在你身边蹦蹦跳跳的可爱的孩子，曾有多么让我惊恐啊？我曾想——噢，赫斯特，那是怎样的念头啊，那种担心真是恐怖啊！——她的一些面容会与我惊人相似，世人兴许会认出来！不过她还是更像你！”
"No, no! Not mostly!" answered the mother, with a tender smile. "A little longer, and thou needest not be afraid to trace whose child she is. But how strangely beautiful she looks, with those wild flowers in her hair! It is as if one of the fairies, whom we left in our dear old England, had decked her out to meet us."
“不，不！不是更像我！”做母亲的温柔地笑着，回答说。“过不了多久，你就不用担心人们追究她是谁的孩子了。不过她头戴那些野花，看上去真是出奇地漂亮啊！就好像一位仙子，她被我们留在了亲爱的老英格兰，现在她把自己打扮起来了，来迎接我们。”
It was with a feeling which neither of them had ever before experienced, that they sat and watched Pearl's slow advance. In her was visible the tie that united them. She had been offered to the world, these seven years past, as the living hieroglyphic, in which was revealed the secret they so darkly sought to hide — all written in this symbol — all plainly manifest — had there been a prophet or magician skilled to read the character of flame! And Pearl was the oneness of their being. Be the foregone evil what it might, how could they doubt that their earthly lives and future destinies were conjoined, when they beheld at once the material union, and the spiritual idea, in whom they met, and were to dwell immortally together? Thoughts like these — and perhaps other thoughts, which they did not acknowledge or define — threw an awe about the child, as she came onward.
他们坐在一块儿望着珠儿慢慢地走来，那种感觉是他俩都前所未有的。她身上联系两人的纽带依稀可见。这过去的七年，她像活生生的象形文字一样来到世上，揭示了他们竭力隐藏的秘密。要是有先知或法师能读懂那火焰般的文字，就会明白，一切都写在符号之中，显而易见。而珠儿就是他们生命的融合。不论先前有怎样的罪恶，一旦看到那肉体的结晶和精神的概念，看到那将永远共存的交汇体时，他们怎能怀疑他们前世今生的命运不是联系在一起的呢？这样的想法——也许还有一些他们没意识到的，或无法描述的想法——让那正走来的孩子显得令人敬畏。
"Let her see nothing strange — no passion nor eagerness — in thy way of accosting her, " whispered Hester. "Our Pearl is a fitful and fantastic little elf, sometimes. Especially, she is seldom tolerant of emotion, when she does not fully comprehend the why and wherefore. But the child hath strong affections! She loves me, and will love thee!"
“和她搭话时不要让她感到奇怪，不要太热情，也别太急切。”赫斯特低声说。“我们的珠儿有时精灵古怪。特别是，她在摸不着头脑时，受不了情感的流露。不过这孩子感情强烈！她爱我，而且也会爱你的！”
"Thou canst not think, " said the minister, glancing aside at Hester Prynne, "how my heart dreads this interview, and yearns for it! But, in truth, as I already told thee, children are not readily won to be familiar with me. They will not climb my knee, nor prattle in my ear, nor answer to my smile; but stand apart, and eye me strangely. Even little babes, when I take them in my arms, weep bitterly. Yet Pearl, twice in her little lifetime, hath been kind to me! The first time — thou knowest it well! The last was when thou ledst her with thee to the house of yonder stern old Governor."
“你难以想象，”牧师偏过头来看了眼赫斯特·普林，说道，“对这次见面，我是多么害怕，又是多么向往！不过说真的，像我对你说的那样，我不太容易赢得孩子的亲近。他们不会爬上我的膝头，不会在我耳边胡乱讲话，也不会回应我的微笑。他们只会离我远远的，奇怪地盯着我看。即便是小娃娃，我抱着他们，他们也会伤心地哭鼻子。不过珠儿才这么大，倒是有两次对我挺好的！第一次嘛，你很清楚！第二次就是你领她到那严肃的老总督家里去时。”
"And thou didst plead so bravely in her behalf and mine!" answered the mother. "I remember it; and so shall little Pearl. Fear nothing! She may be strange and shy at first, but will soon learn to love thee!"
“那次你为了我们母女进行了大胆的申辩。”做母亲的回答。“我记着呢，小珠儿也会记得的。别担心！她起初可能会感到陌生和害羞，但很快就能喜欢上你的！”
By this time Pearl had reached the margin of the brook and stood on the farther side, gazing silently at Hester and the clergyman, who still sat together on the mossy tree-trunk, waiting to receive her. Just where she had paused, the brook chanced to form a pool, so smooth and quiet that it reflected a perfect image of her little figure, with all the brilliant picturesqueness of her beauty, in its adornment of flowers and wreathed foliage, but more refined and spiritualised than the reality. This image, so nearly identical with the living Pearl, seemed to communicate somewhat of its own shadowy and intangible quality to the child herself. It was strange, the way in which Pearl stood, looking so steadfastly at them through the dim medium of the forest-gloom; herself, meanwhile, all glorified with a ray of sunshine that was attracted thitherward as by a certain sympathy. In the brook beneath stood another child — another and the same — with likewise its ray of golden light. Hester felt herself, in some indistinct and tantalising manner, estranged from Pearl; as if the child, in her lonely ramble through the forest, had strayed out of the sphere in which she and her mother dwelt together, and was now vainly seeking to return to it.
这会儿珠儿已经到了小溪对岸，一声不响地望着赫斯特和牧师。他俩依旧一起坐在长满青苔的树干上，等着见她。就在她停下的那个地方，小溪恰好形成了一个水塘，水面平静而光滑；在花朵和树叶编成的装饰下，她那如画的美丽倒映其中，玲珑别致，比她本人更精致而富有灵性。这倒影像极了珠儿本人，仿佛向她传达着自己那阴影般莫测的品性。珠儿站的姿态有些怪，她透过林荫紧紧地盯着他们，同时一束阳光像是受了某种感应，令她光辉四射。她脚下的小溪里，站着另一个孩子，另一个一模一样的孩子，身上同样是金光熠熠。赫斯特莫名而痛心地感到，自己与珠儿变得陌生起来，仿佛那孩子一个人在树林里游荡着，竟走出了她们母女共同生活的界线，努力地想回来却总是徒劳。
There was both truth and error in the impression; the child and mother were estranged, but through Hester's fault, not Pearl's. Since the latter rambled from her side, another inmate had been admitted within the circle of the mother's feelings, and so modified the aspect of them all, that Pearl, the returning wanderer, could not find her wonted place, and hardly knew where she was.
这感觉几分是真，几分是假。这母女是疏远了些，但原因在于赫斯特，而不是珠儿。自从孩子离开母亲那会儿，另一个亲人来到母亲的感情圈内，从而改变了三人的地位。珠儿这个回来的流浪儿，找不到她一向的位置，几乎不知道自己身在何方了。
"I have a strange fancy, " observed the sensitive minister, "that this brook is the boundary between two worlds, and that thou canst never meet thy Pearl again. Or is she an elfish spirit, who, as the legends of our childhood taught us, is forbidden to cross a running stream? Pray hasten her; for this delay has already imparted a tremor to my nerves."
“我有个离奇的想法，”敏感的牧师观察到，“这小溪是两个世界的边界，你再也见不到珠儿了。或者她是我们儿时故事里的精灵，不被允许跨过这流淌的溪流？请快催催她，她迟迟不现身，都让我神经发抖了。”
"Come, dearest child!" said Hester encouragingly, and stretching out both her arms. "How slow thou art! When hast thou been so sluggish before now? Here is a friend of mine, who must be thy friend also. Thou wilt have twice as much love, henceforward, as thy mother alone could give thee! Leap across the brook and come to us. Thou canst leap like a young deer!"
“过来，亲爱的宝贝！”赫斯特伸出双手，带着鼓励的口吻。“你真是慢啊！你什么时候像现在这么懒洋洋的了？这里有我的一个朋友，也会成为你的朋友的。从现在起，你不只有妈妈一个人的爱了，你会得到双倍的爱！跳过那小溪，过来吧。你不是跳得像一头小鹿嘛！”
Pearl, without responding in any manner to these honey-sweet expressions, remained on the other side of the brook. Now she fixed her bright, wild eyes on her mother, now on the minister, and now included them both in the same glance, as if to detect and explain to herself the relation which they bore to one another. For some unaccountable reason, as Arthur Dimmesdale felt the child's eyes upon himself, his hand — with that gesture so habitual as to have become involuntary — stole over his heart. At length, assuming a singular air of authority, Pearl stretched out her hand, with the small forefinger extended, and pointing evidently towards her mother's breast. And beneath, in the mirror of the brook, there was the flower-girdled and sunny image of little Pearl, pointing her small forefinger too.
珠儿呆在对岸，对这甜蜜的话语不作一丝回应。她时而用明亮而狂野的眼睛盯着妈妈看，时而又盯着牧师看，时而同时盯着他们两个看，似乎要弄清并向自己解释两人的关系。阿瑟•丁梅斯代尔莫名地感到孩子正盯着自己，手不自觉地因习惯而悄悄捂住了心口。终于，珠儿作出一副独特的不容置辩的表情，伸出那小小的手指，显然是指向她妈妈的胸部。脚下，镜子般的水面倒映着小珠儿的身影，她戴着花环，身披阳光，同样伸着那小小的食指。
"Thou strange child, why dost thou not come to me?" exclaimed Hester.
“你这奇怪的孩子，怎么不过来呢？”赫斯特叫道。
Pearl still pointed with her forefinger; and a frown gathered on her brow, the more impressive from the childish, the almost baby-like aspect of the features that conveyed it. As her mother still kept beckoning to her and arraying her face in a holiday suit of unaccustomed smiles, the child stamped her foot with a yet more imperious look and gesture. In the brook, again, was the fantastic beauty of the image, with its reflected frown, its pointed finger, and imperious gesture, giving emphasis to the aspect of little Pearl.
珠儿还是伸着食指，皱起眉头，那孩子气的、婴儿般的姿态，让人印象尤深。她母亲还是在呼唤她，脸上露出不同以往的笑容，那孩子就摆出更加专横的神情和姿态，跺起了脚来。小溪里面依旧是那美得出奇的倒影，皱着眉头，伸着手指，姿势专横，为小珠儿平添了几分模样。
"Hasten, Pearl; or I shall be angry with thee!" cried Hester Prynne, who, however inured to such behaviour on the elf-child's part at other seasons, was naturally anxious for a more seemly deportment now. "Leap across the brook, naughty child, and run hither! Else I must come to thee!"
“快啊，珠儿，不然我可要生气了！”赫斯特喊道。无论她平时多么习惯这孩子的古灵精怪，但此时自然巴不得她能规矩些。“跳过那小溪，调皮鬼，跑过来！不然我就过去了！”
But Pearl, not a whit startled at her mother's threats any more than mollified by her entreaties, now suddenly burst into a fit of passion, gesticulating violently, and throwing her small figure into the most extravagant contortions. She accompanied this wild outbreak with piercing shrieks, which the woods reverberated on all sides; so that, alone as she was in her childish and unreasonable wrath, it seemed as if a hidden multitude were lending her their sympathy and encouragement. Seen in the brook, once more, was the shadowy wraith of Pearl's image, crowned and girdled with flowers, but stamping its foot, wildly gesticulating, and, in the midst of all, still pointing its small forefinger at Hester's bosom!
可珠儿似乎对母亲的恳求无动于衷，她一点也没有被母亲的威胁吓到。这会儿，她突然闹脾气了，作出凶狠的姿态，夸张地扭动着小小的身子。她的爆发还伴着尖叫，声音在树林四处回响。这样，她虽是一个人乱发孩子脾气，却好像有许多隐藏着的人在同情她，鼓励她。溪水中还是可以看到珠儿的倒影，头戴花冠，腰缠花带，跺着脚，暴躁地做着动作，同时，还是用食指指着赫斯特的胸部！
"I see what ails the child, " whispered Hester to the clergyman, and turning pale in spite of a strong effort to conceal her trouble and annoyance. "Children will not abide any, the slightest, change in the accustomed aspect of things that are daily before their eyes. Pearl misses something which she has always seen me wear."
“我知道是什么在烦扰孩子了，”赫斯特低声对牧师说道。她虽然努力掩饰着自己的困扰，却还是面色发白。“孩子不能容忍那些习以为常的事物有任何轻微的改变。珠儿看不到我那不离身的徽记了。”
"I pray you, " answered the minister, "if thou hast any means of pacifying the child, do it forthwith! Save it were the cankered wrath of an old witch, like Mistress Hibbins, " added he, attempting to smile, "I know nothing that I would not sooner encounter than this passion in a child. In Pearl's young beauty, as in the wrinkled witch, it has a preternatural effect. Pacify her, if thou lovest me!"
“我求求你，”牧师应道，“要是有什么办法能让孩子平静下来，就马上行动吧！只要不是老妖婆式的发怒，像西宾斯太太那样。”他强笑着补充说，“再也没有比这孩子发脾气更不愿让人见到的了。年轻漂亮的珠儿和那满是皱纹的老妖婆一样，身上有种超自然的力量。让她安静下来吧，如果你爱我！”
Hester turned again towards Pearl with a crimson blush upon her cheek, a conscious glance aside at the clergyman, and then a heavy sigh; while, even before she had time to speak, the blush yielded to a deadly pallor.
赫斯特又转向珠儿，面颊绯红，下意识地斜睨了眼牧师，深深地叹了口气。她还没来得及说话，绯红的脸已经变得苍白了。
"Pearl, " said she, sadly, "look down at thy feet! There — before thee! — the hither side of the brook!"
“珠儿，”她说，“看看你脚下！那里，就在你跟前！——小溪的另一边！”
The child turned her eyes to the point indicated; and there lay the scarlet letter, so close upon the margin of the stream that the gold embroidery was reflected in it.
孩子把目光转向所指的地方——红字就躺在那里，紧靠着岸边，那金刺绣的光反射在水面上。
"Bring it hither!" said Hester.
“把它捡回来！”赫斯特说。
"Come thou and take it up!" answered Pearl.
“你过来捡吧！”珠儿回答。
"Was ever such a child!" observed Hester, aside to the minister. "Oh, I have much to tell thee about her! But, in very truth, she is right as regards this hateful token. I must bear its torture yet a little longer — only a few days longer — until we shall have left this region and look back hither as to a land which we have dreamed of. The forest cannot hide it! The mid-ocean shall take it from my hand, and swallow it up forever!"
“哪有这样的孩子！”赫斯特对一旁的牧师说道。“噢，我有很多关于她的事情要告诉你！不过，关于这徽记的事她确实没有错。我还得多忍受会儿这折磨人的玩意儿——几天就好——直到我们离开这地方。等我们再回头看时，这就只是一块曾经的梦想之地了。这树林藏不住它！大海可以从我手里带走它，永久地把它吞没！”
With these words, she advanced to the margin of the brook, took up the scarlet letter, and fastened it again into her bosom. Hopefully, but a moment ago, as Hester had spoken of drowning it in the deep sea, there was a sense of inevitable doom upon her, as she thus received back this deadly symbol from the hand of fate. She had flung it into infinite space! — she had drawn an hour's free breath! — and here again was the scarlet misery, glittering on the old spot! So it ever is, whether thus typified or no, that an evil deed invests itself with the character of doom. Hester next gathered up the heavy tresses of her hair, and confined them beneath her cap. As if there were a withering spell in the sad letter, her beauty, the warmth and richness of her womanhood, departed, like fading sunshine; and a grey shadow seemed to fall across her.
说完这些，她走到小溪边上，捡起那红字，把它重新戴在胸前。片刻之前，赫斯特还满怀希望地说要把红字沉到海底，可当她从命运之手中接过那致命的徽记时，她感到了一种难逃之劫。她已经把它抛向了无限的空间！她呼吸了一小时的自由空气！这会儿，那红色的痛苦又回来了，在老地方闪闪发光！罪恶的行为不管有否表征，总是厄运在身的。于是，赫斯特挽起那浓密的头发，用帽子盖了起来。那哀伤的字母像是被下了诅咒，她的美丽，和那女性特质的丰富和温情，如落日般褪去了，仿佛有一抹灰影落在她身上。
When the dreary change was wrought, she extended her hand to Pearl.
在这阴郁的变化后，她向珠儿伸出了手。
"Dost thou know thy mother now, child?" asked she, reproachfully, but with a subdued tone. "Wilt thou come across the brook and own thy mother, now that she has her shame upon her — now that she is sad?"
“你现在认识妈妈了吗，孩子？”她有些责备地问道，却克制着自己的语气。“妈妈又戴上了她的耻辱，妈妈又伤心了，你现在可以跨过小溪认你妈妈了吧？”
"Yes; now I will!" answered the child, bounding across the brook, and clasping Hester in her arms. "Now thou art my mother indeed! And I am thy little Pearl!"
“好的，我过来了！”孩子说着，跳过了小溪，搂住了赫斯特。“现在你真的是我妈妈了！我是你的小珠儿！”
In a mood of tenderness that was not usual with her, she drew down her mother's head, and kissed her brow and both her cheeks. But then — by a kind of necessity that always impelled this child to alloy whatever comfort she might chance to give with a throb of anguish — Pearl put up her mouth, and kissed the scarlet letter too!
她拽着母亲的头，用一种不常有的温柔，亲了亲她的额头和双颊。不过，在偶尔给人的欣慰中，这孩子总免不了融进一阵苦恼。她撅起嘴，也亲了亲那红字！
"That was not kind!" said Hester. "When thou hast shown me a little love, thou mockest me!"
“那可不好！”赫斯特说。“你才对我表示了一些爱，就取笑我！”
"Why doth the minister sit yonder?" asked Pearl.
“牧师为什么坐在那里？”珠儿问。
"He waits to welcome thee, " replied her mother. "Come thou, and entreat his blessing! He loves thee, my little Pearl, and loves thy mother too. Wilt thou not love him? Come! he longs to greet thee!"
“他等着欢迎你呢，”她母亲回答。“你过来，请求他的赐福吧！他爱你，我的小珠儿，他也爱妈妈。你难道不喜欢他吗？过来！他可想要问候你呢！”
"Doth he love us?" said Pearl, looking up with acute intelligence into her mother's face. "Will he go back with us, hand in hand, we three together, into the town?"
“他爱我们吗？”珠儿说，抬眼望着母亲的脸，敏锐而聪慧。“他会和我们手拉手，三个人一起回镇里去吗？”
"Not now, dear child, " answered Hester. "But in days to come he will walk hand in hand with us. We will have a home and fireside of our own; and thou shalt sit upon his knee; and he will teach thee many things, and love thee dearly. Thou wilt love him; wilt thou not?"
“现在不行，亲爱的宝贝。”赫斯特回答说。“但以后我们会手拉手走在一起的。我们会有自己的家和壁炉，你会坐在他膝盖上，他会教你很多东西，会深深地爱你。你会爱他的，不是吗？”
"And will he always keep his hand over his heart?" inquired Pearl.
“他会经常把手捂在心口吗？”珠儿问道。
"Foolish child, what a question is that!" exclaimed her mother. "Come and ask his blessing!"
“傻孩子，这算什么问题啊！”她母亲大声说。“过来请求他的祈福！”
But, whether influenced by the jealousy that seems instinctive with every petted child towards a dangerous rival, or from whatever caprice of her freakish nature, Pearl would show no favour to the clergyman. It was only by an exertion of force that her mother brought her up to him, hanging back and manifesting her reluctance by odd grimaces; of which, ever since her babyhood, she had possessed a singular variety, and could transform her mobile physiognomy into a series of different aspects, with a new mischief in them, each and all. The minister — painfully embarrassed, but hoping that a kiss might prove a talisman to admit him into the child's kindlier regards — bent forward, and impressed one on her brow. Hereupon, Pearl broke away from her mother, and, running to the brook, stooped over it, and bathed her forehead, until the unwelcome kiss was quite washed off, and diffused through a long lapse of the gliding water. She then remained apart, silently watching Hester and the clergyman, while they talked together, and made such arrangements as were suggested by their new position, and the purposes soon to be fulfilled.
不过，不知是出于每个宝贝孩子对危险对手本能的嫉妒，还是她那古灵精怪的天性，珠儿就是不喜欢牧师。她被妈妈硬拉着来到牧师跟前，还一个劲地往回缩，扮着奇怪的鬼脸以示不情愿。她自打婴儿时期起，就会做些独特的鬼脸，把那活泼的面孔变成不同的表情，每一个表情都带着新的恶作剧。牧师又是难过又是尴尬。他想，一个亲吻或许会起到奇异的效果，让他和孩子更亲近些，于是便弯腰向前，亲了下她的额头。珠儿一下挣脱她母亲，跑向小溪，俯下身子洗她的额头，直到那不受欢迎的亲吻完全洗干净了，扩散到那长长的流水之中。然后她远远地呆在一边，静静地看着赫斯特和牧师。他们正一起说着话，为新的情况和马上要去实行的目标做着各种安排。
And now this fateful interview had come to a close. The dell was to be left a solitude among its dark, old trees, which, with their multitudinous tongues, would whisper long of what had passed there, and no mortal be the wiser. And the melancholy brook would add this other tale to the mystery with which its little heart was already overburdened, and whereof it still kept up a murmuring babble, with not a whit more cheerfulness of tone than for ages heretofore.
现在，这命运攸关的见面将近尾声。小山谷中留下的是一片孤寂，散落在这幽暗而古老的树木中。老树们七嘴八舌地窃窃私语了很久，谈论着这儿所发生的事情，没有人会比它们知道得更清楚。这忧郁的小溪，将在它那已经不堪重负的小小心灵中，再加上另一个神秘的故事。它依旧潺潺低语，音调不会比先前的那些世纪多半点欢快。
